For the first time, the SFC has partnered with Lingnan University to launch a course on foundations of investing for undergraduates.

The credit-based course is offered in response to the findings of the SFC’s Retail Investor Survey 2003, which showed the public felt investor education should start at school, especially as courses in tertiary institutions (Note 1).

Entitled “The Craft of Financial Investing”, the course consists of two levels of four sessions each. The course aims at helping students acquire fundamental knowledge of market infrastructure and investment risks, and cultivate a proper attitude towards financial management, so that they can become smart investors in future.

The course will be offered as part of Civic Education under Lingnan University’s Integrated Learning Programme (ILP) (Note 2). Students will earn eight ILP units if they attend all the four sessions of either level, and a total of 16 ILP units if they participate in both levels.  

Through interactive lectures, case studies and field trips, students of the Elementary Level course will learn more about stock and fund investment activities and how to better safeguard their interests in using securities services.  The Elementary Level course will start in October this year. The Advanced Level course will focus on more complicated corporate activities of listed companies, as well as characteristics and trading know-how of complex products, such as hedge funds, warrants, derivatives and structured products. Lingnan undergraduates can contact the University’s Student Services Centre at 2616 7304 for enrolment details. 

The SFC has in the past two years organised a general education course with The University of Hong Kong and public lectures with the Open University. The favourable responses showed that there was demand for investor education at universities. The SFC will continue to explore further opportunities to collaborate with the education sector to provide investor education programmes for our younger generation.

Notes:

1. According to the Survey, 67.7% of retail investors considered investor education should begin at school.  The survey report can be downloaded from the SFC website, under the “Press Releases, Publications & Speech” section.
