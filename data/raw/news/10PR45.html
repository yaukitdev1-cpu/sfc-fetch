Before investing, investors should make sure that the intermediary they are going to deal with is operating with a proper licence.

In his latest column, “Find out More about Licensed Intermediaries,” Dr Wise briefly explains the licensing regime of intermediaries in the Hong Kong securities and futures industry, and tells investors where to find licensing information about intermediaries.

Companies and individuals providing services for dealing in or advising on securities, futures and leveraged foreign exchange contracts in Hong Kong must be first licensed by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), Dr Wise notes. With one single licence, an intermediary can carry on more than one type of dealing or advisory activities after fulfilling the relevant requirements. Also, banks have to register with the SFC for conducting securities and futures business in Hong Kong, even though the Hong Kong Monetary Authority is their frontline regulator.

Before handing over their money or investment portfolio, investors can run safety checks on the SFC’s Public Register of Licensed Persons and Registered Institutions on line to verify if an intermediary is properly licensed to conduct the relevant activities. Besides the basic licence details, the public register also provides other useful licensing information about an intermediary, including the contact information of its complaints officer, the five-year public discipline history, and licence conditions, if applicable.

“Dealing with unlicensed intermediaries puts you at risks as they operate outside the carefully designed framework of the market,” Dr Wise reminds his readers. “When in doubt about the intermediary you plan to use, dig deeper. Check before you invest!”

Through his column, Dr Wise, a fictitious character created by the SFC, shares with the public his wisdom on investing and his thoughts on regulatory issues.

Dr Wise’s column is published on the last working Tuesday of every month. The column is posted under the “Dr Wise” section of the SFC’s InvestEd website. Investors are welcome to send their feedback and questions to Dr Wise by email to InvestEd@sfc.hk.
