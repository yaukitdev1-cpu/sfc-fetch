Corporate governance standards of Hong Kong listed companies have improved but are still work in progress, according to Mr Martin Wheatley, SFC’s Chief Executive Officer.

Referring to the HKEx’s Report on implementation of the Code on Corporate Governance Practices, Mr Wheatley said all companies reviewed in the first batch that reported on compliance with the Code met the “comply-or-explain” requirements. However, while nearly 90% of these companies complied with at least 41 of the 44 Code provisions, only a third complied with all 44 provisions.

Speaking in today’s CPA Australia panel discussion, Mr Wheatley said that the Code provisions companies had most difficulty complying with were appointing qualified Non-Executive Directors, separating the roles of the Chairman and CEO, and establishing a remuneration committee with a majority of Independent Non-Executive Directors. Attendance at the Annual General Meeting by the chairmen of the board and the various board committees was also often patchy.

Mr Wheatley said improved transparency could help the market compare and price stocks and manage conflicts of interest, adding that extra care was needed for listed companies that had a dominant shareholder, which was common in Hong Kong.

He said: “Establishing a good corporate governance structure is not merely a box-ticking exercise. It requires the company’s management truly buys into the need for good governance and internal controls, otherwise, the structure will only be a showpiece.”
