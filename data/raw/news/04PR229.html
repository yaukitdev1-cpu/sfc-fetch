The SFC today publishes the Consultation Conclusions and Guidelines on Disclosure of Fees and Charges Relating to Securities Services (Note 1).

The Guidelines supplement the existing Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission in respect of disclosure of fees and charges for securities services to clients and recommend disclosure of such fees and charges under six standardised categories commonly used in the industry. They are:

1. Trade-related services;
2. Scrip handling and settlement-related services;
3. Information services;
4. Account maintenance;
5. Nominee services and corporate actions; and
6. Financing and other services.

The Code of Conduct currently requires all licensed / registered persons (including brokers and banks) to make adequate disclosure of relevant material information in dealing with clients, including information on fees and charges. The Guidelines provide more specific guidance in this respect.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The recommended standardisation is a step forward in enhancing pricing transparency and comparability in the market and promoting fair competition. With more than 70 items of securities-related fees and charges currently adopted by different intermediaries, investors will find it easier to understand and compare them under standardised categories and be able to make informed decision."

"We believe that once the Guidelines are in place, together with the SFC’s investor education efforts, market forces will gradually encourage more and more intermediaries to adopt the Guidelines," Mrs Lam said.

The Guidelines apply to persons licensed or registered for dealing in securities and those licensed for securities margin financing. And they only cover disclosure of fees and charges on services concerning securities traded on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited and exclude disclosure to professional investors.

The Guidelines will come into effect on 1 January 2005.

The Consultation Conclusions paper (which includes the Guidelines) is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website (www.sfc.hk).

Notes to Editor:

1. The SFC issued a consultation paper on the Draft Guidelines on Good Disclosure of Securities Services Related Fees and Charges on 30 October 2003. The SFC received nine submissions from industry participants and other interested parties. The respondents generally welcomed the SFC’s initiative to standardise disclosure of fees and charges under specified categories. Following analysis of the submissions, the SFC further discussed with the respondents, other market participants and relevant parties to finalise and fine-tune the Guidelines, which have been renamed "The Guidelines on Disclosure of Fees and Charges Relating to Securities Services".
