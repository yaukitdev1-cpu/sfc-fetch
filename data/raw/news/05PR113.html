The SFC today publishes for public consultation proposed revisions to the existing  Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Guidance Note.

The main purposes of the revisions are:

1. to bring the Guidance Note in line with the requirements of the revised 40 Recommendations and Nine Special Recommendations for combating money laundering and terrorist financing issued by the Financial Action Task Force on Money Laundering (FATF) (Note 1);

2. to incorporate into the Guidance Note, to the extent appropriate, industry-specific guidance contained in the paper “Client Identification and Beneficial Ownership for the Securities Industry”, issued by the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) (Note 2); and

3. to provide additional guidance on practical measures that licensed corporations and their associated entities are expected to take in effectively implementing anti-money laundering and anti-terrorist financing controls.

One of the key provisions of the Revised Guidance Note is the introduction of a risk-based approach which recognises that certain customers may be of a higher risk category depending on circumstances such as a customer’s background, type of business relationship or transaction, etc.  As such, each of the customer due diligence measures should be applied on a risk sensitive basis. 

The Revised Guidance Note also provides detailed guidance on the customer due diligence measures applicable to specific types of customers, such as omnibus accounts of financial / professional intermediaries and trust / nominee accounts.  Given that there may not be any “one-size-fits-all” approach for licensed corporations and associated entities, the overriding principle is that they should satisfy themselves that the measures taken by them are adequate, appropriate and follow the spirit of the suggested measures.  

Copies of the Consultation Paper are available at the SFC office and on the SFC website, under “Speeches & Publications – Publications – Consultation Papers & Conclusions”.

The public are invited to submit comments by 10 June 2005.  The SFC aims to publish the Consultation Conclusions in the third quarter of 2005 and implement the new guidelines by the end of the year.
