More regulatory issues may arise as electronic trading systems proliferate in world markets, Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, said today.

Speaking at the IOSCO APRC and Roundtable Meetings in Seoul, Mr Wheatley said Asia looked set to be the next frontier for the expansion of electronic trading systems, as competition among the systems in the US and Europe was heating up. (Note 1)

The major markets in Asia have begun to see increased development of electronic trading systems as an alternative to conventional exchanges.

Mr Wheatley said the advent of such electronic “trading venues” made it necessary for regulators to establish a robust regulatory framework within which different market models could operate on a level playing field.

He added that while competition among trading venues and exchanges fostered innovation, the accompanying fragmentation of previously centralised trading could undermine liquidity and transparency in existing exchanges, and therefore the quality of price-formation of the market as a whole.

Mr Wheatley noted that while Hong Kong had developed a regulatory framework for electronic trading systems (Note 2), a regulatory approach had yet to emerge in Asia to deal with liquidity fragmentation, which did not yet represent a major issue given the trading dominance of conventional exchanges in many Asian markets.

Mr Wheatley said the other key issue was the increasing use by intermediaries of electronic “trading tools” such as algorithmic trading and direct market access, which enable brokers or their clients to input a very large number of orders in a short period of time. (Note 3)

“This may impact upon the processing capacity of the exchange systems in extreme situations,” he said. “Fast and large number of order placements at times of extreme market volatility might also raise concern over market stability.”

The increased speed also challenged brokers’ conventional approaches to managing the risk of clients before trading execution, he said, adding that the SFC would keep a watch on whether intermediaries had proper systems and controls for use of electronic trading tools.

Mr Wheatley expected that the increasing use of electronic trading systems would create more issues for regulators in the region and was hopeful appropriate regulations would continue to evolve. He noted, for instance, that two IOSCO standing committees were tasked to examine how exchanges regulate direct market access and how intermediaries manage risk.

Notes :

1. The meetings are organised by the Asia Pacific Regional Committee of the International Organisation of Securities Commissions in Seoul, Korea on 27-29 November.

2. In Hong Kong, under the Securities and Futures Ordinance a provider of trading venues may seek the SFC’s recognition as an exchange company, authorisation as an automated trading service provider, or licence as a broker.

3. Direct market access generally refers to the ability of clients and traders to send orders more directly to the exchange.
