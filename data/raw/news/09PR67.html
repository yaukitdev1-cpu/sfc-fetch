The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) will celebrate two decades of achievement and public service at its 20th anniversary dinner this evening.

The SFC was founded in May 1989, as a direct response to the 1987 market crash, to bring order and structure to Hong Kong’s securities and futures market. Over the past two decades the SFC has developed and established important milestones as Hong Kong itself has grown from a local capital market into an international financial centre.

“Despite the many challenges that have arisen, we have made significant achievements in our 20-year history,” the SFC’s Chairman, Mr Eddy Fong said. “Guiding us through each challenge has been our steadfast commitment to market fairness and investor protection,” he added.

The implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance, the launch of H-shares and red-chips on the stock exchange, and effective enforcement action to deter market misconduct, have each helped keep Hong Kong on track in its evolution into an international financial hub, he said.

“I envisage Hong Kong playing an even more active role in the coming years as a gateway between the Mainland and the rest of the world. The SFC will continue to work ever more closely with our counterparts on the Mainland to facilitate this interaction,” Mr Fong added.

The current global financial crisis has presented governments and regulators worldwide, including Hong Kong, with unprecedented challenges.

The SFC’s Chief Executive Officer Mr Martin Wheatley believes that Hong Kong has weathered the crisis well, thanks in part to its sound financial system.

“We have not seen the sort of major systemic failures witnessed by some overseas markets. I believe we have avoided similar calamities in part because our regulatory environment is robust and in reasonable shape,” he said.

Mr Wheatley agrees that the current financial crisis has highlighted some issues in the regulatory regime that require attention, but he cautions against over-reacting.

“Mistakes are often made through knee-jerk reactions. Any changes to our current regulatory structure must be given careful and balanced consideration. It has always been our approach to ensure that both the industry and public are fully consulted.”

Mr Wheatley said the following guiding principles should be considered when contemplating changes:

  • ensure transparency in the market through timely and meaningful disclosure of information by all participants;
  • monitor conduct and control of market operators and intermediaries to ensure both regulatory compliance and deter market misconduct; and
  • promote and maintain investor confidence in the market.


On its 20th anniversary, the SFC and the Hong Kong Securities Institute will jointly host a dinner for industry participants, during which 2008 Nobel Laureate Dr Paul Krugman will share his insights on the state of the global economy and the economic outlook for Asia.
