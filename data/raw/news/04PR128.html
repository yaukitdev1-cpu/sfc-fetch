The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

For the three months from April to June 2004, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 32 applications (excluding one application for reorganisation) and commented on 15 cases.  Our average response time was seven working days.

As reported previously, for the three months from January to March 2004 the SFC received 34 applications and commented on 16 cases, while for the three months from October to December 2003 the SFC received 19 applications (excluding two applications for reorganisation) and commented on nine cases.  (Note 1)

Of the nine cases filed in the last quarter of 2003 and on which the SFC commented, in two cases the sponsors resigned and the applications were withdrawn, three cases lapsed due to expiry of the six-month application period, one application was rejected by the Stock Exchange, and in one case the SFC indicated that it had no further comments.  The application was subsequently approved by the Stock Exchange for listing.  Two applications are continuing.

Quality of disclosure

The SFC noted two serious disclosure deficiencies common to several cases.  Firstly, there were claims made by companies that they had introduced or would introduce new products to the market and that the products were expected to be very successful.  Disclosure was, however, not sufficient for investors to make an informed judgment about the related research and development work and marketing activities.  Overall, disclosure was insufficient to enable investors to understand why the new products might be as successful as these companies expected them to be.

Secondly, in several cases the applicant had unusual relationships with its suppliers or customers.  Volumes of supplies and/or sales were volatile; financial statements suggested significant financing transactions; and descriptions of corporate restructuring suggested that supply and/or sales operations had been part of the applicant’s own business.  Overall, disclosure in these cases did not present a clear enough picture for investors to be able to make an informed assessment of these relationships.

Mr Ashley Alder, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said:  “In Dual Filing, our emphasis is on the quality, not quantity, of disclosure by listing applicants and listed companies.  The focus is on material and meaningful information disclosure to enable investors to make an informed decision on whether or not to invest.”

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
