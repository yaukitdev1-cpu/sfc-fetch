The SFC is pleased to announce that it has signed a Declaration on Co-operation and Supervision of Cross-Border Investment Management Activity (MoU) with the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC).

This MoU is a significant and eagerly awaited milestone towards strengthening the regulatory ties and co-operation between the SFC and ASIC for the regulatory supervision of fund managers that are licensed in Hong Kong and Australia.  The MoU also lays the groundwork for the recognition of Australia as an Acceptable Inspection Regime (AIR) under the SFC’s Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds (UT Code).

The MoU was signed today by Mr David Knott, Chairman of ASIC, and Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC, with immediate effect.

Under the MoU, both the SFC and ASIC would be able to exchange information and offer assistance to each other concerning the activities of fund managers licensed in their own jurisdiction.  This regulatory co-operation facilitates the SFC in its supervision and inspection of fund managers that are licensed with ASIC and managing SFC authorised funds. 

Based on this co-operative regulatory platform, the SFC will be revising the UT Code to include Australia as an AIR.  Currently, there are six overseas jurisdictions in the list of AIRs, namely US, UK, France, Germany, Luxembourg and Ireland.

Once Australia is officially listed as an AIR, fund managers licensed with ASIC are required to meet the following three conditions, plus any other applicable basic eligibility conditions in the UT Code such as financial resources and management expertise, to be eligible to manage SFC authorised funds:

1.     The fund manager has to hold either an Australian Financial Services licence authorising it to operate a registered managed investment scheme (MIS) or a dealer’s licence from ASIC authorising the fund manager to operate registered MIS of the kind of financial assets. The licence should be free of any conditions or constraints that are not generally applied by ASIC on licensees authorising a fund manager to operate MIS;

2.     The fund manager must be managing registered MIS immediately before it applies to the SFC for recognition under the UT Code, and shall remain as such during the life of any SFC authorised fund that is managed by the fund manager; and

3.     The fund manager has to provide the SFC with a compliance plan and any related supplements for the proposed SFC authorised fund and its related fund management activities.  The compliance plan should follow the requirements as set out by the Australia Corporations Act 2001 with respect to MIS. 

The expanded list of AIR would be effective upon publication in the Government Gazette, which is expected to be within the third quarter this year.

Mr Andrew Sheng said: “With the signing of the MoU, the SFC has taken a positive step towards strengthening its supervisory co-operation with ASIC over fund management activities conducted in Hong Kong and Australia.  The MoU lays the groundwork for a better regional investment fund regulatory framework that facilitates cross-jurisdictional fund management activities and safeguards investor interests.”

“In addition, the SFC welcomes the entrance of Australian fund managers to the Hong Kong market.  I believe the Hong Kong market would benefit from their expertise in managing a wide range of investment funds,” he said.

Mr David Knott said: “This is a further important step towards international reciprocity of regulation. From the perspective of Australian fund managers, today's initiative opens up new marketing opportunities without additional licensing requirements in Hong Kong.”

“Australia greatly values its close working relationship with the Hong Kong SFC and looks forward to further opportunities of this type throughout the Asia-Pacific region,” he said.
