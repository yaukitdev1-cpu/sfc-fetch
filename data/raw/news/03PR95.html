The SFC today publishes the April issue of SFC Enforcement Reporter, summarising the Commission’s enforcement actions in the month of March 2003 and discussing the compliance issues highlighted by recent cases.

A company lent money against a pledge of a substantial number of shares in a stock. The company and its major shareholder were prosecuted for their failure to disclose on time their interests that arose by virtue of the pledge.

The registration of a securities dealer’s representative was revoked for unfairly allocating disadvantageous trades to a client. She did not have the relevant professional knowledge but had also inappropriately advised the client to buy warrants.

The SFC suspended a futures firm and its supervisory director for two weeks. The firm not only failed to maintain the minimum liquid capital required by the Financial Resources Rules, but also window-dressed its financial reports to the SFC.

The SFC also reprimanded a number of licensees, including five persons and two companies, for breaches related to the Placing Guidelines for Derivative Warrants.

For the financial year ended 31 March 2003, the SFC has successfully prosecuted 37 people and firms, and disciplined 86 entities.
 
The SFC Enforcement Reporter also reminds the public that the Securities and Futures Ordinance has become effective since 1 April 2003. The new sanctions available to the SFC, new crimes and increased criminal penalties all apply to conduct on or after that date. Intermediaries are reminded to have systems in place to cope with the new law.
