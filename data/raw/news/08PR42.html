The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

Cases handled

In the three months from October to December 2007, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 30 listing applications. This compares with 32 listing applications in the three months from July to September 2007 (Note 1). Of the 30 applications, we commented on 17 cases. Our average response time was seven working days. In the previous quarter, we commented on 19 cases out of the 32 cases received.

Implications of new laws and regulations

Changes in laws and regulations can affect the operating environment of businesses to varying degrees, depending on the nature of the relevant industry. The SFC noted a lack of disclosures in listing applications in this area, in particular where new government policies or regulations were introduced.

In two cases reviewed, the listing applicants operated in a highly regulated industry. New industry specific government regulations were introduced towards the end of the applicants’ track record periods, which imposed certain limitations that could significantly affect the listing applicants’ cash flows and business prospects.

However, the initial draft prospectuses provided only a general overview of the recent regulatory development. The listing applicants did not provide comprehensive assessment of how their business and financial position were expected to be affected nor how they were seeking to comply with the new regulations, until the SFC raised queries.

As the profit history during the track record period might not have fully reflected the effect of the new regulations, the analysis and assessment by the directors of the listing applicant became even more important for investors in arriving at an informed judgment of the listing applicant’s future prospects.

Non-disclosure would not only have deprived investors of essential information, but would also have left investors uncertain as to whether management had thoroughly considered the implications of the new regulations and prepared the listing applicant for these challenges.

Incomplete submissions

The SFC has had concerns about the quality of information submitted for vetting in various listing applications. In some cases, the absence of information raised the question of whether the sponsor had properly assisted the listing applicant in preparing the draft prospectus for vetting.

In one case reviewed, the draft prospectus not only failed to properly explain the applicability of recently introduced regulations to the listing applicant’s business, it also lacked details in a number of other important areas. These included a detailed discussion on how the listing applicant’s business evolved to its current state, sufficient information on its business model and key operating assets, and certain key financial information.

In another case, the sponsor failed on a number of occasions to provide the information requested without proper explanation.

Mr Brian Ho, the SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: “Directors’ assessment of the impact of regulatory changes to a listed corporation is important to an investor’s decision. As the management of the listing applicant operates the business, it is best placed to assess the likely impact of regulatory changes on the applicant’s business, and make comprehensive disclosure. Sponsors are also expected to help ensure such assessment and other relevant information is properly disclosed in the prospectus.”

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
