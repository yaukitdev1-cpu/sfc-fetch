The SFC reminds all fund managers and intermediaries not to promote to the public any unit trust or mutual fund that has not been authorised by the SFC.  

The SFC notes that certain information about funds that are applying for SFC authorisation, including their names and investment features, has appeared in the media.  

It should be noted that a fund that has applied for authorisation may not necessarily obtain SFC approval.  Any public discussion that implies authorisation of a fund or its timing would be inappropriate, and might reflect on the fitness and properness of an intermediary. 

Unless a fund has been authorised by the SFC, no promotion or marketing of such fund or its features to the public is allowed under the law. This restriction is in the public interest and helps ensure that investors are afforded appropriate protection.  

In general, fund managers and intermediaries who conduct promotional activities and sales of funds should pay close attention to sections 4(1) and 4(8) of the Protection of Investors Ordinance and section 138 of the Securities Ordinance. 

The Protection of Investors Ordinance prohibits any person from issuing any advertisement or invitation to the public to acquire any units in a unit trust or any shares in a mutual fund corporation that has not been authorised by the SFC. Further, the Securities Ordinance prohibits a person from making any statement which contains false and misleading information concerning material facts with a view to inducing the sale of securities including unit trusts and mutual funds.   

Failure by a registered or licensed fund manager or intermediary to comply with these Ordinances may adversely affect the person’s fitness and properness to remain registered or licensed by the SFC and may lead to disciplinary action or, where applicable, criminal prosecution.   

Alexa Lam, Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “While it is one of the SFC's tasks to facilitate market innovation, we must also ensure that investors are being protected. All fund managers and SFC registrants must comply with applicable law and regulation.”  

“The SFC notes that the fund industry has generally acted professionally and responsibly. It would be unfortunate if the integrity of the industry is damaged by the misconduct of a few practitioners,” she said.

If investors are in doubt as to whether a particular investment product is authorised by the SFC, they are encouraged to visit the SFC website to view a regularly updated Register of SFC authorised investment products.
