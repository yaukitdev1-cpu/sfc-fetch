SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, today gave a speech at a lunch organised by the Hong Kong Stockbrokers Association.

Mr Sheng reviewed the co-operation between the SFC and the industry in the past seven years of his chairmanship at the SFC.

He also thanked the industry for their indispensable contributions to the achievements of Hong Kong in creating a modernised regulatory regime, an environment that is conducive to market development, and a stockbroking industry in which investors have confidence.

In particular, Mr Sheng noted the following:

  • Stronger financial position of industry
  • Enactment of the Securities and Futures Ordinance
  • Smooth migration of licensees to the single licensing regime
  • More effective use of regulatory capital under new regime
  • Cost effective fidelity insurance scheme for exchange participants
  • Better investor compensation mechanism
  • Regular liaison with brokers associations and staff union
  • Active engagement of practitioners in policy formulation
  • Initiatives to accommodate industry needs
  • Helping manage down the exposures of higher risk firms.


Mr Sheng said that, with continued strong growth in the Mainland, the future of the capital markets in Hong Kong would be bright.

He said: “Success in the future will depend on the ability of each and every broker to find his or her own market niche. To me, the future of the broking industry depends on the secret of Hong Kong’s success – to struggle without help from anyone except through one’s own blood, sweat and tears.” 

Mr Sheng also re-assured the lunch participants that the transition of the Category C brokers was upper-most in the minds of the SFC and himself.

“You are important liquidity providers in the market, but as technology, derivative products and hedge funds emerge, their share of trading will inevitably increase. In many more mature markets, smaller stockbrokers have either consolidated or changed their business to become financial planners, selling a wider range of financial products,’’ he said.

“These are important strategic decisions that only you yourself and the market can decide, not the regulator. I assure you that there is no policy intent to discriminate against small brokers,” he added.

Mr Sheng concluded: “I want to thank you all for your strong support, your kindness and your understanding.  I have always believed that the spirit of Hong Kong is its sense of fairness [義氣]. I have admired your resilience, your strength and your sense of fair play. I hope you will show the same co-operation and understanding with my successor and my colleagues, as you have shown me.”
