The financial industry is encouraged to play an active role in the implementation of measures laid down in Supplement VI to the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA VI).

Mrs Alexa Lam, Deputy Chief Executive Officer and Executive Director, Policy, China and Investment Products of the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), said industry participants can help cement closer financial collaboration between Guangdong Province and Hong Kong by engaging in communication and exchange with regulatory authorities.

The Outline of the Pearl River Delta Reform and Development Plan has called upon the region to “play a pioneering role in experimenting financial reforms and innovations”.

Speaking today at a seminar on Guangdong-Hong Kong financial co-operation cum roadshow on Guangdong Hi-technology Service Zone for Financial Institutions, Mrs Lam outlined initiatives that will bring financial co-operation between Guangdong and Hong Kong to the next new level.

“With the support of the Central Government and the help of our market participants, the SFC is committed to mapping out and executing policies to best position Hong Kong as an incubator for further and deeper Mainland market experiments,” Mrs Lam said.

“As the country continues to open its capital market and becomes a key player with global reach, the Mainland will be able to leverage on the strengths of our financial market,” she added.

Mrs Lam noted the SFC has been working on the implementation details of the CEPA VI measures.

Under CEPA VI, eligible Hong Kong securities companies and their Mainland counterparts which satisfy the requirements for establishing subsidiaries may set up joint ventures in Guangdong Province to conduct securities investment advisory business.

Mrs Lam said joint venture securities investment advisory company is a brand new concept for the Mainland which does not have precedents to resort to. “We therefore encourage qualified Hong Kong securities companies and their Mainland counterparts to promptly embark on the preparatory work for such joint ventures.”

“They may want to explain their business models to the Mainland regulators, which will help regulators set out implementation details for the securities sectors in both the Mainland and Hong Kong to reap early success,” she added.

Mrs Lam’s speech is available on the SFC website at www.sfc.hk.
