Investors must be aware of their exposure to the knock-on effects of further economic problems in overseas markets, said Securities and Futures Commission Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley.

Speaking at the 2nd Islamic Finance news Roadshow today, Mr. Wheatley said as the global financial market becomes more complex and inter-connected, it’s inevitable that “shocks in one part of the system will have unforeseen effects in another”.

Citing as an example of this knock-on effect, he pointed to the failure of Icelandic banks which had a significant effect on UK and European banks as well as individual holders of credit linked notes in Hong Kong.

“Corporate failures in the US - whether they have already occurred or the potential bankruptcy of others, such as General Motors and the US auto industry, will inevitably have an effect on Hong Kong - both on overall sentiment and more explicitly, on holders of credit linked notes,” Mr Wheatley added.

In light of this, the SFC has already told issuers of structured products to make appropriate information available through distributors. Investors who have any doubts about what the impact of overseas corporate failures might be on their investments should contact the product distributors for further information, he said.

Mr. Wheatley’s speech is available on the SFC website www.sfc.hk.
