To foster the development of retail bonds and other financial products, a consultation paper on proposed amendments to the Companies Ordinance to rationalize the prospectus regime to facilitate the offers of shares and debentures was released by the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau and the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today (March 10).

"This is in line with the Government's policy to encourage development of the bond market to further enhance Hong Kong's position as an international financial centre," Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau Mr Frederick Ma said.

"By streamlining the procedures for the registration and issue of prospectuses and in turn lowering the issuance costs, we hope to promote the issuance of more financial products and thus attract more capital and investors to Hong Kong," said Mr Ma. He added that this was one of the budgetary measures announced by the Financial Secretary in his 2003 Budget Speech in enhancing competitiveness of our financial services industry.

The legislative proposals, expected to be introduced into the Legislative Council by July 2003, also form the second phase of the three-phase approach endorsed by the Financial Market Development Task Force (Information on the three-phase approach is set out at the Annex) to streamline and update the prospectus regime. Major proposals include -

* enhancing the clarity of the application of the prospectus requirements by excluding offering documentation in respect of certain types of offers from the definition of "prospectus", such as offers to not more than 50 persons and offers to professional investors;
* allowing more flexible implementation of the prospectus regime by expanding the exemption power of the SFC;

* providing a more level playing field for local and overseas companies by removing certain discrepancies in requirements applicable to them.

Proposals to permit awareness advertisements and to introduce an alternative dual prospectus structure, as spelt out in guidelines issued by the SFC last month, will also be reflected.

Necessary safeguards are included in the proposals to ensure that investors' interests will not be compromised by the facilitative measures.

"Most of the proposals under the first and second phases represent specific changes to the prospectus regime in response to requests from market participants, which we consider acceptable from the perspective of investor protection," Executive Director of the SFC Mr Ashley Alder said.

"In the third phase, we shall propose a comprehensive update of the regulatory framework for offering of securities. We aim to put forward proposals for public consultation within 18 months," Mr Alder added.

The consultation paper is available on the website of the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau at www.info.gov.hk/fstb and of the SFC at www.hksfc.org.hk. Comments from the public are welcome and should reach the Bureau or the Commission by 31 March 2003.


ANNEX
----------

Overview of the three-phase approach to streamline and update the prospectus regime


- the first phase :

(a) guidelines published by the SFC on 21 February 2003 for -

(i) permitting the issue of awareness advertisements to increase investors' awareness of the relevant offers and allow them more time to arrange their financial and other affairs

(ii) clarifying the SFC's approach to the issue of mini-prospectuses and fact sheets to facilitate understanding of the full prospectuses

(iii) permitting a prospectus to be made up of separate documents which can be authorized, registered and issued separately to better accommodate repeat offerings made through successive tranches

(iv) permitting submission of faxed copies of experts' consent letters and bulk print proof prospectuses to the SFC to reduce the administrative burden of issuers


(b) subject to the approval of the Legislative Council, granting two class exemptions in May/June to reduce the compliance burden of debenture issuers


- the second phase:

Major proposals include -


(a) providing a stronger statutory basis for the substance covered in the guidelines published under the first phase

(b) enhancing the clarity of the application of the prospectus requirements by excluding offering documentation in respect of certain types of offers from the definition of "prospectus", such as offers to not more than 50 persons and offers to professional investors

(c) allowing flexible implementation of the prospectus regime by expanding SFC's exemption power

(d) providing a more level playing field for local and overseas companies by removing certain discrepancies in requirements applicable to them


- the third phase: to comprehensively update/overhaul the prospectus regime; consultation proposals to be released in 18 months
