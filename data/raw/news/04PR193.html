The SFC is looking into some suspicious websites which might be a new trick on investors.

Recently, the SFC has noticed some suspicious websites that appear to promote anti-fraud messages. They claim to provide fraud investigatory services for investors looking for investment opportunities, and ask investors to report frauds to them. These websites may even post a list of suspicious boiler rooms or websites, or provide hyperlinks to similar alert lists of securities regulators in overseas markets.

The SFC is concerned that their anti-fraud appearance may be merely a facade to gain the trust of investors. The trustworthiness of these websites is questionable and they could be scams themselves aiming to defraud unwary investors. Although there have been no complaints or reports of investor loss due to these websites, the SFC is making some inquiries. Investors are reminded to exercise caution when coming across similar websites.

Meanwhile, with the addition of another unlicensed entity today, the number of names on SFC’s Alert List of Unlicensed Overseas Companies, Suspected Scam Websites and Phishing Scams has reached 200 (Note 1).

The Alert List was first introduced in November 2000 when boiler room activities started to become active. The number of entities has increased rapidly since the second quarter of 2003 when many unlicensed entities held out that they operated from Hong Kong by using addresses of business centres and Hong Kong bank accounts, or by making up fictitious addresses altogether. As different scams have emerged, the Alert List has been expanded to include information about fraudulent websites and phishing scams (Note 2). Investors should exercise extreme caution when approached by companies on the Alert List.

Investors’ self-protection is most crucial in defending against frauds:

  • It is important to check who you are dealing with. Reputable brokers and investment advisers do not call people who are not their clients to offer investment opportunities. Cold calling to offer securities is a crime in many places, including Hong Kong.
  • Be careful of requests for up-front payments, particularly if the sales representatives claim that up-front fees are bond / indemnity payments or tax deposits for selling illiquid stocks.
  • Always use contact information you know to be genuine to check the legitimacy of a broker or investment adviser. Don’t submit any personal information or send money to an unknown third party.


From January 2004, the SFC has received 100 complaints about financial scams involving losses by investors of $11 million. Four of the victims were Hong Kong investors who together suffered a loss of about $1.9 million.

The SFC has published focused education materials and created two sample fake scam websites in the section - "Advice to Investors" – "Investor Alert" on its website to help the public understand the tricks of financial fraudsters and how to avoid them. Moreover, the SFC has introduced the Smart Investor Awards (Note 3) to encourage the public to report suspected fraudulent activities.

Notes to Editor:

1. The new entity is Mckenzie Goldstein & Associates.

2. Common financial scams include:

Boiler rooms – Unlicensed professional-sounding salespersons cold call and use high-pressure sales pitches to urge investors to invest. Boiler room operators usually disappear with investors’ cash.

Scam websites – These websites offer unrealistically high-return investment opportunities such as pre-IPO shares at huge discounts or guaranteed funds with multiple returns. In fact, the so-called investment opportunities never exist.

Fraudulent copycat websites – These websites imitate the websites of reputable financial institutions. They may claim to provide investment services and ask investors to disclose their personal information.

Phishing scams – Fraudsters imitate banks and other reputable financial institutions and send emails to entice their customers to disclose their credit card or bank account numbers and passwords/Personal Identification Numbers (PINs). The information is used to authorize credit card transactions or money transfers.


3. The Smart Investor Awards were introduced in February 2004 to encourage the public to report financial scams such as boiler rooms and Internet scams. Eight awards have been given out. Each winner receives an SFC corporate watch and a certificate of recognition. Details of the Awards are available on the SFC website under the section of "Advice to Investors" - "Investor Alert".
