The SFC today publishes the September issue of Dr Wise’s Column, Warrants: If In Doubt, Stay Out.

This issue was written by Mr Andrew Sheng, SFC’s Chairman who will step down at the end of September.

In this issue, Dr Wise reminds investors that derivative warrants are not the same as stocks and that they are too complex for everyone.  In particular, he points out the following:

  • Warrant prices do not simply go up even if the underlying asset prices move in the right direction, as there are other factors affecting warrant prices.
  • The most actively traded warrant is not necessarily the best.  Instead, investors should compare the implied volatility of different warrants on the same underlying asset when choosing a warrant.
  • Warrant prices may not go up at a rate represented by the gearing ratio, which may change as the underlying asset price changes.
  • Short-dated or deeply out-of-the-money warrants may not closely track the movements of underlying asset prices.


Dr Wise also explains to investors the principle of caveat emptor, or buyers beware.  Investors should really know themselves and understand what they are buying when they invest.
