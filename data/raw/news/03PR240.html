The SFC today releases a Consultation Paper on the Draft Guidelines on Good Disclosure of Securities Services Related Fees and Charges, which aim to promote pricing transparency and facilitate comparability of fees and charges.

The Guidelines, endorsed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), set out the recommended best practice for disclosure of securities services related fees and charges by licensed corporations (such as brokerage firms) and registered institutions (such as banks) to their clients (Note 1).

The issuance of the draft Guidelines for public consultation is to implement the recommendations made by the Working Group on the Business Environment of the Stockbroking Industry (Note 2). The Working Group recommended, among other things, that the SFC and the HKMA work with the industry to standardise the broad categories of more than 70 items of securities services related fees and charges in the market (Note 3). The objective of such standardisation is to help promote pricing transparency and facilitate comparability of fees and charges among intermediaries so as to enable investors to make informed choices.

The Guidelines are not mandatory but represent what the SFC and the industry agree as the best practice for disclosure of securities services related fees and charges. It is envisaged that market forces will gradually motivate most intermediaries to adhere to the recommended best practice.

Broadly speaking, the proposals are to:

  • standardise the format for disclosure of securities services related fees and charges into six categories using standardised definition for each category:

    A. Trade-related;
    B. Scrip Handling and Settlement-related;
    C. Information Services;
    D. Account Maintenance;
    E. Nominee Services and Corporate Actions; and
    F. Financing Charges and Others;

  • encourage intermediaries to group all fees and charges applicable to their own securities related services into the above six categories and disclose them to clients in accordance with the recommended format;
  • encourage intermediaries to also provide to clients an illustrative example showing how fees and charges are calculated and when such fees and charges are levied under six prescribed scenarios provided in the Guidelines;
  • encourage intermediaries to follow certain recommended mode of dissemination and disclosure of fees and charges to clients;
  • entitle intermediaries to claim compliance with the Guidelines in their communication with clients after satisfying all the requirements stated in the Guidelines; and
  • encourage intermediaries to give clients advance written notice of at least 30 days if they change their fees and charges or cease to follow the Guidelines.


The SFC invites interested parties to submit written comments on the proposals discussed in the Consultation Paper and the draft Guidelines, and to comment on related matters that might have a significant impact on the proposals no later than 30 November 2003. All comments from the public will be carefully considered before the Guidelines are finalised. A summary conclusion will be published after the end of the consultation period.

In tandem with the issuance of the Consultation Paper, the SFC has published educational information on fees and charges levied by intermediaries under different scenarios on its website under the "Advice to Investors/Questions Corner" section.

Ends

Notes :

1. Professional investors are excluded from the application of the Guidelines as they are generally regarded as more capable of protecting their own interests when they use securities related services provided by intermediaries.

2. On 15 January 2003, the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury announced the establishment of the Working Group on the Business Environment of the Stockbroking Industry to examine with the local stockbroking industry ways to enhance the competitiveness of the small and medium sized brokerage firms. The Working Group comprised representatives from the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau, the SFC and Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited.

3. To tap into the expertise of the industry, the SFC in May 2003 formed a Working Group on Categorisation of Fees and Charges Relating to Securities Services with representatives of Hong Kong Stockbrokers Association Limited, Hong Kong Securities Professionals Association, Institute of Securities Dealers Limited, Hong Kong Association of Online Brokers, and Hong Kong Securities and Futures Industry Staff Union. The working group provided inputs and reached general consensus on how securities services related fees and charges should be categorised and how the proposed categorisation should be set out in the Guidelines and implemented by intermediaries. The Guidelines have been drafted based on the working group’s recommendations.
