The SFC today releases the findings of its Fund Management Activities Survey (FMAS) 2002.

The survey collects information on fund management activities in Hong Kong to enable a better understanding of the local fund management industry and to identify possible trends.  It also helps the SFC’s efforts to facilitate development and long-term growth of the fund management industry in Hong Kong.

FMAS 2002 invited responses from SFC registrants and exempt persons (Note 1) who had gross operating income derived from either the management of funds or the provision of advice on funds as at the end of 2002.  Key findings are as follows (value in HK$ as of 31 December 2002):

  • There were 192 companies that provided fund management or advisory services and derived gross operating income from such activities (+12% from 172 last year).
  • The number of firms that reported Hong Kong as its Asian headquarters increased from 34 in 2001 to 36 in 2002.
  • Total assets under management (AUM, see Note 2) by the respondents amounted to $1,491 billion, representing a slight growth of 0.4%.  Of these, 63% of assets were sourced from overseas investors.
  • AUM sourced from Hong Kong investors totalled $558 billion (+23% from $454 billion).  This also marked the second consecutive growth year for monies sourced from Hong Kong.
  • Total assets managed in Hong Kong, excluding the amount of assets sub-contracted or delegated overseas, amounted to $739 billion (+11% from $668 billion).  Of these, equities were the most significant asset class and accounted for 59% of the total assets managed in Hong Kong.
  • Pure advisory assets sourced from Hong Kong grew to $43 billion (+43% from $30 billion) while pure advisory assets managed in Hong Kong grew to $83 billion (+14% from $73 billion).
  • The amount of assets invested in Hong Kong grew to $364 billion (+21% from $301 billion). 
  • There were 856 investment professionals in the fund management industry. They mainly specialised in equities and were proficient in investing in the Asia-Pacific region, particularly in Mainland China and Hong Kong.


Hong Kong as a leading fund management centre for the region
 
The results of FMAS 2002 indicate that Hong Kong remains a major fund management centre in the Asia-Pacific region for attracting overseas funds for management.
 
The amount of AUM reported under FMAS has been consistent for the past three years at around $1.5 trillion. As at the end of 2002, AUM amounted to $1,491 billion, of which $932 billion (63%) was derived from non-Hong Kong investors.
 
Out of the total 192 respondents this year, 44 were first time respondents to the survey.  In addition, the number of firms that reported Hong Kong as its Asian headquarters has increased to 36.
 
Substantial growth in Hong Kong’s own market
 
Data from FMAS 2002 reveal continuous growth in Hong Kong’s own market in 2002.  The importance of Hong Kong’s role as a fund management centre has increased in 2002.  This is evidenced by the increase in portfolio management activities that are directly attributable to growth in Hong Kong as at the end of 2002 compared with the previous year. In particular, assets managed in Hong Kong (i.e. total assets less assets delegated or sub-contracted overseas) have demonstrated a three-year growth trend, rising from 42.2% of total AUM in 2000 to 49.6% in 2002.  
 
Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said:  “The results of the survey suggest that Hong Kong has maintained its position as a major fund management centre in Asia, attracting overseas funds for management as well as increasing the amount of assets sourced from, managed out of, advised in, and invested in Hong Kong.  The SFC is committed to further enhancing Hong Kong’s advantage as the preferred location for fund management business in Asia.”
 
As competition intensifies around the world and within the Asia-Pacific region, it is of paramount importance that the SFC maintains its transparent approach to the regulation of the fund management industry, and to ensure that Hong Kong stays at the forefront of international best practice. 
 
“By reference to the various initiatives to facilitate market development already completed by the SFC and those in the pipeline, Hong Kong’s fund management industry may rest assured that the SFC will continue to foster an environment conducive to an open and efficient market,” Mrs Lam added. (Note 3)
 
The fund management industry is an integral part of the financial services sector.   Development of local fund management expertise and growth of the industry in Hong Kong will stimulate growth in auxiliary services, such as fund administration, trustee services, global custody, brokerage and banking.
 
Copies of the report of FMAS 2002 are available at the SFC office and on its website under “Surveys and Reports” of the Publications subsection of the “Press Releases, Publications and Speeches” section.
 
Ends
 
Notes :
 
1. As defined under the former regime before the Securities and Futures Ordinance took effect on 1 April 2003.
 
2. “Assets under management” or “AUM” refers to assets under management excluding assets under pure investment advice.
 
3. Various initiatives to facilitate product innovation that were completed in 2002 included:

  • Guidelines on Index-tracking Funds (Jan 2002);
  • Enhancement of the Guidelines on Guaranteed Funds (Jan and Mar 2002);
  • Guidelines on Hedge Funds (May 2002); and
  • Guidelines on Hedge Funds Reporting Requirements (Nov 2002).

 
In response to requests for Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) to be introduced in Hong Kong, the SFC consulted the market in March 2003.  A Code on REITs will be published soon.
 
To further improve the existing regulatory framework for the offering of passively managed index tracking funds in Hong Kong, the SFC has consulted the public on draft guidelines for regulating Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs).  The Guidelines on ETFs are expected in the third quarter of 2003.
 
To increase international co-operation in respect of fund management, the SFC signed a Co-operation Agreement with the Australian Securities and Investments Commission in late June 2003.  In keeping with Hong Kong’s open market policies, the SFC remains open to properly regulated funds.  The agreement with ASIC will give Hong Kong investors even greater choice in the jurisdiction of fund managers.
