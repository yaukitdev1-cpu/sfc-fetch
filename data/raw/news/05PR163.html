The SFC is pleased to announce that it has signed a Letter of Intent with Malaysia’s Securities Commission (SC) on the enhancement of regulatory co-operation between the SC and the SFC (Note 1).

The two regulators aim to work towards the mutual recognition of investment products authorised in their respective jurisdictions for cross-border distribution.
 
This Letter of Intent, the sixth to be signed by the SFC, is an important step for both the SC and the SFC in the area of cross-border regulation of investment products and the development of a cohesive Asian market for investment products denominated in regional currencies, including mutual funds (Note 2).
 
Under the Letter of Intent, both authorities agree to strengthen co-operation, particularly in matters relating to cross-border trading and the supervision of investment products. It is hoped that with enhanced communication and regulatory equivalence of the respective regulatory regimes, both Hong Kong and Malaysia will be able to facilitate the development of deeper and broader investment markets through the trading in each other's markets of mutually recognised investment products that are acceptable to both authorities.
 
Both the SC and the SFC are members of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO).  This Letter of Intent is signed in the spirit of mutual assistance and co-operation among member securities regulators fostered by IOSCO.
 
The Letter of Intent was signed today by Tan Sri Md Nor Md Yusof, Chairman of the SC, and Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC, in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia.
 
Both authorities have also established a bilateral working group which has begun working towards the achievement of the objectives set out in the Letter of Intent.

Mr Sheng said: “The SFC is committed to strengthening regional financial market co-operation in order to raise the quality of the Asian markets and promote investment in financial products originating in the region.  By signing this Letter of Intent, the SFC enters into a partnership with the SC in a demonstration of our mutual intention to enhance the level of regulatory co-operation between our respective markets in line with the objectives and standards of IOSCO.  Malaysia and Hong Kong are important securities markets in Asia. We hope to open up regional fund markets through the cross-border trading of financial products in our respective jurisdictions. This would support the ASEAN + 3 Asian Bond Market Initiative (Note 3) of developing Asian bond markets, and complement the work of regional central banks in developing the Asian Bond Fund (Note 4).  As securities regulators, we will explore together how to remove unnecessary hurdles to cross-border trading, without compromising the level of investor protection in either of our jurisdictions.”

Tan Sri Md Nor said: “The LOI will allow the SC to explore possibilities to increase the breadth and depth of the Malaysian investment products by leveraging on the strengths of the Hong Kong capital markets, in line with the aims outlined in the Capital Market Masterplan (CMP) (Note 5). We have been pursuing strategies to enhance global compatibility and connectivity of the Malaysian capital market. As we move into the third phase of the CMP, there is a need to add diversity to the market landscape, accelerate the innovation of products and services, and create opportunities via collaborations with regulators such as HK SFC to expand Malaysia’s reach in international markets.”

Notes:

1. The SC is the central authority responsible for the regulation and development of the securities and futures industries in Malaysia. It was established on 1 March 1993 under the Securities Commission Act 1993 as a self-funding statutory body with investigative and enforcement powers. It reports to the Minister of Finance and tables its accounts in Parliament annually. More information on the SC is available at www.sc.com.my.

2. The SFC signed similar Letters of Intent with Indonesia’s Ministry of Finance Capital Market Supervisory Agency (BAPEPAM) in May 2004, with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Thailand (SEC Thailand) in November 2004, with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC Sri Lanka) in April this year, with India’s Securities and Exchange Board (SEBI) in May 2005, and Guernsey’s Financial Services Commission (GFSC) in June 2005.

3. In December 2002, the ASEAN + 3 Asian Bond Market Initiative (ABMI) was announced in Chiang Mai, Thailand. It aims to develop efficient and liquid bond markets in Asia, which would enable better utilisation of Asian savings for Asian investments. The ABMI would also contribute to the mitigation of currency and maturity mismatches in financing. The ABMI emphasises the need for joint and comprehensive set of actions by the ASEAN + 3 countries (China, Japan and Korea) in two broad areas: facilitating access to the market through a wider variety of issuers and enhancing market infrastructure to foster bond markets in Asia. For details, please see http://www.aseansec.org/15030.htm.

4. The Executives’ Meeting of East Asia and Pacific Central Banks (EMEAP), which comprises 11 central banks and monetary authorities in the region, announced the initial structure of the Asian Bond Fund 2 (ABF2) on 15 April 2004. ABF2 will promote the development of index bond funds in the regional markets and at the same time enhance the domestic as well as regional bond market infrastructure. The recommendation for the preliminary framework for ABF2 consists of two components: a Pan-Asian Bond Fund Index Fund and a Fund of Bond Funds, covering the local-currency bonds issued by sovereign and quasi-sovereign issuers in EMEAP countries. The EMEAP members are Australia, China, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, New Zealand, Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. For details please see http://www.emeap.org.

5. The Capital Market Masterplan (CMP) was introduced by the SC in 2001. The Masterplan is a comprehensive plan that charts the strategic positioning and future direction of the Malaysian capital market for the next 10 years. It prioritises the immediate needs of the capital market and charts its direction and long-term growth. More information on the CMP is available at www.sc.com.my.
