The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

Disclosure of business model and key business relationships

The SFC is concerned to note the failure by certain sponsors to explain the business fundamentals of their clients, apparently reflecting their lack of understanding of the key features of the business model and key business relationships. The SFC also notes that such information cannot be withheld from disclosure on the basis of confidentiality obligations owed by listing applicants to third parties, or difficulty of access to the ultimate customers, where that would compromise investors’ legitimate interests, which come first.

Understanding a company’s underlying business model is a key part of a prospective investor’s assessment. The business model may involve special features related to the company’s specific circumstances, and unless the business model is properly explained, it is difficult for investors to understand properly how the company has become profitable in the past and evaluate the prospects of its remaining profitable in future.

In one case reviewed, the company’s principal supplier of raw materials is also its principal customer, and on-sells the products to an ultimate customer, with which the company deals directly on matters concerning sales orders, delivery arrangements, and technical product requirements. The sales to this principal supplier/customer are apparently priced above market prices and contributed a substantial proportion of the company’s impressive increases in historical profits. The principal supplier/customer was stated to be an independent third party conducting onward sales of the company’s products under an agreement with the company. Yet the settlement terms in the agreement were not adhered to and no plausible explanation was offered for some unusual fund transfers between the two parties, calling into question the independence of the principal supplier/customer. A review of the company’s competitors revealed that its limited scope of business was quite unusual in the industry, which underlined concerns on its independence and raised further doubts as to its real business substance.

In another case also involving agency arrangements, the company derives a considerable proportion of its income from sales through a handful of sales agents to two designated ultimate customers. Against a background of intense competition in the industry and pricing pressure according to the company, it achieved a multifold increase in sales with continuously increasing profit margin over the past three years without proper explanation of the reasons for its success. Despite the heavy reliance on the sales agents, no clear explanation was offered on the service and functions of the sales agents and the fees charged by the agents to enable investors to establish a proper understanding of how the company managed to generate such strong business.

Mr Brian Ho, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: “Investors need full and accurate disclosure of the business case of a company to decide whether to invest or not. Explaining the business properly is one of the most basic tasks expected of a sponsor. Sponsors are reminded to perform sufficient due diligence on the business of their clients before submitting a listing application.”

Statistics

In the three months from April to June 2006, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 16 listing applications. This compares to 13 listing applications in the three months from January to March 2006 (Note 1). Of the 16 applications, we commented on 10 cases. Our average response time was seven working days. In the previous quarter, we commented on eight cases out of the 13 cases received.

As reported previously (Note 2), in the three months from October to December 2005 we commented on eight out of 14 applications received. Of those eight cases, two cases lapsed due to the expiry of the six-month application period, and six have been listed.

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
