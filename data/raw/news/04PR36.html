Investors must study the company fundamentals of a new listing whether they are subscribing for the IPO shares for a quick punt or for longer-term investment, Dr Wise has advised.

In the latest article in Dr Wise’s Column (Note 1), Investing in an IPO, Dr Wise cautions investors that they must estimate the total costs of the deal and should not commit beyond their means. 

Dr Wise has given some statistics to remind investors not to assume that the share price will always rise above the IPO issue price when trading starts.  Among the 177 IPOs in 2002 and 2003, 76 of them (about 43%) might not have provided a positive return for the first day traders, as 60 shares closed below or at the issue price on the listing debut day. While the other 16 shares recorded gains on the first day, the increases were not more than 2% and unlikely to have been sufficient to cover interest expense and transaction costs (Note 2).

Meanwhile, the SFC has also published a new leaflet entitled “Is IPO Investing For You?” to explain how to get important information from a prospectus, fill in and submit an application form and check allotment results.

The leaflet complements the “IPO Investing” series of articles on the “Features” section of the Electronic Investor Resources Centre located at www .hkeirc.org.

As with all other SFC investor publications, the leaflet is available free at the SFC office and the Consumer Advice Centres of the Consumer Council. The electronic version is accessible on the SFC website under the “Advice to Investors – Investor Education Programmes” section.

Licensed intermediaries are welcome to request copies for distribution by calling the SFC’s Investor Education and Communications Department at 2842 7711.
