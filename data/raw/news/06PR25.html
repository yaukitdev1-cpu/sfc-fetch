Over 40 financial regulators from more than 19 countries congregated today at a meeting hosted by the SFC with participation by Joint Forum members (Note 1).

The purpose of the meeting was to promote sound business continuity planning and raise the standards of market contingency planning in the region. At the meeting, participants shared their experiences in business continuity and market contingency planning. They also gave their views on the regulator’s role and the market’s responsibilities in market rehearsals and testing the plans, lessons learnt from major global operational disruptions such as the 9/11 incident and the London bombings, and how the lessons learnt from these events might be relevant in an Asian context.

Addressing the meeting, SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “Business continuity and market contingency planning is a priority area for Hong Kong and for Asia-Pacific as a whole. In light of the growing threat of terrorist activities, the continued risk of natural disasters and the spectre of Avian Flu and other diseases, we all need to work to ensure that proper planning and testing of business continuity plans has been done. The SFC is reviewing its own plans and procedures and those of our market participants in this area.”

“Thanks to the 6th World Trade Organisation Ministerial Conference, we had a very good chance to test our market contingency and business continuity plans and ensured that business went on as usual. Through IOSCO as well as our participation in the Joint Forum Working Group on Business Continuity planning, we are contributing our experience in market contingency planning and our experience in dealing with the SARS crisis so as to shape international policies and best practice standards.”

“The financial markets are more global now and worldwide events will have repercussions on our markets. It is important that we think globally in terms of market contingency planning and the effects that international issues may have on Asia-Pacific as a whole. We hope that more Asian regulators will work together with us in this and other areas,” he added.

The Business Continuity Planning Meeting (BCP) for Asian Financial Regulators 2006 was held subsequent to the release in December 2005 of the “High-level Principles for Business Continuity” drafted by the Joint Forum and its Working Group on Business Continuity planning (Note 2).
