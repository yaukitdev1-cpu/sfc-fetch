The SFC is pleased to report that implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance has been successful since its commencement a year ago.

The SFO, along with the body of subsidiary legislation underpinning it, came into force on 1 April 2003. The SFC is now better empowered to protect the interests of investors in an appropriate manner. It has made significant progress in maintaining market standards, facilitating market development and strengthening enforcement.

Amendments have already been made to some of the subsidiary legislation. In late 2003, the Securities and Futures (Price Stabilizing) (Amendment) Rules were gazetted to recognise the UK price stabilising regime so as to facilitate offerings in the UK and Hong Kong. More recently, the Securities and Futures (Contract Limits and Reportable Positions) (Amendment) Rules were gazetted to ease the compliance burden on those holding derivative contracts for others.

About six months ago, the SFC embarked on a review of the SFO. The objective is first, to examine if the existing provisions are effective in meeting the SFC’s policy objectives and second, to consider if any provisions in the SFO or subsidiary legislation need to be updated.

The review is expected to result in an amendment bill and amendments rules, which are likely to go out to public consultation in the second half of this year.

The SFC has from time to time published updates on a wide range of SFO issues to keep the market informed. It wishes to take this opportunity to update on the following:

Single licensing regime and intermediaries

Brokers and investment advisers need only one licence to carry out all regulated activities that they are eligible to conduct, greatly reducing the administrative and compliance burden on intermediaries. The SFC has pledged to shorten the processing time for applications for a representative’s licence and for approval as a responsible officer, and introduced pledges on processing applications for a provisional licence and a change of accreditation. In total 24 circulars, six guidelines, and 164 frequently asked questions (FAQs) have been issued to assist compliance with the new regime.

About 8,600 licensees representing half of all those migrating to the SFO licensing regime had lodged migration applications by the end of 29 February 2004.

Under the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules, the SFC has standardised the regulatory capital framework for all licensed corporations in the form of the liquid capital requirement and in particular strengthened regulation over advisers and asset managers, moving away from reliance on net tangible assets. No major compliance issues have been identified so far.

Dual Filing

Introduction of the Dual Filing regime, with its proactive approach to corporate disclosure review, has begun to improve the quality of prospectuses and other corporate communications made under the Stock Exchange listing rules. The SFC expects this trend to continue. Effective co-operation with the Stock Exchange has ensured that there is certainty in the listing process but no additional burden on listing applicants. The SFC has also begun several investigations under its new SFO enforcement powers into suspected false or misleading corporate disclosure and suspected misconduct by sponsors.

In reviewing listing applications, the SFC aims to identify the major issues at the beginning of the process and to communicate them clearly to the applicants and their sponsors so that they are in a position to address the SFC’s concerns at an early stage. The SFC’s average turnaround time is seven working days. The emphasis is on the quality of disclosure – whether it helps investors understand the particular company’s business and therefore assists them in making informed investment decisions.

Up to 31 December 2003, the SFC had received, via the Stock Exchange, 90 applications for new listings and provided comments on disclosure issues in 38 cases. For the seven months from March to September 2003 (Note 1), the SFC received 64 applications for new listings on the Main Board and the Growth Enterprise Market (plus five applications for listing of investment companies). Of the 64 applications, the SFC commented on 29 cases. As of 31 March 2004, 16 of the 29 cases have lapsed, been withdrawn or rejected. Ten companies have become listed or been approved for listing, and three cases remain outstanding.

Our enforcement efforts under Dual Filing are also encouraging. We are however not in a position to give details because of the time necessary to make appropriate inquiries and completing disciplinary or other actions. The SFC will devote the necessary resources to ensure that those who lie to the public – including intermediaries who help companies lie to the public – are held liable for their wrongdoings.

The Dual Filing regime has operated smoothly and effectively, and has been well received by the market. The SFC welcomes the Government’s recent decision to strengthen the regime and to enhance the regulation of listing.

Disclosure of Interests

When developing Part XV of SFO, the SFC took on board many market comments to remove unnecessary compliance burdens. The SFC’s Outline of Part XV provides a practical guide to the provisions. A revised version was issued last year in response to additional queries which came to light as practitioners applied Part XV to complex transactions and business structures.

In April 2003, 5,729 disclosure forms were filed. This number was higher than usual because a significant number of initial notifications had to be filed when the new requirements first came into effect. Thereafter, about 3,000 notices have been filed each month.

Although there has been an upward trend in electronic filings in recent months, these remain relatively low. For efficiency, SFC would encourage users and practitioners to use HKEx’s electronic filing facilities. Forms for reporting interests can now be downloaded from the HKEx website (https://sdinotice.hkex.com.hk/). After filing a notice electronically, users can print out a receipt that records the time and date of the filing.

To implement the disclosure regime, HKEx has also developed a useful new search facility that provides investors with a comprehensive and user-friendly means of searching the "Disclosure of Interests" database. The SFC is reviewing Part XV. The general feedback is that our policy objectives have been met, the Outline is useful, and there is increasing market familiarity with the new regime. However, the market has raised issues especially when handling complex practical situations. Most concerns are technical and the SFC believes that a practical approach can be adopted to resolve these issues, where possible, through changes to the forms and rules and by further guidance in the Outline. Changes to the SFO will be considered if necessary, but these will entail more time.

Enforcement

The SFC has initiated many inquiries that relate to post SFO events or offences under the new law. Some of these cases concern significant and complex matters and investigations are resource intensive and take time. Nevertheless, some of the ongoing investigations will be concluded in the near future, resulting in new SFO sanctions being imposed.

On disciplinary action against licensees, tougher sanctions under the SFO have been imposed, including the first life ban of a person in the industry and the first fine.

With the introduction of the new disclosure of interests regime, there was an upsurge in the number of late filings referred from HKEx. More than 770 cases of late disclosures were opened. Because of market concern about the complexity of the reporting regime, the SFC exercised considerable discretion in determining whether cases should be forwarded for further investigation. The SFC issued 176 warning letters, but in 34 cases filing delays were sufficiently long to justify referral for further investigation.

The SFO has given the SFC a wider scope to investigate listed companies and related corporations. The SFC conducted 10 new company investigations during the year. Legal advice is being sought in two cases about the prospects of seeking orders under section 214 of the SFO. The section enables the court to make a range of orders including disqualification of directors.
