The International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) has launched an initiative to raise the standards of cross-border co-operation among securities regulators.
 
SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, who is Chairman of the IOSCO Technical Committee, said co-operation among securities regulators had become an increasingly important objective.
 
"Increased globalisation of capital markets has generated cross-border violations where the lack of real co-operation has had an adverse impact.  For securities regulators this has meant more focus on combating fraud, market abuse and money laundering. Efforts to improve co-operation and to ensure that appropriate standards are observed by all securities regulators are therefore of fundamental importance," Mr Sheng said.
 
In dealing with the issue of cross border co-operation, relevant IOSCO standards are being used as benchmarks, including the IOSCO Principles and Resolutions as well as the IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding, adopted in 2002, which provides a clear benchmark for international co-operation.
 
IOSCO is embarking on an initiative of the highest importance to identify jurisdictions that present the greatest risks and promote their compliance with IOSCO standards through dialogue designed to explore obstacles to co-operation and paths for their swift resolution.
