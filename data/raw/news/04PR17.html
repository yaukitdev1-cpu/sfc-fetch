The SFC today submitted its views to the Government on its Consultation Paper on Proposals to Enhance the Regulation of Listing.

On 19 January 2004, the Commission at a meeting attended by all 13 Executive Directors and Non Executive Directors decided unanimously on the SFC’s response document and the publication of the response following submission to the Administration.

The SFC’s response is founded on a consensus view. The Commission fully agrees with the Government’s views that we must continue to upgrade our market quality by improving the listing regime, and in doing so consider market development needs and local circumstances.

In particular, there is consensus with Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Ltd (HKEx) about the fundamental direction of reform, which is:

(a) to give statutory backing for the Listing Rules, hence enabling effective enforcement;
(b) to adopt a North American model, where the statutory regulator "seeks to ensure compliance with statutory requirements (generally disclosure-related)";
(c) shared regulation of listed companies with clear division of labour, to be delineated along statutory and non-statutory lines; and
(d) to build on Dual Filing to give statutory effect to the Listing Rules, recognising its current defects.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said: "The general approach of the SFC is to arrive at a pragmatic, feasible and technical solution that will ensure that Hong Kong’s system of listing regulation matches international financial centre standards."

The SFC’s proposals operate within the Securities and Futures Ordinance framework. They preserve and, where appropriate, strengthen the existing checks and balances contained in the "three-tier" regulatory system.

Details of the SFC’s proposals can be found in the attached response document. In summary, the proposals involve:

  • The provision of statutory backing for the detailed disclosure obligations in the Listing Rules under the SFO;
  • The clarification of SFC responsibility as statutory regulator of listed company disclosure (which has already begun under Dual Filing) for the administration of the new statutory rules;
  • Effective sanctions for breaches of the new statutory rules including civil fines and disqualification orders;
  • Flexibility in the SFC’s administration of the new statutory rules by waivers, modifications, or class exemptions, including publication of guidelines to inform the market about the detailed operation of the new rules;
  • Full-merit appeals to the existing Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal from all decisions under the new rules;
  • Extensive safeguards over the imposition of regulatory sanctions for breaches of the new statutory rules – options include referrals of cases for decision to an expanded Market Misconduct Tribunal dedicated to hearing such matters and the expansion of the type of market misconduct in the SFO to include breaches of the new statutory rules;
  • A committee of market participants established under the SFO to advise the SFC;
  • Clarification of the application of the Companies Ordinance to avoid duplicative disclosure requirements for companies applying for listing;
  • Introduction of a statutory public electronic database to ensure transparency of all statutory corporate and other disclosure;
  • More effective regulation of Listing Sponsors; and
  • Enhanced cross-border regulatory co-operation.


The main technical difference between the SFC’s proposals and those of HKEx lies in whether the statutory backing should be along "core" disclosure principles to be included in primary legislation, or whether the detailed disclosure obligations in the Listing Rules would be incorporated into existing subsidiary legislation under the SFO.

Mr Sheng said: "The Commission’s Board wishes to make clear that the Commission, as a statutory body, can only exercise functions given to it by the legislation. It has no desire to extend its jurisdiction beyond the SFO to non-statutory areas, such as corporate governance, admission criteria, trading rules etc, that clearly belong to the market operator. The extent to which the legislation is to be changed is entirely a policy decision for the Administration and approval by LegCo."

"The SFC considers the proposals in its submission will upgrade Hong Kong’s regulatory structure for listed companies to assure Hong Kong’s long-term future as an international financial centre. Implementation will result in clear benefits for investors, listed companies and intermediaries. The SFC also believes that reform should not be delayed," he said.

"We look forward to the Consultation Conclusions and decision of the Administration in this matter," Mr Sheng said.

In the interim, the SFC is committed to working closely with the Administration, HKEx and the industry to continually upgrade the quality of our market.
