The SFC today publishes a Consultation Paper on Proposed Amendments to Schedule 5 to the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO).

Part 2 of Schedule 5 of the SFO defines the different types of regulated activities. Persons engaging in regulated activities, unless otherwise exempted, have to be licensed by or registered with the SFC under the SFO. The proposed amendments aim to fine-tune the legal definitions of certain regulated activities to accommodate the latest development of the industry and comments received from market participants on the licensing regime. The proposed amendments include:

(a) Extending the definition of "asset management" to include management of real estate investment trusts.

(b) Amending the definition of "dealing in securities" so that:

(i) in a case where all parties to the dealing concerned are authorised financial institutions and the dealing is by an approved money broker who represents each of the parties concerned, the dealing is to be excluded from the definition (Note 1); and

(ii) any person who, as principal, disposes of securities other than to a professional investor, is not to be excluded from the definition (Note 2).

(c) Amending the definitions of "advising on securities" and "advising on futures contracts" so that the giving of advice by a licensed asset manager, solely for the purposes of carrying on fund management activities pertaining to collective investment schemes, is to be excluded from the definitions.

Details of the SFC’s proposals are set out in the Consultation Paper, which is available on the SFC website and at the SFC office.

The public are invited to submit comments by 15 March 2005. A Consultation Conclusions Paper will be published as soon as practicable after the end of the consultation period.

Notes to Editor:

1. Approved money brokers are already subject to the regulation and supervision of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, and the proposed exclusions will not result in risk to investors given that the money brokers only deal for authorised financial institutions.

2. Where a person is disposing of securities to persons other than professional investors (whether as agents or principals), the policy intent is that he should be licensed or registered for "dealing in securities". Currently, the exemption to the definition of "dealing in securities" extends to disposals of securities by any person acting as principal, even if he is dealing with a non-professional investor. This could have the unintended effect of allowing a person to dispose of securities to the public at large without being regulated.

Professional investors include, amongst others, licensed intermediaries.
