As a continuation of his last column on borrowing to invest, Dr Wise explains further how leverage is built into some products as a product feature.

In his latest column entitled “Leverage magnifies risk,” Dr Wise explains to investors the significant risks of trading leveraged products on margin with illustrative examples of leveraged foreign exchange trading and a real-life case of futures contracts trading.

When trading a leveraged product, an investor is required to deposit an initial margin, which is a small percentage of the total contract sum. This means that the investor can purchase a portfolio that has a greater value than his initial capital outlay. This use of leverage means that his potential gains or losses are magnified relative to changes in the value of the portfolio. Investors using leverage must fully understand the actual value of the portfolio created with the margin deposit and the magnifying effect on the investment risks.

“Due to the gearing effect, margin deposits can be quickly depleted by adverse market movements, resulting in margin calls and potential losses that far exceed the initial capital outlay,” Dr Wise says.

Dr Wise also highlights that the lower the margin deposited compared to the amount traded then the higher will be the leverage. The higher the leverage employed, the sooner investor will lose his money if the market goes against him.
