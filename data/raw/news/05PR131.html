The SFC today releases the Consultation Conclusions paper following the Review of the Disclosure of Interests Regime.

A Consultation paper containing certain proposals to amend the Disclosure of Interests Regime was published last January. The SFC has received comments from 34 respondents, who generally supported almost all of the proposals.

Most of the recommendations in the Consultation Conclusions Paper follow the consultation proposals, with some changes of detail to take into account public comments.

A number of recommended changes are directed towards making the regime more user friendly. For example, the SFC recommends:

  • changes to make the disclosure forms easier to fill in and easier to understand;
  • changes to clearly define triggering disclosure events for "change in nature of an interest";
  • synchronizing dates for reporting sales and purchases; and
  • the simplification of the de minimis exemption regime.


In other areas, the changes proposed are in specialist areas to better take account of business practices.

However, there is no consensus among the public comments in the important area of disclosure of security interests. In the absence of a consensus the SFC believes that the relevant factors and implications should be further considered in depth before making a policy decision on this matter. A working group comprising market participants, investors and representatives of the lending industry will be set up to see if there are better alternatives.

Implementing the recommended changes will involve amendments to the SFO, related rules and regulations, the disclosure forms and certain guidelines on Part XV. The SFC will also revise the Outline of Part XV.

In addition, in response to market requests for clarification of the SFC’s enforcement policy with regard to Part XV, the SFC releases a new note today. The note outlines the SFC’s policy regarding investigation of suspected breaches in four main categories: late disclosures; failing to complete the disclosure form in accordance with the directions and instructions; including in the form information which is false or misleading; and failure to make a disclosure of a notifiable event.

An SFC spokesman said: "The SFC believes that the recommendations would fine-tune and rationalise the regime, and provide greater clarity for those obliged to make filings and greater understanding by users of the information. It is expected that the recommendations will be implemented in a phased manner with those involving more complicated issues being implemented at a later date."

The Consultation Conclusions paper and the note are available at the SFC office and on the SFC website.
