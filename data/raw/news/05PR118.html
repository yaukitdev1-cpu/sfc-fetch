The SFC has published its Annual Report for the year ended 31 March 2005. The Annual Report was laid before the LegCo yesterday following submission to the Financial Secretary.

The theme of the Annual Report is Investors First. As market regulator, the SFC has a responsibility to educate the public and enable investors to protect their own interests. The well-being of investors is key to the healthy development of the capital markets. This Annual Report delivers a concise message that the SFC puts the investors’ interest first, through Regulation, Enforcement and Education.

The Annual Report gives an account of the SFC’s finances. In 2004-2005, total revenue increased to $625 million, 13% higher than the previous year mainly owing to higher market turnover which lifted levy income. We continued to manage our budget prudently. Additional spending was made only on areas considered necessary to improve market quality. Total operating expenditure after depreciation was $456 million, 7% higher than the previous year, mainly because of the variable pay awarded to staff who performed well and to address the 12% staff turnover. The Commission continued to hold back on a general fixed pay increase, suspended since April 2001, to keep the fixed cost down.

As a result, the SFC achieved a $169 million surplus, as compared with the $125 million surplus recorded in 2003-2004. Our reserves at the end of March 2005 stood at $860 million, equivalent to 1.7 times of estimated expenditures (including depreciation) of $508 million for 2005-2006.

The Annual Report also reports on the significant increases in the SFC’s workload over the last decade. Yet, the SFC has managed its budget responsibly and only increased its staff moderately, especially when compared to its international counterparts.

Besides the financial information, the Annual Report details the SFC’s achievements during 2004-2005 and our work in progress. It also reviews the Commission’s investor education activities over the years.

In the Annual Report, Mr Andrew Sheng said in the Chairman’s Statement: "Our mission in 2005-2006 is to continue to implement the Securities and Futures Ordinance effectively, with emphasis on raising the quality of the market. In our daily work, our staff motto is to remember: Investors First."
