The SFC today publishes a Report of Findings of a Public Perception Survey. The findings show that a great majority of the public knew the overall responsibilities of the SFC. There were, however, some misconceptions about its role and functions.

The Social Sciences Research Centre of the University of Hong Kong conducted the survey on behalf of the SFC early this year. It interviewed 1,375 respondents, including 1,055 investors and 320 non-investors.

The survey aimed to find out the public’s understanding of the SFC’s work, their views of its performance and perception of its image. Being the first such survey, it establishes benchmarks against which the SFC may assess the public’s perception and views in the future. The findings also help the SFC develop its publicity and investor education work.

Some key findings are as follows:

  • A majority (65.7%) of the respondents said the SFC was an overall securities market regulator. But 8.1% and 4.9% mistook the SFC for authorities that approved listings and operated the stock exchange respectively.
  • When asked to name the Chairman of the SFC, 13.8% gave the correct answer       (Mr Andrew Sheng).
  • The three functions that were recognised correctly as the SFC’s responsibilities by most respondents were “Ensure the securities and futures markets are fair, efficient, competitive, transparent and orderly” (96.9%), “Ensure proper disclosures of information by listed companies” (92.9%) and “Handle investors’ complaints about market crimes/misconduct” (90%). The two least recognised SFC functions were  “Educate investors” (49.5%) and “Compensate investors in the event of broker failures” (40.8%).
  • The overall rating of the SFC’s performance in fulfilling its statutory objectives was 6.5 points out of 10. The individual ratings ranged from 6.7 for both “Assisting the Government in maintaining financial market stability of Hong Kong” and “Ensuring that securities and futures markets are fair, efficient, competitive, transparent and orderly”, to 4.8 for “Educating investors”.
  • The SFC was seen to be more a “market regulator” or a “market facilitator”, than a “law enforcer” or “a partner with investors” that educates investors.
  • TV (81.7%) and newspapers (70.5%) were the most frequently mentioned channels through which respondents heard about SFC news.


Mr Ian Johnston, Adviser to Chairman’s Office, said the SFC would consider appropriate publicity or investor education responses in areas of misconception or relatively low recognition levels.

“For instance, more than half of the respondents mistakenly thought that the SFC was responsible for supervising banks’ securities business or that we operated the stock exchange. Many also thought that the SFC could order a stockbroker to compensate clients. The report provides explanations to clarify those misconceptions,” he said.

“We also notice that despite the large amount of investor education work done, there was relatively low level of recognition of the investor education role of the SFC. To further highlight this role, we have included in SFC newspaper columns, articles and seminars a message to remind readers and participants that those are SFC investor education initiatives,” Mr Johnston added.

The SFC will further study the findings and determine, in light of its corporate strategic directions, how it may more effectively get its messages across to the public.
