A rights issue is a common vehicle used by listed companies to raise funds, but investors should not buy rights shares just because they are offered at a discount.

In his latest column, “Know Your Rights to a Rights Issue,” Dr Wise explains to investors how a rights issue works and what they should note.

During a rights issue, investors can subscribe for all or part of their allotted rights, or even top up their subscriptions by applying for “excess rights shares” not taken up by other shareholders. Investors should pay attention to the deadlines for exercising their rights and for settling the purchase. They also should keep in mind that the subscription process differs depending on whether their shares are held as physical certificates in their names or entrusted to their intermediaries.

If they decide not to take up the rights shares, investors do not have to take any action, unless they want to sell their rights in the open market. However, not taking up their rights will result in a dilution of their shareholding in the expanded capital of the company.

Dr Wise also reminds investors that just like investing in a new stock, they should know the purpose of the fund raising and consider their financial capability and the prospects of the company.

“Rights issues are based on commercial decisions of listed companies, and carried out in compliance with the relevant rules and regulations,” Dr Wise notes. “Investors should go through their usual investment evaluation process when deciding on rights issues.”
