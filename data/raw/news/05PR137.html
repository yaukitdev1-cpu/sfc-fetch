The SFC today releases the Consultation Conclusions on Draft Practice Note on Overseas Investments by SFC-authorised REITs, the final version of the Practice Note and the revised Code on REITs which incorporates changes made consequential to the adoption of the Practice Note.

The SFC published in March proposals to allow SFC-authorised REITs to invest in overseas properties. During the one-month consultation period, 19 responses were received and they unanimously supported the relaxation of the geographical restriction and the proposal to regulate all SFC-authorised REITs under the Code on REITs. Responses were also generally in favour of the SFC’s initiative to raise the gearing ratio and to consider special product features, such as payment of management fees by way of REIT units. The Practice Note and the related amendments to the Code on REITs have incorporated their comments where appropriate and clarified issues raised by the respondents.

The major points to note are:

  1. the geographical restrictions on overseas investments are lifted so that REITs may invest in real estate anywhere in the world;
  2. the experience in managing property portfolio investments is recognised as a core competence for the purpose of assessing the qualification of a REIT management company;
  3. gearing ratio is raised to 45% of gross asset value of a REIT;
  4. additional layers of special purpose vehicles may be considered in special circumstances;
  5. proposals to take out professional indemnity insurance and title insurance are removed; and
  6. special product features may be considered by the SFC on a case-by-case basis – e.g. payment of management fees by way of units.


In the revision, the SFC has incorporated into the Code provisions in the Practice Note that are applicable to all REITs, whether they invest in Hong Kong or overseas properties. The Practice Note now serves to elaborate on the application of the Code specifically in the context of overseas investments. The revised Code on REITs, including the Practice Note, will become effective upon gazettal this month.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The Code has been drafted in a flexible manner with an aim to accommodate new product features as the REIT market develops. Thus the SFC is prepared to consider new product features, such as different types and means of structuring management fees, so long as investors’ interests are not prejudiced, there are adequate safeguards to prevent conflicts of interests, and there is proper disclosure of these features to the public."

The Consultation Conclusions Paper is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website.
