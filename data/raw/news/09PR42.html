The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) once again reminds investors to be as up to date as possible about factors that could impact their investments and the options open to them. It is only then that investors are able to make informed decisions.

Many well known companies around the world continue to be affected adversely by the ongoing global economic downturn. In this environment, even when they don’t have a direct investment in these companies, investors must remain alert to how they may be indirectly exposed to the credit risk of these companies, including any collateral securing their investments. For instance, in the case of a credit-linked note (CLN) that is secured by a collateralised debt obligation (CDO), investors need to be aware that any default by entities named in the CDO’s underlying portfolio, could cause the note to be terminated early. In these circumstances the amount recoverable by the investor is likely to be significantly less than the original investment amount.

Where investors who hold structured products, particularly CLNs, are unsure of the current status and risks affecting their investments they should seek further information from the product distributors.

The SFC has written to note issuers requiring them to notify distributors of information that may materially affect the issuers’ ability to perform their obligations. This should include any credit downgrade of the underlying reference entities or the collateral. Investors can obtain this information, including collateral information, directly from distributors.

To understand the current situation and the market prices of their structured notes, investors may ask the relevant distributor from whom they bought the product questions such as:

  • What is the buy-back price of the note (Note 1) and what are the factors affecting its calculation?
  • What are the security arrangements backing this investment? This may include:
    - the mark-to-market value of the collateral;
    - whether the issuer has advised them of any credit rating downgrade of collateral or any other event that may affect the value of the note (Note 2); and
    - the nature of the collateral (Note 3), whether it includes CDOs and, if so, which entities are named in the underlying portfolio. The collapse of a number of reference companies may result in a significant reduction in the value of CDOs that reference those entities and further defaults may lead to early termination of the notes.

Investors can find out more about CDOs by visiting the SFC InvestEd website (www.InvestEd.hk).

Notes :

1.Investors should receive monthly statements with mark-to-market value of their investments.

2.Investors should receive issuers’ notification via their distributors of information that may materially affect issuers’ ability to perform their obligations under the notes. In the context of credit-linked notes, such information may include credit downgrade of the underlying reference entities or the collateral.

3.Collateral information forms part of the “display documents” which should be available from the offices of the arranger during normal office hours. In any event, investors can contact their distributors for information about their investments in retail structured notes including their current prices in case investors may wish to sell back their notes before maturity.
