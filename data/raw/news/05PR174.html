The SFC is pleased to release its first Quarterly Report for the financial year to 31 March 2006 (Note 1).

During the first quarter, the SFC completed or launched many initiatives that aim to strengthen the regulatory regime and protect investors, while facilitating market development. There were three consultation exercises and two sets of code amendments. Internationally, the SFC furthered co-operation with regulators in six jurisdictions in the region and in Europe.

For the three months from 1 April to 30 June, the SFC recorded a surplus of $48.6 million. 

A busy first quarter

During the quarter, the SFC:

  • worked with the Government and HKEx to further develop the Government's proposed statutory framework to enhance listing regulation;
  • continued to review the submissions to the consultation on proposals to codify important listing requirements;
  • concluded the consultation on a review of the disclosure of interests regime;
  • issued a consultation paper on specific entry criteria for and ongoing compliance obligations on sponsors;
  • consulted on proposed revisions to the Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Guidance Note;
  • amended the Code on REITs to allow SFC-authorised REITs to invest in overseas properties;
  • issued a consultation paper on proposals to revise the Hedge Funds Guidelines;
  • facilitated the launch of two index funds under the Asia Bond Fund 2 framework and the FTSE/Xinhua China 25 Index Futures and Options Contracts;
  • amended the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds to allow SFC-authorised funds to invest in REITs that are listed overseas;
  • approved HKEx’s rule amendments on the reduction of minimum spreads;
  • played an active role in the development of eXtensive Business Reporting Language (XBRL) in Hong Kong;
  • prosecuted 18 entities and disciplined 14 licensees for various regulatory breaches;
  • imposed licensing conditions on a brokerage firm to help it adopt prudent business practice and strengthen its risk management;
  • jointly produced a radio drama series with Radio Television Hong Kong to increase investors’ awareness of the issue of analyst conflicts;
  • launched new investor education workshops for teachers;
  • signed Letters of Intent with our counterparts in Sri Lanka, India, Guernsey and Malaysia on the enhancement of regulatory co-operation relating to investment funds;
  • signed a Statement of Intent with the Financial Services Agency of Japan on co-operation, consultation and the exchange of information;
  • exchanged Side Letters with the Monetary Authority of Macau under an existing MOU on exchange of information; and
  • jointly organised a securities regulation conference in Hong Kong with the Financial Stability Institute of the Bank for International Settlements for 30 central bankers from across the region. 


Surplus recorded

The first quarter surplus of $48.6 million compared with a projected surplus of $2.5 million in the approved budget for 2005-2006.

Total revenue was $164.6 million, 13% higher than the same period last year.  Levy, fees and charges and investment incomes all increased owing to increased market activity and higher interest rates. Total expenditure (including depreciation) amounted to $116 million, 8% lower than the approved budget of $126 million. 

At the end of June, reserves stood at $908.6 million, equivalent to 1.8 times the approved annual operating expenditure (including depreciation) of $508 million.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said that based on the current information and by managing the budget prudently, a surplus was expected in the second quarter.
