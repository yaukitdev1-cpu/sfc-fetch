Following the findings of its latest retail investor survey, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) will focus more investor education efforts on educating the investing public on the essential and elementary factors to consider before investing.

Conducted in July and August, just before the recent financial turmoil, the survey is the most comprehensive of its kind commissioned by the SFC (Note 1). The survey reveals that although most respondents were aware that their investments carried risks, many did not buy products they were familiar with when making the investment decisions. Two-thirds of the investors surveyed knew very little about at least one of the products they had invested in.

Also, a significant portion of investors did not harbour realistic expectation on returns and risks and expected a level of returns that was disproportionate to the level of risks they were willing to accept (Appendices).

Only around half of investors exercised a stop-loss strategy. Among the less experienced investors, this proportion was even lower.

“The survey findings show that many investors still need to ask some very basic and fundamental questions before investing,” said the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer Mr Martin Wheatley. “This is an underlying requirement, which is as valid now, in today’s volatile financial markets, as it was at the time of our survey. That’s why in our latest investor education campaign we are going back to basics.”

The SFC’s latest investor education campaign reminds investors to examine the “Five Elements” before making investment decisions by asking these basic, but critical, questions:
1. Why am I investing?
2. What product meets my needs?
3. Do I understand the product?
4. What is my maximum loss?
5. How can I get my money back?

An easy-to-carry calendar card that lists these questions will be distributed to the public as part of the SFC’s investor education initiatives (Note 2).

In the current financial year, the SFC has increased resources for investor education. Other educational activities being planned or conducted include: promulgating Dr Wise’s advice on radio from 20 November and organising the Investment Triathlon, a cross-media financial knowledge quiz, on radio, with a finale on TV to be aired next January. The SFC also will hold an Investor Day and roll out a new TV advertising campaign in January 2009.
