The SFC today releases its Quarterly Report for the financial period 1 April to 30 June 2003 (Note 1).

Deficit Lower than Projected

In the first quarter of the Financial Year 2003-2004, the SFC recorded a deficit of $7.4 million, 70% lower than the projected deficit of $24.8 million in the approved budget.

Total revenue increased to $93.5 million, 3% higher than a year ago, due to higher-than-projected market turnover and the receipt of dual filing fees from the Stock Exchange. Total expenditure also rose 3% to $100.8 million, as a result of office relocation and additional personnel expenses for dual filing. The total number of staff at the end of June 2003 was 408, compared to 405 a year ago.

At the end of June 2003, the SFC's reserves stood at $558 million, compared with $617 million at the end of June 2002

SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng said the financial outlook of the SFC for the rest of the year remained uncertain despite an apparent recovery of the market in the first quarter. But based on current information and by keeping a tight rein on expenditures, he expected that the projected $21.9 million deficit for the next quarter would not be exceeded.

Work Progress
Following the commencement of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) on 1 April, the SFC continued to strengthen its enforcement work and co-operated with the Administration and Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx) to press ahead with reforms to enhance corporate governance standards and facilitate market developments.

During the quarter, the SFC:

  • provided guidance to the market as far as possible to assist practitioners in their understanding of the SFO. The new law has been implemented smoothly;
  • reviewed 42 listing applications under dual filing and commented on 16;
  • joined hands with HKEx to consult the market on the regulation of IPO sponsors and independent financial advisers;
  • together with the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau consulted the market on a proposal to empower the SFC to initiate derivative actions;
  • formed a working group to classify securities related fees into broad and standardised categories to increase fees transparency;
  • chaired an Investor Participant (IP) Account Task Force to develop an enhanced IP account model to help remove custody and settlement risks from brokers;
  • polled investors on analysts’ conflict of interests following an earlier survey of licensed firms. The SFC will consider the survey results when formulating regulatory proposals for consultation;
  • worked with the Administration to introduce the Companies (Amendment) Bill before the Legislative Council to facilitate share offering;
  • consulted the market on the proposed Code on Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs). The final Code was published in July;
  • consulted the market on the regulation of Exchange Traded Funds. The final guidelines will be released in the third quarter of 2003;
  • had 17 successful prosecutions and disciplined 17 licensees for various regulatory breaches;
  • signed a Declaration on Co-operation and Supervision of Cross-Border Investment Management Activities with the Australian Securities and Investments Commission;
  • rolled out an educational campaign on portfolio planning and published more education materials on hedge fund and exotic warrants.
