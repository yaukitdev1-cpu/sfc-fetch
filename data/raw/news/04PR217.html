The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

For the three months from July to September 2004, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 42 listing applications. This is the highest number of applications in any quarter since the Dual Filing regime came into effect on 1 April 2003. In particular, there was a marked increase in the number of applications with planned offer size of over $100 million (33 such cases as compared to 17 cases for April to June).

It was important to monitor this increased activity and the SFC temporarily allocated additional resources to its review of listing applications. We commented on 23 cases. Our average response time remained seven working days.

Disclosure on relationships with other parties

A common problem the SFC has noticed in its administration of Dual Filing since last year concerns the disclosure on an applicant’s relationships with other parties. There have been a number of cases where the company has one or more of the following characteristics:

  • Dependence on a single supplier for substantially finished goods to which it does not add much further processing;
  • Dependence on a single customer for a substantial amount of its total revenue and profits;
  • Reliance on a few distribution agents whose customers it does not know;
  • Reliance on another party for R&D or product development;
  • Acquisition or disposal of significant parts of its operations shortly before the listing application;
  • Provision of financing or generous credit from or to the above parties; and
  • That the above parties are themselves related.


Overall, disclosure in these cases did not present a clear enough picture for investors to be able to make an informed assessment of the relationships between the parties as well as the business model and prospects of the listing applicant.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: "The SFC understands that the business environment is complex and, especially for companies with operations in the Mainland, complex relationships with other parties are often necessary. This increases the need for a listing applicant to explain the relationships to potential investors. The sponsor must also perform its due diligence to understand those relationships and help the applicant present a fair and accurate picture."

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
