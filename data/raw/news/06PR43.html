Nine out of 10 warrant investors were aware that derivative warrants were highly risky and many traded consciously based on their own analysis, according to an SFC Warrant Investor Survey.

After completion of its Retail Investor Survey 2005, the SFC conducted the Warrant Investor Survey to ascertain retail investors’ objectives of trading derivative warrants, their knowledge of the product and whether they gained or lost. A total of 250 warrant investors were interviewed by telephone in January and February 2006 (Note 1).

Retail participation rate

4.7% of Hong Kong adults, or 12.9% of retail investors (Note 2), had traded warrants during the last two years and were identified as warrant investors. By estimation, there are about 270,000 warrant investors in Hong Kong.

Perceived risks of warrants and trading objectives

  • 92.4% of warrant investors perceived derivative warrants as a high risk or very high risk product.
  • 76% of warrant investors said that they had some knowledge about warrants and hoped to make short-term gains; 8.4% used warrants for risk management (e.g. hedging) or long-term investment purposes; 10.8% admitted they knew nothing about warrants and simply bet on their luck.


Trading history and understanding of warrants

  • 73.2% of warrant investors had been trading warrants for more than 12 months.
  • 51.6% said derivative warrants accounted for less than 10% of their investment portfolios.
  • 76% of warrant investors understood that the underlying asset’s price is not the only factor that affects the price of a warrant.
  • 47.6% of warrant investors understood that a higher implied volatility indicates a derivative warrant is more expensive.


Trading decisions and results

  • Warrant investors chose warrants as an investment tool mainly because of their higher volatility (52%) and better liquidity (23.6%).
  • In selecting a specific derivative warrant, 76.8% of warrant investors said they did their own analysis. 19.6% were influenced by market commentators’ views in the mass media and 14.8% by friends or family members. Only 8.4% and 8% considered recommendations of issuers and brokers respectively.
  • 83.6% of warrant investors usually compared similar warrants before deciding to trade a particular one.
  • 30.8% of warrant investors said that they had made a net gain in the past 12 months, 47.2% a net loss, and 12% broke even. 10% said they did not know.
  • Despite the losses, 47.5% of loss-making warrant investors expressed that they would continue to trade warrants. Among them, 53.6% said they understood the risk of investing and were prepared to take the risk. 41.1% believed that they had learnt more about warrants and hoped to do better in future. 37.5% hoped to recover their losses from further trading.


SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “Risk awareness and product understanding are important areas that we always emphasise in our investor education work. As indicated in the Survey, while most warrant investors are aware of the high risks of derivative warrants and choose to take the risks, there is a need to enhance investor understanding of this product and the use of warrant analytics (e.g. implied volatility). To this end, it is important for all parties – regulators, market operators, warrant issuers and brokers – to work together. The SFC has been doing its part through different kinds of investor education programmes, including the forthcoming TV series on warrants and alternative investments that is now in production.”

The detailed report of the Warrant Investor Survey is attached to the electronic version of this press release. It is also posted on the SFC website under “ Speeches, Publications & Consultation” – “Publications” – “Surveys and Reports”.

Notes:

1. Conducted in September to November 2005, the Retail Investor Survey 2005 focused on finding out retail participation in different investment products, measuring the level of financial understanding of retail investors in some areas and soliciting their views on the SFC’s investor education work. Please refer to SFC press release dated 9 March 2006 for details.

The 250 warrant investors were drawn from two samples. 246 warrant investors identified in the Retail Investor Survey 2005 were contacted and 130 of them participated in the Warrant Investor Survey. The remaining 120 warrant investors were separately identified through making calls to 12,702 randomly selected residential phone numbers.

2. In the Retail Investor Survey 2005, out of the 5,210 adults interviewed, 1,915 had invested in one or more kinds of investment products (e.g. stocks, managed funds (excluding MPF investments), bonds, derivatives, structured products, leveraged foreign exchange contracts) in the last two years, and were identified as retail investors.
