A delegation from the SFC attended the Annual Conference of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) in Seoul, Korea this week. The SFC is pleased to draw your attention to the following:

  • Hong Kong has been selected to host the 31st Annual Conference of IOSCO in 2006. This will be the first time that Hong Kong hosts the IOSCO Annual Conference.
  • SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, has been appointed Interim Chairman of the IOSCO Technical Committee until the end of the next IOSCO Annual Conference to be held in May 2004.
  • The SFC welcomes the announcement by IOSCO of a Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding Concerning Consultation and Co-operation and the Exchange of Information (MOU). Hong Kong is amongst the first securities regulators worldwide to have signed the MOU. It participated in the IOSCO taskforce that drafted the MOU and sits on a group that reviews applications to sign the MOU.


Speaking from Seoul, Mr Sheng said: “We are very honoured that Hong Kong has been selected as host jurisdiction of the 2006 Annual Conference. The strong support of the Hong Kong Government has been instrumental in securing our bid. I am grateful that the Financial Secretary, Mr Henry Tang, has also expressed support in his statement today to welcome IOSCO’s decision. We pledge to work together to make the event a success.”

The Executive Committee of IOSCO decided this week that the 2006 Annual Conference would be held in Hong Kong. The SFC will be the host organisation. Other than the Seoul conference this week, IOSCO last held its Annual Conference in Asia Pacific in 2000, when Sydney was host.

Mr Sheng said: “I am also honoured to be appointed as Interim Chairman of the Technical Committee. The SFC is an active member of the Technical Committee and, as a member of its five Standing Committees, contributes regularly to its work.” Former SFC Chairman, Mr Anthony Neoh, chaired the Technical Committee from 1996 to 1998.

Mr Sheng said of the MOU: “It is a significant arrangement for international co-operation. The enhanced level of co-operation enabled by the MOU means regulators worldwide have a greater ability to assist each other in combating cross border securities fraud, market misconduct, terrorist financing and other financial crimes. The SFC is committed to co-operating with regulators worldwide through the MOU. I am pleased that Hong Kong is amongst the first signatories.”

Ends

Notes:

1. IOSCO is the primary forum for international co-operation among securities regulators and is recognised as the international standard-setter for the securities sector. It has 181 members from more than 100 jurisdictions. IOSCO held its 28th Annual Conference in Seoul from 14 to 17 October 2003. Jordan and Sri Lanka will be host countries in 2004 and 2005 respectively.

2. The Technical Committee is formed under the Executive Committee of IOSCO. It is made up of 15 agencies that regulate some of the world’s larger, more developed and internationalised markets. Its objective is to review major regulatory issues related to international securities and futures transactions and to coordinate practical responses to these concerns. The work of the Technical Committee is divided into the following five major functional subject areas:

  • Multinational Disclosure and Accounting;
  • Regulation of Secondary Markets;
  • Regulation of Market Intermediaries;
  • Enforcement and the Exchange of Information; and
  • Investment Management.


Mr David Knott, Chairman of the Australian Securities and Investments Commission, stepped down as Chairman of the Technical Committee prior to the expiry of his term in May 2004. Mr Sheng was appointed as Interim Chairman of the Technical Committee with effect from the end of the Seoul Annual Conference.
