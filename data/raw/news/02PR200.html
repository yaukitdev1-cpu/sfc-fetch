The SFC issues this statement in response to certain media reports today of comments made by Mr Ashley Alder, Executive Director of Corporate Finance, at a conference yesterday.

These reports indicated that the SFC was studying or had an intention to introduce class action shareholder lawsuits in Hong Kong.

In fact, Mr Alder yesterday said that, in contrast with the United States, Hong Kong law did not operate a class action or contingency fee system. As a result, the regulatory environment in Hong Kong is different from that of the US (and closer to that exists in the UK). Hong Kong therefore needs to be aware of this when assessing the local regulatory system and the investor protection measures.

Mr Alder did confirm in his speech that the SFC was studying the Standing Committee on Company Law Reform's proposals regarding regulatory derivative actions.

An SFC spokesman said: "The question of class action suits and contingency fees is a matter of legal policy and does not fall within the SFC's remit. It is not a subject on which the SFC expresses any views."

"The SFC also wishes to emphasise that derivative actions are not the same as class actions," the spokesman said.

Mr Alder was speaking at the In-house Congress Asia Pacific 2002.
