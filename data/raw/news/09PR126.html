Making full use of its legal powers and operating independently have enabled the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) to achieve significant results in its enforcement actions, the SFC’s Executive Director of Enforcement, Mr Mark Steward, said.

Speaking at a seminar organised by the Hong Kong Securities Institute, Mr Steward stressed the need to develop a full spectrum of remedies, as he recounted outcomes of key SFC enforcement actions in the past 12 months.

This has led the SFC to take multi-faceted approaches in tackling market misconduct, he said. The actions ranged from seeking court orders to freeze the proceeds of individuals suspected of securities frauds to prosecuting individuals for insider dealing and other market breaches.

These approaches have enabled the SFC to secure nine criminal convictions with immediate jail terms in five cases within the past 12 months, a unique record which Mr Steward said only Hong Kong can pride on.

“Regulatory problems will continue to arise. Hence, the measure of a regulator is not the extent to which problems can be avoided, but whether they can be resolved when they arise. A regulator is part of the solution and not part of the problem,” he added.

Of equal importance is the ability for the regulator to be able to operate independently.

Citing the landmark agreement the SFC reached with intermediaries over the sales of Minibonds, Mr Steward said: “These types of outcomes can only be achieved by regulators who are able to operate independently and committed to taking into account all relevant points of view and at the same time to resist sectoral interests and influences, from wherever they may come, to prevent them from determining the outcome.”

He also pointed out that the Minibond agreement is “more than just the dollar value of return to investors”.

Apart from enabling investors to recover a substantial portion of the money they have lost and offering the prospect of additional payout upon successful sales of underlying collateral, the Minibond agreement contains programmes aimed at re-engineering sales systems of intermediaries and resolving complaints about the structured products, Mr Steward said.

The commission income which the banks have to forgo to set up a fighting fund will also help to accelerate the process of bringing back the collateral to Hong Kong which will be returned to the investors.

Mr Steward pledged that the SFC will remain “impartial and loyal” to its statutory objectives to achieve “fair and just resolutions”.
