More investors may be drawn to bonds when the stock market is volatile and deposit rates are low, but that doesn’t make bonds work for every investor.

In his latest column entitled “Are bonds the answer to ‘safe’ investment?” Dr Wise notes that certain considerations will have to be taken into account even when investing in so-called plain vanilla bonds or those with standard features.

Bonds may not be suitable for everyone for a number of reasons. For example:

  • As debt instruments that pay a pre-determined interest at fixed intervals and repay the principal by a certain date, bonds typically require financial commitment for a number of years. During the term of the bonds, their market value fluctuates inversely with interest rate movements.
  • Some bonds are not traded in any secondary market and so investors may need to hold them until maturity.
  • Even when holding the bonds until maturity, bond investors bear the credit risk of not getting back the principal if the bond issuer defaults.
  • For a bond that is not denominated in the local currency, fluctuating foreign exchange rates add further risk.


“In light of the risk factors given above, bear in mind that even so-called ‘plain vanilla’ bonds with simple features may not be suitable for all investors,” Dr Wise says. “Even if you decide that a bond suits your profile, find out about its key features and risk factors and make sure that they meet your particular needs.”

Through his column, Dr Wise, a fictitious character created by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), shares with the public his wisdom on investing and his thoughts on regulatory issues.

Dr Wise’s column is published on the last working Tuesday of every month. The column is posted under the “Dr Wise” section of the SFC’s InvestEd website. Investors are welcome to send their feedback and questions to Dr Wise by email to InvestEd@sfc.hk.
