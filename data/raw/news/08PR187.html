In today’s gloomy economic environment, investors may be tempted to look for investments that offer seemingly steadier returns, including preference shares that give priority to dividend payments.

However, the terms of preference shares can vary considerably from one company to another. Whether they are listed or unlisted, preference shares are less liquid than ordinary shares as the market for these shares is less established.

In his latest column entitled, “Find out more about preference shares”, Dr Wise draws investors’ attention to the subject and reminds them to look closely before committing to any investment in preference shares.

“The terms of preference shares can vary considerably e.g. the level of liquidity is considerably greater with some preference shares than others,” Dr Wise says. “Also, the rate of return should not be the sole factor taken into account when investing in them.”

Although preference shares offer higher dividends than common shares and have a priority over common shares in dividend payment and upon liquidation, Dr Wise warns that unlisted preference shares that are not convertible into listed common shares can be difficult or in some circumstances impossible to dispose of.

Dr Wise also explains that offer terms can sometimes be such that a company is not under any legal obligation to buy back preference shares or to locate prospective purchasers for the sellers. There are also some circumstances where the company may defer dividend payment even though the timing of dividend distribution is specified.
