The hedge fund industry has been growing in Asia.  We have seen increasing interest from overseas fund management professionals wishing to set up hedge fund management operations in Hong Kong. 

Licensing of hedge fund managers

Like other fund managers, a hedge fund management company, regardless of whether the hedge fund is offered to the public or to professional investors only, is required to be licensed by the SFC to carry out asset management in Hong Kong.  Once licensed, fund managers will be subject to on-going supervision by the SFC.

The SFC has designated a focus team to handle licence applications of hedge fund managers and deal with enquiries of prospective licensing applicants.  A designated enquiry channel (e-mail: hedgefund@hksfc.org.hk) has been set up for that purpose.

It has been reported in the media that the SFC will relax entry requirements of hedge fund managers.  We would like to reiterate that licensing is a gate-keeping function established to ensure that only fit and proper intermediaries can carry out financial services in Hong Kong. While we will facilitate and expedite market players to enter into the industry as far as possible, the regulatory standards will not be compromised.

Authorisation of retail hedge funds

Under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, where a hedge fund is offered to the public, the product is required to be authorised by the SFC.  This product authorisation is on top of the SFC’s licensing requirements in respect of hedge fund managers that operate in Hong Kong. 

The SFC introduced the Guidelines on Hedge Funds and the Guidelines on Hedge Funds Reporting Requirements in 2002.  Hong Kong is one of the few jurisdictions that allow retail offerings of hedge funds.  As hedge funds have their own risks and may not be suitable to every investor, there must be vigorous regulatory thresholds to protect the interests of retail investors.  Key authorisation criteria as reflected in the guidelines include requirements on experience and competence of fund managers, and disclosures to investors regarding the product features and associated risks.   To date, the SFC has authorised 13 retail hedge funds with assets under management of approximately US$ 1.13 billion.   We would use that experience to review the Guidelines on Hedge Funds within this financial year.   

Given that hedge funds are complex products, the review aims to strengthen the present disclosure standards of hedge funds (including funds-of-hedge funds) to improve transparency to investors and further promote informed investment decisions.  We hope to provide more flexibility in administering the Guidelines, such as accepting a wider range of experience as qualifying experience in respect of management companies of retail hedge funds.    The SFC will discuss with market groups on these areas and take into account suggestions from the industry before issuing a consultation paper.

Investor education is a key factor in the healthy growth of the hedge funds industry.   The SFC has published various investor education brochures, leaflets, FAQs, and articles on different channels in respect of hedge funds.  Hedge funds are not for everyone.  In considering investment in hedge funds, investors must have regard to their financial resources, investment experience, and investment objectives.

Speaking from London, Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said:  “Hong Kong’s regulatory standard is highly regarded as a seal of quality by international market players and clients.  The rigorous licensing and product authorisation framework serves to protect investors and maintain market integrity and quality.”
