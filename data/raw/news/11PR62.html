The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has published today two papers to explain in precise yet simple language its existing regulatory and supervisory framework for intermediaries.

The first paper entitled “Regulatory Framework for Intermediaries” outlines the broad role and approach taken by, as well as the underlying philosophy that guides, the SFC in regulating intermediaries (Note 1). The second paper entitled “Approach to Supervision of Intermediaries” focuses on the detailed processes underpinning the SFC’s approach to supervision of intermediaries (Note 2).

“The papers are intended to further enhance the transparency of our current regulatory and supervisory work over intermediaries,” said Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer. “By publishing these papers, external stakeholders are given a better understanding of how we regulate and supervise intermediaries, and what we thereby aim to achieve.”

The papers are published as part of the SFC's continual effort to enhance its communication with external stakeholders regarding its work, priorities, views on topical and important regulatory issues and other developments.

End

Notes:

1. The term “intermediary” refers to a corporation licensed with the SFC under section 116 or 117 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571) (SFO) as a licensed corporation, or an authorized financial institution (a bank, restricted licence bank or deposit-taking company) registered with the SFC under section 119 of the SFO as a registered institution.

2. The day-to-day supervision of registered institutions is undertaken by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority.
