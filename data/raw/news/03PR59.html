The SFC today publishes a public consultation paper on draft guidelines for regulating passively managed index tracking Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) under the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds. 

In general, ETFs are funds that are listed or traded on stock exchanges. All the ETFs that are currently listed or traded on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and most ETFs in other markets are index tracking.  To track an index, a typical ETF purchases a basket of securities that replicates the constituents of the index.  Because the ETF’s nature is mainly to replicate an index, its composition and pricing are highly transparent.  ETFs are popular with investors who wish to diversify by investing in an index or to use them as arbitraging tools. 

Given the relatively simple investment objective and strategy of ETFs and their transparency, the SFC considers that passively managed index tracking ETFs should be given some relief from strict compliance with certain Code requirements that are designed for funds that are not necessarily exchange traded.  To be eligible for the relief, ETFs would be required to provide certain trading information and product disclosure documents to the public to ensure there is sufficient transparency. 

The draft ETF Guidelines aim to achieve three objectives:

  • To provide appropriate regulations in the Code for regulating passively managed index tracking ETFs: this is achieved through the granting of general and specific relief from strict compliance with certain Code provisions;
  • To enhance the disclosure and transparency of ETF documentation: we propose the use of a Key Features Summary, a Product Description Document and the publication of ETF documentation through electronic media such as the HKEx website etc;
  • To create a platform for the admission of overseas ETFs based on the principle of “regulatory equivalence”: overseas jurisdictions that share similar investment regulatory principles and are already effectively regulating ETFs in their home markets are considered “Acceptable ETF Regimes” under the Code.  Our studies support the admission of the US as the first Acceptable ETF Regime.


The draft ETF Guidelines represent the first step by the SFC towards creating a regulatory framework to facilitate the development of Hong Kong into a regional ETF market.  The SFC is looking into other initiatives including the streamlining of listing rules and procedures for the listing of SFC authorised funds on the stock exchange and enhancing investor awareness of ETFs as an efficient and effective investment or hedging tool.

Ms Tina So, SFC’s Director of Investment Products, said: “We have seen rapid development in ETF markets worldwide and increased investor interest in ETFs.  While Hong Kong is home of one of the world’s top 10 largest ETFs, the Hong Kong Tracker, we see a lot of potential in further developing Hong Kong into a regional ETF market.  The draft ETF Guidelines are intended to serve as a platform on which we will develop a fair and appropriate regulatory framework for ETFs without compromising investor protection.” 

The consultation will last for one month and end on 17 April 2003.  The SFC will prepare a consultation conclusions document to summarise the views expressed by the respondents and set out the final guidelines.
