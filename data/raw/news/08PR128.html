Investors who conduct leveraged trading of foreign exchange may consider bringing disputes with SFC-licensed traders before an arbitrator as an alternative to civil litigation, Dr Wise suggests.

In his latest column entitled “Arbitration – resolving leveraged foreign exchange trading disputes”, Dr Wise reminds investors that the Securities and Futures Ordinance entitles them to choose arbitration as a means of resolving trading disputes.

Dr Wise explains that arbitration, presided over by a single expert acting much like a referee, involves a set of established and recognised procedures bearing many similarities to civil litigation. However, while proceedings of a civil case are conducted in the open, those of arbitration are conducted in private.

An arbitrator’s ruling is legally binding, final and enforceable in Hong Kong and other jurisdictions. Compared with civil litigation through the courts, arbitration is usually less expensive and less time-consuming.

Investors who wish to take trading disputes over leveraged forex transactions to arbitration should contact the Hong Kong International Arbitration Centre, which helps administer the arbitration proceedings.
