The SFC today releases its Quarterly Report for the financial period 1 July to 30 September 2003.

Surplus recorded for the first six months

For the second quarter of 2003-04, the SFC recorded a surplus of $24.4 million. This has reversed our financial position from a deficit of $7.4 million in the first quarter to an accumulated surplus of $17 million for the first six months of this financial year. This compares favourably to a budgeted projection of half-year deficit of $47 million.

At the end of September, the reserves stood at $583 million, equivalent to 1.35 times of the approved annual operating expenditure of $433 million.

Total revenue in the first half was $219 million, 24% higher than a year ago. The operating results were better than expected mainly because levy and fees incomes had exceeded the original estimates. While the SFC underspent in all expenditure categories except premises, total expenditure for the six months rose 3% (compared to the same period last year) to $202 million mainly because of the office relocation expenditure and additional personnel expenses for dual filing.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said that although market sentiment had improved and the SFC had a surplus instead of a deficit as projected for the first six months, the SFC would continue to exercise stringent controls on its expenditure. The expenditure for the October-December quarter is forecast to be lower than the budgeted expenditure by $4 million.

Work Progress

During the second quarter, the SFC:

  • continued to provide guidance to the industry on the implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO). Migration to the new licensing regime by intermediaries gathered pace;
  • reviewed more new listing applications under dual filing. It had reviewed 61 new applications from April to September and commented on 24 of them;
  • worked with the Stock Exchange to analyse the responses to the consultation on enhanced regulation of sponsors;
  • worked closely with the China Securities Regulatory Commission and the Mainland industry associations in implementing Mainland’s commitment under Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement to simplify the procedures for SFC licence holders who are Hong Kong permanent residents applying for securities and futures industry qualifications in the Mainland;
  • successfully concluded the review of Chapter 20 of the Listing Rules of HKEx for streamlining the listing of SFC-authorised collective investment schemes;
  • gazetted the Code on Real Estate Investment Trusts;
  • published the consultation conclusions for a proposed scripless securities market. The proposed split register model was broadly accepted by the industry;
  • had 20 successful prosecutions and disciplined 25 licensees for regulatory breaches;
  • produced for the first time a Cantonese documentary video drama, "Know the SFC", to explain its work and the improved regulatory framework under the SFO;
  • included Australia as an Acceptable Inspection Regime under the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds;
  • briefed legislators on the risk facing the securities margin financing industry. The SFC is working with the industry on appropriate interim measures to manage down the risks of imprudent practices; and
  • welcomed the publication by the International Organization of Securities Commissions of regulatory principles and measures to tackle conflicts of interest faced by sell-side securities analysts. The SFC had taken part in the taskforce developing the principles.
