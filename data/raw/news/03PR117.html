The SFC today publishes its Annual Report for the year ended 31 March 2003. The Report is today laid before the LegCo following submission to the Financial Secretary.

Theme

The theme of this year’s Annual Report is "New Regulatory Framework – Better Quality of Market". The SFC believes the commencement of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) will further improve market quality.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said: "The translucent "equal" symbol featured on the cover represents fairness, openness and transparency. The SFC adheres to these principles when performing its regulatory functions. We are also mindful of our duty to ensure the Hong Kong market is fair, open and transparent. The SFO will help us further this objective."

Disclosure

The Annual Report gives a detailed account of the Commission's work and financial results in the financial year 2002-2003 and discusses plans for the current year.

To further enhance accountability and transparency, the Annual Report has a dedicated chapter on corporate governance and has for the first time published individual attendance rates of members of the Commission and other internal committees. Remuneration of the top three tiers of senior executives is also disclosed, in line with the recommendations of the Government-commissioned Hay Report published in mid-2002.

A new chapter on the SFO highlights all the positive changes the new law brings to the market. Extensive discussions of the SFO also appear in various chapters.

Finances

Total revenue of the SFC dropped by 7% to $337.2 million last year due to market conditions. The Commission’s tight cost controls, imposed since the second half of 2001, successfully reduced total operating expenditure after depreciation by 5% to $395.6 million.

Staff costs were down 4% to $302.4 million as the SFC freezed staff salary. For the second consecutive year, there were no variable pay awards made in 2002-2003. Vacancies were not filled except for dual filing functions and urgent operational needs. Training expenses and overseas secondment were also scaled down.

As a result, the SFC managed to contain the deficit for last year at $58.4 million, 50% lower than the original budgeted deficit. By the end of March 2003, the Commission's reserves stood at $565.6 million, equivalent to 16 months of estimated expenditure (including depreciation) of $433.3 million for 2003-2004.

Market conditions remain largely uncertain. With revenue estimated to increase marginally to $339.9 million, the SFC forecasts an operating deficit of $93.3 million for 2003-2004.

Priority

Looking ahead, the SFC is confident that the new regulatory framework under the SFO will lead to better market quality. The SFC will enforce the law to give appropriate protection to investors.

"We have clear objectives laid down by law, and pledge to do our best to implement the law to protect the interests of investors," Mr Sheng said.
