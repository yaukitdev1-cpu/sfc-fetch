The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

In the 12 months from April 2004 to March 2005, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 122 listing applications.  This represents a moderate increase in the number of listing applications from 117 for the 13 months from March 2003 to March 2004 (Note 1).  There was a marked increase in applications with planned offer sizes over $100 million (88 such cases as compared to 54 cases for the previous 13 months).

Of the 122 listing applications, we raised comments on 64 cases, of which 26 remained active as at 31 March 2005, 33 had lapsed or been withdrawn or rejected, and five had been listed.

In the three months from January to March 2005, the SFC received 30 listing applications.  Following an historical low number of 18 applications in the three months from October to December 2004, the market regained momentum in the following quarter, particularly towards its end.  The SFC received about half of the 30 applications during the second half of March 2005.

Of the 30 applications, we commented on 19 cases.  Our average response time was seven working days.  As reported previously, for the three months from October to December 2004, we commented on 8 cases out of 18 applications received.

Disclosure on applicable regulations

Issuers are often subject to particular rules and regulations by virtue of the industry in which they operate.  Comprehensive disclosure of this regulatory environment and the compliance level is fundamental to permit an understanding of the issuer’s operations and the risks involved.  During the quarter ended 31 March 2005, the SFC noted insufficient such disclosure in several cases.

For example, in one case the listing applicant was exporting most of its products to the USA, and might be exposed to significant anti-dumping duties.  However, disclosure was not sufficient for investors to understand the circumstances in which such duties could become payable.  Accordingly, there was insufficient disclosure to permit an informed assessment of the applicant’s operations, and the SFC required this to be remedied.

Complete and consistent disclosure

The SFC also noticed, in the quarter covered by this update, an increase in cases of  incomplete or clearly inconsistent disclosure.  The SFC is concerned to ensure that sponsors dedicate sufficient resources to ensuring that listing applications are ready before submission.  The quality of disclosure must not be compromised.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said:  “Knowing the full story is fundamental when investors decide whether to invest or not.  The sponsor plays an important role in presenting a fair and accurate picture to investors.  As SFC licensees, sponsors are expected to act professionally and diligently.”

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
