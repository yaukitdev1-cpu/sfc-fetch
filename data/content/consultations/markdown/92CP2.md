# INTRODUCTION

> The Commission has issued a Consultative Document inviting comments on draft proposals for changing the capital adequacy rules currently applicable to securities dealers under Section 65B of the Securities Ordinance. The Consultative Document also contains proposals for futures dealers and advisers.
>
> This pamphlet provides a simplified outline of the proposals. Persons interested in more detail should examine the Consultative Document.

# WHAT ARE THE EXISTING RULES?

- Securities dealers must maintain net capital and a liquidity margin in excess of $5M ($1M for sole proprietors) and $0.5M ($0.1M for sole proprietors) respectively.

- Net capital (NC) is the excess of approved assets (AA) over ranking liabilities (RL).

  > **NC = AA - RL**

- Liquidity margin (LM) is the excess of liquid assets (LA) over ranking liabilities (RL).

  > **LM = LA - RL**

- Approved assets, liquid assets and ranking liabilities are defined in detail in a Notice issued by the Commission [The Securities (Specification of Approved Assets, Liquid Assets and Ranking Liabilities) Notice 1990].

- Discounting factors ("haircuts") of between 20 and 100 per cent are applied to securities owned by the dealer ("house positions"). In addition, amounts due from third parties who settle for cash are reduced by 100 per cent after they have been outstanding for more than 5 days (the well-known "5" day rule).

# WHY CHANGE EXISTING RULES?

> The existing rules have significant deficiencies from a risk management point of view.

**Examples are**

- They treat major international dealers and small agency dealers alike

- They are too harsh in measuring the risks of holding "house positions"

- They are too harsh in measuring the risk that a cash client or other broker will not pay against delivery of securities purchased

- They do not pick up sufficiently the risks associated with undertaking large volumes of business compared with small volumes

- They do not measure off-balance sheet risks

- They do not give dealers credit for using hedging techniques to reduce risk

In addition, the two-tier test for capital and liquidity is duplicative, cumbersome and uses arbitrary measures

---

# THE PROPOSALS

- To replace the existing net capital and liquid margin tests by a single liquid capital requirement

- To link the new liquid capital requirement with the level of business undertaken by the dealer (but only after a minimum level of activity is reached)

- Dealers to have liquid capital in excess of

    $3M for corporations ($500,000 for sole proprietors); or

    5% of all liabilities (for corporations and sole proprietors)

    whichever is the greater (see Illustration I)

- To relax significantly the existing haircuts applied to a dealer's house positions (see Illustration II)

- To relax significantly the existing discount applied to a dealer's accounts receivable against delivery (see Illustration II)

- To introduce requirements for off-balance sheet risks; and

- To give dealers credit for hedging techniques where appropriate

# COMPARISON OF CURRENT NET CAPITAL AND PROPOSED LIQUID CAPITAL (cont'd)

## Notes on comparison

(a) The proposed rules marks a relaxation of the existing harsh basis of calculating house securities positions. In this example where the dealer holds investments mainly in equities listed in Hong Kong, there is an increase in liquid assets resulting from the reduction in the haircut from 40% to 25%.

(b) Receivables from brokers and cash clients which are included in liquid assets show a significant increase in the proposed rules. This is because of the more relaxed ageing reduction scale for such receivables.

(c) It is proposed to accept receivables from margin clients up to the discounted value of the underlying collateral. This will provide a reasonable cushion for the position cum counterparty risks exposed by the dealer.

(d) In the proposed rules, counterparty risks relating to accounts payable have been recognised in the same way as for accounts receivable.

(e) The proposed rules recognise off balance sheet exposures which are not properly recognised in the current rules.

# TESTING THE PROPOSALS

> The proposals were tested using the latest balance sheets of securities dealers. On this basis it was found that:

- Over 97% of sole proprietors would comply with the proposed requirements and would not have to inject additional capital at their existing level of business (299 out of 308 in number).

- 8 of the 9 who would not have complied with the proposed requirements were having difficulty in complying with the current requirements.

- 15 per cent of securities dealers would trigger the 5 per cent requirement. Of these, 80 per cent would comply with the proposed requirements.
