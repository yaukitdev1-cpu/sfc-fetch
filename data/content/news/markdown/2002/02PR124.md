The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today releases the findings of its Fund Management Activities Survey (FMAS) 2001.

The annual FMAS was first conducted for the year 1999. It aims to collect information on fund management activities in Hong Kong with a view to enabling better understanding of local fund management industry and identifying possible trends. It also helps the formulation of policies to regulate the fund management industry and to facilitate its long-term growth in Hong Kong.

FMAS 2001 invited responses from intermediaries and exempt persons who have declared fund management as a primary business, as it did in the previous two surveys. For the first time, it also gathered responses from those registrants whose primary business was the provision of advice on funds or who had gross operating income derived from either the management of funds or the provision of advice on funds. The inclusion of these registrants was intended to achieve a more comprehensive picture of the size of the fund management industry in Hong Kong.

Below are some key findings of FMAS 2001 (value in HK$ as of 31 December 2001):

- Total assets under management (AUM) by the respondents amounted to $1,484 billion, of which $1,469 billion were reported by respondents whose primary business was fund management, down 1% year-on-year.
- $668 billion worth of the total AUM were managed in Hong Kong, of which $654 billion were reported by respondents who had fund management as a primary business, representing a 4% increase over the previous year.
- About 31% of the AUM as reported by respondents who had fund management as a primary business was attributable to Hong Kong investors, an increase of 11% over the 2000 figure.
- 20% of the total AUM was invested in Hong Kong (figure available for the first time).
- Respondents provided advisory services on $140 billion worth of assets (figure available for the first time).
Alexa Lam, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The results of the survey suggested that Hong Kong remained a major centre in the region for attracting overseas funds for management. It is now for Hong Kong to step up efforts and to ensure that the flow of funds into Hong Kong continues and funds are retained in Hong Kong for management."

In terms of product development, the SFC is committed to accommodating different types of investment products that fund managers can offer to their clients to cater to different investor needs. The existence of a wide range of investment products for management in Hong Kong will also enable the development of local expertise which is crucial to Hong Kong's development as a major fund management center.

As regards the fund management industry, the SFC must maintain its proactive and transparent regulatory approach in order to attract more foreign fund management companies to choose Hong Kong as their Asian base. The SFC also works closely with the government, in consultation with industry representatives, with an aim to better understand the characteristics of the industry, to keep up-to-date with its evolvement and to formulate policies that are conducive to the further development of the industry in Hong Kong.

On the international front, the SFC continues its efforts to attain mutual recognition of fund managers with overseas authorities who adopt regulatory standards that are consistent with those in Hong Kong. The latest achievement is the consensus reached with the Australian Securities Investment Commission, with both authorities now working towards the conclusion of the co-operation and mutual recognition agreement in the third quarter of this year. Such mutual recognition will strengthen the status of Hong Kong as a regional fund management centre, enhance the exchange of fund management expertise and enable greater product innovation.

In the longer term, the growth of the fund management industry in Hong Kong will stimulate growth in auxiliary services, such as fund administration, fund accounting, trusteeship, global custody, foreign exchange and cash management.

Copies of the report of FMAS 2001 are available at the SFC office and on the Commission's webstie at www.hksfc.org.hk.
