The SFC announces that the first batch of seven items of subsidiary legislation under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) are gazetted today.

The remaining items will be gazetted in the coming weeks.

The seven items published in the Gazette today are (S&F stands for Securities and Futures):

1.     S&F (Unsolicited Calls – Exclusion) Rules

2.     S&F (Recognized Counterparty) Rules

3.     S&F (Registration of Commission Disciplinary Orders) Rules

4.     S&F (Professional Investor) Rules

5.     S&F (Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading – Exemption) Rules

6.     S&F (Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Arbitration)) Rules

7.     S&F (Exempted Instruments – Information) Rules

A brief background of the rules is attached to the endnotes.

Background of the SFO

The SFO was enacted on 13 March 2002. It consolidates and modernises 10 existing ordinances governing the securities and futures markets into a composite piece of legislation to keep the Hong Kong regulatory regime on a par with international standards and practices.

For effective regulation, the SFO provides flexibility in addressing changing market practices and global conditions by empowering the Chief Executive in Council, the Financial Secretary, the Chief Justice and the SFC to prescribe detailed and technical regulatory requirements as necessary by way of subsidiary legislation, to supplement the regulatory framework laid down under the primary legislation.  A total of 39 sets of subsidiary legislation would have to be put in place for commencing the SFO.

Each set of the draft subsidiary legislation has gone through a consultation process where appropriate.  The relevant rules or orders have been amended where necessary to reflect the comments received during the consultation.

On 22 February 2002, the House Committee of the Legislative Council established the Subcommittee on Draft Subsidiary Legislation to be made under the SFO. From March to October, the Subcommittee has held 12 meetings to study the subsidiary legislation in draft form.

Way forward

Once gazetted, the subsidiary legislation is subject to the LegCo negative vetting process, which takes seven weeks.

The subsidiary legislation will come into operation on the day appointed for the commencement of the SFO. An announcement of the date for commencement is expected to be made before the end of 2002.

The Gazette is available at the Government Publications Centre, and accessible on the website of the Government Printer or via the SFC’s website (under the SFO Section).

Notes to Editor:

1.         A brief background introduction of the rules

1.         S&F (Unsolicited Calls – Exclusion) Rules

Section 174 of the SFO prohibits intermediaries from making unsolicited calls so that members of the public will not be pressurised into buying financial products they do not want or cannot afford during the course of such calls. However, this prohibition is subject to exceptions for certain legitimate activities. The Rules prescribe exclusion of certain agreements and unsolicited calls from the application of section 174.

2.         S&F (Recognized Counterparty) Rules

The Rules captures, in addition to authorised institutions and licensed leveraged foreign exchange traders, four other types of institutions under the definition of recognized counterparty (i.e. certain local and overseas futures dealers which deal in currency futures contracts, certain overseas banks and corporations that issue debts instruments with qualifying credit rating).  The SFC can also grant relief where particular circumstances and anomalous cases do not come within the main categories but it is still appropriate to grant recognition as a recognized counterparty.

3.         S&F (Registration of Commission Disciplinary Orders) Rules

The Rules supplement the disciplinary regime for persons regulated by the SFC as laid down in Part IX of the SFO. Part IX sets out the sanctions that the SFC may impose in respect of breaches of regulatory requirements. In certain circumstances the SFC may order a regulated person to pay a fine, provided that it has had regard to the fining guidelines published in the Gazette, and may apply to the Court of First Instance to register such an order. On registration, the order is regarded for all purposes as an order of the Court of First Instance for payment of money. The Rules prescribe the manner in which the SFC may make an application to the Court to register a disciplinary order. 

4.         S&F (Professional Investor) Rules

The Rules prescribe four additional classes of persons as “professional investor” in terms of the value of assets they manage or have.  They are trust corporations having been entrusted with assets of at least $40 million, individuals having a portfolio of at least $8 million, corporations or partnerships having either a portfolio of at least $8 million or assets of at least $40 million, and corporations whose sole business is to hold investments and who are wholly owned by an individual professional investor.  The implication of falling within any of the additional classes is that certain investor protection measures are to be dispensed with or modified when the relevant activities are targeted at them. As professional investors are generally considered more capable of protecting their interests, a sufficient degree of investor protection would still be maintained while reducing the compliance cost to the market.

5.         S&F (Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading – Exemption) Rules

The Rules prescribe that certain acts performed by a person belonging to any of the three qualifying classes are to be excluded from the definition of leveraged foreign exchange trading.  The first qualifying class consists of corporations that have, or whose parent company has, a qualifying credit rating, provided that either (a) the principal business of the corporation is not in leveraged foreign exchange spot transactions, or (b) the average principal amount in each leveraged foreign exchange spot transaction entered into by the corporation is not less than $7.8 million. The second and third qualifying classes are basically licensed corporations or clients of such corporations who are performing an act in connection with the sale, purchase or transfer of a listed currency warrant; as well as issuers, and corporations in the same group of companies as the issuer, of a listed currency warrant.  The first class is already exempted under the current regime while the second and third classes have been included to facilitate the development of the listed currency warrant market by removing unnecessary technical obstacles.

6.         S&F (Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Arbitration)) Rules

Under section 118(1)(b) of the SFO, corporations licensed for Type 3 regulated activity (i.e. leveraged foreign exchange trading) shall be subject to a licensing condition that it is obliged to have disputes with clients settled by arbitration. The Rules provide for the establishment and constitution of such an arbitration panel, the selection and replacement of the arbitrator, the procedural rules for the commencement, conduct and termination of the proceedings, evidence and pleadings and other miscellaneous matters. The Rules are largely based on the existing Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Arbitration) Rules and do not reflect any changes of policy.

7.         S&F (Exempted Instruments - Information) Rules

The Rules supplement the regulatory regime for offers of investments laid down in Part IV of the SFO.  Part IV of the SFO contains the general prohibition on the issue to the public of advertisements, invitations or documents for a wide range of investments.  The prohibition is subject to a number of exemptions.  In particular, the issue of any advertisement, invitation or document made in respect of the issue of a certificate of deposit or commercial paper by certain specified persons is exempted under section 103(3)(e), (f) or (g) of the SFO if the corresponding minimum denomination and capitalisation tests are satisfied.  In order to properly monitor the development of capital markets in Hong Kong, section 110(1) of the SFO requires certain information about the issue referred to in the above exemption be filed to the SFC. The Rules prescribe such information.
