The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today publishes the revised provisions for guaranteed funds under the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds (the Code). 

The provisions aim to strengthen the qualification requirements for guarantors, enhance the disclosure requirements of offering documents and advertisements, and hence increase investor protection.

Under the revised provisions, the guarantor of a guaranteed fund must be either a licensed banking institution authorised under the Banking Ordinance or an authorised insurer authorised under the Insurance Companies Ordinance. Other substantial financial institutions may be considered to act as guarantors on a case-by-case basis, subject to the presence of sufficient regulatory supervision and acceptable financial standing.

The revised disclosure provisions essentially codify the existing disclosure practices that have evolved as a result of exchanges with sponsors in the process of vetting applications and knowledge gained on guaranteed funds. Such disclosure practices represent consensus with fund sponsors in the enhancement of existing Code. The revised provisions require more detailed disclosures in the offering documents and advertisements on the following:

1. Guarantor - information about the guarantor;
2. Guarantee - terms and conditions of guarantee;
3. Underlying investments - counterparties and nature of the underlying investments;
4. Risk warning statements - additional risk warning on investment risks and counterparty risks; and
5. Reference to offering document - advertisements should direct potential investors to the offering documents for details.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "In view of the increasing number of guaranteed funds offered in the retail market and the increasingly complex nature of these funds authorized over the past year, it is necessary to update the Code provisions on guaranteed funds to ensure that investors' interests are adequately protected."

"The revisions have been supported by market practitioners who share our view that the revised disclosure requirements will enable potential investors to better understand the features of the guaranteed funds," she added.

The revised provisions are incorporated into Chapter 8.5 of the Code and come into effect immediately. A copy of the Code is available on the SFC website (http:// www.hksfc.org.hk).

Notes for editor:

1. As at 19 March 2002, there were 68 SFC authorised guaranteed funds with a total net asset value exceeding HK$40 billion.

2. "Substantial financial institution" is defined in the Code as a licensed banking institution authorised under the Banking Ordinance or financial institution with a minimum paid-up capital of HK$150 million or its equivalent in foreign currency.
