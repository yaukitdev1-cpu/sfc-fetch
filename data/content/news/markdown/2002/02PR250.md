The SFC announces that the third batch of 12 items of subsidiary legislation necessary for the commencement of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) are gazetted today.

Together with the 14 items published in the previous two weeks (note 1) and the 11 items gazetted by the Government today, a total of 37 items have now been gazetted.

The 12 items published in the Gazette today are (S&F stands for Securities and Futures):

1. S&F (Short Selling and Securities Borrowing and Lending (Miscellaneous)) Rules

2. S&F (Contracts Limits and Reportable Positions) Rules

3. S&F (Price Stabilizing) Rules

4. S&F (Disclosure of Interests - Securities Borrowing and Lending) Rules

5. S&F (Financial Resources) Rules

6. S&F (Keeping of Records) Rules

7. S&F (Accounts and Audit) Rules

8. S&F (Contract Notes, Statements of Account and Receipts) Rules

9. S&F (Investor Compensation – Claims) Rules

10. S&F (Stock Market Listing) Rules

11. S&F (Licensing and Registration) (Information) Rules

12. S&F (Miscellaneous) Rules

A brief background of the rules is attached to the endnotes.

Background of the SFO

The SFO was enacted on 13 March 2002. It consolidates and modernises 10 existing Ordinances governing the securities and futures markets into a composite piece of legislation to keep the Hong Kong regulatory regime on a par with international standards and practices.

For effective regulation, the SFO provides flexibility in addressing changing market practices and global conditions by empowering the Chief Executive in Council, the Financial Secretary, the Chief Justice and the SFC to prescribe detailed and technical regulatory requirements as necessary by way of subsidiary legislation, to supplement the regulatory framework laid down under the primary legislation.  A total of 40 sets of subsidiary legislation have to be put in place for commencement of the SFO (note 2).

Each set of the draft subsidiary legislation has gone through a consultation process where appropriate. The relevant rules or orders have been amended where necessary to reflect the comments received during the consultation.

On 22 February 2002, the House Committee of the Legislative Council established the Subcommittee on Draft Subsidiary Legislation to be made under the SFO. From March to October, the Subcommittee has held 12 meetings to study the subsidiary legislation in draft form.

Way forward

Once gazetted, the subsidiary legislation is subject to the LegCo negative vetting process, which takes seven weeks.

The subsidiary legislation will come into operation on the day appointed for the commencement of the SFO. The Government today announces that the target date for commencement of the SFO is 1 April 2003.

The Gazette is available at the Government Publications Centre, and accessible on the website of the Government Printer or via the SFC’s website (under the SFO Section).

3. A brief background introduction of the rules:

1. Securities and Futures (Short Selling and Securities Borrowing and Lending (Miscellaneous)) Rules

* *The Rules provide for exemptions from sections 170 and 171 of the SFO and set out the record keeping requirements for stock lenders. Section 170(1) of the SFO prohibits uncovered short selling. Exemptions have been given to certain categories of market makers including market makers registered with the Stock Exchange and the Futures Exchange in the performance of market making obligations and hedging of market making positions. This will make it easier for market makers to perform their functions, which will in turn help provide liquidity to the markets. Section 171 sets out reporting and record keeping requirements for covered short selling transactions.  The Rules introduce alternative means for sellers placing short selling orders pursuant to a stock borrowing and lending agreement to comply with section 171, to enable timely execution of transactions. The Financial Secretary has been consulted on the new provision pursuant to section 397(2) of the SFO. 

2. Securities and Futures (Contracts Limits and Reportable Positions) Rules

The Rules prescribe limits on the number of exchange traded futures and stock options contracts which a person may hold (the prescribed limits). The Rules also require a person holding or controlling a “reportable position” in exchange-traded futures and stock options contracts to lodge a notice with the relevant exchange within one day. The prescribed limits and reportable positions for futures and stock options contracts are respectively set out in Schedules 1 and 2 to the Rules.

The Rules introduce some new provisions. A person lodging a notice of a reportable position with the relevant exchange is required to provide information relating to the number of contracts held and, if the contracts are held on behalf of a client, the identity of that client. The Financial Secretary has been consulted on this provision in accordance with section 35(2) of the SFO. The relevant exchange is empowered under the Rules to authorise specified persons to hold futures and stock options contracts in excess of the prescribed limits. These persons are market makers and issuers of exchange listed structured products. Other categories of persons may be authorised by the Commission on a case by case basis provided that the Commission is satisfied that there is a need for the person to hold or control futures or stock options contracts in excess of the prescribed limits and that the authorisation is not prejudicial to the interests of the investing public. These new provisions are based on the current practice of the exchanges.

3. Securities and Futures (Price Stabilizing) Rules

The Rules prescribe the circumstances in which the prices of certain securities offered to the public may be stabilized, the period in which they can be stabilized in accordance with the Rules, the type of stabilizing action which may be undertaken and related requirements for carrying out stabilizing actions such as disclosure and record keeping. The Rules will only apply to offers of shares or debentures to the public where the offer size exceeds HK$100 million. Both primary offers and secondary offers will be eligible provided that the relevant documentation requirements are met. The Rules will put Hong Kong on a par with other international markets in allowing underwriters of securities offerings to take price stabilizing actions to reduce short-term price fluctuations resulting from the sudden increase in supply of the securities in the market, thereby promoting the orderliness and efficiency of the Hong Kong market. The Financial Secretary has been consulted on the Rules in accordance with sections 282(2) and 306(2) of the SFO.

4. Securities and Futures (Disclosure of Interests - Securities Borrowing and Lending) Rules

The Rules, on which the Financial Secretary has been consulted in accordance with section 377 of the SFO, supplement the regulatory regime for disclosure of securities interests laid down in Part XV of the SFO. In the securities borrowing and lending (SBL) business, a large number of loans and returns of shares can be transacted from the same lending pool. This would give rise to a large number of disclosures for movements in the same lending pool, which may be of limited value to investors. The Rules will establish a simplified disclosure regime that will limit the disclosure obligations of approved lending agents (ALAs), substantial shareholders and regulated persons with respect to their SBL transactions –

(a)   substantial shareholders, who lend through an ALA that uses a specified form of agreement when lending the shares, will be exempt from making disclosures of changes in the nature of their interests that result from the lending and return of shares;

(b)  ALAs – persons approved by the Commission as ALAs (generally custodians) will be exempted from disclosure requirements when they lend shares from their lending pool or when shares are returned to their lending pool. ALAs will have to disclose changes in the percentage level of their lending pool but the amount of information required to be included in their notification has been simplified; and

(c) regulated persons – interests in shares borrowed by local brokers and overseas brokers in approved jurisdictions that merely act as a conduit (i.e. regulated persons who borrow and on-lend the shares within five business days) are to be disregarded.

The simplified disclosure regime for ALAs will also be extended to cover holding companies of ALAs that are taken to be interested in shares in which an ALA is interested under section 316(2) of the SFO. ALAs and regulated persons taking advantage of the simplified disclosure regime will have to keep records of the transactions.

5. Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules

The Rules, on which the Financial Secretary has been consulted in accordance with section 145(1) of the SFO, prescribe the financial resources requirements imposed on corporations licensed by the Commission. Essentially, the Rules consolidate, streamline and rationalize the requirements in the current Financial Resources Rules made under the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance (including the amendments effected by the Financial Resources (Amendment) Rules 2002) and the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Financial Resources) Rules made under the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading Ordinance, which they will replace. 

In support of the new single licence regime, the Rules introduce one standard set of capital rules for universal application to licensed corporations licensed for all types of regulated activities designed to accommodate the conduct of multiple regulated activities by any licensed corporation.  The Rules are designed to address risks arising from various aspects of the regulated activities carried out by licensed corporations and aim to ensure that licensed corporations have sufficient liquid assets to meet ongoing liabilities as they fall due. In addition, the Rules also require submission of financial returns and notification of specified events. Most changes from the existing Financial Resources Rules are made for the purpose of facilitating market development and reducing compliance costs without compromising the degree of protection afforded by compliance with the Rules for clients of licensed corporations. These include allowing licensed corporations to use their own fiscal reporting dates rather than calendar month-ends and requiring quarterly submission instead of monthly submission for part of the financial returns.

6. Securities and Futures (Keeping of Records) Rules

The Rules prescribe the records that are to be kept by an intermediary and its associated entities to ensure that they maintain comprehensive records in sufficient detail, relating to their businesses and client transactions, to enable proper accounting of their business operations and clients’ assets. The requirements are mainly prepared with reference to the existing section 83 and Division 5 of Part XA of the Securities Ordinance, section 45 of the Commodities Trading Ordinance and section 3 of the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Books, Contract Notes and Conduct of Business) Rules. In designing the requirements, the Commission was conscious of the need to strike an appropriate balance between providing investor protection and reducing the compliance burden of its regulatees.

7. Securities and Futures (Accounts and Audit) Rules

The Rules prescribe the financial statements and other documents that have to be prepared and submitted to the Commission by a licensed corporation and an associated entity of an intermediary. In addition, the Rules prescribe the matters upon which an auditor must make a statement in his report on the accounts of the licensed corporation or associated entity he is auditing. The requirements are mainly drawn from the existing Securities (Accounts and Audit) Rules, the Commodities Trading (Accounts and Audit) Rules and the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Accounts and Audit) Rules. In designing the requirements, the Commission was conscious of the need to strike an appropriate balance between providing investor protection and reducing the compliance burden of its regulatees.

8. Securities and Futures (Contract Notes, Statements of Account and Receipts) Rules

The Rules rationalize the requirements found in three existing ordinances and two codes of business conduct relating to the preparation and provision of contract notes, statements of accounts and receipts. They apply to an intermediary (and where applicable, an associated entity of an intermediary) that enters into contracts for dealing in securities or futures, or leveraged foreign exchange trading with or on behalf of clients, or receives or holds client assets, in the conduct of regulated activities for which the intermediary is licensed or registered. They ensure that an intermediary’s clients receive timely and meaningful information about transactions conducted for them or on their behalf. With a view to alleviating the compliance burden on intermediaries without compromising investor protection, the Rules provide certain exemptions for (a) dealings with clients who are professional investors; (b) intermediaries licensed or registered for asset management; and (c) avoidance of duplicative documents being sent to clients.

9. Securities and Futures (Investor Compensation – Claims) Rules

The Rules (Claims Rules) supplement Part XII of the SFO by prescribing the circumstances in which a person is entitled to claim compensation, the manner in which a claim for compensation is to be made, the information to be supplied to the Commission to enable the claim to be determined and the persons who are not entitled to make a claim. The Financial Secretary has been consulted on the Rules in accordance with section 244(3) of the SFO. They should be read in conjunction with four other items of subsidiary legislation that it is proposed will be made by the Chief Executive in Council –

(a) the Securities and Futures (Investor Compensation – Levy) Rules which impose a 0.002% levy on the sale and purchase of securities and 50 cents per contract levy for futures contracts;

(b) the Securities and Futures (Investor Compensation – Compensation Limits) Rules which impose a “per investor” compensation limit of $150,000; 

(c) the Securities and Futures (Transfer of Functions – Investor Compensation Company) Order which provides for administration of claims by the Investor Compensation Company Limited set up recently and recognized by the Commission for the purposes of the SFO; and

(d) the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Amendment of Schedule 8) Order 2002 which provides for appeals by claimants who are dissatisfied with certain decisions made under the Claims Rules to apply to the Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal for a review of the decisions.

The new arrangements established under the Claims Rules will significantly enhance the compensation available to investors. They will not be limited to exchange participants but cover defaults by all securities and futures dealers and margin financiers, who receive and hold client securities and/or money whether or not they are exchange participants in relation to all products traded on markets operated by Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited. Coverage will also be extended to retail investors who deal through registered institutions. Institutional investors will be excluded from claiming compensation.

Initial funding of the new Compensation Fund to be set up under Part XII of the SFO will come from a transfer of assets in the existing compensation funds under sections 74, 75 and 76 of Schedule 10 to the SFO, but with sufficient amounts reserved in those funds to cover outstanding liabilities. The net assets of the Unified Exchange Compensation Fund as of 30 September 2002 were approximately $800 million with the assets of the Futures Exchange Compensation Fund being approximately $100 million. Ongoing funding will be provided under the Levy Rules. 

10. Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules

The Rules set out the disclosure arrangements for listed companies and listing applicants both in applications for listing and ongoing public disclosure. The Rules provide for the filing of copies of such applications and of disclosures to the public made by listed corporations to be filed with the Commission. The Rules will enable the Commission to require a company to supply further information and to object to the listing in certain circumstances. The Commission may object to the listing if the applicant fails to comply with such a requirement, or it appears to the Commission that the applicant has supplied false or misleading information in its application, or it is not in the public interest or in the interest of the investing public. They also prescribe the circumstances in which the Commission may suspend dealings in the securities of listed corporations.

The Rules also contain provisions relating to approved share registrars and prohibit the listing of any securities of a corporation unless the corporation is, or employs, an approved share registrar and suspension of dealings in the event that the corporation ceases to employ an approved share registrar. Both the Financial Secretary and the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited have been consulted on the Rules in accordance with section 36(2) of the SFO.

11. Securities and Futures (Licensing and Registration) (Information) Rules

The Rules supplement the regulatory regime in Part V of the SFO. The Rules, which have been prepared in consultation with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (the HKMA), ensure that the Commission and the HKMA are provided with information required for proper consideration of applications under Part V of the SFO, that the information provided to them is updated to reflect major changes that occur, and that important information on licensed persons and registered institutions is available to the public. In designing the information requirements, we have been conscious of the need to strike an appropriate balance between providing adequate investor protection and reducing compliance burden of licensed persons and registered institutions. The information requirements are modeled very closely on existing requirements.  The major change is that most of the information requirements are now applicable to registered institutions, as part of the package to remove the exempt status currently enjoyed by banks in respect of their securities operations. 

12. Securities and Futures (Miscellaneous) Rules

The Rules, as their name suggests, contain a range of provisions that do not readily fit into other subsidiary legislation made under the SFO. They:

(a) provide for the service of documents on the Commission under all Parts of the SFO;

(b) supplement the regulatory regime in Part V of the SFO, requiring an intermediary to –

(i) exhibit its licence or certificate of registration in a prominent place at its places of business; and

(ii) return its licence or certificate for cancellation or amendment in certain circumstances; and

(c) supplement the regulatory regime in Part VIII of the SFO by widening the category of persons considered to be “auditors” for the purpose of section 179 of the SFO.

The Rules will be complemented by the Securities and Futures (Offences and Penalties) Regulation that it is proposed will be made by the Chief Executive in Council. This Regulation will make it an offence, without reasonable excuse, to contravene the provisions of sections 3(1) or 4 of the Rules (i.e. failure to exhibit or return a licence/certificate as required).
