The Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission, Mr Andrew Sheng, today shared his views on the implications of China's accession to WTO for corporate governance with over 150 executives and managers of cross-border businesses at the "Ethical Road to Prosperity" Seminar on Shanghai-Hong Kong Business Vistas. 

The "Ethical Road to Prosperity" Seminar was jointly organized by the Independent Commission Against Corruption and the Shanghai People's Procuratorate.

Mr Sheng likened good corporate discipline to a three-legged stool of discipline. Together, *self-discipline, regulatory discipline,* and *market discipline* contribute to good corporate governance. China's accession to WTO would introduce more intense competition and global standards, both important agents for changing the corporate governance culture in China.

Mr Sheng said the quality of markets also depended on six "I"s and one "R". The six "I"s are information, incentives, investor, intermediaries, issuer, and infrastructure, and the "R" represents regulatory framework.

Mr Sheng concluded: "All three disciplines - *self-discipline, regulatory discipline* and *market discipline* - ultimately boil down to people. It is the conduct of all market participants, bound or unbound by disciplines, that determines the quality of markets, and markets are all about confidence and trust."

"China is changing very fast, but I am confident, with such a wealth of people talent, and the three disciplines brought by the opening of WTO, the Mainland market will grow to its rightful place as one of the largest capital markets in the world in the 21st century."
