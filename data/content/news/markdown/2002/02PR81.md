The Securities and Futures Commission today releases the Annual Report for the year ended 31 March 2002. The Report is today laid before the LegCo following submission to the Financial Secretary. 

The SFC is also pleased to release a book titled Securities Regulation in Hong Kong and a web-based Regulatory Handbook.

The theme of the Annual Report is "Regulatory Partnership". It shows the SFC's commitment to building a close partnership with market participants in maintaining a transparent, fair and orderly market.

"Regulators must work with the majority of the market participants, who play by the rules, and sanction those who flout the rules. As the theme of our Annual Report suggests, every regulator and market participant has a role to play to enhance the quality of our markets," SFC Chairman Andrew Sheng said.

The publication of the Annual Report 2001-2002, the Securities Regulation in Hong Kong and the Regulatory Handbook helps foster partnership with stakeholders by promoting their understanding of the Commission's work, and the regulatory philosophy and framework in the Hong Kong securities and future market.

Annual Report

The Annual Report 2001-2002 summarises the Commission's work and financial results in the past financial year and discusses plans for the current year.

Total revenue of the Commission for 2001-2002 dropped by 27% from the previous year to $363 million. We strengthened strict cost control measures last year and were able to achieve a reduction of 4.4% in actual staff costs over the previous year. Deficit was $54.2 million, 38% lower than the projected deficit at mid-year of $88 million and only 2.5% higher than the $52.9 million deficit approved by LegCo in March 2001.

The SFC submitted its Budget for 2002-2003 to the LegCo in March 2002. The forecast deficit was $117.6 million. The SFC is targeting to cut a further 5% from the expenditure in the Budget, which would result in a projected deficit of $96.5 million for 2002-2003. Despite the deficits, the SFC has not asked for Government funding for the 10th consecutive year.

The Annual Report this year offers more details than ever before on the SFC's corporate governance matters, and for the first time three-year comparisons of key statistics. See more in Appendix I.

Securities Regulation in Hong Kong

Securities Regulation in Hong Kong is a collection of selected speeches and articles by Chairmen of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and Hong Kong Futures Exchange, and members of the Securities and Futures Commission since 1989. The Book also contains excerpts from Robert Fell's book Crisis and Change: The Maturing of Hong Kong's Financial Markets, which chronicles the merger of the four exchanges in Hong Kong into a unified exchange that began trading in 1986. Excerpts are also taken from the Davison Report, which recommended a series of reforms for the securities and futures industry in the aftermath of October 1987, including the establishment of the Securities and Futures Commission.

The book offers readers a useful perspective for understanding the thinking behind the regulatory framework and development of Hong Kong's securities and futures markets. See more in Appendix II.

Regulatory Handbook

The electronic Regulatory Handbook provides a one-stop web-based guide on the regulatory requirements for various securities and futures activities. It offers practitioners and other persons who may be interested in operating in the Hong Kong markets a convenient access to the necessary regulatory information.

The Handbook comprises two volumes: Volume One describes the SFC's regulatory philosophy and how the SFC regulates the market; Volume Two contains the most updated SFC regulatory codes, guidelines and important circulars. See more in Appendix III.

How to obtain the publications

All the publications are available on the SFC's corporate website www.hksfc.org.hk.

Printed copies of the Annual Report and the Securities Regulation in Hong Kong (in English and Chinese versions) are available at $100 and $120 each respectively. Academia, students and members of the Hong Kong Securities Institute may buy the Securities Regulation in Hong Kong at a discounted price of $100.

***Appendix II: Securities Regulation in Hong Kong*** 

"To prepare for the uncertain future, we need to understand the lessons of history, our traditions and our environment. These speeches contain important perspectives of key leaders of the securities community in Hong Kong." (Preface, Securities Regulation in Hong Kong)

About Securities Regulation in Hong Kong

Securities Regulation in Hong Kong is a collection of selected speeches and articles by Chairmen of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and Hong Kong Futures Exchange, members of the Securities and Futures Commission, as well as key excerpts from Robert Fell's book Crisis and Change: The Maturing of Hong Kong's Financial Markets and from the Davison Report.

1. Major Highlights

The book collects 28 speeches and works of 12 members of the Securities and Futures Commission, the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong, the Hong Kong Futures Exchange; a former Commissioner of Securities; and key excerpts from the Report of the Securities Review Committee on "The Operation and Regulation of the Hong Kong Securities Industry" (also known as the "Davison Report"). This compilation offers the reader a useful perspective for understanding the thinking behind the regulation and development of Hong Kong's securities and futures markets.

2. List of Contributors

- Former and present Chairmen, Deputy Chairmen and Executive Directors of the Commission: Robert Owen, Robert Nottle, Anthony Neoh, Andrew Sheng, Michael Wu, Laura Cha, Mark Dickens and Paul Bailey;
- Former and present Chairmen of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and the Hong Kong Futures Exchange: QW Lee, Edgar Cheng, Charles Lee and Geoffrey Yeh;
- Former Commissioner for Securities, Robert Fell; and
- Members of the Securities Review Committee headed by Ian Hay Davison, who prepared the report on The Operation and Regulation of the Hong Kong Securities Industry, May 1988

***Appendix III: Regulatory Handbook***

"When making decisions or taking actions, we are guided by the following principles: Firmness; Fairness; Consistency; Proportionality; and Negotiate not dictate." (Regulatory Philosophy, the Regulatory Handbook)

About the Regulatory Handbook

The Regulatory Handbook is launched in conjunction with the recent enactment of the Securities and Futures Ordinance. The handbook incorporates all the existing regulatory codes and guidelines in an easily accessible and user-friendly place. The handbook will regularly be updated on our website at www.hksfc.org.hk .

1. Major Highlights

The Regulatory Handbook comprises two volumes. Volume One describes the SFC's regulatory philosophy. Volume Two contains codes, guidelines and guidance information relevant to persons conducting activities regulated by the SFC.

Codes and guidelines are categorised by types of regulated activities, hence allowing licensees and concerned parties quick and easy access to the information relevant to their areas of business.
