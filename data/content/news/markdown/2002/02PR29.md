The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) welcomes the Financial Secretary's plan to strengthen Hong Kong's securities and futures market.

The SFC believes that the proposals announced by the Financial Secretary, Mr Antony Leung, in today's Budget Speech will serve to enhance the competitiveness of Hong Kong as an international financial centre.

Mr Leung said that Hong Kong needed to increase liquidity of its market and further enhance its market system.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said the SFC would fully co-operate with the SAR Government and work with the industry in order to achieve the objectives of the proposals.

Mr Sheng said the Securities and Futures Bill, upon enactment, would lay the foundation for further development of Hong Kong's financial infrastructure.

"Bringing our legislation on a par with international standards will enhance Hong Kong's position, facilitate market innovation and provide better investor protection. The Commission will focus on finalising the subsidiary legislation this year and getting the market ready for the implementation of the legislation," he said.

Market development is a priority for the SFC. The SFC is working closely with Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited, market participants and other parties to facilitate the introduction of new products and services that will enhance Hong Kong's reputation as an international financial centre.

The SFC supports the Financial Secretary's call for streamlined procedures and lower costs associated with product development. It is also doing its part to lower the operating costs of securities industry by cutting licensing fees.

Under the new licensing regime of the Securities and Futures Bill, the SFC proposes to reduce licensing fees by 3% and offer an additional one-off discount of 5% to intermediaries who apply during the first year of the transitional period provided for by the Bill. Details will be announced soon for public consultation.

In terms of enhancing the market infrastructure, the SFC is consulting the public on proposals for a scripless securities market. The implementation of a scripless market, which was first outlined in a report released by the Steering Committee on the Enhancement of Financial Infrastructure in 1999, represents a major milestone in the development of Hong Kong's financial infrastructure. The SFC will take into account the market's views when preparing draft legislation to enable the introduction of a scripless market in Hong Kong.
