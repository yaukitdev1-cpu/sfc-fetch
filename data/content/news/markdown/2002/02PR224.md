The Securities and Futures Commission today releases its *Quarterly Report* for the financial period 1 July 2002 to 30 September 2002.

Expenditure down by 6.4% from a year before

The SFC recorded a deficit of $13 million for the quarter, significantly below the projected deficit of $29 million in the approved budget. The deficit for the first half was $20 million, 66% below the projected half-year deficit of $58 million. At the end of September, the SFC's reserves stood at $604 million, equivalent to 1.34 times the approved annual operating expenditure estimate of $450 million.

In the first half, the SFC’s total revenue was $177 million, about 6% higher than the estimate. However, it was 8% lower than that of the same period last year due to the shrinkage of average daily stock market turnover from $8.5 billion last year to $7.4 billion for the first half.

Total expenditure (including depreciation) for the first half was $197 million, 12% below the approved estimate and 6.4% lower than that of the same period last year. Staff costs dropped by 5.8% due to a decrease in staff number and cash compensation for overtime work, and downward adjustment of salaries to reflect the current market trend when filling vacancies. As of 30 September 2002, the SFC had 402 staff compared to 415 staff at end March 2002.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said that while market conditions remained challenging, if there was no significant deterioration in market conditions, there would be no reason to believe that the budget deficit for the third quarter would be exceeded. The budgeted deficit in the third quarter to 31 December 2002 is $29 million.

Work Progress

During the quarter, the SFC:

- Completed the consultation process on the subsidiary legislation required to underpin the Securities and Futures Ordinance;
- Took action to protect the interests of clients of two brokers and disciplined an account executive of a third who misappropriated clients’ assets;
- Disciplined 20 intermediaries and prosecuted nine entities;
- Referred two cases of insider dealing to the Financial Secretary;
- Strengthened co-operation with the Commercial Crime Bureau through staff secondment;
- Consulted the public on the disclosure requirement for hedge funds’ periodic reports;
- Assisted HKEx in introducing Equity Linked Instruments for trading;
- Recognised four more institutions to provide continuous professional training;
- Completed the drafting of international standards on ongoing disclosure for IOSCO;
- Participated in the annual Internet Surf Day organised by IOSCO; and
- Took part in the Financial Sector Assessment Programme jointly carried out by the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank.
