The Securities and Futures Commission releases today the Consultation Conclusions for the Proposed Amendments to the Financial Resources Rules ("FRR") made under section 28 of the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance. 

On 4 March 2002, the SFC issued a consultation paper to solicit comments on the Proposed Amendments.

The Proposed Amendments sought to amend the FRR by requiring firms providing securities margin financing (each, a "firm"):

(a) to apply a steep "illiquid collateral discount" of 90% to the value of stocks and warrants that are difficult to quickly liquidate but nevertheless accepted as collateral for margin loans; and,

(b) where a firm's total borrowings which are secured by repledging margin clients' securities exceed 50% of total amount lent to the firm's margin clients, to include the amount that exceeds 50% in the firm's ranking liabilities.

The Proposed Amendments were designed to safeguard the interests of investors whose securities are repledged with banks by requiring firms to strengthen their financial position.

During the one-month consultation period, 23 submissions were received. The overall tone of the comments was positive and commentators generally recognized the need for measures to reduce firms' financial and credit risks. Concerns were expressed, particularly by smaller firms, regarding the practical effects of the Proposed Amendments on the market as a whole.

Different commentators suggested varying levels of discount as well as the firm borrowing to margin loan ratio. Others expressed concern over the possible effect on market liquidity in general and the liquidity of so called "third and fourth liner" stocks in particular as well as possible negative connotations being associated with any stock or warrant subject to discounting. Concerns were also expressed as to the impact on the IPO market and a level playing field vis-a-vis banks.

Several sessions were held before, during and after the consultation period with various commentators and market participants whose business might be affected by the Proposed Amendments. After studying the submissions, the SFC had several discussions with market participants during which all parties tried to reach a consensus on formulating an approach that would balance the need for investor protection and giving the industry more flexibility.

As a result, further revisions were made to the Proposed Amendments which we believe strike the right balance between the two interests. These are now embodied in the Financial Resources (Amendment) Rules 2002.

Under the Amendment Rules, the illiquid collateral discount has been lowered to 80% and the firm borrowing to margin loan ratio has been increased to 65%.

The Amendment Rules will become effective on 1 October 2002.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The SFC is grateful to the members of the public, leading academics and industry representatives who submitted suggestions and contributed to the development of the Proposed Amendments. Going forward, the SFC is preparing to conduct a consultation exercise later this year on broad proposals to rationalize the capital and financial resources requirements applicable to registrants. The new framework should provide robust protection for investors and facilitate industry growth."

Copies of the Consultation Conclusions are available at the SFC's office and on the SFC's website at www.hksfc.org.hk.
