The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today published its third Quarterly Report, covering the period 1 October to 31 December 2001. 

Andrew Sheng, SFC Chairman, reported that the SFC has recorded a deficit of $43.6 million in the first three quarters of the Financial Year 2001/2002, higher than the approved budgeted deficit of $35.6 million. Comparing the three quarters results between years, levy income was 25.8% lower than that of the previous year due to sluggish market turnover. License fees and corporate finance charges fell by 14% because of lower activity. Investment income fell by 25.7% as interest rates continued to drop. Other income was also reduced.

Although below budget, operating expenditure was 12% higher than that of the first nine months of last year, largely as a result of staff increases to cope with additional regulatory functions transferred from the Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx) in 2000. Other expenses were also higher due to the outsourcing of computer operation services, external consultancy for market studies and external relations work with the Mainland and overseas regulators.

As at 31 December 2001, the SFC's reserves stood at $634.6 million and the revised deficit for the Financial Year 2001/2002 is estimated to reach $87.9 million. The SFC has introduced additional cost control measures during the quarter, including the implementation of a wage and hiring freeze in November 2001. It is expected that the overall expenditure of the SFC for the financial year would be slashed by 10.3% compared with the approved budget.

Despite the tight control on expenditure, the SFC continued to enhance its efficiency through various initiatives, such as staff training and workflow automation, the objective of which is to enable the Commission to do more, in a more effective manner, with fewer resources.

Major achievements during the quarter included:

- assisting the Legislative Council Bills Committee and the Administration to vet the Securities and Futures Bill, which is expected to be passed by the Legislative Council in early 2002 and come into force later in the year;
- issuing 6 consultation papers relating to the offering of Hedge Fund and key sets of draft rules under the Securities and Futures Bill;
- implementing 3 new codes and guidelines to raise market standards;
- improving the quality of listing by working with the Stock Exchange to amend the Listing Rules of the derivative warrant market;
- taking disciplinary action against 13 SFC registrants and successfully prosecuting 13 parties for market malpractice;
- strengthening investor education on topical issues like equity-linked notes and unauthorised trading, through newspaper columns; radio programme; Teachers' Workshops; and educational leaflets and articles;
- organising and participating in major conferences to enhance external relations and hosting in Hong Kong 3 meetings for the International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO).
