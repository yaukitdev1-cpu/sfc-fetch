The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) announces today that it has successfully prosecuted {X1} and {X2} for intentionally creating a false and misleading appearance of active trading in the shares of Grand Field Group Holdings Limited (Grand Field). {X1} and {X2} each pleaded guilty before Mr P C White, a Magistrate at Western Magistracy, respectively to one summons of intentionally creating a false and misleading appearance of active trading in the shares of Grand Field during the period 20 March 2000 and 7 June 2000 (the Relevant Period). Sentencing for both {X1} and {X2} was adjourned until 24 June 2002 pending their background reports.

The SFC investigation found that {X1} had started trading in Grand Field shares through a broker on 20 March 2000. During the Relevant Period, {X1} had traded actively in Grand Field shares on 39 of the 52 trading days.

{X1} introduced {X2} to open a securities trading account with a different broker on 10 March 2000. On 21 March 2000, {X1} transferred $1.2 million to {X2}'s account and {X2} started trading actively and exclusively in Grand Field shares during the Relevant Period.

During the Relevant Period, {X1} and {X2} traded almost exclusively with each other in Grand Field shares. They traded with each other on 38 out of 52 trading days and their trading accounted for 62.3% of market turnover during those 38 trading days. In addition, 90.6% of {X1}'s purchases of Grand Field shares were from {X2} whilst 97.9% of his sales were to {X2}. Without their active trading, the average daily turnover in Grand Field shares during the 38 trading days would have reduced significantly from 1,201,716 to 453,452 shares. {X1}'s and {X2}'s co-ordinated trading activities created a false and misleading appearance of active trading in Grand Field shares, and contravened section 135(1)(a) and section 139 of the Securities Ordinance.
