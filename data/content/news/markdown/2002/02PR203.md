The SFC has eased certain proposals in the draft financial resources rules to be made under the new Securities and Futures Ordinance to help compliance by intermediaries.

The SFC today releases the above Revised Draft Rules with Consultation Conclusions.

The original proposed rules were released for public consultation on 14 June 2002 (Note 1). A total of 13 submissions were received from practitioners including fund management firms, international brokerage firms and their legal adviser, industry representative bodies and professional associations. The overall tone of the comments was positive and commentators generally welcomed the proposed rules. The submissions were carefully considered and the proposed rules were revised as appropriate.

In addition to the drafting changes that have been suggested by the Department of Justice and a few minor changes, the following policy changes have been incorporated in the Revised Draft Rules in response to the market comments:

- a six-month transitional period to be given to advisers to enable them to comply with the liquid capital requirement. During this transitional period, existing advisers will only need to comply with the net tangible asset requirement;

- grace period for exempt dealers and exempt investment advisers to be extended from three months to six months;

- fees receivable not outstanding for more than a month to be included in liquid assets;

- equity linked instruments listed on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong to be accepted as liquid assets, subject to the same haircut as is applicable to the underlying securities;

- certificates of deposit issued by authorized financial institutions or approved banks incorporated outside Hong Kong to be accepted as liquid assets; and

- given the market's concern over the readiness of small and medium sized brokers for electronic filing, we propose not to make electronic filing of financial returns mandatory at this stage and will revisit this issue in two years' time.

The SFC is confident that the relaxations would not compromise the purposes of the financial resources requirements or investor protection.

As the Revised Draft Rules are subject to deliberation of the Legislative Council, they may not be in the final form. They are issued primarily to demonstrate how we have revised the rules in response to the market comments and not for the purpose of another round of consultation. They should be read together with the Consultation Conclusions that provide an analysis of the submissions and explain the Commission's perspectives.

Copies of the Consultation Conclusions and Revised Draft Rules are available at the SFC's office, on the SFC's website (www.hksfc.org.hk) and the FinNet communication network.
