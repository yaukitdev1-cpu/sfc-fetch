The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today releases for public consultation the draft Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules (the draft Rules). These are to be made under section 145 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance. 

Essentially the draft Rules follow the requirements of the existing Financial Resources Rules (as applicable to securities***** and futures dealers and advisers) and the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Financial Resources) Rules, proposing only limited policy changes to:

****** The draft Rules will incorporate the two amendments made in May 2002 to better regulate securities margin financing, namely the introduction of an 80% "illiquid collateral discount" and a 65% borrowing-to-loan ratio.*

(a) consolidate these two sets of existing rules, streamlining and rationalising the requirements as appropriate;

(b) introduce one standard set of capital rules for universal application to all types of licensed corporations (i.e. all licensed corporations should be subject to the liquid capital requirement, instead of the net tangible assets requirement for advisers, and adopt the same capital calculation base);

(c) accommodate the conduct of multiple regulated activities by licensed corporations (e.g. if a firm is licensed for more than one regulated activity, the highest of all the monetary requirements for establishing the required liquid capital and the highest of the paid-up share capital requirements which are applicable to the different regulated activities should apply);

(d) require electronic submission of financial returns by licensed corporations; and

(e) fine-tune existing requirements to facilitate market development and reduce compliance cost (e.g. licensed corporations should be able to elect to use their own fiscal month-ends as their financial return reporting date).

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "These minimal changes are designed as part of the SFC's ongoing effort to de-regulate, recognizing the industry's desire for a more streamlined regulatory approach, and the market's need for a facilitative regime. In the longer term, the SFC is preparing to conduct a consultation exercise later this year on broad proposals to rationalize the capital and financial regulatory requirements so as to provide robust protection for investors and facilitate industry growth."

The SFC has sent copies of the consultation document to licensed corporations via the FinNet communication network. In addition, the public may obtain copies of the consultation document free of charge at the SFC's office and on the SFC's Internet website at [www.hksfc.org.hk](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=02CP19).
