The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today launches a new round of investor education initiatives to educate investors the rules of investing. 

Video Drama Broadcast on Buses

For the first time, the SFC has produced video dramas for broadcast on public transportation. Entitled "Invest with Your Heads Up", the series comprises 12 episodes, which aim to explain stock trading and settlement procedures, as well as measures investors should take to protect their own interests when dealing in stocks.

Airing will begin on 15 May 2002 on RoadShow Media on 2,400 buses of the KMB and Citybus and 200 public light buses for 12 consecutive Wednesdays, with an estimated 3.5 million passenger journeys every day. The 12 episodes, each running for three minutes, will feature different themes and give practical advice, including the following:

- Check and keep all your contract notes and monthly statements. These documents are different and serve different purposes for investors. Look out for unauthorized trades in your statements;
 - Make cheques payable to the brokerage firm. Never deposit cash directly into the bank account of your account executive;
- Respond properly to "circularization" requests, in which the SFC or an auditor of a brokerage seeks your help to confirm your trading records with the brokerage's;
- Understand the "pooling arrangement" in margin accounts, and consider if such an account is suitable for you;
- Do not open a discretionary account without seriously considering whether this arrangement is necessary;
- Do not allow others to trade in your account, as you may be liable for any losses suffered. Likewise, do not let a third party use your identity to set up an account, as you may be liable for the act of the third party.

Popular television artistes, like Mr Mak Cheung Ching, Ms Lai Tsz Shan, Mr Wong He, Ms Chan Yan Yan and Ms Louisa Wong, will appear in the programmes. In addition, market professionals are interviewed in each episode to explain the proper dealing procedures.

A synopsis for each episode will be made public the day before broadcast. Attached is the synopsis for the inaugural episode entitled "Trading Step by Step". After broadcast, each episode will be made available on the SFC-operated Electronic Investor Resources Centre, located at www.hkeirc.org.

Easy-to-understand Cartoon Leaflet

Supporting the launch of video dramas, the SFC has also published a Chinese language cartoon leaflet called "Wise Ways when Dealing with Brokers". This new approach of using cartoon is expected to make it easier and more interesting for the general investors to learn to protect their interests in stock trading.

To make this new investor leaflet more accessible to the public, the SFC has also arranged with all the 519 offices of district council members for its distribution.

The leaflet and all other SFC investor publications are also available at the SFC Office and the 12 Consumer Advice Centres of the Consumer Council, as well as on-line on the "Advice to Investors" section on the SFC's corporate website at www.hksfc.org.hk.

Invest with Your Heads Up

A Twelve Episode Programme Broadcast on RoadShow Media on Wednesday

Episode 1: Trading Step by Step


Synopsis:

Mr and Mrs Chu (played by Mak Cheung Ching and Lai Tsz Shan) are newcomers to the stock market. Worried about the seemingly complicated trading procedures, they first consult Tracy (played by Chan Yan Yan), a broker introduced by their friends. Details of the trading and settlement procedures are then explained.

Key messages for investors:

- Accurate instructions are vital in stock trading;
- An account executive has to promptly place clients' orders to the market. Orders will either be automatched immediately or queued up for execution;
- Once an order is filled, an account executive has to confirm execution with the client as soon as practicable. It is important to listen carefully and note down the details;
- A brokerage firm must issue a contract note containing all the transaction details to its clients on the next trading day;
- The settlement process at the brokerage level takes two days, known as T+2 (T being the trade date). Some brokerages demand payments from their clients before T+2. This is a client/brokerage arrangement and may vary with the risk management policy of individual brokerages.
