The following remarks were made earlier today by Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC, at a press conference where the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury, Mr Frederick Ma, unveiled a number of measures to improve the quality of the Hong Kong securities market:

"As you are aware, the Commission has always been a strong advocate for higher quality markets as the best protection for investors. We believe that the measures announced by the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury today will go a long way to upgrade the quality of listings, demarcate much more clearly the regulatory role between the Stock Exchange [of Hong Kong] and the Commission, and improve the efficiency of the listing process.

First of all, as a market regulator, I believe that the market must do what the market does best in efficiently allocating capital, while matters of public interest should be undertaken by the public sector. The revamping of the Listing Committee will ensure that the Stock Exchange will continue to draw upon market expertise to assess the quality of IPOs.

The new streamlined listing process to be introduced by the Exchange is an especially significant development. It will result in a fast track and non-intrusive process whereby quality companies advised by quality intermediaries will be able to achieve listings with a minimum of regulatory interference. On the other hand, a firmer approach to the quality of disclosure documentation - prospectuses and the like - will penalize badly performing companies and their advisers.

In a securities market that is disclosure based, and where investor protection is centred on the reliability of what issuers disclose, it is unequivocally the responsibility of companies and their advisers, and in particular listed company sponsors, to ensure compliance with the rules and to ensure the quality of disclosure.

As a listed company itself, the Exchange's role as regulator with the other listed companies is a contractual one. The Exchange cannot employ statutory investigatory powers in order to get to the bottom of a problem, and can only use contractual sanctions against those who breach its Listing Rules.

With a clearer alignment of responsibilities under the new system, credible enforcement of our market rules becomes of prime importance. Consequently, the proposals announced on 6 May 2002 to firmly establish the Securities and Futures Commission as the statutory regulator of corporate information disclosure will have even greater significance in ensuring quality. The Commission will not hesitate to use the full range of enforcement options available to it under the new Securities and Futures Ordinance in order to tackle false or misleading disclosure.

We will work closely with the other corporate regulators, such as the Commercial Crime Bureau of the Police to tackle firmly and effectively issues of corporate misgovernance. We will also ensure that the corporate finance advisers, who assist issuers in their work, will maintain high standards. Failure to do so will lead to consequences under our own recently introduced Code of Conduct, and under our licensing rules.

Finally, I would like to emphasise that all of these measures are designed to reduce the overall regulatory burden; those who play by the rules will be able to take advantage of an efficient and responsive regulatory approach. Those who do not will suffer the consequences. Over the coming months the SFC will work closely with the Exchange to further review the listing and other regulatory processes, particularly those to do with vetting and disclosure standards."
