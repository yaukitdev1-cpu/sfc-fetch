The Securities and Futures Commission has proposed rules that crack down on persons who lie to or mislead the public in listing documents or company announcements.

The SFC today publishes for public consultation draft subsidiary legislation relating to the effective regulation of information disclosure by listed companies and listing applicants. The subsidiary legislation comprises an order to be made by the Chief Executive in Council and certain rules to be made by the SFC under the recently enacted Securities and Futures Ordinance.

Introduction

The Hong Kong securities market stakes its credibility on transparency and the integrity of information issued to the investing public. Our regulatory regime is disclosure-based and investor protection is furthered through providing the public with reliable information and enabling investors to make well-informed decisions for themselves. This is the approach adopted internationally in all major jurisdictions.

An effective disclosure regulatory regime benefits the investing public as well as other market and industry participants. The credibility of a market as being fair and transparent, with proper safeguards on the integrity of information disclosure, translates into a lower cost of funds to issuers of securities, attracts more local and international investors, thus providing further opportunities for intermediaries and professionals.

The proposed Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules set out the disclosure arrangements for listed companies and listing applicants and regulation by the SFC. Most of the provisions are based on the existing Securities (Stock Exchange Listing) Rules and the Securities (Stock Exchange Listing) (Approved Share Registrar) Rules. The SFC has also carefully reviewed the market practice and legislative framework and now proposes certain minimum changes to enhance the disclosure regulatory regime.

Statutory filing of corporate disclosure materials

Under the draft Rules, companies that disseminate information to public investors pursuant to applicable rules or laws (e.g. annual reports and interim accounts, circulars to shareholders for approval of connected transactions, announcements of price-sensitive developments, explanations of sudden share price movements) will have to file a copy of the disclosure materials with the SFC. Accordingly, under existing statutory laws concerning the provision of false or misleading information, re-enacted in section 384 of the Ordinance, any person that intentionally or recklessly provides false or misleading information when making the disclosure - in other words, any company that lies to the public - would be subject to the statutory powers of the SFC. The SFC will be able to employ its existing investigatory powers in establishing the facts and gathering evidence. No new investigatory powers are sought under the Rules.

In appropriate cases, the SFC may bring (or refer to the Department of Justice to bring) the offenders to prosecution in the courts. Suspected wrongdoing other than disclosure abuses that is uncovered during an investigation may be referred to the Commercial Crime Bureau or the future Market Misconduct Tribunal.

To avoid unnecessary administrative burden, the draft Rules allow a listed company, listing applicant or other person to file the disclosure materials only with the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong, provided that the person has authorized the Stock Exchange to make the statutory filing to the SFC on its behalf. There will be no additional filing or compliance costs. In addition, the public disclosure by listed companies will not be subject to any SFC pre-vetting. There will be no additional administrative process or any time delay in disseminating the information.

Statutory filing of listing documents

Prospectuses and other listing documents constitute the single most important category of corporate information disclosure to the public. Disclosure in these materials forms a key element of the relationship between a company and its public shareholders. Accordingly, the draft Rules also require listing applicants to submit their application materials as a statutory filing to the SFC. In line with the international practice in all major jurisdictions, the SFC will be able to comment on the draft disclosure materials (principally the prospectus) and object to the company accessing the public market on the basis of insufficient or inadequate disclosure.

Listing applicants will continue to make their applications to the Stock Exchange, which, with the authorization of the applicants, will make the appropriate filings with the SFC. The Stock Exchange will remain the frontline regulator of listing and listed companies. The ability of the SFC to object to a listing will be a matter of reserve power.

The SFC may also investigate and take prosecutorial actions under existing powers against false or misleading information in prospectuses and other listing documents.

In order that the Stock Exchange can continue to act as the frontline regulator, the proposed Securities and Futures (Transfer of Functions - Stock Exchange Company) Order is substantially identical to the present Securities (Transfer of Functions) Order.

Consultation

SFC's Executive Director of Corporate Finance, Mr Ashley Alder, said: "Strengthening our enforcement of disclosure regulation in Hong Kong is an important part of the overall effort to enhance the transparency, credibility, and competitiveness of our securities market."

"The proposals in the draft Rules and draft Order will bring Hong Kong further in line with the international model of having a disclosure regulator able to employ the necessary tools to promote the quality of corporate information disclosure."
