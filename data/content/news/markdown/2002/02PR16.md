The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today released for [public consultation the draft Securities and Futures (Price Stabilizing) Rules (draft Rules)](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=02CP3). The rules are to be made under the Securities and Futures Bill (Bill) when the Bill is enacted. The Bill is currently being considered by a Legislative Council Bills Committee.

The Bill gives the SFC the necessary rule-making power to prescribe the circumstances in which conduct that would otherwise be regarded as market misconduct under the Bill shall be excluded from the application of the market misconduct provisions. This approach is consistent with modern securities legislation in order that regulators can respond efficiently to changing market conditions and practices.

Price stabilizing actions are potentially manipulative and may contravene the market misconduct provisions under the Bill. In order to facilitate public offerings in Hong Kong, the draft Rules prescribe the circumstances where price stabilizing actions may be undertaken by stabilizing managers where shares or debentures are publicly offered in Hong Kong.

The draft Rules are intended to commensurate with international practice and standard. In preparing the draft Rules, the SFC has reviewed recent developments and securities regulations in various securities markets. The draft Rules are now modelled closely on the UK Price Stabilizing Rules in view of the similarity between the Hong Kong securities regulatory framework and that of the UK.

The draft Rules provide a "safe harbour" for those who wish to conduct price stabilizing actions in connection with public offerings. In essence, the draft Rules are applicable to offers of shares or debentures to the public whose offer size meet a certain threshold. Prior disclosure of intended stabilizing actions will have to be made in communications issued to the public. To conduct price stabilizing actions permitted under the draft Rules, a stabilizing manager has to be appointed to oversee the stabilizing actions, to keep records of such transactions and to make them available for inspection by the issuer and the SFC. Stabilizing bids for shares will be subject to an upper limit which is equivalent to the public offer price, however, such pricing limit does not apply to stabilization of debentures. The stabilizing period proposed in the draft Rules is a 30 day period from the date of the prospectus issued in connection with an offer of shares or debentures.
