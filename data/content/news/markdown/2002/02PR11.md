The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today released a public consultation document on the draft Securities and Futures (Professional Investor) Rules (draft Rules). The rules are to be made under the Securities and Futures Bill (Bill) when the Bill is enacted. The Bill is currently being considered by a Legislative Council Bills Committee.

The Bill gives the SFC the necessary rule-making power to prescribe requirements in the subsidiary legislation. This approach is consistent with modern securities legislation in order that regulators can respond efficiently to changing market conditions and practices.

While there are controls already built into the legislative system whereby any rules made by the SFC must be subject to negative vetting by the Legislative Council, the SFC now releases the draft Rules for public consultation.

A definition of "professional investor" was introduced in the Bill, published in November 2000. The definition identifies various classes of persons as professional investors, such as intermediaries, authorized financial institutions, insurance companies and recognized exchange companies, etc. The idea is that certain investor protection measures in the Bill imposed upon intermediaries can be dispensed with in the case of "professional investors".

In essence, the proposed draft Rules expand the definition of "professional investor" in the Bill to include also the following classes of persons:

- trustee companies that have been entrusted with at least HK$40 million in assets;
- high net worth individuals, with a portfolio of at least HK$16 million; and
- businesses with either a portfolio of at least HK$16 million or not less than HK$40 million in assets.
The reasoning is that these persons should be sufficiently sophisticated that certain investor protection requirements imposed upon intermediaries could be disapplied. Releasing intermediaries from compliance with such requirements when dealing with such persons will facilitate the provision of investment services to these clients. 

The proposed expanded definition of "professional investor" however will only apply to clauses 102, 108 and 169 of the Bill. The rationale for this partial relaxation is that while these investors should be sufficiently sophisticated to merit a relaxation of the provisions specified, they may not be so experienced that they should be regarded as "professional investor" for all purposes under the Bill.

Generally, clauses 102, 108 and 169 prohibit intermediaries from engaging in certain conduct, except (among other exemptions) in relation to professional investors. In brief, clause 102 prohibits the issuance of unauthorized advertisements, invitations or documents relating to investments. Section 108 generally prohibits the offer of securities not accompanied by an offering document containing relevant information. Section 169 essentially prohibits cold calling.

In formulating the draft Rules, the SFC has already consulted representatives from the securities and futures industry.

The SFC has used the FinNet communication network to send copies of the consultation document to all registered firms. Copies of the consultation document are also available free of charge at the SFC's office and can be downloaded from the SFC's Internet website at [http://www.hksfc.org.hk](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=02CP1).
