The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today releases for public consultation proposed fees under the streamlined licensing regime of the Securities and Futures Bill.

It is proposed that the licence application and annual fees for companies and individuals be reduced by 3% from existing levels. An additional discount of 5% for the two-year transitional period would be offered to intermediaries who apply to migrate to the new licensing regime in the first year of the transitional period. In other words, intermediaries may be able to enjoy up to 8% savings in application and annual fees during that period.

The proposed new licensing fee schedule is designed to align with the single licensing regime proposed under the Bill. The regime means a firm will no longer need to apply for two or more licences or set up different subsidiaries to engage in different regulated activities.

The current fee levels have remained unchanged since 1993/94. The Consumer Price Index, meanwhile, has risen 28.3% since March 1993. At present, licensing fee revenue accounts for only 68% of the costs of the SFC's licensing and supervision work.

In determining the fee schedule, the SFC has also considered that it is running a deficit of about $88 million for the financial year 2001/02. A deficit of $118 million is projected for 2002/03.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The SFC is doing its part to help trim the compliance costs of intermediaries in view of the difficult economic environment."

"We are glad that intermediaries will be able to benefit from the SFC's streamlined procedures. The full economic benefits will be apparent when intermediaries also make an effort to streamline their businesses and reduce the number of licensed entities," she said.

Certain fee waivers are also proposed so that during the transitional period, intermediaries do not incur additional fees over and above those that they are required to pay under the existing regime.

The proposed fees would form part of the Securities and Futures (Fees) Rules to be made under the Securities and Futures Bill when enacted.

The SFC has sent copies of the consultation document to licensed intermediaries via the FinNet communication network. In addition, the public may obtain copies of the consultation document free of charge at the SFC's office and on the SFC's Internet website at [http://www.hksfc.org.hk](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=02CP6).
