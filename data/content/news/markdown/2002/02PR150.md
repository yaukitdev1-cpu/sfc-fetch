The Securities and Futures Commission urges brokers to strengthen their internal control to prevent frauds and minimise staff misappropriation.

Know your clients to reduce your own exposure

In 2001, a number of brokerages suffered financial losses as a result of frauds by walk-in clients. In those cases, the fraudsters, sometimes using false Identity Cards, opened trading accounts with the brokerages and placed orders to buy shares in listed companies. Typically such purchases were for an unusually large quantity of speculative stocks. The fraudsters produced pay-in slips that showed that funds had been deposited into the brokers' bank accounts by cheques. After the purchases had been concluded, the cheques bounced. By then, the fraudsters were nowhere to be found.

The SFC issued two circulars to alert brokers of these scams in August and November 2001. Similar suspected frauds have occurred to other brokerages recently. To protect their own interests, brokers should observe the "Know Your Client" requirement and exercise caution when dealing with new clients. They should put in place effective internal control procedures to protect their operations from frauds and other dishonest acts. It is also in the interests of the brokers to ensure that clients have cleared funds to satisfy orders. In addition, brokers suspecting similar fraudulent acts by walk-ins should report to the Commercial Crime Bureau immediately.

Segregation of duties to protect client assets

The SFC has also issued a general circular to all securities brokers recently, reminding them of the importance of proper segregation of duties to the protection of client assets.

The SFC notes that a lack of segregation of duties between the front office and back office functions of securities brokers breeds misappropriation of client assets. This is because settlement clerks who also act as account executives may intercept the contract notes and statements of accounts issued by the brokers to clients.

To minimise the potential for conflicts, errors or abuses which may expose brokers and their clients to inappropriate risks, it is of utmost importance for brokers to segregate their operational functions.

The SFC urges brokers to critically review their operations and ensure that adequate segregation of duties is in place. If not, brokers have to be satisfied that their management supervision can compensate for the lack of segregation of duties.

Investors are also advised to only deal with firms that have proper internal controls and management supervision.

The above-mentioned circulars to brokers are available on the SFC website ([www.hksfc.org.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/intermediaries/supervision/supervision.html%20%20)) under the section of *Intermediaries, Licensing and Investment Products - Updates for Intermediaries.*
