The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today releases for [public consultation the draft Securities and Futures (Insurance) Rules](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=02CP20) (the draft Rules). These are to be made under section 116(5) of the Securities and Futures Ordinance. 

The draft Rules set out the insurance requirements for the purposes of licensing. In essence, it is proposed that corporations licensed or applying to be licensed (whether or not they are exchange participants) to carry out securities dealing, futures dealing and/or securities margin financing activities have to take fidelity insurance cover against specified risks (being fraudulent acts and theft by employees or other parties, errors and omissions, etc).

To avoid excessive regulatory burden on licensees or applicants, the draft Rules also provide that a licensed corporation or an applicant to be licensed for the relevant regulated activities may be exempted from complying with the insurance requirements under the following circumstances:

(1) The company concerned is not an exchange participant; and any of (2), (3) or (4)

(2) It is covered by an insurance policy against specified amount and risks (irrespective of whether the policy covers the company only or a group of companies including the company); or

(3) It is a related company of an exchange participant and it has:

(i) entered into a client contract with this exchange participant being that company's executing broker; and
 (ii) entered into third party client contracts or bears responsibility in respect of services or activities of the exchange participant; or

(4) It does not handle client assets.

The insurance scheme to be implemented under the draft Rules will provide coverage similar to the current Brokers' Fidelity Insurance (BFI) scheme arranged by the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited for its participants. Therefore, upon implementation, the new scheme will eventually replace the BFI scheme.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The proposal is an extension of the recommendation first raised by the Securities Review Committee (chaired by Mr. Ian H Davison) following the market crash in 1987. After years of review and market consultation processes, it is now further put forth with a view to fostering stability of the financial system and investor protection as well as enhancing confidence of the Hong Kong market."

The SFC has sent copies of the consultation document to registered firms via the FinNet communication network. In addition, the public may obtain copies of the consultation document free of charge at the SFC's office and on the SFC's Internet website at www.hksfc.org.hk.
