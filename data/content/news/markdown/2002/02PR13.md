The Securities and Futures Commission has today published the Guidelines for the Regulation of Automated Trading Services (the Guidelines). The Commission also released the [Consultation Conclusions](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/conclusion?refNo=01CP3) following earlier public consultation on the Draft Guidelines for the Regulation of Automated Trading Services.

The Securities and Futures Bill (the Bill) proposes the new legislative framework for the regulation of Automated Trading Services (ATS). As provided in the Bill, the Commission has prepared the Guidelines which detail:


- The principles, procedures and standards for the regulation of ATS.
- The seven core standards that a person providing ATS will be generally expected to meet, in relation to financial resources and risk management; operational integrity; fitness; record keeping; transparency; surveillance; and reporting.
- The application process for obtaining a licence (or an exemption) under Part V of the Bill and the procedures under Part III of the Bill with respect to authorizing persons who wish to offer ATS.
- The definition and operation of a stock market, futures market, or a clearing house.
- Examples of hypothetical ATS operations and the likely application of the Guidelines in practice.

The Commission received 17 submissions during the two-month consultation period, which ended on 4 May 2001. The Consultation Conclusions highlight the main comments raised and explain the rationale for the Commission's conclusions in relation to the revisions. A number of revisions to the Draft Guidelines have been made to reflect the comments. The Guidelines will take effect after the commencement of the proposed legislation for the regulation of ATS, which can be found in the Bill, in particular Division 7 of Part III and Part V.

The Consultation Conclusions and the Guidelines have been posted on the SFC website at [http://www.hksfc.org.hk](http://www.sfc.hk) and hardcopies are also available at the SFC's office at 12th Floor, Edinburgh Tower, The Landmark, 15 Queen's Road Central, Hong Kong.
