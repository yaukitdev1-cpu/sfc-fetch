The Securities and Futures Commission has received broad support for rules that crack down on persons who lie to or mislead the public in listing documents or company announcements.

The SFC today releases the Consultation Conclusions on the proposed subsidiary legislation relating to the effective regulation of information disclosure by listed companies and listing applicants. The subsidiary legislation comprises the Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules, which set out the disclosure arrangements for listed companies and listing applicants, and the Securities and Futures (Transfer of Functions - Stock Exchange Company) Order, which enables the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong to continue to act as the frontline regulator of listed companies.

The draft Rules and Order were published for public consultation on 6 May 2002. Eleven response submissions were received from industry practitioners, legal and accounting professionals, and other interested parties. The respondents were generally in favour of the proposed disclosure arrangements and regulation by the SFC. Most of the detailed comments relate to operational and technical issues. They were carefully considered and consequent amendments were made. There were no comments on the draft Order. The Consultation Conclusions provide an analysis of the amendments to the draft Rules and explain our perspectives in making these changes.

Statutory filing of corporate disclosure materials

Under the Rules, companies that disseminate information to public investors pursuant to applicable rules or laws (e.g. annual reports and interim accounts, circulars to shareholders for approval of connected transactions, announcements of price-sensitive developments, explanations of sudden share price movements) will have to file a copy of the disclosure materials with the SFC. Under existing statutory laws concerning the provision of false or misleading information, re-enacted in section 384 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance, any person that intentionally or recklessly provides false or misleading information when making the disclosure - in other words, any company that lies to the public - would be subject to the statutory powers of the SFC. The SFC will be able to employ its existing investigation powers in establishing the facts and gathering evidence.

In appropriate cases, the SFC may bring (or refer to the Department of Justice to bring) the offenders to prosecution in the courts. Suspected wrongdoing other than disclosure abuses that is uncovered during an investigation may be referred to the Commercial Crime Bureau or the future Market Misconduct Tribunal.

To avoid unnecessary administrative burden, the Rules allow a listed company, listing applicant or other person to file the disclosure materials only with the Stock Exchange, provided that the person has authorized the Stock Exchange to make the statutory filing to the SFC on its behalf. The public disclosure by listed companies will not be subject to any SFC pre-vetting and there will be no additional filing or compliance costs. There will also be no additional administrative process or any time delay in disseminating the information.

Statutory filing of listing documents

Prospectuses and other listing documents constitute the single most important category of corporate information disclosure to the public. Disclosure in these materials forms a key element of the relationship between a company and its public shareholders. Accordingly, the Rules also require listing applicants to submit their application materials as a statutory filing to the SFC. In line with international practice in all major jurisdictions, the SFC will be able to comment on the draft disclosure materials (principally the prospectus) and object to the company accessing the public market on the basis of insufficient or inadequate disclosure.

Listing applicants will continue to make their applications to the Stock Exchange, which, with the authorization of the applicants, will make the appropriate filings with the SFC. The Stock Exchange will remain the frontline regulator of listing and listed companies. The ability of the SFC to object to a listing will be a matter of reserve power.

With the proposed arrangements, the SFC will be able to employ its investigation powers in gathering evidence and establishing the facts surrounding false or misleading corporate information disclosure, and bring offenders to prosecution in the courts.

The proposals were generally accepted by respondents in the public consultation exercise as a necessary and effective deterrent against disclosure of false or misleading information, and a positive move to improve the quality of corporate information disclosure in Hong Kong.

SFC Executive Director of Corporate Finance, Mr Ashley Alder, said: "The Rules and the Order will strengthen our enforcement of disclosure regulation and are an important part of our overall effort to enhance the transparency, credibility, and competitiveness of the Hong Kong securities market."

"Public investors have a right to know what they are buying and what companies are doing with their money. And company management has an obligation to provide truthful information. With the proposed rules, the SFC can, and will, use the full range of enforcement options under the new Securities and Futures Ordinance to crack down on those who deliberately misinform or mislead the market," he said.

The Rules and the Order are subject to negative vetting by the Legislative Council and scheduled to become effective on the same day the Securities and Futures Ordinance comes into operation.
