The Securities and Futures Commission is enhancing its co-operation with the Hong Kong Police in order to tackle corporate crime in particular.

Two officers from Commercial Crime Bureau will be seconded to SFC's Enforcement Division from 9 September 2002 for a three-month period. It is hoped that secondments will become a regular occurrence between CCB and SFC. This will bring benefits to both organizations in terms of an exchange of experience and expertise.

SFC's Executive Director of Enforcement, Mr Alan Linning, said: "We have always enjoyed an excellent working relationship with the Police in general and CCB in particular. It is no exaggeration to say that we are in almost daily contact with CCB by telephone on matters ranging from corporate fraud and theft by brokers staff to boiler rooms and market manipulation. There is good rapport between SFC and CCB at both a management and an operational level that has led to some excellent results, such as the recent convictions for manipulating the market in Gay Giano shares."

"We are extremely grateful to CCB for all its assistance in the past which we know will continue and which will be strengthened by initiatives such as the secondments and the continued sharing of training courses. All such measures will enhance our mutual understanding of the challenges faced by each other in pursuing investigations and combating corporate misdeeds," Mr Linning said.

He added: "The SFC is also fully aware of the importance of close co-operation with other law enforcement agencies, on cases of corporate misconduct, including the ICAC which has given the SFC tremendous support."

Action against corporate misgovernance is one of SFC's enforcement priorities for the coming year. Since April 2002, the SFC has initiated four major investigations under Section 29A of the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance (SFCO) and three Section 33 SFCO investigations all involving suspected corporate misconduct. Where there is a clear suspicion of criminal conduct, the SFC refers such cases to CCB at the earliest possible stage.

Mr Linning said the SFC could not comment on individual on-going investigations because of the secrecy provisions under the law. There are good reasons for them in terms of fairness to those under investigation and avoiding any premature market reaction. However, the SFC would where possible keep the public informed. Announcements can be made when investigations are concluded and formal court action is taken by the SFC or another agency, or where some form of public sanction is imposed.

"Nevertheless, the message is - we are on the alert for suspicious corporate activity. Vigilance is our watchword and, in co-operation with our law enforcement colleagues, we will not hesitate to take the strongest action against those who would undermine Hong Kong's reputation as an international financial centre," Mr Linning said.
