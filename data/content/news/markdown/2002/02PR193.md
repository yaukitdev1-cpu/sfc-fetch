SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng has issued a strong warning to those who commit corporate misdeeds.

In a speech today to more than 300 financial market practitioners, academics, and the public, Mr Sheng reiterated the SFC's important objective of promoting investor protection in Hong Kong.

He said the SFC had strengthened its co-operation with both the Commercial Crime Bureau and the China Securities Regulatory Commission in the investigation of corporate misconduct in Hong Kong and also the activities of Hong Kong listed companies in the Mainland.

"We will increase our vigilance and we will strive to complete our investigations as fast as we can, working together with our regulatory counterparts," he said.

"In short, those who break the securities law are now warned. We will pursue them without fear or favour," he said. "They will be investigated and prosecuted in accordance with the law."

Mr Sheng said the SFC was investigating a number of cases that had received high media profile in recent months.

In order to tackle corporate misconduct as a priority, he said the SFC was increasing its enforcement and corporate finance resources, with 15 more staff by the end of the year.

Mr Sheng added that the Government had asked the SFC to look into the possibility of developing a derivative action power under the Companies Ordinance. The Commission was actively studying the matter, he said.

Mr Sheng's remarks were made at the Vocational Training Council's 20th Anniversary Distinguished Lecture Series. [Note 1] He also gave his personal views on the key questions facing the regulatory framework on corporate misconduct.

"First, whether we should move to a statutory regime to improve corporate governance to protect shareholders' rights; and second, whether the present "middle ground" regulatory structure and processes should be simplified so as to avoid duplication and delays in regulatory response to corporate misconduct," he said. [Note 2]

Mr Sheng said: "These are important questions that only wide public consultation and the government and legislature can answer. All I can do is to point out that there is ample international experience and debate on these issues that we can draw upon."

"I personally therefore welcome the announcement by the Financial Secretary that the Government is considering appointing an expert group to look at these complex issues," he said.
