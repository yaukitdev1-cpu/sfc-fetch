The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) releases today the Consultation Conclusions on the draft Securities and Futures (Disclosure of Interests - Securities Borrowing and Lending) Rules as proposed in the corresponding consultation paper released on 19 December 2001. 

Part XV of the Securities and Futures Ordinance requires a shareholder who has an interest in 5% or more of the shares of a listed corporation (a substantial shareholder) to give notice of interests in voting shares of listed corporations in certain specified circumstances. It also requires a director or a chief executive, who is interested in the shares or debentures of a listed corporation (of which he is a director or chief executive) or any of its associated corporations, to give notice in certain specified circumstances.

The Consultation Paper sets out the disclosure obligations that may arise under Part XV when a substantial shareholder lends or borrows shares in a listed corporation. It then explains the simplified disclosure regime that will be available under the draft Rules for the persons who are most active in lending and borrowing shares.

The SFC received five submissions during the consultation period. Most comments were supportive of the proposed subsidiary legislation but they called for a widening of the simplified regime and exemption provided in the draft Rules in a number of respects. Having regard to these submissions the SFC proposes to enlarge further the simplified disclosure regime for approved lending agents and the exemptions for substantial shareholders and regulated persons:

- "approved lending agents" - persons approved by the Commission (generally custodians) who will merely have to disclose changes in the percentage level of their "lending pool". The revised proposals encompass all stages of the stock borrowing and lending process in which approved lending agents are involved. The simplified disclosure regime has also been extended to cover holding companies of approved lending agents that are taken to be interested in shares in which an approved lending agent is interested.
- substantial shareholders - substantial shareholders who lend through an approved lending agent that uses a specified form of agreement when lending the shares, will be exempt from making disclosures of changes in the nature of their interests that result from the lending and return of shares. This represents a significant enlargement of the exemption set out in the draft rules exposed for consultation, which was confined to "institutional investors" who lent shares.
- Regulated persons - interests in shares borrowed by local brokers and overseas brokers in approved jurisdictions that merely act as a conduit (i.e. regulated persons who borrow and on-lend the shares within five business days) are to be disregarded. The draft Rules have been amended to provide for interests to be disregarded if the shares are passed to a corporation in the same group as the regulated person - provided they are on-lent or returned to a person outside the group within the period of five business days.
These changes to the draft Rules extend and simplify the exemptions with a view to facilitating market development and enhancing liquidity, whilst maintaining adequate transparency in the disclosure regime to protect the interests of investors. The details are set out in the Consultation Conclusions. 

The Rules are subject to negative vetting by the Legislative Council and will become effective on the day appointed for the commencement of Part XV of the Ordinance.

Copies of the Consultation Conclusions are available at the SFC's office and on the SFC's website at www.hksfc.org.hk and the FinNet communication network.
