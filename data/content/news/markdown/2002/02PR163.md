The Securities and Futures Commission today releases its Quarterly Report for the financial period 1 April 2002 to 30 June 2002. 

The SFC recorded a deficit of $7 million for the financial quarter, significantly below the projected deficit of $29 million in the approved budget. At the end of June, the SFC's reserves stood at $617 million, equivalent to 1.37 times the approved annual operating expenditure of $450 million.

During the quarter, the SFC's total revenue fell to $91 million, 16% lower than that for the same quarter last year. About half of the reduction was attributable to a 14% drop in levy income as average daily market turnover shrank from $10 billion to $8.2 billion. Income from fees and charges also decreased with fewer corporate finance activities. Investment income declined because interest rates remained low and part of the investment funding was drawn down to fund the SFC's operations.

Total expenditure (including depreciation) fell by 8% over the same quarter last year. Staff costs dropped due to a freeze on variable pay. Other expenditures also decreased in the absence of major projects.

SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng said that the financial position of the Commission for the next quarter was difficult to project under current market conditions. The budgeted deficit in the second quarter to 30 September 2002 is $29 million. However, Mr Sheng said that based on current information and tight expenditure control, there was no reason to believe that the deficit would be exceeded.

Mr Sheng added that improving corporate governance standards had been a priority for the SFC. The SFC has proposed a dual filing requirement to strengthen the enforcement of corporate disclosure in Hong Kong. At the same time, the SFC has stepped up investigations into corporate misgovernance, one of its enforcement priorities this year. These measures, together with other proposals being studied by the SFC, HKEx and other relevant bodies, will help lift the corporate governance standards of listed companies in Hong Kong.

During the quarter, the SFC also:

- Prepared subsidiary legislation for the Securities and Futures Ordinance. Of the 38 pieces of subsidiary legislation, the SFC consulted the public on nine pieces and published conclusions on 17 pieces.
- Identified a problem intermediary - Lawsons Securities Company - and took action to preserve the asset of the firm's clients.
- Strengthened Financial Resources Rules requirements to enhance the risk management of intermediaries engaging in margin financing.
- Formed a working group to review the financial regulatory framework for intermediaries.
- Formalized the Shareholders Group as an SFC standing committee.
- Assisted the Financial Market Development Task Force in making its quick-win recommendations.
- Clarified the existing short selling reporting and stock lending record keeping requirements.
- Received HKEx's report on the 28 May disruption of the Automated Trading Systems of the derivatives market. The SFC will monitor HKEx's implementation of the report's recommendations.
- Issued guidelines on the public offering of hedge funds and facilitated rule changes by HKEx for the launches of new products.
- Disciplined 14 registrants for misconduct and prosecuted nine entities for breaches of laws including market manipulation.
- Referred two insider dealing cases to the Financial Secretary for consideration of appointment of an Insider Dealing Tribunal.
