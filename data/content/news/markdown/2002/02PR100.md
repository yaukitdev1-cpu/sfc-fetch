The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today releases for public consultation the draft Securities and Futures (Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading - Exemption) Rules. The rules are to be made under section 397 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) when it comes into effect.

Under the existing Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading Ordinance (LFETO), the term "leveraged foreign exchange trading" is initially described in broad terms and then a range of acts and transactions are excluded from the definition. A similar approach is followed in the SFO where items excluded from the definition of "leveraged foreign exchange trading" include an act performed in connection with any contract or arrangement by a class of persons as prescribed by rules made under section 397 of the Ordinance.

The draft Rules prescribe that certain acts (except cold calling) performed by any person belonging to three qualifying classes are to be excluded from the definition of "leveraged foreign exchange trading". This means that persons belonging to the qualifying classes, if they perform these acts, need not be licensed for carrying on a business in activities that may constitute leveraged foreign exchange trading.

The first qualifying class consists of corporations that carry on any form of leveraged foreign exchange trading but whose principal business is not in leveraged foreign exchange spot transactions, and provided that:
· the average principal amount in such transactions is not less than $7.8 million; and
· the corporation or its sole holding corporation or partnership has a qualifying credit rating.

Persons constituting the second qualifying class are licensed persons who deals in currency warrants that are to be listed, and clients of the licensed persons dealing through the licensed persons.

The third class consists of:

- issuers of such currency warrants; and
the issuers' fellow group companies
undertaking transactions in the currency warrants through licensed persons, or undertaking intra-group transactions in the currency warrants.While the first qualifying class replicates that of the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Exemption) Rules prescribed under the LFETO, the latter qualifying classes constitute a new policy initiative. The aim is to facilitate the development of the listed currency warrant market by removing unnecessary technical obstacles. As with the exclusion for the non-retail end of the foreign exchange market in the first qualifying class, the Commission believes that the extension of this exclusion to the second and third qualifying classes should not pose a material regulatory concern. Further, investor protection would not be compromised, as the exempted classes of persons are limited to licensed persons and their clients carrying out a limited category of transactions, and certain companies with a defined relationship carrying out intra-company transactions not involving investors.
