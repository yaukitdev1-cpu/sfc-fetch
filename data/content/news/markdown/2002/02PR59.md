The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today issues new guidelines which set out the factors the SFC should consider when imposing fines on intermediaries. The Disciplinary Fining Guidelines will enhance the transparency of the SFC's future fining power.

Draft guidelines were published for public consultation last year and the SFC received 12 submissions from intermediaries, the banking sector, various professional bodies and individuals.

In preparing the Guidelines and considering the public comments, the SFC had also sought input from the Disciplinary Fining Guidelines Working Group, members of which include directors and compliance staff from international and local intermediaries, a solicitor and a representative from the Consumer Council. The public and the working group were generally supportive of the concept of fining and the principles listed in the Guidelines.

The Guidelines provide a list of flexible considerations that the SFC will take into account when deciding whether to fine and how to fine. The more serious the improper conduct, the greater the likelihood that the SFC will impose a fine and that the size of the fine will be larger. In making a decision, the SFC will consider all the circumstances of each individual case.

The Guidelines will be effective when the Securities and Futures Ordinance commences later this year. Under the Ordinance, the SFC may impose a fine on a regulated intermediary up to the higher of $10 million or three times the amount of profit made or loss avoided, if the intermediary is guilty of misconduct or is not fit and proper. The Ordinance requires the SFC to publish guidelines on how it will impose fines and consider those guidelines when it fines.

Mr Alan Linning, the SFC's Executive Director of Enforcement said: "The Guidelines will help the SFC impose consistent, proportionate and just fines in a fair and transparent manner."

Copies of the [Guidelines](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/legislation/regulatory/codes_guide/e_fining_guidelines.pdf) and a consultation conclusion paper are available at the SFC office and on the SFC's website at http:// www.hksfc.org.hk.
