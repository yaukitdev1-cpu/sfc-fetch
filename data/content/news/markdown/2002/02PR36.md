The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today releases the consultation conclusion (Consultation Conclusion) on the draft Securities and Futures (Unsolicited Calls - Exclusion) Rules (the Rules) as proposed in the consultation paper released on 20 October 2001. 

The Rules define situations where intermediaries are not considered to have breached the provisions regulating the making of unsolicited calls to investors under clause 169 of the Securities and Futures Bill (the Bill). The Rules also set out a few examples of permissible communications.

The provisions on unsolicited calls in the Bill and the Rules serve to offer better investor protection by curtailing improper selling techniques by intermediaries.

During the one-month consultation period, the SFC received a total of 14 responses from practitioners including fund management firms, international brokerage firms, legal firms, industry representative bodies, professional associations and industry regulators. One submission was the survey result of the members conducted by the Hong Kong Securities Institute. Commentators generally welcomed the proposed Rules.

A Consultation Conclusion released today by the SFC provides an analysis of the submissions and explains the Commission's perspectives.

The Rules will become effective on the day appointed for the commencement of Part VII of the Bill.

Copies of the Consultation Conclusion are available at the SFC's office and on the SFC's website http://www.hksfc.org.hk.
