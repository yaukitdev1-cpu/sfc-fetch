The Securities and Futures Commission has formed a working group to advise the SFC on how to improve the financial regulation of securities and futures intermediaries and increase investor protection.

Background

In a paper to the Legislative Council Panel on Financial Affairs last April (please see Note to Editor below), the SFC undertook to review the adequacy of the existing regulatory framework in Hong Kong for managing brokers' financial risks.

The SFC believes that certain fundamental issues should be addressed in improving the overall financial regulatory framework for intermediaries and market participants. As part of the project, the SFC would study the regulatory framework in other securities market jurisdictions.

The aim is to set out the results and proposals in a White Paper to be released for public consultation later this year.

The Working Group

A working group has since been formed. It is called the Working Group on Review of Financial Regulatory Framework for Intermediaries.

The group has 23 members of whom a majority are market representatives, the rest being SFC staff. It is chaired by SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, Mrs Alexa Lam.

Market members of the group include local and international brokers, representatives of brokers and fund managers associations, members from the academia and media, and the Consumer Council.

The group is formed with a view to developing a robust risk-based financial and capital regulatory framework for the regulation of the securities and market intermediaries. The framework should both facilitate the development of the industry and its intermediaries in Hong Kong, and provide adequate investor protection.

The group will be asked to analyse the major risk areas of the industry, in particular risks inherent in the current regulatory structure under which intermediaries provide margin financing, and recommend an appropriate regulatory model most suitable for Hong Kong.

Mrs Lam said: "The SFC is pleased to have the crucial input of the industry and other interested parties in this exercise. This regulatory partnership will ensure that the future regulatory framework will be conducive to the operation of intermediaries engaged in different activities. Sound risk management attracts more clients and it will be a win-win situation for both brokers and investors."

The group has met two times and will from time to time report to the SFC. The SFC will consider the views and proposals of the group in preparing the White Paper for public consultation later this year.

Note to Editor:

1. This refers to the joint paper submitted by the Financial Services Bureau and the SFC to the Panel on Proposed Amendments to the Current Financial Resources Rules dated 29 April 2002. These changes will become effective on 1 October 2002. Separately the SFC is today consulting the market on the draft Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules, which are to be made under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO). The SFO is expected to come into effect early next year.
