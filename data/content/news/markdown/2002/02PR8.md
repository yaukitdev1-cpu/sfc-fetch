The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) is pleased to announce that it has today published a set of provisions for index funds. The provisions have been incorporated as Chapter 8.6 into the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds (the Code). They set out the regulatory requirements for the authorisation of index funds under the Code.

A public consultation on the proposed provisions last year had attracted submissions from fund managers, index providers, lawyers and others. In general, comments were positive. The market welcomed more transparent and consistent guidelines on the authorisation of index funds and believed this would facilitate market development.

A second round of consultation was conducted with some respondents on certain issues arising from their comments, and this was completed in last November.

An index fund is a collective investment scheme that aims to track or replicate an index on equities, debts or other securities. The underlying index of a fund seeking authorisation must be acceptable to the SFC and the acceptance criteria have been set out in the provisions.

In addition, the provisions set out investment restrictions and reporting requirements. As a continuous responsibility, the fund manager is required to report to the SFC and notify holders upon occurrence of significant events relating to the index, such as changes in the characteristics of the index. The provisions also require clear risk disclosure in the fund prospectus so that investors can make informed decisions.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "We have carefully considered the market's comments and appropriate amendments have been made as a result. We are confident that the provisions now represent a reasoned balance between protecting investors and allowing fund managers to comply with reasonable ease."

"We have worked closely with the industry when developing the provisions and I would like to thank all those who gave us their valuable comments," she said.

New applications will have to comply with the provisions in full immediately. Existing funds will be given a grace period of six months.

Comments by the respondents and the SFC's conclusions are set out in a Consultation Conclusions paper, which is published with the revised provisions.

The [Consultation Conclusions](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/conclusion?refNo=01CP8) paper is available on the SFC website. A limited number of printed copies are also available at the SFC's office.
