The SFC is conducting enquiries into five suspicious websites identified in a regionally coordinated campaign to fight securities crimes on the Internet. 

On 30 September 2002, the SFC took part in the Internet Surf Day arranged by the Asia-Pacific Regional Committee of the IOSCO (Note 1). In this campaign, 13 participating regulators within the Asia Pacific Region surfed the Internet to identify possible fraudulent or abusive activities relating to the securities and futures markets. The other participants included regulators from Mainland China, Australia, Thailand, Singapore, etc.

This is the third time the SFC has taken part in this annual activity. The participation of the SFC demonstrates its resolution to co-operate with overseas regulators in the monitoring of the Internet to identify and eradicate illegal activities.

The SFC found five websites that were suspected of offering unregistered dealing or investment services to the Hong Kong public. Enquiries are being made and enforcement actions will be taken where appropriate.

SFC Internet surveillance program

Although the Internet has been a valuable technological development for the financial markets, it has also provided a relatively inexpensive and efficient means to carry out potentially illegal activities across borders. The surf day not only helps to increase public awareness of potential investment fraud on the Internet, but also sends a clear message to wrongdoers that they cannot escape detection by the international regulatory community.

The SFC has an Internet surveillance program, which involves the daily monitoring of websites, chat rooms and bulletin boards. The program aims to detect any activities which target Hong Kong and which may infringe the legislation that the SFC enforces.

In deciding whether or not Hong Kong is a target, the SFC will consider the nature of the business activity, as well as other factors, including whether a local distribution agent is used, reference is made to Hong Kong dollars, or the Chinese language is used in the website.

If potentially illegal activities aimed at another jurisdiction are detected, information concerning these activities will normally be passed to the regulator in that jurisdiction.

To date, the program has resulted in the SFC prosecuting nine entities and disciplining a registered intermediary.

Check before invest

Investors should always take care in investing in any scheme by understanding what the scheme entails, who is behind it and, if an intermediary is involved, ensuring that it is properly licensed or authorised in the jurisdiction where it operates. Many regulators offer investors access to their list of licensed intermediaries. In Hong Kong, the "Register Section" on the SFC website provides a full list of registrants and authorised investment products.

Furthermore, in order to help investors make more informed choices about who they deal with when trading shares, the SFC has set up an Investor Alert on its website to list names of firms which are not registered with the SFC but which, based on information available to the SFC, claim to be carrying on the business of dealing in securities or giving investment advice in Hong Kong, or holding out that they do by giving a Hong Kong address on their websites.

If investors are considering an investment opportunity found on the Internet, they should always take care. Investors are advised to:

- do some research, get hold of an annual report, check out that the company really exists;
- be aware that what looks genuine and a good investment on the Internet may just be clever marketing;
- be on your guard if there is a promise of "privileged" information, any encouragement to "hurry" or where there is a promise of unusually high returns and low risk; and
- if in doubt, seek professional advice.
Moreover, investors should take great care when considering investing based on unconfirmed information that may be available on the Internet, in particular, through chat rooms and on bulletin boards. Any investment decision should be based on information that can be verified. 

The public are invited to report any suspicious activities they come across, whether they be identified in advertisements, websites, chat rooms or bulletin boards, pertaining to the securities and futures market in Hong Kong. This can be done by contacting the SFC's hotline on 2840-9333, or completing the complaint form in our website. All such reports will be treated with the utmost confidence.
