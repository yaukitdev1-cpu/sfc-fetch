The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) welcomes the enactment of the Securities and Futures Bill in the Legislative Council today following resumption of the second and third readings of the Bill. 

This major achievement represents the culmination of nearly a decade of hard work by the Administration, legislators, and all those who have participated in the consultation process. They include market organisations, trade and industry associations, professional bodies, other market participants and members of the public.

SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng said: "Our aim is to produce a clear, user-friendly and modern law which would encourage and support the development of the market. The legislation offers greater investor protection and will strengthen Hong Kong's competitive edge internationally."

"I firmly believe that we have taken a major step forward today. The legislation enacted today reflects the basic policy objectives to have a world class regulatory framework for a fair, efficient and transparent market. It is the product of extensive market consultation and debate, with a good consensus among all the interested parties."

The newly enacted Securities and Futures Ordinance consolidates and modernises the 10 existing ordinances regulating the securities and futures markets which were written over the last 25 years.

In addition to the broad changes necessary to address the developments in the securities and futures markets brought about by globalisation and advances in technology, the Ordinance introduces a number of key initiatives aimed at enhancing the quality of the markets and lowering compliance costs by market participants.

The major features are listed in Appendix 1. They include:

- a new streamlined single licensing regime;
- new proportionate disciplinary sanctions to combat market misconduct;
- new measures to protect the interests of investors, such as personal rights of action through the civil courts for loss caused by market misconduct or false or misleading public statements concerning securities;
- a new and comprehensive investor compensation scheme; and
a tighter regime for disclosure of interests in listed companies.
Mr Sheng said: "I sincerely thank all those who have helped realise the legislative reform, especially members of the Legislative Council Bills Committee, its Chairman the Hon. Sin Chung-kai, and deputy Chairman the Hon. Margaret Ng. They have devoted countless hours of their time to scrutinising the Bill and working with the Administration in improving it in the interests of the Hong Kong market and investors." 

"Particular thanks must go to those in the Administration, including the former Financial Secretary Mr Donald Tsang and his successor Mr Antony Leung, the Secretary for Financial Services Mr Stephen Ip and his predecessor Mr Rafael Hui, Deputy Secretary for Financial Services Ms Au King-chi, and their team in the Financial Services Bureau, as well as the Hong Kong Monetary Authority," he said.

"Our gratitude also goes to the Law Drafting Division of the Department of Justice and the other divisions who provided invaluable legal advice and support during the course of this project. Special mention must be made of Mr David Little of the Department, who began the drafting of the Bill when he was Chief Counsel in the Commission," Mr Sheng said.

"Finally, I thank my colleagues here in the Commission who have worked so hard on the Bill," he said.

In March 1999, Mr Donald Tsang announced in his Budget plans for the legislative reform for the securities and futures markets to enhance protection of investors and Hong Kong's competitiveness as an international financial centre.

The Securities and Futures Bill was published as a White Paper Bill for public consultation in April 2000. Taking into account comments on the White Paper Bill, a Blue Bill was gazetted in November 2000 and introduced into the Legislative Council (LegCo) in the same month. A chronology of events is in Appendix 2.

A total of 55 submissions of comments on the Blue Bill were made by 21 organisations and 4 individuals. About 140 hours were devoted to discussion of the Bill in 55 formal Bills Committee meetings. A total of about 300 papers explaining the Bill or providing relevant information were prepared by the Administration and put before the Bills Committee.

As a result of Bills Committee discussions, more than 500 Committee Stage Amendments have been formulated to address technical and policy issues.

The Ordinance will be gazetted on 28 March, and it is hoped that the new regulatory regime can be brought into operation before the end of the year.

A considerable body of subsidiary legislation will be needed to enable commencement of the Ordinance. Work on the 38 items of such legislation identified as necessary is well underway, with almost a third of these already issued for public consultation and many other items in the pipeline for consultation before the end of March 2002. A LegCo Subcommittee has been established to consider the subsidiary legislation.

Hong Kong is the third major securities market to pass new securities regulatory legislation in recent months. The United Kingdom Financial Services and Markets Act, 2001 came into force on 1 December 2001. The Financial Services Reform Act 2001 in Australia became effective on 11 March 2002.

Mr Sheng said: "Looking ahead, the Commission's primary consideration is to ensure a smooth transition into the new regime, both for us as the regulator and the market. We are committed to working with the industry in finalising the subsidiary legislation and will be issuing all items of subsidiary legislation for formal and open public consultation."

The Securities and Futures Ordinance will be available on the SFC website following its gazettal.

Appendix 1: Major Features of the Securities and Futures Ordinance

**A new streamlined single licensing regime**
Each licensee need only have one licence which will cover all the regulated activities that the licensee is eligible to conduct. This should result in a simplified licensing and compliance system and reduced costs for licensees.**New proportionate disciplinary sanctions on licensees**
The Ordinance provides for additional types of sanctions including the power to fine. It also has provision for the Commission to suspend or revoke a licence in respect of only part of a licensee's business. These innovations will result in greater flexibility in tailoring appropriate sanctions.**Combating market misconduct**
There will be parallel civil and criminal regimes to enable the Commission more effectively to combat market misconduct. A Market Misconduct Tribunal will be created to build on the work of the Insider Dealing Tribunal by handling civil cases of market misconduct such as market manipulation and other types of market abuse defined in the Ordinance, in addition to insider dealing. The Tribunal will be able to impose a greater range of civil sanctions such as imposing "cease and desist" and "cold shoulder" orders, disqualifying a person from directorship or management of a company and ordering disgorgement of profits. As an alternative to civil proceedings, all types of market misconduct will be subject to criminal prosecution which, if successful, may result in more severe penalties on conviction, including up to 10 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to HK$10 million.**Redress through the civil courts**
There will be statutory rights of action through the civil courts for individuals who have suffered pecuniary loss caused by market misconduct or false or misleading public statements concerning securities.**Strengthening disclosure of interests regime**
Equal and timely access for investors to information about controlling interests in listed companies will be ensured through a tighter regime of disclosure of interests in listed companies. The disclosure requirements will apply to those acquiring or disposing of an interest of 5% or more of the issued voting share capital of a listed company (including interests in underlying securities) - a reduction from the existing 10% threshold. Furthermore, the notification period will be reduced from the existing 5 days to 3 business days.**Investor compensation**
A new investor compensation scheme will be introduced with a per-investor limit of HK$ 150,000. Under the new scheme, coverage will extend to all intermediaries and not just participants of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and the Hong Kong Futures Exchange. An independent investor compensation company will be formed to administer the scheme.**Facilitating market innovation**
The emergence of electronic trading networks and the new regulatory issues posed by this are addressed in the Ordinance with a licensing regime extending to cater for companies providing automated trading services. Different procedures will apply depending on the nature of the services. Where they resemble stockbroking, the licensing regime will apply; and where they are more reminiscent of a stock exchange, a separate authorisation regime will apply. The intention is to ensure that the regime is flexible enough to enable it to cater for future market development in this area without the need to amend the Ordinance.**Checks and balances: the SFC's accountability**
Along with the extension of the Commission's powers in certain areas such as its supervision, investigatory and intervention powers, the corresponding checks and balances on the Commission are improved and enhanced in the Bill. These include the preservation or raising of the existing statutory thresholds applicable to the exercise of its powers by the SFC, provisions in the Bill which clearly preserve the privilege against self-incrimination and the stringent secrecy and confidentiality obligations to which the SFC and its staff are subject. A powerful safeguard will lie in the establishment of an independent full-time appeals tribunal chaired by a judge - the Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal. Many SFC decisions will be appealable to this tribunal.
