Three investors have been given the Smart Investor Awards for reporting suspected boiler rooms to the SFC.

As a result of their reports, the names of four dubious operators have been posted on the Alert List of unlicensed overseas companies and suspected scam websites on the SFC’s website.

All three investors have been cold-called by suspect fraudsters. In one case, the investor was approached by a company, which claimed to operate from Malaysia, to buy an overseas stock. While he was still considering the offer, the company claimed that it had already bought the stock for him and asked him to pay the proceed of $48,000. This technique is common among boiler room scams. The investor obligingly sent a cheque at first, but as he became suspicious of the company, he asked the SFC for advice and managed to stop the payment in time.

In the other two cases, the investors were cold-called by companies, purportedly based in the US, Hungary, Canada, PRC and Costa Rica, which offered brokerage services for overseas stocks. However, they were asked to make an up-front payment, of 30% of the value of stocks involved in one case, for possible tax payment in the market concerned or other seemingly credible reasons. Both investors decided to collect more information about the companies and noted the names of several companies that were associated with these cold-calling companies had been posted on the SFC Alert List. They therefore reported their cases to the SFC.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer, presented the Awards yesterday (Note 1) at the SFC’s office. He said: "Dishonest tricksters undermine the credibility of the financial markets. Investors can assist in regulatory action to remove such fraud. We want to hear from you."

Recipients of the Awards each received an SFC corporate watch and a certificate of recognition.

The public are welcome to report other suspected boiler rooms and scam websites for consideration of awards in the future.

The Smart Investors Awards were introduced in February 2004 to raise public awareness of how financial scams such as boiler rooms and scam websites operate so that investors can better protect themselves.

Two fake scam websites have also been created for the first time on the SFC website to demonstrate the typical pitfalls of financial scams. When investors click to contact the fake companies, educational tips would pop up to remind the public how to protect themselves against fraudsters (Note 2).

The SFC first introduced the Alert List in November 2000 when boiler room activities became active. Investors have found the information useful. 65 new entities were posted on the Alert List in the last 12 months to the end of March. There are over 150 entities on the List.

In 2003-04, we received complaints from 34 victims of financial scams who suffered losses of $10.9 million. Eight were Hong Kong investors.

The SFC believes that to tackle these scams, investors’ self-protection is the best and first line defence. Investors should be very vigilant. Here are some tips:


- reputable brokers and investment banks do not call people who are not their clients to offer investment opportunities. Cold calling is a crime in places including Hong Kong;
- investments that sound too good to be true usually are;
- never invest in anything which you have to sign a secrecy agreement;
- be careful of being asked to pay money up-front; and
- always check out who you are dealing with.
