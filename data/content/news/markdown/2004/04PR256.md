The International Organization of Securities Commission (IOSCO) has published its Code of Conduct Fundamentals for Credit Rating Agencies (CRAs). 

The CRA Code Fundamentals represents a global, converged view of specific measures CRAs can use to protect their analytical independence, eliminate or manage conflicts of interest, help ensure the confidentiality of certain types of information shared with them by issuers and maintain the integrity of the credit rating process.

Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the IOSCO Technical Committee, said: “The essential purpose of the Code Fundamentals is to promote investor protection by safeguarding the integrity of the rating process.  IOSCO expects all CRAs to give full effect to the Code Fundamentals.  The reason we devoted so much effort to this policy area is because we recognise the importance of reliable and accurate credit ratings in helping investors assess the credit risks they face when making investment decisions.”

CRAs are expected to incorporate the contents of the Code Fundamentals  into their own code of conduct, and to publish their code with an explanation of how each of the Code Fundamentals’ provisions is addressed. If a particular provision is not incorporated into a CRA’s own code of conduct, it is expected to disclose this fact, explain why it has not incorporated the relevant provision and state how the objectives of that provision are otherwise addressed.  Market participants will then be able to judge for themselves whether the CRA has implemented the Code Fundamentals satisfactorily and react accordingly.

Mr Sheng believes that there will be sufficient market pressure on CRAs to fully comply. “Failure to comply would undoubtedly have an adverse impact on individual CRAs as the industry and investors become aware of instances of non-compliance.  We believe that in order to gain advantage in the market-place most CRAs will work to ensure their compliance with the IOSCO Code,” he said.

The SFC does not regulate CRAs and adoption by a CRA of the Code Fundamentals is not mandated by law. However, CRAs have been supportive of the development of the Code Fundamentals and recognise their responsibility to maintain confidence in the credit rating process.

The SFC actively participated in the Chairmen's Task Force of the Technical Committee responsible for developing the Code Fundamentals.
