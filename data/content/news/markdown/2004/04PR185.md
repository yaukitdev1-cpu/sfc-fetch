The SFC has successfully prosecuted {X} under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) for the issuance of unauthorised advertisements, and holding out as performing regulated activities without being licensed by the SFC.

The SFC found that between 15 and 18 March 2004, {X} had issued advertisements in local newspapers inviting the public to enter into an agreement to acquire or dispose of securities. These advertisements were not authorised by the SFC prior to their issuance. Responding to the enquiries made on these advertisements, {X} held himself out as providing securities trading and advisory services, though he was not a licensed person of the SFC.

{X} pleaded guilty to two summonses before Mr Ian Candy at Eastern Magistracy today. {X} was fined $10,000 and ordered to pay investigation costs of $4,500 to the SFC.

An SFC spokesman said: "Any advertisement, invitation or document which contains an invitation to the public to enter into or offer to enter into an agreement to acquire or dispose of securities, unless otherwise exempted from compliance with the SFO, must be authorised by the SFC before issuance. On the other hand, the licensing regime ensures that firms and individuals providing investment services are fit and proper. Unlicensed activities are detrimental to investor interests and market integrity. A person must be licensed before engaging in regulated activities or representing that he or she is."
