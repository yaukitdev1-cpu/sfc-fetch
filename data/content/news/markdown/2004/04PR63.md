The SFC today publishes a Consultation Paper with a view to devise regulations to reduce and manage analyst conflicts of interest.

To address the analyst conflicts issue, the SFC has been actively monitoring local and international developments, and studying policy proposals most suitable for Hong Kong.

"Having taken into account the opinions of market practitioners and the investing public (Note 1), as well as regulatory developments abroad, we take the view that clearer and more specific guidelines are necessary to address the potential and actual analyst conflicts that may exist in the Hong Kong market," said Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products.

"We are mindful that the proposed guidelines should not stifle legitimate investment or sales ideas or, unnecessarily constrain the flow of information. We have tailored these guidelines to the characteristics of the local market, without impacting brokers’ sales and dealing operations," she said.

"The proposals aim to strengthen the regulation of SFC licensees only. The SFC does not seek to impose any regulations on persons who are not licensed by the SFC, including journalists and media organisations (Note 2). We will keep an open mind to any suggestions regarding the proposed regulations and all comments will be duly considered in the consultation process," Mrs Lam added.

The guidelines are intended to be incorporated into the Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission.

The proposals set out in the Consultation Paper have been informally discussed with a range of market practitioners. They cover the eight important areas suggested by the International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) (Note 3). They are:


- Analyst trading and financial interests
- Firm financial interests and business relationships
- Analysts’ reporting lines and compensation
- Firm compliance systems and senior management responsibility
- Outside influence
- Clarity, specificity and prominence of disclosure
- Integrity and ethical behavior
- Investor education

The SFC aims to publish the consultation conclusions in the third quarter of 2004 and implement the guidelines by the end of the year.

Copies of the Consultation Paper are available at the SFC office and on the SFC website, under "[Bills, Legislation & Codes" – "Consultation Papers & Conclusions](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=04CP1)".
