The SFC today releases its *Quarterly Report* for the financial period 1 October to 31 December 2003.

Surplus continued to grow

For the third quarter of 2003-04, the SFC recorded a surplus of $48.2 million, nearly doubling the surplus of $24.4 million in the previous quarter. The accumulated surplus for the first nine months of the financial year was $65.2 million, compared a projected deficit of $70 million in the approved budget.

At the end of December, the reserves stood at $631 million, equivalent to 1.46 times the approved annual operating expenditure of $433 million.

Total revenue in the first nine months was $369.4 million, 42.5% higher than a year ago. The operating results were better than expected largely because levy income had benefited from the improvement in market turnover. Total expenditure was 3.3% higher at $304.2 million mainly because of the office relocation expenditure and additional personnel expenses for dual filing functions.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said that the SFC had accumulated a healthy surplus so far this year. Barring sudden changes in market conditions, the SFC expects further surplus in the final quarter to 31 March 2004. Nonetheless, the SFC would continue to exercise tight control on its expenditure during the rest of the year.

Work progress

During the third quarter, the SFC:

- saw a steady continuation of intermediaries’ migration to the new licensing regime. As of the end of December, 7,426 licensees or 42% of intermediaries had lodged their migration applications;
- reviewed more new listing applications under dual filing. It had reviewed 82 new listing applications from April to December and commented on 33 of them;
- signed with the China Securities Regulatory Commission the arrangements in relation to mutual recognition of securities and futures industry qualifications under the Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA);
- relaxed its licensing competence requirements to facilitate re-entry into the industry by former practitioners;
- consulted the market on draft guidelines setting out the recommended best practice for disclosure of securities services-related fees and charges by intermediaries;
- continued to work on proposals to address analyst conflicts of interest with a view to consulting the public;
- continued to review the financial regulatory framework for licensed corporations;
- oversaw the orderly transfer of client positions and return of client monies by Yicko Futures Limited;
- revoked the licence of Tai Wah Securities Limited, after the firm had been declared a defaulter by Hong Kong Securities Clearing Company Limited;
- began discussion on the development of benchmarks Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) should consider when investing in overseas properties;
- issued the Guidelines for Regulating Index Tracking Exchange Traded Funds;
- authorised the first group of five Automated Trading Services providers;
- approved 18 subordinated loans or subordinated loan facilities aggregating $2.955 billion to assist brokers who lent to investors to meet financial requirements;
- had 14 successful prosecutions and disciplined 22 licensees for misconduct;
- launched a monthly on-line column to educate investors on the key principles of investing; and
- attended the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) Annual Conference in Seoul, during which the IOSCO selected Hong Kong to host its 31st Annual Conference in 2006.
