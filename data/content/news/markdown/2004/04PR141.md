The SFC today publishes the August issue of the *SFC Enforcement Reporter*, summarising the SFC’s enforcement action in the month of July 2004 and discussing the compliance issues highlighted by recent cases. 

In July, the SFC successfully prosecuted one company and one person for market manipulation.

The SFC disciplined 10 licensees during the month for failings relating to conflicts of interest, Financial Resources Rules breaches, making illegal unsolicited calls, violating the know-your-client requirement, and misstating a firm’s licence status. 

In one of the disciplinary cases, the SFC decided to suspend an analyst for 18 months for frontrunning research reports.  The analyst appealed to the Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal (SFAT) and argued that the penalty was too severe. The SFAT upheld the SFC’s decision in the belief that the public interest demands the SFC to take firm action against such misconduct.

During the month, the SFC also entered into a settlement under which a licensee made a voluntary payment. The SFC can only fine licensees who engaged in misconduct after the commencement of the Securities and Futures Ordinance on 1 April 2003.  Settlement by payment of money provides a bridge between the old and new disciplinary regimes, in appropriate cases where the subject of the decision agrees, so that the same outcomes may be achieved regardless of when the conduct occurred.  The SFC is open to settlement offers by licensees in disciplinary cases. But, it is in the SFC’s sole discretion whether to accept any such offer, and it will settle a case only when it considers that it is in the public interest to do so.

For example, the SFC will not settle for a voluntary payment in exchange for a suspension if dishonesty is involved or the person’s conduct or attitude suggests that he or she would be a continuing risk to the investing public. The SFC is also unlikely to agree to settle a case by receiving a payment in circumstances serious enough to warrant revocation.

Since 1 April 2004, the SFC has successfully prosecuted 20 entities. We have withdrawn summonses issued against five persons resulting in their acquittals and a further three persons were acquitted after trial. The SFC has also disciplined 25 licensees for various regulatory breaches and entered into settlements with voluntary payments with two licensees. Disciplinary proceedings have been taken against six licensees, which were concluded with no formal sanction imposed, although five of them received private warnings. Such proceedings were also commenced and discontinued against one deemed licensee who left his firm before the conclusion of the action.
