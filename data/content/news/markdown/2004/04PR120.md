The SFC today releases the findings of its Fund Management Activities Survey (FMAS) 2003.

The survey collects information on fund management activities in Hong Kong to enable a better understanding of the local fund management industry and to identify possible trends. It also helps the SFC’s efforts to facilitate development and long-term growth of the fund management industry in Hong Kong.

Main findings

In line with previous years, licensed corporations that were engaged in asset management or advisory business were invited to participate in the survey. In addition, for the first time and with assistance from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, we invited registered institutions (Note 1) to participate in the survey. Key findings are as follows (value in HK$ as of 31 December 2003):


- Total assets in the fund management business, which included asset management business, advisory business and other private banking business (Note 2), amounted to $2,947 billion in 2003.
- Licensed corporations reported $2,317 billion worth of fund management business and accounted for 79% of the total fund management business.
- Registered institutions reported $630 billion worth of fund management business and accounted for the remaining 21%.
- 37 (2002: 36) licensed corporations and 10 registered institutions responded that Hong Kong was their regional headquarters.
- Funds sourced from overseas investors amounted to $1,860 billion, representing 63% of the total assets in the fund management business.
- In the asset management business, the percentage of assets managed in Hong Kong had steadily grown from 42% of the assets under management in 2000 to 53% in 2003. In value terms, $1,194 billion worth of the assets was managed onshore.
- Of the $1,194 billion worth of assets managed in Hong Kong, 73% or $878 billion was invested in Asia, including $481 billion in Hong Kong and Mainland China.
- The respondents reported that 15,195 staff (Note 3) were engaged in fund management activities. Among them, 81% were responsible for sales and marketing.

Hong Kong’s fund management business recorded strong gains in 2003

The amount of total assets in the fund management business in Hong Kong, at $2,947 billion, was substantially higher than the $1,635 billion reported in 2002. This was due to two factors, namely:

(a) registered institutions were included in the survey for the first time. They contributed $630 billion worth of assets to the survey (comprising $142 billion in asset management and $488 billion in other private banking activities); and

(b) licensed corporations recorded a 42% increase in their asset management and advisory businesses to $2,317 billion (comprising $2,108 billion in asset management and $209 billion in advisory business). This was on the back of strong performance in the financial markets, inflows of investment funds and gain in investment mandates.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The fund management business has generally benefited from market appreciation and renewed interest in investing in financial products."

Hong Kong is a magnet for overseas funds and possesses the expertise for managing investments in the Asian region

Hong Kong remained strong in attracting overseas funds for investment management. Of the $2,947 billion worth of assets in the fund management business, 63% ($1,860 billion) was sourced from overseas investors.

According to statistics collected from licensed corporations in the last four years, overseas investors had consistently accounted for 60-70% of their asset management and advisory businesses.

Out of the total $2,250 billion worth of assets in the asset management business, 53% or $1,194 billion was managed in Hong Kong. Asia remained the primary destination - 73% or $878 billion, was invested in markets in Asia, including $481 billion in Hong Kong and Mainland China.

Mrs Lam said: "The substantial investments in the Asian markets reflected not only the attractiveness of these markets in 2003, but also the expertise that the fund management industry in Hong Kong has developed over the years in investing in Asia. Such skills have been and will continue to be vital to Hong Kong’s ability to attract overseas funds for management."

"The SFC is fully aware of the rapid developments in the fund management industry and is committed to working closely with industry participants to help facilitate the continuous growth of the industry. At the same time, the SFC believes that it is equally important to keep the public abreast of the changes and new developments in the investment arena. To this end, the SFC is committed to its investor education efforts through various media, such as television, radio, newspapers and information booklets," she added (Note 4).

Copies of the report of FMAS 2003 are available at the SFC office and on its website under "[Press Releases, Publications and Speeches" – "Publications" - "Surveys and Reports](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/surveys/fmas2003_eng.pdf%20%20)".

Notes to Editor:

1. A "registered institution" means an authorised financial institution which is registered under section 119 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO). An "authorised financial institution" means an authorised institution as defined in section 2(1) of the Banking Ordinance.

2. "Asset management" refers to the provision of services which constitutes type 9 regulated activity as defined in Schedule 5 of the SFO.


"Advisory business" refers to the provision of pure investment advisory services on funds/portfolios, which constitutes type 4 and/or type 5 regulated activity as defined in Schedule 5 of the SFO.

For the purpose of this survey, "other private banking activities" are defined as the provision of financial services to private banking clients other than by means of type 9 regulated activity. They include providing the service of managing clients’ portfolio of securities and/or futures contracts wholly incidental to the carrying on of type 1 and/or type 2 regulated activity.


3. The total of 15,195 staff in the fund management business is analysed as follows:


Licensed corporations - 3,763 staff, including 1,526 staff in sales and marketing. Licensed corporations reported a net increase of 1,053 staff in sales and marketing during the year.

Registered institutions – 11,432 staff, including 10,779 staff in sales and marketing (as this is the first year that registered institutions are surveyed, no comparison with previous year’s staffing levels indicating net increase / decrease could be drawn).


4. Initiatives to facilitate product innovation that were completed in 2003 included:

a. Code on Real Estate Investment Trusts (August 2003); and
b. Guidelines for regulating index-tracking exchange traded funds (October 2003).


In order to establish a set of practical benchmarks that address the risk of overseas property investments by REITs and protect the interest of investors, a taskforce has already been commissioned to specifically examine the issues involved. The public will be consulted on the proposals.

To facilitate the further development of hedge funds in Hong Kong, the SFC will also review its hedge fund guidelines this financial year, based on the experience gained in authorising 12 hedge funds so far. The public will be consulted on the proposals.
