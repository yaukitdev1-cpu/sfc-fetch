The SFC Working Group on Review of the Financial Regulatory Framework for Licensed Corporations (Working Group) convened today to discuss a report which includes proposals made by the Working Group to address the risks in securities margin financing and the pooling and re-pledging of client collateral by securities margin finance providers (SMF providers).

Key points:


- The SFC intends to submit the report to the Financial Affairs Panel of the Legislative Council (FA Panel).
- The purpose of the proposed measures is to benefit Hong Kong’s 1,000,000 investors. These measures will also benefit the development of Hong Kong’s securities market, and strengthen Hong Kong’s position as an international financial centre. The SFC recognises that fair treatment and appropriate protection of investors are pre-requisites for any successful securities market and a healthy securities industry.
- Other major financial centres do not permit the re-pledging of collateral belonging to clients who have not borrowed. In contrast, Hong Kong allows firms to re-pledge all client collateral, including that belonging to clients who have not borrowed, for a variety of purposes.
- The proposed measures would impact only a handful of firms. These firms would be required to cut down their re-pledging, lend more prudently or put in their own capital. The ability of firms to freely re-pledge for any purpose will also be affected to some extent. The SFC will work with these firms.
- Membership of the Working Group is broad-based and comprises individuals representing securities firms of different sizes, academia, the Hong Kong Investment Funds Association and the Consumer Council.
- The SFC proposes to consult the public on the recommended measures, whether they are appropriate, and if so the timetable for implementation. On these issues, the SFC maintains an open mind.


**Background**

The collapse of C.A. Pacific Securities Limited and C.A. Pacific Finance Limited in January 1998 highlighted the risks to which the pooling and re-pledging of client securities collateral could expose margin clients. At the time of the collapse, over 5,000 clients were affected and C.A. Pacific Finance Limited had capital of only $16 million, but had borrowed $548 million by re-pledging client collateral of over $2.5 billion. Losses claimed by clients amounted to $983 million and the Compensation Fund made payments totalling $300.4 million.

In the wake of the incident, the Securities (Margin Financing) (Amendment) Bill was introduced whereby all SMF providers, including unregulated finance companies, were brought within the purview of the SFC and made subject to much stricter rules than those applied to C.A. Pacific. In May 2002, the SFC introduced two new financial resources requirements – the 65% gearing ratio adjustment and the illiquid collateral haircut. These interim measures have helped manage down these risks.

At the same time, the Working Group was formed by the SFC in response to the FA Panel’s suggestion that the SFC should review the practices of pooling and re-pledging client collateral and the risks in securities margin financing activities and examine the effectiveness of the overall financial regulatory framework for the securities industry, with a view to enhancing investor protection.

Other major financial markets do not permit the pooling and re-pledging of collateral belonging to clients who have no current borrowing from the firm. The US goes a step further by imposing a limit on the re-pledging of collateral belonging to borrowing margin clients.

In contrast, Hong Kong currently allows the unrestricted pooling and re-pledging of client collateral, provided the margin clients have authorised their SMF providers to do so, irrespective of whether the clients have actually taken any margin loan. The current regime exists due to historical reasons and the lack of infrastructure in the industry to enable total segregation in a feasible manner.


**The Working Group’s objective**

The Working Group recognises that the failure of a securities firm would threaten the interests of the investing public, cause loss to clients and harm investor confidence in the securities market. At stake is the reputation not only of the securities industry but also of Hong Kong as a competitive international financial centre. In particular, any such failure could seriously hurt Hong Kong’s bid to be, and remain, a premier capital-raising hub for the Mainland.

Hong Kong should be moving towards compliance with international standards in this very important area as a long term objective. However, complete segregation of collateral belonging to clients who have not borrowed would impose substantial financial and administrative costs on firms. Yet, there is a need to reduce pooling risk for the protection of investors and in the overall interests of the market.

Thus, the objective of the Working Group is to devise workable measures that target high risk lending and indiscriminate re-pledging practices so as to provide better protection to investors, without causing undue hardship to the industry.

As at the end of September 2003, there were 453 SEHK exchange participants including those providing securities margin financing. Of the 242 SMF providers, only a small number are considered to be in a financially tenuous position due to aggressive lending and re-pledging.

The SFC will submit a report to the FA Panel in the first quarter of 2004 and consult the market on the recommended measures.


**The measures recommended for consultation**

There are two core measures and two supplementary measures.

A. Two core measures:

(1) Limit the amount of client collateral that can be re-pledged by a broker to secure its borrowings.


In order to address the unrestricted pooling and re-pledging of client collateral, the Working Group recommends setting a limit on the re-pledging of client collateral on a per-firm basis, i.e. capping the aggregate value of client collateral that can be re-pledged by a firm at a percentage of the firm’s total margin loans to clients.

Although this measure would not meet the international standard of prohibiting the re-pledging of non-borrowing clients’ collateral, investors’ exposure to pooling risk would be reduced.

The SFC intends to consult the public on the appropriate limit, as well as other possible options.

It should be noted that the proposed re-pledging limit represents an improvement to the 65% gearing ratio that was introduced in 2002. The gearing ratio requires a SMF provider to use its own capital to fund at least part of its margin loans to clients. While it has been effective in discouraging a firm from borrowing excessively from banks, it does not limit the amount of client collateral that can be re-pledged to banks. The new measure will ensure that at least a good portion of client collateral remains with the broker.

The SFC is prepared to consider relaxations such as the replacement of the gearing ratio by an acceptable re-pledging limit.


(2) Increase the haircut percentages under the Financial Resources Rules (FRR).


This measure is designed to encourage SMF providers to adhere to prudent collateral ratios by either collecting additional collateral or better quality collateral from clients. Those SMF providers that lend on aggressive collateral ratios would then be required to use their own capital.

The proposed new FRR haircut percentages would still be *significantly* lower than the average haircut percentages used by banks and brokerages, so the majority of firms would not be affected by this measure.

The new haircut percentages would not restrict investors from trading in the stocks of their choice nor prohibit SMF providers from lending against those stocks.


Only a small number of SMF providers would be impacted by the core measures.

These firms would have to cut down their re-pledging, lend more prudently or put in their own capital, in order to comply with the new requirements.

However, with the recent increase in market turnover and improved profit levels, these firms should be in a better position to achieve compliance.

B. Two supplementary measures:

(1) Improve the Code of Conduct disclosure obligations by requiring a firm to disclose additional information to its clients and/or the regulator.

(2) Step up investor education on pooling risk.


C. Transitional Period

The Working Group recommends a 12-month transitional period for currently licensed firms to attain full compliance with the proposed measures. The SFC will work closely with affected firms during the transitional period.

SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, Alexa Lam, said: "The Working Group’s proposed reforms are a significant step forward in the continued development of Hong Kong’s securities industry. These proposed reforms seek to provide margin clients with better protection against the loss of their collateral re-pledged to banks. They are important for Hong Kong to maintain its status as an international financial centre."

"We look forward to presenting these proposals to the FA Panel before consulting the market and the public," Mrs Lam said.
