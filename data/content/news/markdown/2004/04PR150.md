The SFC today releases the key findings of a Review of the Financial Performance of Category C Stock Exchange Participants since 2001.

The review tracks the financial performance of Category C brokers from the first quarter of 2001 to the second quarter of 2004. The analysis was based on the monthly financial data reported by the brokers. The key findings are summarised below.


- Overall profitability of Category C brokers has improved significantly since the market recovery in mid-2003.
- The aggregate operating profit in the past 18 months (ended 30 June 2004) has more than covered the aggregate operating losses incurred by Category C brokers in 2001 and 2002.
- Only a small number of Category C brokers recorded a net loss over the last 12-month period (ended 30 June 2004). In aggregate terms, these brokers accounted for less than 2% of total Stock Exchange turnover in June 2004.
- Employment by Category C brokers remained steady over the past 12 months in terms of the number of licensed representatives and the amount of staff salaries.
 

- Brokerage commission income continued to be the major source of revenue of Category C brokers. Staff related costs constituted almost half of the total expenses of Category C brokers.

The review highlights the positive fact that most Category C brokers improved their financial performance in the 18-month period ended 30 June 2004. The Commission will continue to work together with the industry to increase the competitiveness of the industry. Updated financial information of Stock Exchange Participants will be provided to the market regularly.

Findings of the Review are detailed in the Appendix to this press release.

4. Category C brokers experienced a tough period from 2001 to early 2003 as their number of active clients, profitability as well as market share experienced a continuous drop under highly competitive and adverse market conditions.

5. Despite the drop in market share from 23% to 15%, Category C brokers made a substantial profit over the last 12 months. Amid unfavourable market conditions, Category C brokers saw a 10% increase in the average shareholders’ fund per firm compared to March 2001.

**Category C brokers’ profitability has improved significantly since the market recovery in mid-2003**

6. Excluding proprietary trading profit and loss, Category C brokers recorded an operating profit of $252 million in the second quarter of 2004, as compared to an operating loss of $28 million a year ago (second quarter of 2003).

**B. Income and cost structure of Category C brokers**

**Brokerage commission income continued to be the major source of revenue of Category C brokers**

9. The following chart illustrates the income composition of Category C brokers in June 2004: brokerage commission (69% of total) was the major source of income, followed by other income (14%) and interest income (13%). Brokerage commission appears to be a stable source of income given that it also contributed 64% in July 2003.



# Other income mainly comprised of dividend income, placing fee, IPO related income, etc.

**Staff related costs constituted almost half of the total expenses of Category C brokers**

10. Staff related costs, including salary expense as well as commission rebate paid to licensed representatives or account executives (i.e. front office sales and marketing staff serving client accounts), constituted almost half of the total expenses of Category C brokers.

11. Over one-third of the total expenses of Category C brokers consisted of salary expenses (25%) and rental expenses (8%). These expense items remained stable in both June 2004 and July 2003 in terms of absolute amounts.



## Other expenses mainly comprised of repair and maintenance, foreign exchange loss, information cost, clearing expense, etc.


**C. Analysis of loss reporting Category C brokers**

**Only a small number of Category C brokers recorded a net loss for the 12-month period ended 30 June 2004**

12. We also performed an analysis on those Category C brokers that reported a net loss for the 12-month period ended 30 June 2004. A total of 52 Category C brokers were identified as making losses and together they accounted for less than 2% of total SEHK turnover in June 2004.

13. Among the 52 brokers, 16 have affiliates that conduct other activities so there should be opportunities for cross-selling and business synergy. The other 36 Category C brokers employ only a few licensed representatives and on average have less than 400 clients.


**D. Employment status of Category C brokers**

**Employment of Category C brokers has remained steady over the past 12 months.**

14. The employment by Category C brokers has remained stable in light of the number of licensed representatives (about 4,400) and the staff salaries paid (about $84 million) per month since July 2003.
