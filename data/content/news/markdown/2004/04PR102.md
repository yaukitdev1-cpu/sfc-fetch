The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

Overall Review

In the 13 months between 1 March 2003 and 31 March 2004 (Note 1), the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, a total of 117 applications for listing (excluding five applications for listing as private investment companies under Chapter 21 of the Listing Rules and three applications for reorganisation). There were 64 applications up to 30 September 2003, of which the SFC commented on 29 cases.

Of the 29 applications that the SFC commented on, 15 did not proceed, i.e., they were rejected by the regulators, withdrawn by the companies, or lapsed (after six months from the date of application). Eleven have been listed, and three remain outstanding.

Of the 35 applications that the SFC did not comment on, three did not proceed, 23 have been listed, and nine remain outstanding.

Sponsors

During the 13-month period ended 31 March 2004, the SFC exercised its statutory powers to inspect and require documents from three licensed corporate finance advisers in relation to their activities as sponsors. One case is being considered for possible disciplinary action.

The SFC noticed that, during this period, a few sponsor firms each filed a relatively large number of listing applications, many of which did not proceed following SFC comments. The highest number of applications made by a firm during the period was 12 (including two re-submissions after the initial applications lapsed), of which two have been listed, three did not proceed following SFC comments, and five remain outstanding. The firm has 11 licensed professional staff.

There were two sponsor firms none of the applications made by which were successful. The firms made four and three applications respectively during the period.

The SFC is concerned whether these and similar firms have sufficient resources to fulfil the professional and regulatory obligations required of them. We consulted the Dual Filing Advisory Group and believe that such situations warrant particular attention. The SFC will raise the matters with the relevant firms and take appropriate follow-up actions.

Last Quarter

As reported previously (Note 2), the SFC received a total of 19 applications for listing (excluding two applications for reorganisation) in the three months from October to December 2003, and commented on nine cases.

For the three months from January to March 2004, the SFC received 34 applications and commented on 16 cases. Our average response time was seven working days.

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
