Retail investors were discerning with respect to their investment decisions, according to the SFC’s Retail Investor Survey 2003.

Carried out in October and November 2003, the Retail Investor Survey 2003 looked into the investment behaviour of retail investors, what deterred people from investing and factors that might prompt them to invest in the future.

Out of 5,168 Hong Kong adults who answered the screening question about their investment experience, 1,541 said they had invested in one or more products in the previous two years. The participation rate was almost 30%. While we cannot directly compare this with the results of SFC’s last retail investor survey in 2001 (Note 1), we believe in general there was increased participation.

Of the 1,541 respondents, 1,010 provided further information on their investment choices and trading habits. The result indicates that Hong Kong stocks continued to overshadow other types of investment products, with a 23.8% of projected rate of participation (see Table 1).



More importantly, the Survey finds that stock investors took proper measures to protect their interests in dealing with their intermediaries. Prior to dealing, 78.2% of brokerage clients had checked the licence status of the brokerage, the account executive, or both. 82.9% of stock investors understood fees and charges of stock trading services well. On monitoring their investments, 91.6% checked both contract notes and account statements.

Stock investors were concerned about a listed company’s fundamentals before buying its stock (See Figure 1). Besides, 82.5% of stock investors conducted their own analysis.

When buying IPO shares, 36% of stock investors looked at the background and reputation of the newly listed company, 27.5% based on comments in the media and on financial information websites and 23.4% relied on the prospectus.


Most impressively, all fund investors in the survey knew a fund’s investment objectives before buying it. Furthermore, they were cognizant of fees (See Figure 2). 82.2% looked at both the offering documents and marketing materials or either one of those sources in order to make informed purchase decisions.

However, shareholder activism was still weak in Hong Kong. 91% of stock investors had never voted, whether in person, by proxy or via their intermediaries at shareholder meetings. This is mainly because they did not think minority shareholders’ votes could influence a listed company’s decisions.

Retail investors did look to advice of market professionals and media analyses in evaluating investment opportunities. Over 42% of stock investors referred to celebrity analysts’ comments and analyses of newspapers and televisions. 56.5% of fund investors followed recommendations by sales people of fund houses or distribution agents.

If money were no object, the main reason of non-investing seemed to be the lack of investment knowledge - 3% of non-investors (defined as potential investors in the Survey) indicated that they would invest in the coming six months, particularly in Hong Kong stocks and funds (excluding MPF), mainly because of market-driven (e.g. market sentiment turning good) or event-driven (e.g. saving for marriage, retirement, etc.) reasons.

About three-quarters of retail and potential investors who were aware of SFC’s investor education programmes rated the radio segments, documentary dramas on television and websites (including the SFC corporate website and the Electronic Investor Resources Centre) as most useful.

An SFC spokesman said: "The SFC strongly believes that investor education is key to investor protection. Information collected in this survey will facilitate the SFC in its future investor education work. The SFC will also conduct similar surveys in the future to keep itself abreast of investors’ education needs."

There are more details in the survey report, which is available at the SFC’s office and on the SFC website.

Notes to Editor:

1. In the Retail Investor Survey 2003, retail investors are defined as Hong Kong adults aged 18 or above who had traded investment products during the last two years, whereas the Retail Investor Survey 2001 defined retail investors as those who had traded in the previous three years. Even so, the retail participation rate in the 2001 survey was 26%.

In the 2003 Survey, investment products include stocks and derivatives (e.g. warrants, futures) traded in Hong Kong, Mainland or other foreign markets, funds (excluding MPF), bonds, leveraged foreign exchange contracts and non-listed equity-linked instruments. The 2001 Survey did not provide for a separate category of non-listed equity-linked instruments.
