The SFC is pleased to announce that its Academic and Accreditation Advisory Committee (AAAC) (Note 1) has approved courses to be conducted by the Hong Kong Securities Institute (HKSI) and the Vocational Training Council (VTC) to facilitate former practitioners re-entering the industry. Completion of the courses will serve as a new alternative to passing licensing examinations in fulfilling re-entry competence requirements (Note 2).

The approved courses are:

 

- Certificate in Securities Dealing conducted by the HKSI
- Certificate in Derivative Dealing conducted by the HKSI
- Certificate in Corporate Finance conducted by the HKSI
- Certificate in Asset Management conducted by the HKSI
- Certificate in Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading conducted by the VTC

These courses are expected to be offered in the first quarter of 2005.  Further details will be announced by the HKSI and VTC.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “By completing the re-entry courses, former practitioners will have refreshed their professional knowledge and be in a position to carry out their functions competently.  The SFC will continue to administer the licensing competence criteria pragmatically, while not compromising investor protection.”

In addition, the AAAC has recognised the Management and Executive Development Centre of the Hong Kong Polytechnic University, and the Hong Kong Capital Markets Association as Continuous Professional Training providers for SFC licensees.  This brings the total number of such recognised providers to 14 (Note 3).

Notes:

1. The AAAC comprises representatives from the academia and the industry. It advises the SFC on policy matters regarding competence and continuous professional training requirements for the industry.

2. To re-enter the industry, former practitioners who have left the industry for three to eight years are required to pass licensing examinations unless they have either: (i) completed five hours of relevant continuous professional training (CPT) for each year of absence from the industry, or (ii) attended an approved re-entry course.  Former practitioners who have left the industry for less than three years are exempted from this requirement.

3. The 14 recognised CPT providers are:
