The SFC is investigating unusual trading activities relating to the placement of shares by listed companies or their controlling shareholders. In most cases, these placements have involved the placing of shares at discounts to reference prices, very often the closing prices on the date when these placements took place. Such discounts give rise to potential market abuses.

Under common market practices, actual placement activities should only commence after the signing of a mandate between the listed company and the placing agent or agents, which usually takes place immediately after the close of the market.

However, the SFC is aware that some investment banks acting as placing agents have tested the acceptance of such placements by consulting selected clients prior to the close of the market. Matters discussed might involve the level of discount and indications of demand.

The SFC believes that mere awareness of such placement activities might constitute insider information and create an unequal market. Persons accustomed to trade in such securities might potentially profit from or avoid losses in the securities in question by acting or avoiding acting on the basis of such information.

The SFC has made inquiries in a number of cases. On aggregate, these placements raised a total of $26.26 billion from the market. The unusual activity in such cases is indicated by significant increases in market turnover, significant increases in short selling activities and deviations in the share price performance when compared to the general market conditions.

The cases that the SFC is looking into are at different stages. In some cases, the SFC has reasonable grounds to believe some institutional investors had prior knowledge of such placement activities and acted upon it. Formal investigations will soon commence.

An SFC spokesman said: "The SFC wishes to remind investment bankers of their duties to maintain market integrity, particularly a duty to maintain an equally informed market. Failure to do so might attract disciplinary action or prosecution."

"Furthermore, institutional investors or other persons receiving insider information and acting on the back of such information are also liable to prosecution under market misconduct offences or disciplinary actions by the SFC," the spokesman said.
