The SFC today publishes the [Consultation Conclusions and guidelines to address analyst conflicts of interest](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/conclusion?refNo=04CP1) (Note 1).

The guidelines aim to remove, reduce and manage conflicts. They have taken into consideration the International Organization of Securities Commissions’ (IOSCO) principles (Note 2), developments in other jurisdictions and local characteristics.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “We are mindful of the need to strike a balance between protecting investors’ interests and facilitating the flow of legitimate market information. At the same time, we need to ensure that our approach is practical for the Hong Kong market.”

In response to market feedback, the SFC has modified the consultation proposals. The scope of application is limited to listed shares, and stock warrants and options, which are the focus of the regulation. Post research issuance trading blackout is extended slightly to three days for greater investor protection. There will be safe harbours for quiet periods and trading blackouts so that the normal business of firms is not disrupted. A simpler threshold for firms to disclosure interests has been adopted for easier compliance. There is also a more practical restriction on reporting lines of analysts.

The guidelines impose stringent processes on investment banks to prevent, eliminate and disclose conflicts of interests.  For example, firms should prohibit their research analysts from taking part in sales pitches and deal road shows.  These requirements are on par with international standards.

Under the guidelines, licensed analysts need to make only simple disclosures during media appearances. However, they must disclose their names for greater accountability and transparency. This enhances protection of investors, especially retail investors who rely on analysts’ media commentaries when making investment decisions.

The guidelines will only apply to licensed or registered persons. The SFC has been careful not to fetter the free flow of information, but would like to remind all media commentators that they will continue to be subject to the market misconduct provisions of the Securities and Future Ordinance.

The SFC will work towards increasing the public’s awareness of the potential conflicts that an analyst may face and how these guidelines may serve to help.  The SFC also stands ready to assist the media in dealing with the analyst conflicts issue.

Mrs Lam said: “The guidelines seek to establish clear industry benchmarks. Together with investor education, the SFC believes that once these guidelines are in place, market forces will push towards better information flow and transparency in the market place, as well as higher professional standards in the industry.”

The guidelines will form part of the Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission. They will be gazetted and are scheduled to come into effect on 1 April 2005 to give the industry sufficient time to establish the necessary compliance systems.

The Consultation Conclusions paper (which includes the guidelines) is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website.
