**Dr Wise’s Column Special Chinese New Year Supplement**

***The Tao of Investment***

Last month, we likened investment to hiking – it’s a long-haul exercise with ups and downs. Investment is a life-long exercise and we can win or lose. But we must learn to invest wisely, because all of us have limited resources.

But investment is a process, and all processes must be learnt. There is a price to learning, including the fact that we can make losses.

There are many techniques of investment, but the basic process is quite simple:-

Know what you are buying, particularly with respect to three things:- liquidity, risk and return. Liquidity means: can you turn the product into cash straight away, or does it take you a long time to sell this asset? For example, buying a currency such as US dollars, would mean quick conversion into HK dollars. But if you invest in a fixed deposit, you cannot convert it into cash straight away. Buying real estate may mean that your cash is tied up, as an apartment cannot easily be sold for cash.

Understand the balance between risk and return. As a general rule, the higher the return you seek, the higher the risk you must be prepared to accept. For example, shares may offer higher returns than bonds but they generally carry higher risks than bonds do. Each product has its own risk characteristics.

If you do not understand, ask the experts. Most of us turn to experts for advice in everything, and choosing the right broker or banker is an important decision. A professional who gives you advice needs to understand what is your appetite for risk and your need for returns and liquidity before he can provide the right advice. Get a second opinion if you are not sure. Some people offer you free advice, but remember it is your money that you are putting at risk, not that of the person who offers that advice.

Well, if Dr Wise is so wise, why can’t I provide you with advice? The reason why a regulator cannot give advice on what product to buy and when to buy, is because we would be seen to be biased in favour of one product or a particular timing. Markets can go up and down. No-one can predict the future. Because it is your money, only you, the investor, have the prerogative to decide whether to buy or to sell. The difference between gambling and investment is rational choice. The outcome of gambling depends more on luck than skill. Good investment needs skills that a rational investor must learn.

Because the market goes up and down, the key decision after the buy decision is the decision to sell, and when. The basis of investing is to buy low and sell high. Some of my friends who are first-time investors like to buy, and do not sell. Not to sell is also a conscious decision with its own risks. When the market goes up, they become more spirited and wish to take profits later, and when it goes down, they hold these shares hoping that the price will go up again.

If the price went up because the market was good, and you did not take the profit, who bears the consequence if the price subsequently falls? If there is information that a company is doing badly for one reason or other, and the price begins to fall, it may be time to re-think your investment.

It is very difficult for anyone to give advice to the investor because only the investor understands his or her own appetite for risk. Some people like to take on more risks, while others are more conservative. But market experience tells us that markets have cycles of ups and downs. You can never buy at the lowest price and you may not be able to sell at the highest price. But not to take profits or stop a loss may be a decision that many people regret in hindsight. This is why Sun-tse said "Know yourself, know your enemy; victory will not be at risk."

Knowing your own strengths and weaknesses is not easy. This is why investment is not easily taught, but has to be learnt. I want to make it clear that I am not saying that this or any particular time is the time to take profits. Neither am I saying that buy-and-hold is necessarily a bad strategy – in fact, some smart, patient and successful long-term investors simply buy and hold good quality stocks and make profits in the long run. My job as a regulator is to remind investors to think very carefully about the risks that they are undertaking.

In the next article, I will talk about how we can manage risks better.

May I wish everyone a Happy and Prosperous Year of the Monkey.
