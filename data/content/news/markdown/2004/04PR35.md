The SFC has submitted a report to the Panel on Financial Affairs (FA Panel) of the Legislative Council on the proposals made by the SFC Working Group on Review of the Financial Regulatory Framework for Licensed Corporations.

The proposals aim to address the risks involved with securities margin financing and the pooling and re-pledging of client collateral by securities margin finance providers. The proposals would increase protection for investors, benefit the development of Hong Kong’s securities market, and strengthen Hong Kong’s position as an international financial centre by moving closer to international regulatory standards. Several measures have been recommended by the Working Group, which comprises many industry practitioners. (Note 1)

The SFC looks forward to the opportunity to explain the proposals to the FA Panel members at a Panel meeting next Monday (1 March 2004). The SFC will welcome any views of the FA Panel members on the report.

Subject to the views of the FA Panel, the SFC proposes to consult the public on the recommended measures and relevant issues, whether they are appropriate and, if so, the timetable for implementation.

The SFC is fully aware of the market response over the past two weeks to the recommended measures mentioned in the SFC press release dated 6 February 2004. The SFC maintains an open mind to any suggestions. Comments from the industry and the public will be duly considered in the consultation process.

SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, Alexa Lam, said: “We will fully consider any views of the FA Panel members before consulting the market and the public. In the meantime, we have begun discussions with the industry. We will continue our dialogue with all industry bodies. We believe we can reach a broad consensus so that the final proposals will better protect investors and still allow brokers sufficient space and flexibility to operate.”

Note:

1. For details of the proposed measures and the objective of the Working Group, please see SFC press release entitled “Measures to Enhance Regulation of the Brokerage Industry and Investor Protection” which was issued on 6 February 2004 and is available on the SFC website. In particular, please note the following:

- The purpose of the core measures is to benefit Hong Kong’s 1,000,000 investors.  These measures would also benefit the development of Hong Kong’s securities market, and strengthen Hong Kong’s position as an international financial centre.  Fair treatment and appropriate protection of investors are pre-requisites for any successful securities market and a healthy securities industry.
- Other major financial centres do not permit the re-pledging of collateral belonging to clients who have not borrowed.  They require complete segregation. US goes further by imposing restriction on the re-pledging limit on each client who has borrowed. In contrast, Hong Kong allows firms to re-pledge all client collateral, including that belonging to margin clients who have no current borrowing from the firms, for different purposes.
- The Working Group has not recommended complete segregation at this time. However, it has proposed to impose a limit on excessive re-pledging of client collateral. This means that while firms could continue to re-pledge collateral that belongs to non-borrowing margin clients, the re-pledging limit would ensure that at least a portion of client collateral would be retained by the broker so that it would be available for return to clients in the event of brokerage failure.
- The Working Group also proposes that brokers comply with new haircut percentages for brokers’ risk management and capital calculation but these percentages will still be more lenient than those adopted by banks. These haircut percentages do not stop clients from freely trading any stocks or prevent brokers from lending on any stocks. The haircut will not cause any stock to cease trading or be delisted.
- The proposed measures would impact only a handful of firms. With the market rise in the last six months, the affected firms should be able to comply with the requirements. These firms would be required to cut down their re-pledging, lend more prudently or put in their own capital. The ability of firms to freely re-pledge for any purpose will also be affected to some extent. The SFC will work with these firms.
