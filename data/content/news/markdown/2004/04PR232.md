The SFC today publishes a Consultation Paper on a Review of the Codes on Takeovers and Mergers and Share Repurchases. This consultation paper proposes various amendments to the Codes and invites public comments on these proposals.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: "The SFC believes that to ensure continued fair treatment for shareholders who are affected by takeovers and mergers it is important to keep the Codes up-to-date with market developments and international practice."

The current proposals result from a review by the SFC in consultation with the Takeovers Panel and reflect issues identified by both the SFC and market practitioners. The SFC has also considered recent changes to the London Takeovers Code where appropriate. The SFC would like to highlight a number of proposals.

Some of the proposed changes result from the recent increase in the number of hostile offers in Hong Kong. In particular, a series of "one-cent" or "low-ball" hostile offers in 2002 to 2003 gave rise to concerns that this type of offer might be used as a means to frustrate a target company’s business without any genuine intention to seek control. The paper therefore consults the public about possible changes to the Codes to clarify the circumstances in which such offers should be allowed to proceed.

In a recent hostile takeover, difficulties arose when the incumbent offeree board deliberately delayed holding a shareholders’ meeting to reconstitute the board after receipt of a valid request by the successful offeror. Whilst the board’s action did not breach the laws of the country of incorporation (in this case Bermuda where the law relating to the calling of shareholders’ meetings differs from the Hong Kong law), it effectively prevented the incoming controlling shareholder from exercising its rights of control. The paper consults the public on two options for change to address the risks to shareholders that arise from an incumbent board taking deliberate lawful action (e.g. issuing new shares or taking other action to reduce the overall value of the company) to frustrate the exercise of board control by a successful offeror.

The paper also sets out proposed changes relating to vetting of documents under the Codes. The Takeovers Executive has noted that the current vetting process (where the Executive normally follows up each comment or question raised until it is satisfied that it has been dealt with) may encourage over-reliance by issuers and their advisers on the Takeovers Executive’s input and "*clearance*" and the apparent safeguards it is perceived to provide. Given these concerns, and recognising the international trend away from pre--vetting towards enforcement, the Takeovers Executive recommends that where possible the commenting process be shortened.

The paper also consults the public about possible changes to the Codes to provide a framework for dealing with telecom mergers that are subject to review by the Telecommunications Authority under the new laws introduced in July 2004.

The public are invited to submit comments in writing before close of business on 14 January 2005. A consultation conclusions paper will be published after the end of the consultation period.
