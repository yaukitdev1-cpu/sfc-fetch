The SFC is pleased to announce that it has signed a [Letter of Intent](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/aboutsfc/cooperation/thailand_loi_0411.pdf) with Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC Thailand) on the establishment of regulatory co-operation between the SEC and the SFC.

The two regulators aim to work towards two main objectives: the mutual recognition of investment funds authorised in their respective jurisdictions for cross-border distribution and entering into a revised Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the SEC Thailand and the SFC (Note 1).

This Letter of Intent is an important step for both the SEC Thailand and the SFC in the area of cross-border regulation of funds. Under the Letter of Intent, both authorities agree to strengthen co-operation in matters pertaining to cross-border trading and the supervision of regulated funds. It is hoped that with enhanced communication and harmonisation of the respective regulatory regimes, both Hong Kong and Thailand will be able to facilitate the development of deeper and broader investment markets through the trading in each other's markets of mutually recognised funds that are acceptable to both authorities.

Both the SEC Thailand and the SFC are members of the International Organization of Securities Commissions. This Letter of Intent is signed in the spirit of mutual co-operation of member securities regulators.

The Letter of Intent was signed today by Mr Thirachai Phuvanatnaranubala, Secretary-General of the SEC Thailand, and Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC, in Bangkok, Thailand.

Both authorities have also established a bilateral working group which, in late July this year, began working towards the achievement of the objectives set out in the Letter of Intent.

Mr Sheng said: "The SFC is committed to strengthening regional financial market co-operation in order to raise the quality of the Asian markets. By signing this Letter of Intent, the SFC joins hands with the SEC Thailand in a demonstration of our mutual intention to enhance the level of regulatory co-operation between our respective markets in line with the objectives and standards of IOSCO. Our ultimate goal is to open up regional fund markets through the cross-border trading of financial products in our respective jurisdictions. This would support the ASEAN + 3 Asian Bond Market Initiative (Note 2) of developing Asian bond markets, and complement the work of regional central banks in developing the Asian Bond Fund (Note 3). As securities regulators, we will work together to remove unnecessary hurdles to cross-border trading, without compromising the level of investor protection in either of our jurisdictions."

Mr Phuvanatnaranubala said: "The signing of this Letter of Intent between the SFC and the SEC Thailand today is the very first step for Thailand in exploring the move towards the integration of our regional capital markets. From now, we will start our consultation process and explore each other’s work methods. This will not only lead us to a better understanding between our two organisations but also open up other areas where we can co-operate and assist each other. With this closer collaboration and mutual recognition of rules and standards, we hope that Hong Kong and Thailand will progressively be able to allow our domestic investment funds to finally go across our borders."

Notes to Editor:

1. The Securities and Exchange Commission of Thailand was founded under the promulgation of the Securities and Exchange Act B.E. 2535 (A.D. 1992) with the mission to develop and supervise the Thai capital market. The power and functions of the SEC Thailand include the implementation, enforcement, and administration of the Act, rules and regulations prescribed thereunder, to ensure proper disclosure of information in the public offering of securities, financial stability and proper conduct of securities companies, proper performance of various self-regulatory agencies and other institutions related to securities businesses, public compliance with takeover codes, as well as integrity and fairness of the Thai securities market. The SEC Thailand and the SFC signed a MOU of regulatory co-operation in November 1993. Both authorities aim to revise the MOU towards meeting the objectives of the IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding Concerning Consultation and Co-operation and The Exchange of Information.

2. In December 2002, the ASEAN + 3 Asian Bond Market Initiative (ABMI) was announced in Chiang Mai, Thailand. It aims to develop efficient and liquid bond markets in Asia, which would enable better utilisation of Asian savings for Asian investments. The ABMI would also contribute to the mitigation of currency and maturity mismatches in financing. The ABMI emphasises the need for joint and comprehensive set of actions by the ASEAN + 3 countries (China, Japan and Korea) in two broad areas: facilitating access to the market through a wider variety of issuers and enhancing market infrastructure to foster bond markets in Asia. For details, please see [http://www.aseansec.org/15030.htm](http://www.aseansec.org/15030.htm).

3. The Executives’ Meeting of East Asia and Pacific Central Banks (EMEAP), which comprises 11 central banks and monetary authorities in the region, announced the initial structure of the Asian Bond Fund 2 (ABF2) on 15 April 2004. ABF2 will promote the development of index bond funds in the regional markets and at the same time enhance the domestic as well as regional bond market infrastructure. The recommendation for the preliminary framework for ABF2 consists of two components: a Pan-Asian Bond Fund Index Fund and a Fund of Bond Funds, covering the local-currency bonds issued by sovereign and quasi-sovereign issuers in EMEAP countries. The EMEAP members are Australia, China, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, New Zealand, Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. For details please see [http://www.emeap.org](http://www.emeap.org).

4. The SFC signed a similar Letter of Intent with Indonesia’s Ministry of Finance Capital Market Supervisory Agency (BAPEPAM) in May this year.
