The SFC announces today that certain measures to help reduce the opportunities of theft of IPO refund cheques have been implemented, while further preventative measures are being studied.

In light of investor complaints about missing IPO refund cheques in early January, the SFC met the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), the Commercial Crime Bureau (CCB), the Immigration Department and the Federation of Share Registrars in January and February 2004 to discuss preventative measures for future IPOs. To date, three meetings have been held.

In addition to the immediate advice given by the SFC to investors (Note 1), certain preventative measures have been implemented by the share registrars in the IPOs which took place recently*.* These include:

(a) share registrars have begun to use plain, unmarked envelopes to send return refund cheques;

(b) irrespective of the value of the cheques, share registrars may entertain individual requests to collect the refund cheques in person and may entertain individual requests to use registered mail for the posting of refund cheques (but have the discretion to have parties to submit their requests in written form).

The SFC will also be hosting a sharing session with members of the Federation of Share Registrars this month to discuss how investor complaints can be better handled in the future.

Also today, the HKMA issued a circular to banks with guidance on banks’ account opening procedures when temporary HKID documents are used, and on the handling of cheque deposits for accounts newly opened by holders of temporary identity documents.

A number of measures, which require systems or process changes by the relevant parties in the IPO process, are being considered further. These suggestions are preliminarily considered to be feasible but require further discussions among the parties as well as other stakeholders involved. These are:

(a) to print the IPO shares applicant’s HKID number (or part of the HKID number) next to the applicant’s name on the refund cheque and request banks to cross-check both name and HKID number; and
(b) to consider changing the IPO application process to provide for an electronic refund instead of sending physical cheques and request banks to cross-check the account name and HKID numbers of the payee in the auto-payment process. This may require amending the IPO application forms.

The SFC has been monitoring recent IPOs to see whether there are any further cases of misappropriated refund cheques. So far, none have been reported. The SFC will continue to monitor the upcoming IPOs and liaise with the relevant parties on the measures that have not yet been implemented.
