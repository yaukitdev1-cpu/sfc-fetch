Further to the SFC’s press release of 11 October 1999, another open letter from a group of listed companies was published in the Apple Daily and Hong Kong Economic Times today. 

Listed companies, under the Listing Rules of the Stock Exchange, have duties and obligations to ensure that price-sensitive information is promptly disclosed to the public. Large movements of price or turnover of shares often indicate that there is unequal dissemination of price-sensitive information. Large changes in prices or turnover may also indicate that there is a false or misinformed market. Where that happens, the investing public may be at risk and may suffer loss because they can only trade on the basis of incorrect or incomplete information.

It is common for the listed company concerned to request suspension of trading in its securities on the Stock Exchange pending release of relevant information. Such suspensions of trading are generally for a limited period only and trading resumes once the relevant information is disseminated to the public.

In rare cases, the suspension of trading may be for a prolonged period. Generally that will only happen if a company is subject to winding-up proceedings or has no material business activity. The SFC aims to see trading in securities resume as quickly as possible and as soon as the market is properly informed.

The SFC’s objective is a transparent, fair and orderly market. The SFC discharges its duties and responsibilities without fear or favour. The SFC administers the relevant laws and regulations on an equal, consistent and fair basis. The SFC does not target small listed companies. All listed companies are treated fairly, equally and consistently. All listed companies need to comply with the same relevant laws and regulations.

Unusual movements sometimes occur because some parties have information that is not available to other investors. Unequal dissemination of price-sensitive information could lead to insider dealing, and the possible formation of a false or misinformed market for these shares. In accordance with the Listing Rules of the Stock Exchange and after discussion with the Stock Exchange and the SFC, the companies requested for their suspension respectively.

It is not the policy nor the intention of the SFC to delay the resumption of suspension. What we are concerned is that if the company concerned has important information that could affect its share price materially, both the Stock Exchange and the Commission would ensure that this information be disclosed as fully and as soon as possible, so that investors can judge for themselves the risks and rewards.

The SFC is not targetting Hi-tech shares or any other particular industry sector. The SFC is concerned about full and timely disclosures particularly after untoward movements of all listed companies irrespective of their business activities. Recent drop in prices of second and third liners are normal market adjustments after their rapid surge in prices or reacting to companies’ announcements in relation to their capital raising activities.
