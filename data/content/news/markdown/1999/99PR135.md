The Securities and Futures Commission today published a summary survey report on the business activities of securities and futures intermediaries and exempt persons during 1998. 

The 1998 survey on intermediaries is a continuation of the exercise conducted in 1996 and 1997. This is the first year that the survey has been extended to cover exempt persons.

The summary survey report includes findings on primary business activity, market share, client profile, profile on margin financing of securities, custody of client assets, human resources profile, financial performance and business development. The findings are found to be consistent with the general market environment of 1998.

Some of the major findings of the 1998 survey are as follows:

- as a result of the bearish market sentiment, gross operating income of the securities and futures intermediaries experienced a substantial drop of 32% in 1998. Securities dealers suffered the largest drop in income among the intermediaries in light of the decrease in commission received from stock broking activities;
- intermediaries controlled by United States, United Kingdom and other European countries dominated the securities and futures markets in 1998. As a whole, they attained a 70% share in the market. Hong Kong-controlled entities accounted for 15% of the total market share;
- the percentage of retail clients to institutional clients remains unchanged between 1997 and 1998 for both securities dealers and commodity dealers. However, the contribution from retail clients to the gross operating income of securities dealers had dropped by 5% in 1998. It reflects the fact that weak market sentiment generally has a greater impact on retail clients than institutional investors in securities trading. The contribution of income from retail and institutional clients remains rather stable for commodity dealers;
- an increasing number of securities and futures intermediaries have indicated interest in providing dealing or advisory services via the Internet.
Information collected in the survey enables the SFC to maintain an up to date profile of market intermediaries which is valuable to the SFC when it formulates policies catering for the needs of the industry. 

The SFC wishes to thank those intermediaries who had responded to the survey.
