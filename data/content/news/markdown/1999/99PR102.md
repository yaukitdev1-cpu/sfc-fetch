As a result of the open letter from a group of investors to the Chief Executive published in the Hong Kong Economic Journal and Hong Kong Daily News today, the SFC would like to clarify the following.

The primary function of the Commission is to ensure transparent, fair and orderly markets. When share prices have exhibited unusual movements, it is normal for the Commission and the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong to ask the management of any listed company, whose shares exhibit unusual movement to give an explanation, if available, why such movements occur.

Unusual movements sometimes occur because some parties have information that is not available to other investors. Unequal dissemination of price-sensitive information could lead to insider dealing, and the possible formation of a false or misinformed market for these shares. In accordance with the Listing Rules of the Stock Exchange and after discussion with the Stock Exchange and the SFC, the companies requested for their suspension respectively.

It is not the policy nor the intention of the SFC to delay the resumption of suspension. What we are concerned is that if the company concerned has important information that could affect its share price materially, both the Stock Exchange and the Commission would ensure that this information be disclosed as fully and as soon as possible, so that investors can judge for themselves the risks and rewards.

The SFC is not targetting Hi-tech shares or any other particular industry sector. The SFC is concerned about full and timely disclosures particularly after untoward movements of all listed companies irrespective of their business activities. Recent drop in prices of second and third liners are normal market adjustments after their rapid surge in prices or reacting to companies’ announcements in relation to their capital raising activities.
