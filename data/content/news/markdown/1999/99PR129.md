The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today published a report on the main findings in its retail investor survey carried out in September and October 1999. 

The objective of this survey is to collect information on the investment behaviour of retail investors, how they manage and safeguard their investments, and their awareness and understanding of the work of the SFC.

Some of the key findings in the report are:

- 20% of the people in Hong Kong had traded risk investment products in the past three years. Risk investment products include stocks and derivatives listed in Hong Kong and overseas markets, funds, bonds and leveraged forex contracts. 14% had traded these products in the past 12 months and 19% would trade in the coming 12 months.
- The same percentage (46%) of retail investors traded through brokerage firms and banks, and 8% traded through both.
- 91% of the retail investors had traded Hong Kong stocks in the past three years. Most of them (64%) had only cash accounts, a small portion (2%) had only margin accounts and 6% had both. However, a considerable proportion (28%) did not know whether they had a cash account or margin account.
- 1% of retail investors had traded through the Internet, but 8% said that they would definitely or very likely trade through the Internet in the coming 12 months.
- 47% of retail investors considered they had properly safeguarded their rights when making investments. 9% thought otherwise, mainly because they had not obtained adequate professional advice, nor clarified information in transaction documents, nor understood the nature and risk of the financial products invested.
- The majority of investors were aware of the SFC’s various functions and that the SFC had carried out investor education work through different channels. Investors generally wished to see strengthened enforcement work against insider dealing and market manipulation, improved transparency of listed companies and closer supervision of conduct of brokerage firms.
Responding to the survey results, the SFC spokesman said: “The SFC understands investors concerns about market malpractice, transparency issues and recognises the importance of public confidence in the work of the regulator. The SFC will continue its monitoring and supervision of the markets and their practitioners diligently to ensure compliance by all participants, as well as administering together with the Stock Exchange, a policy to seek to ensure prompt and proper disclosure of information by listed companies which is material to investors. A new sophisticated computer system “SMARTS” has recently been made operational to help to identify possible insider dealing and market manipulation activities. 

In addition, the SFC wishes to remind registrants of their duty to “know their clients” under the Code of Conduct. Review of the Code of Conduct will be undertaken in the first half of 2000 to ensure requirements therein are up to date. Moreover, as part of the SFC’s plan to revamp the licensing regime, proposals will be put forward to emphasise competency standards of all registrants.

The SFC is also committed to continue running a proactive investor education programme through different media, including the development of a web-based Investor Resources Centre, which will give investors round-the-clock access to a one-stop site to acquire knowledge about investing and obtain information about investments.”
