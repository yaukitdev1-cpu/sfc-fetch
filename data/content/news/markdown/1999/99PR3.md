The Securities and Futures Commission today presented the main features of the proposed regulatory framework for share margin financing at a meeting of the Legislative Council Panel on Financial Affairs.

The SFC was represented by Deputy Chairman and Executive Director, Mrs Laura Cha, and Executive Director (Intermediaries and Investment Products), Mr Andrew Procter. They were attending the meeting together with Deputy Secretary for Financial Services, Mrs Rebecca Lai, who led the inter-agency working group to study regulation of share margin activities.

The proposed regulatory regime would bring share margin finance activities into the purview of the Securities Ordinance and subject them to the supervision of the SFC.

Mrs Cha said the present proposals represented a balance between investor protection and the need for margin financing operations to remain commercially viable.

The Financial Resources Rules (FRRs), which set the minimum prudential standards on the financial resources of SFC-regulated intermediaries, would be extended to share margin finance providers (SMFPs) and strengthened to reflect the nature of those activities.

The features of the proposed regulatory regime, including legislative changes and amendments to the FRRs, include the following:

- Sole business requirement for SMFPs;
- Start up capital requirement of $10 million;
- SMFPs to hold at all times minimum liquid capital of $3 million or 5% of liabilities, whichever is higher;
- Additional liquid assets to be held in case of concentration of risk to a stock or to a client;
- Client assets to be safeguarded – money in trust account within four bank days;
- Written authorisation from clients required for pooling;
- Enhanced client disclosure – next day report, and dual language and enhanced risk disclosure in contract;
- Regular reporting to SFC about margin book and bank lines and special reporting in respect of unusual events;
- Changes to the Code of Conduct to reflect margin finance business;
- SFC power to appoint auditor where there is failure to account to client for money or securities;
- SFC power to directly inspect, monitor and discipline; and
- Enhanced investor education, including on risks involved in authorising pooling.
The Government intends to present the proposed legislation to the Legislative Council in February. It is hoped that the new framework can be put in place in the middle of this year.

Speaking at a media briefing this afternoon, Mr Procter said the SFC had noted from recent analysis some areas of improvement in share margin financing.

The analysis was based on information of margin finance activities carried out by members of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and their associated but unregulated finance companies as at late last year.

Mr Procter said the number of margin clients had fallen by 55% to 14,800, from 33,000 in December 1997.

The indebtedness of margin financiers to Authorised Institutions had dropped to $3 billion, from $10.1 billion in December 1997. Margin loans outstanding had been more than halved during the period to $12.3 billion from $27.6 billion.

Mr Procter added that despite the improvement, undercollateralised loans and margin shortfall at some finance providers remained a serious issue which had yet to be resolved, and those continued problems highlighted the need for legislative reform.

Therefore, the SFC would continue its close watch on the margin loan positions of the registered intermediaries and their associated finance companies, he said.

In addition, a further survey of the industry will be conducted at the end of this month.
