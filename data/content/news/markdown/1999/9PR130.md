The SFC announced today that it had publicly reprimanded**:**

- {X1} and
- {X2}.
The decision to reprimand {X1} and {X2} stemmed from an investigation into events in early to mid 1993. At the time of these events, {X2} was the sole proprietor of Kwok Hing Securities Company (Kwok Hing) and {X1} was the manager of that business. As a result of the investigation, the SFC found that:

- in mid 1993, {X1} handled assets belonging to a deceased client of Kwok Hing in a manner that created a conflict of interest for both himself and {X2};
- {X2} failed to supervise his business allowing the assets involved to be misapplied and the conflict of interest to arise; and
- the relationship between {X2} and {X1} and the deceased client was insufficiently documented.
The SFC concluded that the actions of {X1} and {X2} respectively impugned the fitness and properness of each to be registered.

In imposing these penalties upon {X2} and {X1}, the SFC has taken into account that:

- {X1} has surrendered his registration as a dealer and will not reapply for registration for 1 year and then only as a dealer’s representative;
- both {X2} and {X1} have expressed remorse for their conduct which occurred some 6 years ago;
- {X2} has incorporated Kwok Hing and since taken steps to improve Kwok Hing’s compliance and internal control systems; and
- {X2} has undertaken to the SFC to appoint an independent accountant to review Kwok Hing’s compliance and internal control systems and implement recommendations made.
Paul Bailey, the SFC’s Executive Director of Enforcement, said that the SFC viewed conflicts of interest very seriously. He said that, if similar conduct occurred without the mitigating circumstances of this particular case it would be dealt with more severely. He advised registered persons to properly document their client relationships to protect both their clients and themselves.
