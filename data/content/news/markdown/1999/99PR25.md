The Securities and Futures Commission has begun a vigorous inspection and surveillance exercise on the Year 2000 compliance status of intermediaries to ensure that the interests of investors are safeguarded.

According to quarterly progress reports of securities and futures intermediaries, 60.2% of them (651 firms) said at the end of last year that their mission critical systems were Year 2000 compliant (please see note). Another 34.3% (371 firms) expected to have completed their testing by the end of this month.

SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, Mr Andrew Procter, said registrants directly supervised by the SFC had been required to complete internal testing for mission critical systems by 31 December 1998. Firms having failed to meet this key milestone would be subjected to closer supervision.

The SFC has adopted a risk-based approach in targeting registrants for on-site inspection and telephone review. The failure to meet key milestone dates is one of the criteria in risk targeting. The other, more important, factors are client interest and asset protection and whether the failure of an intermediary would cause any systemic risks to the market.

In connection with asset protection, the SFC notes that the majority of the intermediaries that are under the SFC's direct supervision and failed to meet the above milestone are investment advisers who do not handle client assets. Nevertheless, the SFC is closely monitoring the progress of these firms.

Mr Procter said the SFC had put in place a more intensive inspection and surveillance programme to review intermediaries’ testing and contingency planning. This includes the formation of a special Year 2000 review team comprising seven staff members.

The SFC notes that the overall 60.2% compliance in the sector was slightly lower than the 64% recorded last year based on September 1998 progress reports. This is partly because some firms had taken a more prudent approach and decided not to report their systems as fully Year 2000 compliant as they continued their testing and improvement.

This also highlights the importance the SFC has placed on requiring registered firms to also devise comprehensive contingency plans. The milestone date for a validated contingency plan is 30 June 1999. Firms are required to update the contingency plan to cater for any changing circumstances as an ongoing exercise.

Client protection is of prime importance. As part of contingency planning, management is expected to have adequate backup data and records available to identify and return client assets in an orderly and speedy manner should operations be disrupted.

Mr Procter said: "The SFC and the exchanges are determined to take actions against registered intermediaries whose failure to resolve the Year 2000 issue may pose threats to investors and the market. Actions may include the appointment of external consultants, restriction of business and suspension or revocation of registration or membership."

The SFC and the exchanges are considering the best way to impose certain disclosure requirements on intermediaries who have been objectively identified as having failed to make adequate progress.

Many members of the exchanges participated in the first round of street-wide testing in January 1999 and the assessment of their readiness should take into account the testing results, which are still being analysed. Joint inspections on these members by the SFC with either of the two exchanges may begin in April pending the results of joint risk assessments with the exchanges.

A second round of street-wide test, which allows participants to verify the readiness status of their business operations and back office systems that interface with the exchanges and clearing houses, will start on 20 March 1999.

Note: The compliance rates of completing internal testing for mission critical systems by 31 December 1998 for exchange and non-exchange members were 65% and 54%, respectively.
