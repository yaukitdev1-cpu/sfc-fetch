The Securities and Futures Commission today issued a consultation paper seeking public comment on the proposals to amend the existing laws and practices governing the licensing of intermediaries providing services in respect of securities, futures or leveraged foreign exchange. The consultation is being undertaken in the context of the legislative reform programme to consolidate the Ordinances that the SFC currently administers.

Proposals upon which public and industry comment is sought include:

- Issuance of a single licence to investment intermediaries, specifying the scope of permitted business;
- New legislation to re-define the activities for which a licence is required;
- Power for the Commission to issue provisional licences to representative applicants with a view to saving time and costs;
- Measures by the Commission and the Exchanges to harmonise their licensing process;
- A requirement that all who are able to exercise a significant influence over the conduct of a licenced entities and those who are directly responsible for the management and supervision of the operations of a licensed corporation, including all executive directors, be licensed and designated as responsible officers who must satisfy additional licensing criteria;
- A requirement that licensed corporations be supervised by at least two responsible officers, including one executive director;
- A limitation that only corporations will be licensed to carry out regulated functions;
- Continuation of exempt status for persons who deal solely with professionals, but subject to a new requirement that they should be subject to reporting and certain Code of Conduct requirements;
- A provision that investment contracts made with an unlicensed intermediary be voidable at the client's option;
- Scope for the Commission to authorise a person to perform a licensed function to a limited extent;
- A requirement that a licensed corporation engage only those who have proper credentials to be senior officers directly responsible for the performance of the key internal control functions of the corporation;
- The recognition of approved industry courses as prerequisites for obtaining a licence as a representative or responsible officer;
- Power for the Commission to specify competence standards for responsible officers;
- A continuous training requirement to be included as an on-going obligation for licensed persons;
- A requirement that licensed corporations have a training policy to provide continuous training to accredited representatives;
- Power for the Commission to issue orders against persons who are not fit and proper banning them from participation in the industry;
- A substantial revision of existing criteria for the granting of exempt status;
- Exempt status to be limited to Authorised Institutions only;
- The extension of the Commission' power of inquiry to cover exempt persons; and
- Clarification of the Commission' attitude to the dealing or advisory activities performed by lawyers and professional accountants, that are incidental to their ordinary business and the carrying out of which does not require a licence.
Mr. Andrew Procter, Executive Director of the SFC's Intermediaries and Investment Products Division said, "The proposals follow a review of the licensing requirements for the securities, futures and leveraged foreign exchange industries in Hong Kong. The proposals reflect twin objectives of business facilitation and investor protection. The market place is changing, new products are being offered, and new services are being delivered. This review is part of the SFC's efforts to encourage those new developments whilst maintaining Hong Kong's reputation for good regulation. The changes should also reduce cost to industry."
