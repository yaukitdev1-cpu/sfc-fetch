Subsequent to its last meeting in Paris in September, the Financial Stability Forum (FSF) has formed and issued terms of reference for a new task force on implementation of international standards.

The FSF was initiated by G7 Finance Ministers and Central Bank Governors in February this year and expanded later in June to include four non-G7 financial centres, namely, Hong Kong, Australia, Singapore, and Netherlands. The FSF aims to promote international financial stability through enhanced information exchange and international co-operation in financial market supervision and surveillance.

The task force is chaired by Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman, Securities and Futures Commission and has been asked to examine ways of fostering the implementation of international standards relevant to the strengthening of financial systems. The task force will consider official and market incentives to encourage implementation, explore strategies for assisting countries in the practical implementation of standards, and evaluate options for generating information on progress in implementing standards.

The task force comprises officials from Australia, China, France, India, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Netherlands, South Africa, UK and US and officials from international institutions and supervisory groups such as Basle Committee on Bank Supervision, CGFS(Committee on the Global Financial System), CPSS(Committee on Payment and Settlement Systems), IAIS(International Association of Insurance Supervisors), IMF(International Monetary Fund), IOSCO(International Organisation of Securities Commissions), OECD(Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development) and the World Bank. The task force will draw on work completed or under way in various public and private sector forums.

The terms of reference for the task force, and further information about the Financial Stability Forum, are available on the Forum’s website ([www.fsforum.org](http://www.fsforum.org)).
