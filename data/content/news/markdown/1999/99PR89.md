The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has today posted on its web-site a note on "Issue of Advertisements by Intermediaries" (Note) reminding finance houses to adhere to the relevant legal and regulatory obligations while offering trading incentives to attract customers.

"While it is the SFC's task to facilitate the market to innovate and become more competitive, we must also ensure that investors are being protected, " said Mr Andrew Procter, the Executive Director of SFC.

In general, information including advertisement given out to induce the sale and purchase of securities should not contain false or misleading information, added Mr Procter.

The Note reminds intermediaries of certain relevant legal and regulatory requirements. It also stated that marketing materials should not be false, biased, misleading or deceptive and appropriate statements are required to be included where appropriate.

Intermediaries offering promotional schemes organised in conjunction with their affiliates are particularly reminded to observe the licensing requirements before introducing the schemes to the market.

Certain incentive schemes offering remuneration to any member of the public who could successfully introduce clients to the intermediary is unacceptable to the SFC. It could potentially result in the introducing party engaging in unregistered dealing activities and the finance house aiding and abetting such offence. Moreover clients so introduced may not receive the appropriate protection afforded by the regulatory regime.

Intermediaries are advised to review the Note which is posted under the 'What's New' item of the SFC Web-site at [http://www.hksfc.org.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/). They are also welcomed to call the Licensing Hotline at 2840 9393 on any queries encountered.

***ISSUE OF ADVERTISEMENTS BY INTERMEDIARIES***


***Background***

The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has taken note of a recent trend in promotional activities undertaken by financial houses whereby trading incentives, often in the form of discounts or coupons for the purchase of goods or services rendered by affiliated companies, are offered to clients.

While the SFC’s welcome legitimate business promotional and lawful competition, it is concerned that some of the incentive schemes may have ventured beyond the limits of acceptable practices.

The SFC thus advises intermediaries to adhere to relevant legal and regulatory obligations while conducting promotional campaigns.

Legal and Regulatory Obligations

1. ***Advertisement******1******:*** 

- *Section 138 of the Securities Ordinance - prohibits a person from making any statement which contains* ***false or misleading*** *information in any material fact with a view to inducing the sale of securities.*
- *Section 64 of the Commodities Trading Ordinance - prohibits a person from making any statement containing* ***false or misleading**** information in any material fact for the purposes of inducing the purchase or sale of a futures contract.*
- *Section 18 of the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (Books, Contract Notes and Conduct of Business) Rules - traders licensed under the Ordinance must ensure that any advertisement issued in relation to their businesses is* ***not misleading or deceptive**** and that the advertisement contains a* ***prescribed risk disclosure statement.***
- *Code of Conduct - the Code requires adherence to certain fundamental principles in the conduct of business. These fundamental principles oblige registered persons to act* ***honestly, fairly**** and* ***in the best interests of their clients****. They are expected to make adequate disclosure of relevant material information to clients.*
- *The Conduct of Business Guidelines for license holders under the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading Ordinance contains similar fundamental principles.*
*Failure by a registered or licensed person to comply with these fundamental principles in the conduct of business including advertising or other promotional activities may cast doubt on that person’s fitness and properness to remain registered and may lead to disciplinary action.*

A registered person who is a member of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited ("SEHK"):

- *must comply with the Advertising Regulations set out in the 12**th** Schedule to the SEHK Rules which prohibits an invitation or advertisement that contain* ***false, biased, misleading or deceptive**** information.*
- *A* ***risk disclosure statement**** that the price of shares may fluctuate must be included in the advertisement.*
*In respect of members of the Hong Kong Futures Exchange Limited ("HKFE"):*

- *pursuant to Rule 523 of the HKFE, members are required to seek* ***prior written approval**** from the HKFE before any invitation or advertisement in relation to trading in futures contracts can be published, circulated or distributed.*
- *Rule 523 stresses that any advertisement must comply with the Commodities Trading Ordinance or any other regulatory requirements from time to time prevailing.*
*The SFC has also published two product-specific advertising guidelines:*

- *Advertising Guidelines for Collective Investment Schemes; and*
- *Advertising Guidelines for Pooled Retirement Funds*
*These guidelines are premised on the general principle that no advertisement should be* ***false, biased, misleading or deceptive.*** *Appropriate* ***warning statements*** *are required to be included in the advertisement.*

2. ***Incentive Schemes*** 


*The implementation of incentive schemes, whereby any member of the public who could successfully introduce clients to the intermediary would be remunerated, could potentially result in the introducing party engaging in unregistered dealing activities by virtue of section 3(1A) of the Securities Ordinance, and the finance house aiding and abetting such offence. In the SFC’s view, this is clearly unacceptable. The clients so introduced may not receive the appropriate protection afforded by the regulatory regime. In addition the introducing party may not be fit and proper to carry out that function.*

3. ***Promotional Schemes*** 


*The SFC would also wish to remind intermediaries that in relation to promotional schemes organised in conjunction with their affiliates, care should be exercised in observing the licensing requirements. In particular, their attention is drawn to section 48 of the Securities Ordinance which prohibits a person from carrying on the business of dealing in securities without a valid registration. "Dealing in securities" has been defined to include* ***inducing or attempting to induce**** any other person to enter into an agreement for or with a view to trade in securities.*

Note 1:

For the purposes of this paper, advertisement includes every form of advertising, whether notified or published–

1. *in a newspaper, magazine, journal, or other periodical publication;*
2. *by display of posters or notices*
3. *by means circulars, brochures, pamphlets, or handbills;*
4. *by an exhibition of photographs or cinematograph films; or*
5. *by way of sound broadcasting or television.*
