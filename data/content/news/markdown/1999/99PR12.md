The Securities and Futures Commission has updated its investor booklets and made them more widely available to the public.

First launched in 1996, the booklets now contains additional advice to investors taking into account recent market developments and practice, and rule changes.

As part of SFC's investor education programme, the SFC has produced free investor publications. The booklets, in Chinese and English, discuss the importance of "doing your homework" when selecting an investment or choosing a broker, basic issues in stock and fund investments, rights of shareholders and fund investors, and what the SFC can do to help investors who encounter market malpractice. The titles of these brochures are:

1. *Your First Moves Towards Investing*
2. *Choosing Your Broker or Financial Adviser*
3. *The Importance of Monitoring Your Investments*
4. *When to Pursue a Complaint*
5. *What Every Stock Investor Should Know*
6. *What Every Fund Investor Should Know*


The first four booklets were launched in August 1996, and the other two in December 1997. All investor publications have been highly popular, with distribution so far of more than 200,000 copies.

The second edition updates the first four booklets, which have expanded coverage in areas such as:

- Margin accounts: for example, investors are reminded that their written authority to a broker to use their securities as collateral to borrow money from a bank should only be valid for up to a year, and should be renewed in writing upon expiry. Investors are also reminded that if a margin loan comes from a finance company, they will usually have to sign a separate margin agreement with the finance company instead of the broker. Investors must seek full explanation of the agreement and find out how the assets may be used by the finance company;
- Custody of stocks, including the use of investor account in the Central Clearing and Settlement System (CCASS);
- Trading of derivative warrants and the risks involved; and
- Danger signals that investors should look out for when investing: for example, investors must be cautious of brokers or financial advisers who agree to share in any investment losses, and unusual changes in settlement method.
In order to make the booklets more accessible to the public, the SFC has widened its distribution network to include more than 30 other locations of public libraries and Hong Kong Housing Society estate management offices.

The revised booklets are immediately available at the SFC, the Investor Service Centre and Depository Service Counters Hall of Hongkong Clearing and most of the new locations, and will soon be available at the other distribution venues including all 16 Consumer Council centres.

Investors who wish to find out the nearest location to pick up the second edition booklets may call the SFC Investor Hotline at 2840-9333.
