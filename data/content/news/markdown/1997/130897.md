The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today announces a review of the Code on Investment-linked Assurance and Pooled Retirement Funds in the light of recent developments in the markets for these product types.

The suggested revisions aim mainly to reinforce and formalize current treatment and interpretation, and are house-keeping in nature. Certain new provisions, mainly for pooled retirement funds, have been introduced to cater for the increasing proportion of schemes set up under trust - as opposed to under a contract of insurance - in anticipation of the introduction of the Mandatory Provident Fund.

As part of the review and to facilitate the authorisation process for new products, the SFC proposes to split the Code into two - one for *investment-linked assurance schemes and savings plans* and another for *pooled retirement funds*.

The main proposed changes are:

- *Structure of the codes* - the new codes are divided into parts which clearly set out the requirements prior to authorisation and the ongoing obligations after authorisation.
- *New provisions to formalize current practice regarding pooled retirement funds* - a new requirement that where a scheme is set up under trust, an experienced management company must be appointed; and a provision that investment restrictions should generally be in line with those under the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds.
- *Improved disclosure for guaranteed funds* - Greater transparency of these products should help potential investors to make more informed decisions.
- *Rebates* - provisions are proposed on cash and soft dollar rebates to conform with the industry practices for other collective investment schemes.
The draft codes are published for public consultation until 30 September 1997. Copies of the codes are available at the SFC. Written comments can be sent in for the attention of Mr Harold Ko, Senior Manager, Investment Products Division, Securities and Futures Commission.
