The Securities and Futures Commission’s (SFC) eighth annual report, for the year ended 31 March 1997, was tabled before the Legislative Council today.

SFC Chairman Anthony Neoh commented, "During the past year, the Commission’s work has been devoted to the three-pronged strategy set out in our 1996-1999 Corporate Plan, focusing on: competitive markets and competitive regulation, development of the China dimension and development of market integrity. I’m pleased to say that we’ve made great strides in each of these areas, further strengthening Hong Kong’s status as an international financial centre as it approaches the return to Chinese sovereignty on 1 July 1997."

During the year under review, the SFC undertook two major initiatives aimed at keeping Hong Kong’s markets competitive. The first involved the publication of the Draft Securities and Futures Bill, which involved extensive public consultation. The Bill, which consolidates and updates Hong Kong’s existing securities legislation, promises to provide a vastly more convenient and efficient reference for industry participants, establishing a modern framework upon which future innovations can be grafted.

The second major initiative was the market consultation on, and subsequent issue of, Internal Control guidelines. Good internal controls are good business, as a number of high-profile international cases, such as Barings and Sumitomo, have demonstrated. The SFC is confident that these guidelines will assist in enhancing market integrity as well as the professionalism of market practitioners.

Reviews of the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading regulatory system, the Financial Resources Rules, and the reporting system for OTC derivatives activities were also completed during the year, all involving market consultation, to further ensure that Hong Kong’s regulatory system is international in outlook and application.

The SFC has also worked with the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and the Hong Kong Futures Exchange to develop the regulatory framework for new products while improving disclosure and risk management.

The SFC remains committed to developing the China dimension of its work by improving Hong Kong’s position as the principal market for capital formation by China outside of the mainland. The growing number of both H-share and red-chip companies consolidates Hong Kong’s position in this regard. Overseeing this growth, the SFC has worked closely with the China Securities Regulatory Commission and other mainland financial regulators to ensure that mainland-capitalised intermediaries and companies comply with both Hong Kong and mainland regulatory requirements.

The internationalisation of our markets has necessitated joint efforts among regulators worldwide. During the year, the SFC signed four Memoranda of Understanding with other regulators, bringing the total number of such agreements to 24. The SFC also continues to take an active role in the formulation of international regulatory policy through its participation in the International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO). In September 1996, Mr Neoh was elected to serve a two-year term as Chairman of the Technical Committee of IOSCO.

As confidence in the markets requires knowledgeable investors, the SFC expanded the Investor Education Programme it introduced last year. Two television series, a public television advertisement, and a set of brochures aimed at helping the public "invest wisely" have been enthusiastically received by investors and the media.

To enhance market integrity, which remains the cornerstone of confidence in our markets, the SFC conducted 350 focused inspections during the year, taking the opportunity to assist and counsel management in compliance. Complementing good compliance and supervision is determined enforcement action, enhanced by the Commission’s improved surveillance and detection ability. In all its 376 enforcement cases this year, the SFC has sought to take a remedial approach, ensuring that firms and individuals who breach regulations implement procedures to prevent recurrences.

Looking to the future, Mr. Neoh said, "As we enter the era of the Special Administrative Region of Hong Kong, we enjoy the confidence of international and domestic investors. Nevertheless, we must work to retain Hong Kong’s competitiveness and global orientation. To that end, the SFC will continue to focus its efforts on maintaining market integrity, forging international standards of regulation, and taking full advantage of its position as China’s premier capital formation centre."
