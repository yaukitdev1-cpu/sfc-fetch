The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today issues a new Fund Manager Code of Conduct (FMCC), which was developed in cooperation with Hong Kong's fund management industry, and follows a period of public consultation.

The FMCC has two main objectives. First, it supplements existing codes and guidelines by clarifying SFC expectations regarding certain requirements for fund managers. Second, it highlights legislative and other requirements applicable to fund managers, consolidating references from some 14 pieces of legislation, codes and guidelines. In doing so, the FMCC aims to assist compliance officers in keeping track of relevant requirements related to their businesses.

"The Fund Manager Code of Conduct not only enhances Hong Kong's reputation as a well-regulated international fund management centre but also demonstrates the fund industry's commitment to international best practice," said Deborah Glass, the SFC's Senior Director for Investment Products. "We appreciate the hard work and support of the Hong Kong Investment Funds Association (HKIFA), compliance officers, and the industry itself in developing this Code."

By the close of the consultation period, which began in September 1997, the SFC had received 19 submissions from fund managers, accountants and professional associations. The overall feedback was extremely positive; compliance officers especially felt the FMCC was a valuable contribution to the regulatory framework.

The industry's views on various issues covered in the FMCC, and the SFC decisions regarding these issues, are as follows:

- *Functional Separation and Segregation of Duties*
The responses made clear that small and large fund houses have different ways of ensuring proper segregation, and some amendments have been made to the Code reflecting these views. Nevertheless, the Code avoids spelling out compliance structures in detail, providing a flexible framework within which firms can structure their procedures according to individual circumstances, including size. 

- *Compliance Arrangements and Responsibility*
A number of comments were made seeking clarification of compliance responsibilities, obligations of senior management, and delegation of responsibilities, and amendments have been made accordingly. The concept of Designated Compliance Officer was generally supported by the industry, and has been retained in the Code. The SFC is also of the view that the FMCC encourages, but does not mandate, central dealing. 

The Code, moreover, states clearly that the responsibility for compliance, as well as the development of a compliance culture within the firm, rests ultimately with a firm's senior management.

- *Personal Account Dealing Rules*
Comment was sought during the consultation as to whether the HKIFA's more stringent guidelines on personal account dealing (from which the FMCC's rules were derived) should be adopted or whether more flexibility should be introduced. Commentators were overwhelmingly in favor of retaining detailed rules, including specific black-out periods (the number of days when personal account deals should be prohibited). Taking these comments into account, the SFC has reduced the blackout period from five days to one day, but subject to the over-riding principles of client priority and avoidance of conflict. While this is more flexible than the HKIFA guideline, it is necessary for the SFC to take into account different forms of international best practice, especially as the rules will be mandatory. 

While many comments were submitted regarding the scope and application of these rules (e.g. the types of investments and persons which should be covered), the SFC has avoided narrowing the guidelines, opting for minimum standards which leave firms free to widen the scope as they see fit.

- *Other Matters*
The FMCC also reflects industry comments regarding, and clarifies the guidelines for, cross trades, house trades, order allocation, participation in initial public offerings, and custody arrangements.

Copies of the FMCC and Consultation Conclusions are available at the SFC. The English version of the Consultation Conclusions can also be found on the SFC website at http: //www.hksfc.org.hk.
