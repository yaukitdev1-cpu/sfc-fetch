The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) announced today that it had successfully prosecuted T.P.S. Investment Limited (TPSIL) and {X}, as a director of TPSIL, for unregistered dealing in securities; and Mr Mui Au Nin (Mui) for aiding and abetting the unregistered securities dealing of TPSIL and Mrs Wong Chui Bik Wah (Wong).


An investigation by the SFC found that, from 1 May 1995 to 15 February 1996, TPSIL had entered into margin agreements with 87 persons (the margin clients), according to which it agreed to act as an agent of the margin clients to buy and sell securities. It was found that TPSIL had placed orders to Mui, trading as Tim Po Securities Company (Tim Po), to buy and sell securities on behalf of those clients.


The investigation also found that Wong had introduced 44 persons to TPSIL to enter into margin agreements and that she had received orders from such clients to buy or sell securities and relayed those orders to staff of Tim Po for execution during the period 1 May 1995 to 4 January 1996. TPSIL then paid remuneration to Wong on the basis of the trading volume of her clients. It was found that Mui had provided Wong with a working desk, teletext machine and telephone line at the premises of Tim Po, as well as name cards describing her as a representative of TPSIL. According to the records of the SFC, Mui had applied in 1994 for Wong to be a representative of Tim Po but had subsequently withdrawn her application.


TPSIL, {X} and Mui all pleaded guilty before Mr Selwyn Au at San Po Kong Magistracy on 20 October 1997. All the charges involved breaches of Section 48 of the Securities Ordinance. On 21 October 1997, TPSIL was fined $7,500, {X} was fined $7,500 and Mui was fined $ 30,000. They were ordered to pay costs totalling $46,000 to the SFC.


Wong was convicted on 10 March 1997, of unregistered securities dealing and theft of cash and share scrip valued at $4.3 million from clients of TPSIL, and was sentenced to 30 months imprisonment.


Mark Dickens, Executive Director, Enforcement, of the SFC, commenting on the prosecution said, "members of the broking community should take note of these prosecutions as they show the importance of ensuring that the activities of the finance arms of broking firms do not amount to unregistered dealing. Such activities deny members of the investing public the protections afforded by having their dealings executed directly through registered entities which are Exchange members."
