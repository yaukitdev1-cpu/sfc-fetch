The SFC today announces the release of the third edition of the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds, following a "housekeeping" review carried out earlier this year. The revised Code clarifies existing policies, and also includes new provisions designed to further strengthen the regulatory system for fund management and to protect investors.

Since 1991, the Code has been amended on a regular basis in response to market developments. However, given the pace of change since 1991, the SFC believed it was time for a more comprehensive review and therefore consulted the market about necessary amendments earlier this year.

The suggested revisions, for the most part, clarify current policies and procedures. However, there are several new provisions, as set out in the attachment. In summary, the major changes are:

- new Code structure , to facilitate the authorisation process;
- inclusion of existing practices and precedents, e.g. procedural requirements for fund merger/ liquidation;
- new requirement that trustees be subject to supervision;
- new provision to clarify policy on pricing errors;
- clarification of some investment restrictions;
- expansion of list of "recognised jurisdiction schemes" to include France, Germany and the US; new appendix listing "inspection regimes";
- advertising guidelines now included as an appendix, with simplification of warning statements.
"In reviewing the Code, consideration was given to existing practices, ease of understanding and industry feedback," said Deborah Glass, Senior Director of the SFC's Investment Products Division. "We have also taken into account developments overseas to ensure that Hong Kong stays at the forefront of best international practice."

"The underlying spirit of the Code continues to be core standards for investor protection, especially the appropriate supervision of all parties, whether in Hong Kong or overseas. In keeping with Hong Kong's open market policies, the Code not only remains open to properly regulated overseas funds, but also expands the list of overseas countries to give Hong Kong investors even greater choice."

The industry has been consulted on several occasions regarding the Code revisions, and overall feedback has been supportive of the changes.

ATTACHMENT:

EXPLANATION OF CHANGES


*Structure of the Code*

The Code is now divided into three parts: General Matters, Authorisation Requirements, and Post-Authorisation Requirements. This should facilitate the authorisation process, as it is now clearer what is required prior to authorisation, and what the ongoing obligations are in connection with authorisation.

 

Regulation of Trustees/Custodians

Questions about the role and regulation of trust companies have been increasing in recent years, both overseas and in Hong Kong. The new provisions are in part a reflection of problems noted overseas, but were also adopted in view of the anticipated Mandatory Provident Fund, in which trustees are to play an important supervisory role.

An industry working group on this issue agreed that trust companies should be subject to some form of ongoing supervision, either by a regulatory authority or by way of a periodic internal controls audit (see: explanatory note to 4.1 of the Code).

 

Revision of investment restrictions

- Section 7.1 of the Code regarding 10% restriction on a single issuer is clarified so that the limit applies separately to issuers of covered warrants and other issuers of underlying securities, provided that the exercise of the rights of convertibility of those instruments would not result in a breach of the Code.
- Section 7.2 of the Code regarding 10% restriction on any class of security issued by any single issuer has been revised for greater consistency with European Community rules, where the majority of funds are domiciled.
- A new Section 7.3A has also been added to take into account the requirements for investment in wholly-owned subsidiaries under the scheme.
- Section 7.13 has been revised to incorporate existing requirements of feeder funds.
 

Offering document

Amended requirements highlight the importance of a short, simple Hong Kong offering document in both English and Chinese. In addition, the provisions referring to English and Chinese have been amended to place both languages on an equal footing.

 

De-authorisation, Mergers and Liquidation

Existing policies in relation to de-authorisation, mergers and liquidation of funds, such as requirements for adequate notice to investors (11.4 and 11.5) have now been included.

 

Pricing Errors

A new provision (10.2) has been included to clarify SFC policy on pricing errors. This will require errors resulting in an incorrect price of 0.5% or more of the scheme's net asset value per unit/share to be reported to the trustee and the SFC, and compensation paid to individual investors whose loss is HK$100 or more. This is consistent with existing best practice in Hong Kong, as well as both UK and US requirements.

 

New Appendices

Expanded List of Recognised Jurisdictions

The list of recognised jurisdiction schemes in Appendix A1 has been updated and expanded, as recommended by industry feedback. "Recognition" is granted on the basis that such schemes are subject to regulations comparable to those in Hong Kong, with arrangements for exchange of information and assistance from the overseas regulator. The new list includes US, French, and German funds, and more may be considered in future. The SFC has already authorised funds from all of these jurisdictions.

 

New List of Acceptable Inspection Regimes

The Code requires that investment management operations of a fund manager be based in a jurisdiction with an inspection regime acceptable to the SFC . A new Appendix A2 has been included in the Code to set out both the criteria for determining acceptability and a non-exhaustive list of inspection regimes. This list currently includes the countries with which the SFC already has a formal Declaration of Co-operation regarding investment management (the US, UK, France, Ireland and Luxembourg.) Others may be added in future.

 

New Appendix F

A new Appendix F has been added to formally incorporate the Advertising Guidelines into the Code. Minor changes have been made for clarification. The warning statement provisions are now included in the Appendix, and have been simplified.
