The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) and the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited (the Exchange) today issue a joint consultation paper on offering mechanisms. The public is invited to express views or comment on the recommendations and proposals set out in the paper which is the result of a review conducted by the SFC and the Exchange following the issuance in November 1994 of the Joint Policy Statement on Offering Mechanisms.

In 1994, the SFC and the Exchange accepted that global equity offerings which involved the listing of large scale new issues on the Exchange raising funds from the domestic and international markets could be conducted by the simultaneous offer of shares in Hong Kong by way of the traditional public subscription method (the public subscription tranche) and the placing of shares to international investors by using the book-building method (the placing tranche).

Mrs Laura Cha, Executive Director of the SFC, said: "Since the issue of the Joint Policy Statement in 1994, the SFC has held discussions with market participants to better understand how the two offering mechanisms have interfaced in practice. In the past two years, the SFC has also reviewed underwriters’ records relating to the placing tranche of each global offer. Our study has highlighted certain policy issues that the Hong Kong market needs to further consider. We are now putting forward these issues for consideration and discussion by the market."

Mr Lawrence Fok, Executive Director, Listing Division of the Exchange, said: "The SFC and the Stock Exchange consider that providing Hong Kong investors with access to new issues continues to be one of our priorities. We are also aware of the importance of facilitating the efficient distribution of Hong Kong securities to global markets and international investors. In this context, maintaining a proper balance between the shares available for investors under the public subscription and placing tranches is an important policy issue. We would very much like to hear the public’s views on this."

In the consultation paper, comments are sought on eight principal areas including the following:

Minimum size for the public subscription tranche

- The SFC and the Exchange recommend that the initial size of the subscription tranche of an issue be set at a minimum of 10% of the total issue size, with a clawback provision allowing that tranche to be increased to up to 30% of the issue size if the shares under the tranche are 15 times subscribed, and up to 50% of the issue size if the shares under the tranche are 50 times subscribed.
*The Exchange’s discretion on the use of placing*

- The Exchange wishes to remind underwriters and issuers that the combined offering mechanism involving public subscription and book-built placing in an initial public offering (IPO) was specifically approved for facilitating large scale multi-jurisdictional equity offerings. It was not intended that such offering mechanism be used in all IPOs. Therefore, the Exchange may, pursuant to the Listing Rules, exercise its discretion not to allow, or to impose restrictions on, any part of an issue to be offered by way of placing.
*Underwriters’ discretion on the allocation of shares in the placing tranche*

- The SFC and the Exchange do not recommend that rules be introduced either to require more detailed disclosure on how discretion is to be exercised, or to prescribe how underwriters ought to exercise their discretion, in allocating shares to investors under the placing tranche.
*Eligibility to apply for shares in the subscription tranche and the placing tranche*

- Traditionally, the subscription tranche is intended for the participation of retail investors, and the placing tranche for institutional investors. However, the SFC and the Exchange note that high level of subscriptions by non-retail Hong Kong investors have reduced the proportion of shares that could be allocated to retail investors. Over-subscriptions of IPOs have also caused imbalance in the supply and demand of funds in the banking system resulting, among other things, in the increase of short term interest rates on Hong Kong dollar. The SFC and the Exchange wish to seek the public’s views on the approach that should be adopted to address these issues. Apart from maintaining the existing system, two possible approaches have been identified. Under the first approach, all investors are eligible to apply for shares under the placing tranche and the subscription tranche, but rules would be introduced to prevent investors who have applied for shares under one tranche from applying for shares under the other tranche of the same issue. In addition, the present interim arrangements whereby half the shares available in the public subscription tranche are allocated to applicants applying for HK$5 million or less of securities and the remaining half are allocated to applicants applying for more than HK$5 million of securities could be adopted as part of this proposal. Under the second approach, participation in the subscription tranche would be limited to "retail" investors only (those who apply for shares worth HK$5 million or below), and participation in the placing tranche would be limited to "non-retail" investors only (those who apply for shares worth more than HK$5 million).
