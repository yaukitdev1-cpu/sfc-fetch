The Working Group on Plain Language today announces proposals to improve the way listed issuers present information in public announcements to investors.

The Working Group was formed in March this year by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC). Its convener is Mrs Laura Cha, Executive Director of Corporate Finance of the SFC, and members include Mr Lawrence Fok, Executive Director of Listing Division of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (the Exchange); Mr David Shaw, Partner, Norton Rose, and Consultant, Johnson, Stokes & Master; Mr John Brewer, Company Secretary of a listed company; Mr Eamonn McManus, Managing Director, Corporate Finance & Equity Capital Markets Division of HSBC Investment Bank Asia Limited; Mr Mark Dickens, Executive Director of Enforcement of the SFC; Ms Christine Wong, Director of Corporate Finance of the SFC; Ms Bonnie Chan, Senior Manager of Listing Division of the Exchange; and Ms Cate Guthleben, Consultant - Special Projects of the SFC.

The Working Group aims to improve the way information is presented to investors by encouraging issuers to make their documents more readable and easier to understand, in particular in relation to public announcements and prospectuses.

It has reviewed five common types of public announcements and has prepared plain language versions of them. Samples of each of the five announcements have also been created.

The main features of the plain language versions, aiming to make the announcements more readable and thus more effective, include:

- introducing a summary box for the transaction, its effect, and its impact on shareholders;
- arranging the information in a more meaningful order, giving investors the most important information first;
- using frequent and meaningful headings to break up the text;
- highlighting the warnings to investors;
- spacing of paragraphs and other design features to make reading easier; and
- using shorter sentences and simple English.
Mrs Laura Cha said: "The wording of the sample announcements is not the only wording that will be acceptable to the SFC or the Exchange when they vet announcements. The regulators will adopt a flexible approach and have regard to the particular features of the announcements."

"The principal objective is to ensure that investors can better understand public announcements. This exercise is part of our efforts to achieve a more transparent and informed market," she said.

Mr Lawrence Fok said: "We have noted that the current forms and contents of announcements are sometimes complicated and incomprehensible to the ordinary investors. We doubt how many people ever read these corporate announcements. We are confident that the new format will be easier to understand without sacrificing important messages contained in these announcements."

"We wish to emphasize that we will not change our disclosure requirements. Corporate announcements using plain language will continue to include all the important information relating to all types of transactions," Mr Fok said.

While the plain language versions serve as a useful guide to issuers, the Working Group also observes it will be easier for the SFC and the Exchange to vet announcements if the plain language format is followed.

Folders containing a set of guidelines for issuers called "How to Create Clear Announcements", and "before" and "after" samples of actual announcements, are available at the SFC and the Exchange.

The Working Group is now reviewing the use of language and contents of prospectuses, and expects to make proposals in that regard later this year.
