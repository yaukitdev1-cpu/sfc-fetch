The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today announced that some of its fees will increase in order to move further towards full cost recovery. The announcement follows the endorsement of these fee revisions on 26 August by the Chief-Executive-in-Council and the Government's announcement today concerning the endorsement. The revised fees will become effective 16 September 1997.

The funding principles on which the SFC was established require it wherever possible to recover the full cost of services it provides to particular market participants.

Since the last fee increase in March 1994, inflation has eroded the level of cost recovery. Forecast cost increases of 7% in 1997 will further lower the cost recovery level for the SFC if the fees and charges remain unchanged.

The main increases to fees and charges include the following:

*Intermediaries*: Fees relating to intermediaries will be increased by 7% to 11%, which is broadly in line with cumulative inflation since the last fee increase.

*Investment Products*: The current fee structure for mutual funds and unit trusts has been streamlined. The aggregate increases after consolidation are 4% for "umbrella funds" and 11% for single funds. However, consolidated application fees for each sub-fund under an umbrella fund will be reduced by 17%.

*Corporate Finance*: The fees relating to Corporate Finance have not been increased since April 1993. To recover costs, the document fees in relation to takeovers, mergers and share repurchases will be increased by $25,000 for offers between $75 million to $1,200 million. The fee for offers below $75 million will be increased by $15,000 while the fee for offers above $1,200 million will remain unchanged.

SFC Chairman Anthony Neoh said, "The SFC has made a determined effort to limit the levels of fee increase by applying strict efficiency criteria to the management of the Commission's resources while carrying out its functions. The fee increases are necessary to meet the funding principles on which the SFC was established, which require full cost recovery from market participants wherever possible."

The SFC's review of its fees and charges was completed after consultation with, and with the support of its Advisory Committee. The Committee is broadly representative of the different market sectors which will be affected by the fees.

A schedule of the revised fees and charges is available at the SFC's office and can be found on the SFC's website at http: //www.hksfc.org.hk.
