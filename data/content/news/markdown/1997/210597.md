The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) and the Sandra Ann Morsilli Pacific-Basin Capital Markets (PACAP) Research Center of the University of Rhode Island today issued a report on the perceived drift of trading in Hong Kong stocks to overseas markets, particularly London and New York. Among its major conclusions, the study reveals that:

- there is no evidence which indicates migration of trading from Hong Kong to New York. Hong Kong stocks trade in New York in the form of the American Depositary Receipts (ADRs), but the trading volume, primarily retail OTC transactions, is usually low.
- London trading volume for 18 selected Hong Kong stocks has been rising steadily, increasing from an average of 13% of Hong Kong volume in 1991 to 38% in 1995;
- there are three main reasons cited by London market makers why trades of Hong Kong stocks take place in London; (i) the time-zone difference, (ii) easier accessibility of capital in London to support large institutional trades, and (iii) the existence of stamp duty in Hong Kong, which is considered by London market makers as an extra cost. However, the magnitude of overall transactions costs is not an important factor which determines where Hong Kong stocks trade.
- Offshore trading exhibits very little impact on Hong Kong market volatility and liquidity, and is consistent with the London and New York market markers' view that the Hong Kong market is efficient and that Hong Kong is the major market for price discovery of Hong Kong stocks. Closing prices in Hong Kong usually serve as benchmark prices for London and New York market makers.
- London trading facilitates the price discovery process of Hong Kong stocks, without jeopardizing the systemic stability of the Hong Kong market. The belief that offshore trading increases Hong Kong price volatility and jeopardizes the systemic stability of the Hong Kong market is therefore unfounded.
Commenting on the study, David White, Executive Director of the SFC’s Supervision of Markets Division, said: "This study has been very useful to the SFC in its examination of the dynamics of our market, and we are grateful to the PACAP Research Center for their work on this project. While the volume of trading in Hong Kong stocks in London has grown, the study shows that this increase has not come at the expense of Hong Kong trading volume. Hence, we have a growing pie rather than migration of trading."

The report was co-authored by Rosita P. Chang, Henry R. Oppenheimer, and S. Ghon Rhee, professors of finance at the PACAP Research Center. Chang and Oppenheimer said: "Our analysis indicated that the increased trading in London actually facilitated the price discovery process and the efficiency of the Hong Kong market."

Rhee, Director of the Center, added: "As the Hong Kong market becomes more global, the international demand increases. As a result, we observe increased trading volume in London. We will see the same trend in New York in the near future. In fact, this has already happened to NYSE-listed Hong Kong stocks such as Hong Kong Telecom and Asia Satellite Telecom."

Based on the study’s findings, and with a view to facilitating Hong Kong’s continuing development as an international financial centre, the study recommends that:

- members of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong increase their capital commitment to facilitate large institutional trades in Hong Kong;
- an extension of Hong Kong’s trading hours be considered, in the form of off-hour "crossing sessions" which overlap London trading hours, thereby facilitating increased trading in both markets;
- reduction or elimination of stamp duty be encouraged; and
- offshore listing of Hong Kong-based stocks may be beneficial in facilitating price discovery and to broaden investor interest in these securities.
