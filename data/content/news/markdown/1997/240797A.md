The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today issued Revised Guidance Notes Regarding Money Laundering, reflecting recent international anti-money laundering initiatives, including a revised set of 40 recommendations on the subject issued in June 1996 by the International Financial Action Task Force (FATF). The purpose of the Guidance Notes is to remind securities, futures and leveraged foreign exchange trading intermediaries of their obligations under Hong Kong’s anti-money laundering laws and to outline policies and procedures for reporting and preventing money laundering.

The Guidance Notes also provide background on the nature and practice of money laundering and summarise the main provisions of the Drug Trafficking (Recovery of Proceeds) Ordinance (DTRPO), and the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (OSCO), the two primary bodies of law in Hong Kong concerned with money laundering. The Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU), a unit formed by the Police and Customs and Excise Department of the Hong Kong Government, is responsible for receiving and processing disclosures concerning money laundering. The JFIU also determines which disclosures merit investigation and which unit (e.g. Customs and Excise Department, Narcotics Bureau, etc) carries out the investigation.

The Guidance Notes, first issued in July 1995 and revised in December 1995, were issued after consultation with relevant Government departments and do not impose any additional obligations on registered persons or licensed traders.

An SFC spokesman said, "Registered persons and licensed traders are advised to remain vigilant in guarding against money laundering and establish procedures for identifying clients, maintaining records of transactions, and disclosing and reporting suspicious transactions to the appropriate law enforcement authorities. They should also ensure that the contents of the Guidance Notes are communicated to and understood by their representatives and staff. Furthermore, they should develop and implement education programmes for training new employees and current staff in the detection and prevention of money laundering."
