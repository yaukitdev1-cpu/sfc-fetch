The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) and the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (the Exchange) have received a number of complaints from investors following the closure of the Kwun Tong Branch of Hing Shing Investments Company and the disappearance of its branch manager.

The SFC and the Exchange are in close liaison with the owners of Hing Shing regarding the complaints, who are co-operating fully with the regulators.

The first task is to establish the extent of losses to ensure that the company has sufficient resources to cover its potential liabilities and has the ability to continue trading. At the moment, known investor claims appear to be in the region of about $14 million.

In addition, the company has undertaken to publish a press announcement inviting clients trading through its Kwun Tong Branch to come forward to register their claims in order to assist it in establishing its potential liabilities. Affected clients who have not already registered their claims are urged to contact Mr Richard Chiu of Hing Shing Investments Company at tel. 2529-0160.

Pending completion of this exercise, the owners of the company have demonstrated to the satisfaction of the SFC and the Exchange that it has liquid resources, in excess of $100 million, to meet its operational requirements and to cover potential claims. The SFC and the Exchange will monitor the position closely to ensure that the company continues to have sufficient resources to meet its liabilities and is in full compliance with the applicable financial resources rules.

The SFC and the Exchange would like to take this opportunity to remind investors that they must take all the necessary steps to protect their own investments. They should ensure that they enter into proper Client Agreements with their brokers before commencing trading, that they receive proper contract notes after execution of trades, that they receive regular statements of accounts and that these records are in order and retained for verification purposes. In particular, they must ensure that all cheques are made payable to their brokers instead of to individuals or their sales representatives.

The SFC and the Exchange would like to stress that investors should be alert to any departure from such standards because they are most likely to be early warning signals of improper behaviour. Similarly, any undue delays in the return of client funds and/or scrips are also likely to be early warning signals of improper behaviour. In such events, investors should immediately contact the senior management of their brokers and, if necessary, the Exchange and the SFC to ensure that their investments are safeguarded.
