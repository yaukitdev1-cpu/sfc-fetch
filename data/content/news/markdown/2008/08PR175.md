With the increasingly complex structure of investment products and a fast-changing market environment, understanding the risk and return of investment opportunities is sometimes not as simple as meets the eye.

In his latest column entitled, [“Understand the risk and return of your investments”](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2008articles/riskoct08.html), Dr Wise cautions that high risk does not guarantee high return. He also advises investors to consider risk and return on a relative rather than absolute basis when considering the trade-off between them, as well as assess alternative investment opportunities.

“Most people will tell you that high risk brings high returns,” Dr Wise says. “Of course, the first point to make is that this is only half the story! High risk is exactly that and instead of high returns you could suffer serious losses and in extreme cases, you could even lose more than you invested.”

Dr Wise offers three pointers to investors:
1. Think about risk and return on a relative rather than an absolute basis when weighing the trade-off between the two. Investors should be aware that higher returns offered by products generally reflect “increased risk” relatively to other products with lower return.
2. Don’t assume that the investment type or category will define the level of risk. Risk levels can differ even within the same product category. For instance, certain funds will be considered riskier than others and corporate bonds will generally be riskier than sovereign bonds and some even riskier than funds.
3. Risks may change over time along with market conditions, so investors must review their portfolio regularly. As a result of the current financial tsunami, investments that were previously considered of low or medium risk may well have increased levels of risk in today’s volatile markets.
