Hong Kong’s stock market turnover shrank in the first half of 2008 during a global correction, but participation by different groups of investors remained little changed from the second half of 2007, the SFC’s latest half yearly market review shows.

The share of trading activity by institutional brokers rose slightly to 67% from 65% while that of retail brokers dropped to 33% from 35%.

In the same period, the market share of short selling activities rose relative to the reduced market turnover, but the SFC found no irregularities, the report notes. At a daily average of $6,484 million, short selling contributed to 7.4% of total market turnover in the first half, compared to $7,635 million or 6.6% in the second half of last year.

“Despite the weak market performance during the period, operations of the Hong Kong securities and futures market were largely smooth,” said the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer Mr Martin Wheatley. “Systemic risk of the local market also remained limited, but the SFC will continue to monitor the development of the market closely.”

In the first half of 2008, the HSI lost 21% and the HSCEI lost 26%, following the weak performance of the US and the Mainland markets. This was attributable largely to the lingering subprime problem, high oil prices and worries over the Mainland’s monetary tightening measures.

Given the subprime problem is yet to be resolved and oil prices remain high, the Hong Kong market is expected to continue to be volatile. The performance of local shares may also be affected by the performance of the regional stock markets – notably the Mainland – and movements in the currency markets.

The [research paper](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/research/research/RS%20Paper%2041.pdf) is available on the SFC website under the section “Research and Statistics.”
