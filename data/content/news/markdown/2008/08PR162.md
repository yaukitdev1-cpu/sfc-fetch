In the first of two seminars, starting today, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) is sharing with brokers the results of a review of margin lending practices to call for tighter risk management.

Earlier this year, in anticipation of a deteriorating market situation, the SFC and an international accounting firm jointly reviewed the share margin financing policies and practices of 20 brokerage firms. These were selected as a representative sample of margin brokers of different sizes. This thematic exercise complemented the SFC’s regular inspection and on-going surveillance over risk management practices of securities firms.

“It is important that senior management of each firm properly manage the risks associated with the firm’s business by establishing appropriate limits for both proprietary and client trading and ensuring that margin lending policies are strictly enforced,” said Mr Martin Wheatley, the Chief Executive Officer of the SFC. “Brokers should also carry out periodic stress tests, particularly in times of significant market volatility, to assess the potential impact on their financial position” he added.

The review identified a number of cases where brokers’ margin policies were not strictly adhered to. These included allowing margin clients to carry out further purchases after failing to meet margin calls, a lack of audit trail for margin call follow-up action, and inadequate control over collateral and client concentration.

At the seminar, attendees from the industry were reminded to:


- adhere to a prudent written margin lending and margin call policy for strict enforcement;
- promptly collect margins due from clients and refrain from allowing further purchases or extending further credit if a client repeatedly fails to meet margin calls;
- avoid building up excessive concentration of individual stocks as collateral and of exposure to individual margin clients; and
- maintain detailed records of margin calls and obtain management approval for any deviation from margin call policies.
Following the review, brokers found not to have followed appropriate procedures were issued with notices cautioning stricter compliance. Disciplinary action will be taken against firms who are found to persistently breach sound risk management practices.
