There is a need to strengthen the resilience of the financial system in the wake of the latest crisis, and it is not just the case of tightening regulation.

That’s the message the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley, will deliver when he speaks at the 2008 Lujiazui Forum in Shanghai today.

He will discuss the transmission of global financial risks as a result of the changing financial landscape, the lessons of the global market crisis, and how the market and the authorities should respond to the latest failure of the global banking system.

Mr Wheatley stresses the importance of maintaining “a judicious balance” between financial innovation and the need for regulation.

“I think there is consensus among regulators that we do not wish to see the baby being thrown out with the bathwater. A knee-jerk response to tighten regulation would not be appropriate,” according to Mr Wheatley.

“However, there have clearly been failures at different parts of the chain – from selling practices in loan origination, to the failure of the rating models to the explosion of off-balance sheet exposure. These need to be fixed,” he says.

Collective engagement and dialogue are necessary to resolve the challenges to ensure that the financial system would be much more robust and resilient.

“Regulators and authorities also have to strengthen supervisory oversight, enhance co-operation and information sharing, as well as strengthen operational frameworks to better deal with financial stress,” he concludes.

Mr Wheatley’s speech will be available on the SFC’s website [www.sfc.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/) after he had delivered it.
