Securities regulators need to work together to tackle the challenges brought about by the increasing consolidation of stock and derivative exchanges worldwide.

Speaking at the Annual Conference of International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) in Paris, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley noted that as markets become increasingly global, regulatory cooperation among securities regulators becomes increasingly vital.

“Markets today are de facto networks and a market is only as strong as its weakest link. When one market within the global network fails, there is a domino effect, this can create serious consequences for other markets,” Mr Wheatley said.

“In areas where regulatory convergence is considered inappropriate, regulators should seek enhancements in regulatory co-operation. Regulators should enhance information sharing arrangements to cope with enforcement issues and market oversight of exchange operations,” he added.

Mr Wheatley also encouraged regulators to “review their regulatory approaches to domestic and overseas exchanges to minimise regulatory arbitrage”, and strengthen the risk management standards, as consolidation of exchanges inevitably heightens the risk of contagion effect across different markets.

Mr Wheatley’s [speech](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/speeches/08/mw_20080530_iosco.pdf) is available on the SFC’s website [www.sfc.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/).
