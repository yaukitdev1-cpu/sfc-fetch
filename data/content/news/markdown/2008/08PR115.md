Hong Kong’s combined fund management business (Note 1) hit $9,631 billion in 2007, a record increase of 56.5% year on year and 112.8% more than in 2005, the SFC’s newly released annual Fund Management Activities Survey (FMAS) shows.

Reaffirming Hong Kong’s draw as a hub in the region’s fund management industry, overseas investors contributed $6,547 billion or 68.4% to assets under management, exclusive of real estate investment trusts (non-REIT fund management business). The survey further finds:


- Asset management, historically the largest segment in the combined fund management business, expanded 57.5% last year to $6,511 billion on top of a 27.5% gain in 2006;
- Assets managed in Hong Kong logged a new high of $4,072 billion or 62.5% of $6,511 billion in the asset management segment;
- Hong Kong favoured as a hub for managing investments in the Mainland and other Asian markets with 82.1% ($3,345 billion) of the assets managed locally invested in the region;
- Rapid accumulation of wealth in Asia boosting the fund advisory and private banking business, which expanded 102.9% and 36.7% respectively between 2006 and 2007.
Bolstering the industry were the strong performance of financial markets, more diversified sources of fund inflow and a wider selection of products, the survey notes. 

“The impressive growth of Hong Kong’s asset management industry last year reflects both industry participants’ commitment to broaden their expertise and range of asset management services as well as our commitment to facilitate growth,” said Mrs Alexa Lam, the SFC’s Deputy Chief Executive Officer and Executive Director of Policy, China and Investment Products.

The FMAS report notes that the SFC has been taking initiatives to facilitate market development and widen the product range, which are likely to bear fruit over time. Such measures include:


- Reaching an agreement with the China Banking Regulatory Commission in April 2007, which enabled Hong Kong to become the first offshore market for Mainland commercial banks to invest for their clients;
- Reaching an agreement with the China Insurance Regulatory Commission in March 2008 to facilitate co-operation and exchange of information and to promote Hong Kong as a platform for Mainland insurers conducting investment activities offshore;
- Having a senior SFC representative sit on the China Securities Regulatory Commission’s expert committee that approves funds under the Qualified Domestic Institutional Investor (QDII) Scheme to expedite its implementation;
- Signing a “declaration of mutual recognition” with the Australian Securities and Investment Commission in July 2008 to streamline the regulatory process and facilitate the cross-border offering of retail funds;
- Authorising 257 retail funds – including a number of firsts (Note 2) – thus contributing in part to boost the gross sales of all retail funds. (According to a Hong Kong Investment Funds Association tally, a total of $355 billion in retail funds was sold in 2007, up 86.8% from 2006 and nearly six times more than in 1999, the first year of the FMAS survey.)
In the report, the SFC also pledges to devote more effort to develop Hong Kong as “the platform of choice” for Mainland asset managers to implement the QDII scheme. Since May 2008, Mainland fund managers have been allowed to set up operations in the city under an implementation rule as part of the Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement IV. “We welcome Mainland fund managers becoming a part of our asset management industry, they will bring new opportunities and growth,” Mrs Lam said.

But Hong Kong is not immune from external risks. The FMAS report highlights challenges for this year, citing “headwinds” created by the subprime-related credit crisis, the global economic slowdown and worldwide markets under pressure.

The survey has been conducted annually since 1999 to help the SFC assess the industry’s state of affairs for policy setting and operations planning. Included for the first time in 2007, insurance companies surveyed (Note 3) contributed $113 billion or 2% to the overall asset management business. Banks were responsible for $187 billion or 3%, while $6,211 billion or 95% came from licensed asset management and fund advisory houses.

A total of 371 respondents, namely, 329 licensed asset management and fund advisory houses, 36 registered financial institutions and six insurance companies (Note 4), completed and returned the questionnaires. Details are contained in the [report](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/surveys/08/fmas_080724.pdf).

Notes :

1. The term refers to the overall value of assets reported in the sub-sectors of asset management, fund advisory, private banking (broadly categorised as non-REIT fund management business), as well as SFC-authorised REITs.
2. Among the new products were: the first commodities futures exchange traded fund (ETF), the first actively managed close-ended fund focusing on A shares, the first retail Islamic fund, the first gold ETF and the first retail 130/30 fund with a long-short strategy.
3. Life Insurance Councillors’ members of the Hong Kong Federation of Insurers, who had a 70% market share of the total in-force business as of 2006, were surveyed.
4. Respondents fell into these categories: 1) asset management and fund advisory companies licensed under section 120 or 121 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO); 2) registered institutions under section 119 of the SFO, which are authorised financial institutions as defined in section 2(1) of the Banking Ordinance; and 3) insurance companies registered under Chapter 41 of the Insurance Companies Ordinance providing long-term business.
