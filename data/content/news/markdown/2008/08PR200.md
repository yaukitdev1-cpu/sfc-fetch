Although many investors know of boiler-room scams, some of them still fall for the scams – even more than once - and get conned into buying worthless or non-existent investments.

In his latest column entitled, “[Boiler rooms – don’t be a victim](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2008articles/boiler_dec08.html),” Dr Wise warns investors that these classic tricks pulled by fraudsters still work and that even well-educated, experienced professionals sometimes become victims of investment scams.

“To fraudsters, educated professionals hold a certain appeal; they have the money, are too busy to do any research, and are open-minded about investing,” Dr Wise says. He illustrates this point by citing survey findings of the British Financial Services Authority that a considerable percentage of victims of boiler-room scams are professionals or senior executives of companies.

Dr Wise highlights the existence of a so-called “suckers” list which contains contacts and information of victims of investment scams, thus making it easier for people to be approached twice by fraudsters. “People who have become victims of a scam often have their contact details placed on a “suckers” list, which can be used again by the same scam operator or distributed or sold among other fraudsters,” Dr Wise says.

In addition to reminding investors that fraudsters are continually adjusting their tricks to make scams seem more plausible, Dr Wise also offers a checklist of what to look out for when one receives unsolicited calls or emails.
