The SFC proposes to exempt SFC-authorised open-ended collective investment schemes organised in corporate form and listed on The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited (Stock Exchange) from the disclosure of interests requirements of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Note 1).

At present there are no Exchange Traded index-tracking Funds (ETFs) listed in Hong Kong which have been structured in corporate form. Since this structure is common in other jurisdictions, the proposed amendment will assist the development of Hong Kong’s ETF market by facilitating the listing of ETFs in different forms.

This proposal will also bring Hong Kong’s regulatory requirements in line with international standards and practices in this area.

“We are committed to enhancing Hong Kong’s position as a hub for ETFs in the region and attracting more of these funds to come to Hong Kong and to list on the Stock Exchange. The SFC’s policy is to encourage the development of ETFs in the Hong Kong market,” said Mrs Alexa Lam, the SFC’s Deputy Chief Executive Officer and Executive Director (Policy, China and Investment Products).

Hong Kong ranked first in terms of market capitalisation of ETF in Asia (ex Japan) and registered the highest volume of trading in the region in 2007.

The proposed amendments to the Guidelines for the Exemption of Listed Corporations from Part XV of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Disclosure of Interests) are set out in a consultation paper issued by the SFC today (Note 2).

The public are invited to submit comments in writing before 6 June 2008. Following the consultation, the SFC will publish a conclusions paper to summarise the views collected and set out the final proposal.

The [Consultation Paper](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=08CP3) is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website at www.sfc.hk.

Notes:

1. A corporate form collective investment scheme listed on the Stock Exchange, such as an ETF in corporate form, is technically a listed corporation under Part XV of the SFO. That means its corporate insiders come under the disclosure obligations under Part XV. Part XV requires corporate insiders (i.e., substantial shareholders, directors, shadow directors and chief executives of a listed corporation) to give notice of their interests to the listed corporation and the Stock Exchange on occurrence of certain events.

The proposed amendments will be made to the Guidelines for the Exemption of Listed Corporations from Part XV of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Disclosure of Interests) (Guidelines).

2. Section 309(2) of the SFO gives the SFC the power, after consultation with the Financial Secretary, to exempt any listed corporation from all or any of the provisions of Part XV, subject to such conditions as it considers appropriate, having regard to the Guidelines.
