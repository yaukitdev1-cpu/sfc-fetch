In its second financial quarter of 2008-09, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) continued to deter misconduct and encourage compliance by making full use of the civil, disciplinary and criminal remedies available to it.

The SFC’s [Quarterly Report](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/quartely/jul_sep_08.pdf) for July to September 2008-09 highlights the first criminal conviction for insider dealing under the Securities and Futures Ordinance in 2003. The accused was sentenced to six months’ imprisonment (suspended for two years) in addition to being ordered to pay a fine and investigation costs.

Following the collapse of Lehman Brothers Holdings Inc, the SFC has commenced formal investigations into the conduct of a number of distributors of Lehman Brothers structured products and also into whether any facts, matters or circumstances that should have been disclosed to the SFC were not disclosed at the time the offer prospectuses and marketing materials were submitted for vetting.

Due to uncertain global financial conditions and various restrictions some major overseas markets implemented on short selling, the SFC has deferred relaxation of the tick rule on short selling. Licensed corporations also were urged to observe stricter risk management.

Among other initiatives to promote efficiency, the SFC approved rule amendments and fees related to liquidity providers for the gold futures market. It also has approved the first gold exchange-traded fund, offering investors an alternative means to participate in the gold bullion market.

On the investor education front, the focus was on alerting investors to increasing market volatility, and in particular, credit risks of product issuers and counterparty risk in products with underlying collateral.

The latest Quarterly Report is available on the SFC website at www.sfc.hk.
