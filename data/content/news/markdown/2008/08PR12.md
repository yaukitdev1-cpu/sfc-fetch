Dr Wise today debunks some of the myths surrounding investment in derivative warrants - a popular choice among retail investors.

In the latest column - [*“Common Misconceptions on Derivative Warrants”*](http://www.invested.hk/invested/html/EN/features/doctor/features_87_226_351.htm) - Dr Wise points out that the price of a warrant does not always go up even when the underlying price moves in the direction a warrant buyer has hoped for.

Dr Wise notes that many investors appear to buy deeply out-of-the-money short-dated warrants in the hope of capitalizing on a big swing in the underlying price before their expiry, without realizing that they are usually less sensitive to changes in the underlying price.

Investors appear to be unaware of the obligations of liquidity providers, with some of them mistakenly believing liquidity providers are required to support warrant prices, Dr Wise says.

He urges investors to seek independent advice on investments in warrants and to carefully consider the appropriateness of recommendations by issuers or other public commentators.
