More than 60 senior representatives of regulatory bodies, exchanges, and financial institutions from the securities, futures, banking, and insurance sectors throughout Asia today attended a Global Year 2000 Workshop in Hong Kong. The workshop was sponsored by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), and the Global Year 2000 Coordinating Group (the Group), a body recently established by several global financial institutions to address the problems associated with the Year 2000 computer problem. 

The purpose of today's workshop was to discuss how the Group's international initiatives can assist the Year 2000 preparations already under way in Asian markets. The Group is now working closely with the Joint Year 2000 Council*, which was established by the international regulatory community to coordinate official efforts in response to the millennium bug.

The Group's initiatives are targeted at assessing and improving Year 2000 readiness in three distinct areas: market infrastructure, the financial industry, and third party service providers.

Among the specific proposals discussed at today's meeting were:

- a status checklist of financial firms, to help identify the state of readiness of different firms and the actions required by firms which may not be fully Year 2000 compliant by the end of 1998;
- a market information template, setting out in standard format the arrangements in different markets for preparing for the Year 2000; and
- a framework for determining the requirements for infrastructure testing in different markets.
"Today's Year 2000 Workshop is a major step for Asia in the development of a coordinated, measurable response to the Year 2000 problem," said SFC Chairman Anthony Neoh. "Hong Kong and the other Asian members of IOSCO, all of whom are represented at today's workshop, will work closely with the Global Year 2000 Coordinating Group in the months ahead to monitor and improve the Year 2000 readiness of Asia's financial institutions and market infrastructure."

David Carse, Deputy Chief Executive of the HKMA said : "The involvement of the Coordinating Group in this workshop is a practical recognition that the Year 2000 problem is a global issue. It also demonstrates how regulators and the industry can work together to promote Year 2000 compliance."
