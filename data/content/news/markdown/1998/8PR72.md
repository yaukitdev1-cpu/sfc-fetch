In view of recent media reports and market comments, the Securities and Futures Commission today makes the following statement:

The SFC is required to maintain the integrity of the securities market as one of its statutory duties. It has always acted firmly against any malpractices in the market including illegal short selling.

Section 80(1) of the Securities Ordinance (SO) states that "a person shall not sell securities at or through the Unified Exchange unless, at the time he sells them, (a) he has or, where he is selling as agent, his principal has; or (b) he reasonably and honestly believes that he has or, where he is selling as agent, that his principal has, a presently exercisable and unconditional right to vest the securities in the purchaser of them".

Any person who contravenes Section 80(1) shall be guilty of an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine of $10,000 and to imprisonment for six months. SFC registrants who are found guilty of the offence may also be subject to disciplinary proceedings under the SO.

In the year ended 31 March 1998, the SFC issued 120 summonses for offences under Section 80(1) resulting in total fines of $316,000. A total of 452 summonses were issued in the year before, resulting in fines of $1.35 million.

The SFC is inquiring into all failures to deliver stocks on time to settle outstanding short positions under the Central Clearing and Settlement System in relation to trading last Thursday and Friday (27 and 28 August). The SFC will take prosecution and/or disciplinary action in all cases where there is evidence of a breach of the legislation or of the rules of the Stock Exchange or the Hongkong Clearing.

Failure of a broker participant to deliver stocks on time to settle outstanding trading positions does not in itself necessarily mean an offence under Section 80(1) of the SO has taken place. A failure may, for example, be due to logistical difficulties in delivering scrips and not because of naked short position where the seller did not have a presently exercisable and unconditional right to sell the shares at the time of the sale order.

The SFC wishes to assure the investing public that it will not hesitate to utilise the necessary resources to stamp out illegal practices in the market.
