The Securities and Futures Commission today issued a consultation paper seeking public comment on the desirability of introducing treasury shares in Hong Kong. The consultation is being undertaken in the context of the SFC’s review of the Share Repurchase Code. The SFC believes that a review of the existing framework is appropriate in light of the Government’s Report on Financial Market Review and the prevailing economic climate in Hong Kong. 

"Treasury shares" refer to issued shares in a company which are repurchased and held by the company in treasury without being cancelled. Treasury shares are available for resale at a later date. The Companies Ordinance currently requires a Hong Kong incorporated company to cancel shares which have been repurchased. It has been argued that allowing companies to hold and re-sell repurchased shares when market conditions allow would give them greater flexibility to adjust their share capital more quickly and might lead to a reduction in their overall cost of capital, thereby stimulating investment. Possible objections to the introduction of treasury shares include increased risk of market manipulation or artificial support of share prices, increased risk of directors seeking to benefit themselves personally, and the recognition that treasury shares do not represent a panacea.

The introduction of treasury shares will require changes to be made to existing company law in Hong Kong. Such changes will apply to Hong Kong incorporated companies, but not those incorporated overseas. Companies incorporated overseas, which currently account for two-thirds of the listed companies on The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (SEHK), will only acquire any perceived benefit if the company law in their own jurisdiction also provides for treasury shares.

In light of these considerations, the consultation paper also explores whether there should be non-legislative regulatory changes, such as to the provisions of the SEHK Listing Rules, which affect the process and speed of reissue of shares which have been repurchased and cancelled. Any such changes would be designed to provide some of the perceived benefits and flexibility of treasury shares to all companies listed on the SEHK, whatever their place of incorporation.

Mrs Laura Cha, Deputy Chairman and Executive Director of the SFC’s Corporate Finance Division said: "We believe that listed companies are likely to be in favour of the two measures outlined in the consultation paper. Both measures offer companies certain flexibility in dealing with their share capital which has not existed before. Naturally, any proposal to modify the existing regime must include consideration of the controls required to ensure that the perceived benefits of the facility are not abused. These checks and balances would seek to ensure a fair market in a company’s shares and to protect the interests of public shareholders, creditors and others who do business with a company."

The consultation paper consults the public on the following principal issues:

- Should listed companies incorporated in Hong Kong be allowed to hold shares in treasury for subsequent resale?
- If so, what regulatory framework should be in place to allow treasury shares to be held and resold in the Hong Kong market?
- With regard to the disparity which may result if Hong Kong incorporated companies were allowed to hold treasury shares, should the Listing Rules be amended to enable listed companies to expedite the listing of new shares that are reissued following a repurchase and cancellation?
- If so, would it be necessary or desirable to introduce additional safeguards in the Listing Rules to protect shareholders and the securities market generally in cases where companies wish to take advantage of such a change?
