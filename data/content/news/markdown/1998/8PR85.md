Attached for your reference is a speech given today by SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, at the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong's 12th Anniversary cocktail reception.

 


**Address by Mr Andrew Sheng**

Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission

at the 12th Anniversary Cocktail Reception

of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong

on 15 October 1998


 

Chairman HC Lee,

Members of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong,

Ladies and gentlemen,

I feel very honoured today to have been invited to be the Guest of Honour, only two weeks after I have succeeded Mr Anthony Neoh as the Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission. I would like to acknowledge first and foremost the significant contributions of Mr Anthony Neoh to the work of the securities industry in Hong Kong. I hope I will be able to pick up the work as fast as he did. I must also confess that given the massive changes globally and locally, I have yet to acquaint myself with all the issues of the securities markets in this short time. But I hope with your help, understanding and co-operation, this learning curve will be short and steep.

In the past year we have seen some dramatic changes in our market - from a high level of retail participation and maximum bullish sentiment, to the present consolidation. The Asian crisis has imposed tremendous stresses on our markets, including huge volatility and pain throughout the region, not least Hong Kong. We have to realistically accept that a period of adjustment will persist for sometime to come. It is a pain of adjustment, with important lessons that we will all have to share and bear, markets and regulators alike. The public expects no less.

However, I am an optimist at heart. Despite all the stresses, we can take pride in the liquidity of our markets and the stability and efficiency of our trading, settlement and clearing systems. This is to the credit of the Chairman and his colleagues in the Exchange Council, as well as its management and staff.

What I find most heartening is that despite the tremendous stresses on our markets, the Hong Kong fighting and entrepreneurial spirit is undaunted. Under the able leadership of your Chairman, Council and management, the Stock Exchange has come up with new ideas and strategies to revive business and develop new opportunities. You have made many efforts to strengthen the Hong Kong market's competitiveness - especially in relation to its position as the leading international market for PRC securities which you have stated in your Strategic Plan for 1998 - 2001.

I want to assure you that the SFC is fully committed to work with you to further enhance Hong Kong as the premier international financial centre of the mainland and the Asian region. Since the announcement of my appointment as the SFC Chairman in July, I have been asked on how I would work with the Stock Exchange. As a market regulator, there will always be an element of creative tension between us. But I sincerely believe, just as I am sure that you all believe, that the securities market must be developed along four major principles: - confidence and trust, market integrity and fairness, level playing field in competition, and transparency and disclosure. Business is clearly moving towards those markets that have the highest liquidity, the least intermediation costs, the most transparent and the most fair in terms of competition and regulation. All these are in the long term interest of the Hong Kong securities market.

There are many challenges ahead of us. One very important issue that we all face in the next 12 months is the Year 2000 issue which has implications on all business sectors worldwide. The coming period of stock exchange systems and cross industry tests will be very important to ensure that we are properly prepared for the millennium, and will require maximum members' involvement to ensure success.

The Securities and Futures Commission will continue to build on the partnership with the Stock Exchange that has evolved over the past decade. We have unity of objectives and purpose. We need to work more closely and directly to ensure that our markets are vibrant, healthy, transparent and fair. This is vital to us all. Mr. H. C. Lee has my assurance that we will have a continuous dialogue to ensure that your prosperity, which is the key to prosperity of Hong Kong, will be maintained in the years to come.

On behalf of the Commission and all its staff, I congratulate the Stock Exchange on its 12th Anniversary. The fact that the market has been much more buoyant in the last few days shows that the market is also celebrating this Anniversary. There will be many more Anniversaries to come and it is my sincere wish that each Anniversary will witness greater prosperity than before.

Thank you.
