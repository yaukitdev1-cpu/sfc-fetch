The Securities and Futures Commission's ninth annual report, for the year ended 31 March 1998, was tabled before the Legislative Council today.

In his Chairman's Statement in the Annual Report 97/98, Mr Anthony Neoh said the year under review had seen unprecedented boom and decline in rapid succession. He said while volatile market conditions throughout much of the year had put the Hong Kong markets to their most severe test since 1987, the market systems, particularly the trading, clearing and settlement systems, had operated efficiently and effectively under the strain.

Mr Neoh said the SFC, in addition to undertaking a heavy operational workload in all its major areas of work, had completed a number of major initiatives. In particular, the SFC had taken actions in four directions during the year in response to changing market conditions. They were:

- Action against market malpractice
- Investor education
- Inspections of brokerages and affiliated finance companies
- Development of policy response to increased margin financing
To ensure that the markets remained fair and orderly, the SFC and Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (SEHK) in May 1997 jointly announced their concern over the increase in usual price and volume movements in certain shares which appeared to bear no relationship to fundamentals, warning that trading in such shares would be suspended if necessary . During the year, there were 69 suspensions due to unusual price and volume movements, which resulted in 9 investigations into possible market manipulation and 13 investigations into possible insider dealing.

The SFC's investor education campaign was further developed during the year to take advantage of all available distribution channels, including brochures, print media, television, radio, and the Internet. Two new brochures - made available at the SFC and Consumer Council offices and on the SFC website - explained issues to consider when investing in stocks and funds. Meanwhile, SFC Hotline staff handled a record number of investor enquiries and complaints.

The third prong of the SFC's action was focused on reducing high levels of margin trading and directed at brokerages and their affiliated finance companies. Although the SFC did not have regulatory powers over finance companies, it was able to persuade firms to reduce their margin lending exposures, thereby helping the broking industry weather the sharp market decline in October 1997. A task force formed in January 1998 conducted further inspections and succeeded in persuading broking firms and finance companies to increase capital.

As a policy response, the SFC submitted in December 1997 a report on the increased activities of unregulated share margin finance companies to the Government, recommending that a working party be established to propose a course of action. By the end of the year under review, the working party had proposed legislative amendments to bring margin lending under tighter regulation. These recommendations were outlined in a Government consultation paper issued in May, and would be considered by the Legislative Council soon.

The SFC's other major policy initiatives and achievements during the year included:

- Public consultations on a review of the Code on Takeovers and Mergers and offer mechanisms for initial public offerings involving large-scale multi-jurisdictional offers;
- Issuance of internal control guidelines for registered intermediaries;
- Issuance of new Fund Manager Code of Conduct and third edition of the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds;
- Five cases of suspended insider dealing referred by SFC either completed or currently before the Insider Dealing Tribunal;
- Establishment of the Hong Kong Securities Institute; and
- Continued efforts to ensure rectification of the Year 2000 computer problem.
Mr. Neoh concluded: "An effective financial regulatory agency must continue to hone its skills and professionalism to meet the challenges of change in the markets. Thus, we hope it will be thought that we have lived up to the motto that we exist to serve the needs of the markets and the community, without fear or favour."
