The Appeal Committee of the Court of Final Appeal today dismissed an application for leave to appeal by Nomura International (Hong Kong) Ltd (Nomura HK) in relation to the Securities and Futures Commission's disciplinary proceedings against the company.

Chief Justice Li, Mr Justice Litton PJ, and Mr Justice Ching PJ dismissed the application, saying that it was "wholly without merit". The Court also awarded costs to the SFC.

Nomura HK and three of its officers had sought to appeal against a decision by the Court of Appeal on 7 May 1998 which allowed the SFC to continue its disciplinary proceedings against them.

In May and July 1997, the SFC had issued "Letters of Mindedness" regarding possible disciplinary action against Nomura HK and certain of its employees following an inquiry into their role in certain securities and futures trading on Australian markets. The letters gave the recipients the opportunity to make representations and submit evidence before any final determination was made.

On 21 July 1997, Nomura HK and the employees requested the SFC to extend the time for receiving representations and to stay its proceedings pending the outcome of litigation in Australia between the Australian Securities Commission and Nomura International plc, London (Nomura UK). On 23 October, the SFC indicated it was unwilling to accede to this request. On 24 October, Nomura HK obtained leave from the High Court to apply for judicial review of the SFC's decision. The application was heard on 9 and 11 December when Nomura HK sought, among other things, to stay the SFC's proceedings.

On 22 December 1997, the Court of First Instance granted Nomura HK an order which effectively prohibited the SFC from proceeding further before 31 May 1998, having regard to the Australian litigation against Nomura UK. The SFC lodged an appeal to the Court of Appeal, which was heard in April 1998.

The Court of Appeal decided that Nomura HK had not established sufficient evidence of a real risk of prejudice and unanimously allowed the SFC's appeal and awarded costs to the SFC.

The Court of Appeal judgments made it clear that the SFC should not be prevented from carrying out its statutory and public duties unless there are clear and strong ground for doing so.

Nomura HK sought to appeal against the Court of Appeal decision and the application was heard by the Court of Final Appeal on 16 October.

SFC's Executive Director of Enforcement, Mr Mark Dickens, welcomed the Court of Final Appeal decision.

"The decision reaffirms the importance of the role of the SFC and of its statutory responsibilities to encourage proper conduct among intermediaries, suppress dishonourable and improper practices, and promote integrity among Hong Kong registrants," Mr Dickens said. "The SFC's ability to regulate the conduct of Hong Kong registrants is not to be compromised merely because other overseas regulators may also have jurisdiction over that conduct."

The disciplinary proceedings against Nomura HK and other registrants are continuing.
