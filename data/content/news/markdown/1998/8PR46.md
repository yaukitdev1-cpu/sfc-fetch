The Securities and Futures Commission and the Commission des valeurs mobilières du Québec (Québec Securities Commission or CVMQ) signed a Memorandum of Understanding in Paris yesterday. The MOU establishes a framework for mutual assistance and facilitates the exchange of information between the two authorities. 

The scope of this MOU includes the enforcement of laws, rules and regulations relating to securities and futures markets; clearing and settlement activities; takeovers and mergers; the disclosure of information relevant to investors; the prevention of insider dealing, market manipulation and other fraudulent practices; and the fitness and properness and conduct of business standards of advisers, traders and other financial market practitioners. The CVMQ possesses powers to provide investigatory assistance, similar to section 59A of the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance, which they are willing to exercise on behalf of the SFC.

The MOU was signed by Mr Anthony Neoh, Chairman of the SFC, and Mr Jean Martel, Chairman of CVMQ during a meeting of the International Organisation of Securities Commissions in Paris.

"This Memorandum provides regulatory links between the Hong Kong and Québec securities and futures markets, enhancing the two authorities’ market protection and law enforcement abilities," said Mr. Neoh. "Together with the two existing MOUs the SFC has signed with the British Columbia Securities Commission and the Ontario Securities Commission, this agreement further strengthens the SFC’s co-operation with the securities market regulators in Canada."

Mr Martel said: "The increasingly international activity between the financial markets and the need to establish co-operation and mutual assistance between the authorities responsible for regulating their respective financial markets, have led us to sign this agreement."

Including this MOU with the CVMQ, the SFC has signed a total of 30 co-operative arrangements with securities regulators around the world including those of the United States, Europe and Asia Pacific region. For its part, the CVMQ has signed 13 co-operative agreements with authorities from Asia, North America, South America and Europe. Such co-operative arrangements greatly facilitate the exchange of information and foster regulatory co-operation in today’s increasingly global capital markets.
