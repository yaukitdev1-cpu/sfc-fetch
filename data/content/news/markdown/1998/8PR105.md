The Securities and Futures Commission today published a survey report on the business activities of securities and futures intermediaries.

The report summarises the findings of two surveys conducted on the registered firms' activities during 1996 and 1997 respectively. The survey areas included primary business activity, market share, client profile, profile of managed funds, profile on margin financing of securities, custody of client assets by securities intermediaries, and human resources.

Some of the major findings in the report are as follows:

- In common with other advanced markets, the industry, especially the securities sector, was dominated by a small number of well capitalised and established houses. This is particular so in a bullish market sentiment such as during 1997.
- While investors from overseas and Mainland China accounted for only 15% of the total clients of securities intermediaries in 1997 (8% in case of futures intermediaries), they contributed 43% of gross operating income to the firms (46% for futures). This suggests that the momentum of local markets depends significantly on overseas and PRC investors.
- Retail clients, who are mainly local residents, accounted for 85% of the clients of securities and futures dealers, and contributed some 30% of the total gross operating income. Investment advisers derived 16% of their income from retail clients who represented 70% of their total clients.
Findings from the surveys will be used by the SFC to improve its understanding of the needs of registrants, measures that may further stimulate growth in the financial services sector, and the means to ensure better investor protection.

Information collected will also assist the SFC in its review of the current requirements for licensing of intermediaries and the formulation of new investor compensation arrangements in Hong Kong.

The SFC wishes to thank those intermediaries who had co-operated in the surveys. It plans to conduct similar surveys in the future as part of its ongoing efforts to understand the needs of the financial sector and to facilitate the business of intermediaries.
