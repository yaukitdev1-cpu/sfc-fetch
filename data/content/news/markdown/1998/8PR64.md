The Securities and Futures Commission announces that the revised Hong Kong Code on Takeovers and Mergers (the Takeovers Code) has been gazetted today. The revised Takeovers Code will become effective on 1 August 1998.

The Takeovers Code was first introduced in 1975, and was reviewed between 1989 and 1991. Since then the only major amendment was the introduction of Rule 2.10 in 1993 regarding privatisations by way of scheme of arrangement. The current revision was intended to refine the Takeovers Code and to provide additional interpretative guidance based primarily on the relevant experience in Hong Kong over the last 5 years.

The review was carried out by the Corporate Finance Division of the SFC, with participation of the Takeovers and Mergers Panel (the Panel) and assistance of the Special Adviser to the SFC, Mr Christopher de Boer, a former Chairman of the Panel. The SFC issued a consultation paper in February 1998 after extensive discussions by the Panel. The consultation period ended on 27 March 1998 and a total of 26 submissions were received from the public. The submissions were reviewed and refinements to the proposed amendments were adopted by the Panel and approved by the Commission.

Mrs Laura Cha, Deputy Chairman and Executive Director of the SFC, said: "The submissions from the market have enabled us to make improvements to some of the proposals raised in the consultation paper. I am grateful for the market's input since the fundamental basis of the Takeovers Code is that it should represent a consensus of opinions between market participants and the SFC on the standards of commercial conduct acceptable for takeover and merger transactions."

In the consultation paper, the SFC invited comments on several options in connection with the level of voting and the removal of the requirement for a fair and reasonable opinion from an independent financial adviser in connection with privatisations by way of scheme of arrangement. Following a review of the submissions and deliberation by the Panel and the Commission, Rule 2.10 on privatisations by way of scheme of arrangement will be amended as follows:

i. In addition to any legal requirements, a scheme can only be blocked by (a) more than 10% in value of the independent shareholders voting in person or by proxy against the proposal *and* (b) those shareholders holding more than 2.5% of the total voting rights (the 2.5% proposal).


That is to say, under the new Rule 2.10, a privatisation scheme may be passed with the vote of less than 90% (but more than 75% as required under the Companies Ordinance of Hong Kong in the case of a Hong Kong incorporated company) in value of the independent shareholders voting in person or by proxy at a general meeting so long as the independent shareholders who vote against the privatisation do not constitute more than 2.5% of the total voting rights of the company.

In deciding to adopt the 2.5% proposal, the SFC has taken into account the experience since 1993 - in particular the results of two privatisation attempts last year which narrowly failed to achieve the required level of approval. The SFC believes that maintaining the 90% voting requirement and introducing the 2.5% proposal would be an appropriate relaxation of the rule. The SFC will be sensitive to market reaction to such change and will be prepared to conduct further review should the circumstances require.

ii. The requirement that the terms of the scheme must be fair and reasonable in the opinion of an independent financial adviser is removed.


On the assumption that "opinion shopping" will be reduced, the SFC believes that removing the requirement for a "fair and reasonable" opinion will improve the likelihood for public shareholders to receive a more accurate independent financial adviser's opinion and to make their own informed decision on a privatisation proposal.

All other proposed amendments set out in the consultation paper have been adopted.

The revised Takeovers Code will apply to takeover and merger transactions with effect from 1 August 1998 other than those transactions which have already been announced prior to 1 August 1998. When in doubt, market participants should consult the Takeovers Executive in the first instance.

A full review of the Share Repurchase Code, introduced in November 1992 and currently printed together with the Takeovers Code, will be undertaken later this year.
