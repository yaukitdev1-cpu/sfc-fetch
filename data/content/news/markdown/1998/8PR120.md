The following are remarks made by the Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission, Mr Andrew Sheng, at a year-end meeting with the media today:

1998 was a year of correction from the boom year of 1997. Up to today, the Hang Seng Index is down only 5% from last year end and 39% from its peak. Our market capitalization is US$344 bn, down 16% from last year end. Average daily turnover of the HIS Futures was 28,363, about 8% up from last December.

It has been an extremely busy year for the Commission. To mention a few, we have been busy with the new investor compensation arrangements after the failure of CA Pacific. We are getting the hitherto unregulated margin finance problem under control and completing a review leading to proposals for regulation. We have just completed a review of licensing arrangement, which we hope to bring to public consultation soon. The Corporate Finance Division completed the Plain Language project, thus improving the transparency in disclosure and also a review of the Takeovers Code and the SDIO. Supervisory and enforcement work increases during a recession. We successfully completed three major insider dealing cases and yesterday resolved the Nomura case. We also successfully installed a new, sophisticated computerized market surveillance system, together with the SEHK. Throughout the recent crises, we have indeed strengthened our good cooperation and ties with the Exchanges and the clearing houses.

I have now been in office for 3 months, so the honeymoon period is over. I have had the opportunity to meet all my colleagues in the Commission, the Exchanges and clearing houses and the market community. You will recall that I said at my first press briefing that I intended to use this period to get to understand the securities markets in Hong Kong, the mainland and the region better. Let me say a few words on how I view the work of the Commission in 1999.

First, I am extremely grateful to all the warm and unstinting support I have received from the Government, market institutions and practitioners and my colleagues in the Commission in the past three months. My personal reading is that everyone feels that the Asian crisis has brought many important lessons for Hong Kong, including intensified competition, and that 1999 should be a year of consolidation and reform. More specifically, I should say that one of my priorities is to build the consensus for reform to meet the increased competition to Hong Kong from abroad, both in terms of costs, technology and new products.

I have taken the opportunity during the last 3 months to visit the stock and futures exchanges and the clearing house, and met members of the stock brokers association, as well as leading investment and securities houses. I have had very good advice from all of them, especially the present and past chairmen of the stock and futures exchanges. Last month, I had my first courtesy call on my counterpart in the CSRC in Beijing, and also met the major securities regulators overseas, when I visited the FSA in London and when we hosted the Asia-Pacific Regional Committee of IOSCO in Hong Kong in November.

1998 has been a historic and difficult year of adjustment. The year began with the failure of Peregrine and CA Pacific. As the year closed, we had the renewed challenge from SIMEX in the HiMSCI product. We find the response of the SEHK and HKFE in strengthening our competitiveness very encouraging. The linkage with NASDAQ, for example, is a first class example of the SEHK's global vision and I congratulate Mr HC Lee and his excellent team for their initiatives.

However, we cannot be complacent, and there is much to do in 1999. Let me start with work in the Commission itself.

1999 will witness on 1 May the Tenth Anniversary of the establishment of the SFC. I think the Tenth Anniversary is a very useful point of reference, whereby we can review the history of the SFC and the markets in Hong Kong, our achievements, strengths and weaknesses. The new Research Unit, which is headed by Professor Stephen Cheung, on secondment from City University, will help us produce an Anniversary booklet that will undertake this review of market developments in the last decade. I hope that the research would assist the Commission in formulating the future directions for the regulatory framework of our markets within a regional and global context. As we end one Millenium, we need to think very strategically for the new Millenium. That is the work of the Research Unit.

In my maiden speech, I said that legal reform will be a major priority for the Commission. We have already assembled a small team of legal specialists from Hong Kong and overseas to undertake the updating of our securities and futures related ordinances in Hong Kong. As part of the exercise, the team will study the relevant legislation in the United Kingdom and United States. I am personally chairing a steering committee to ensure the blueprint of the new composite Securities and Futures Ordinance will reflect not only the characteristics of the Hong Kong markets, but also the best international standards. A new composite securities and futures ordinance is a key strategy to keep Hong Kong in the forefront of financial innovation and services. After the legal team has had the chance to settle down and produce a good draft, we shall be in a better position to brief you all on its contents.

As you know, we are very fortunate to obtain the services of Mr David Stannard as our new Executive Director, Corporate Finance. He brings with him considerable experience of the corporate finance law in Hong Kong. His appointment, and that of Prof. Cheung, are examples of our efforts to bring fresh market and academic experience to the Commission. These are additions to a very strong and professional team in the Commission, as we enter into the new Millenium. As I said before, I am extremely fortunate to inherit such a strong organization from my predecessor, Anthony Neoh.

We have also appointed Ms Alexa Lam, as Senior Adviser, Chairman's Office to assist Laura Cha, the Deputy Chairman and myself in some of our work. With the arrival also of Mr Albert Lam, our Commission Secretary, our interface with the Administration has been strengthened considerably.

I think one of the most important challenges facing us all is the dramatic transformation of the securities industry through technology and financial innovation. The rise of electronic trading and new derivative products with new players, such as asset management funds and hedge funds, has created unprecedented opportunities for Hong Kong as the freest economy in the world and a leading international financial centre. These developments have also brought with them new risks and market volatility.

These profound developments have outpaced the existing legislation and major overseas markets are updating their regulatory and legal framework with great speed and innovation. We have to recognize that global markets mean that businesses vote not just with their feet, but also their terminals. Financial business is migrating rapidly to those markets which are transparent, stable, fairly and efficiently regulated and with easy entry and exit rules. Such markets require modern legislation that gives flexibility and capacity to deal with financial innovation and related global shocks, effectively minimize financial crime and improve transparency and give appropriate investor protection.

In conjunction with the legislative reform, the Commission will embark on a review of the market's technological infrastructure, including AMS 3 development at the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and the migration to electronic trading of the Hang Seng index products at the Hong Kong Futures Exchange. The Y2K issue will be a major priority for the SFC in 1999, as we embark on street-wide testing and ensure industry compliance. Internet trading will also grow very rapidly worldwide. The SFC will be strengthening our in-house technology capacity to improve our information technology and work with the industry to study how the financial technology infrastructure can be made more efficient and robust. We have appointed Dr Peter Hsueh as our new Chief Information Technology Officer.

Given the rapid changes in the markets through technology and innovation, I am not surprised that many market participants are not fully aware of their rights and responsibilities and also the risks involved. As a knowledge-based institution, we see one of the Commission's key roles as educating market participants, investors as well as intermediaries of their roles, risks, rights and responsibilities. We will work closely with the Exchanges and clearing houses, the professional associations, the Securities Institute, the academic community and the media, to strengthen investor education. We will strive to improve transparency in our work and to ensure that there is continuous dialogue with the market before making important policy decisions and when implementing these decisions.

In light of the adverse economic conditions, we are exercising stringent control over our expenditure. As we announced recently, there will be a general wage freeze next year. We will be fully accountable for the use of the public funds.

Finally, I would like to thank the SFC staff for their dedication and support in 1998. The SFC will work even harder to ensure the markets are regulated to the best international standards in the New Year. We look forward to the continued support of the Government, the financial community, the investing public and the media in 1999.

Happy New Year!
