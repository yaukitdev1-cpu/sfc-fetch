The Court of Appeal today handed down its judgment in the Securities and Futures Commission's appeal against a decision by Mr Justice Stone in the Court of First Instance. In that decision, Mr Justice Stone stayed the SFC's disciplinary proceedings against Nomura International (Hong Kong) Limited ("Nomura"), one of its directors and certain of its employees.

The Court of Appeal unanimously allowed the SFC's appeal, dismissed Nomura's cross appeal and made an order that the costs of the appeal, the cross appeal and the costs in the Court below be costs to the SFC.

In May and July 1997, the SFC issued "Letters of Mindedness" regarding possible disciplinary action against Nomura and certain of its employees following an inquiry into their role in certain securities and futures trading on Australian markets. The letters gave the recipients the opportunity to make representations and submit evidence before any final determination was made.

On 21 July 1997, Nomura and the employees requested the SFC to extend the time for receiving representations and to stay its proceedings pending the outcome of litigation in Australia between the Australian Securities Commission and Nomura International plc, London. On 23 October, the SFC indicated it was unwilling to accede to this request. On 24 November, Nomura obtained leave from the High Court to apply for judicial review of this SFC decision. The application, which was opposed by the SFC, was heard on 9 and 11 December when Nomura sought, amongst other things, to stay the SFC's proceedings.

On 22 December, the Court granted Nomura an order which effectively prohibited the SFC from proceeding further in relation to Nomura and the employees before 31 May 1998. Given the Australian litigation against Nomura plc, the Court's view was that this is the earliest date upon which Nomura and the employees should be obliged to respond to the issues raised by the SFC in its letters of mindedness. The Court accepted Nomura's argument that there would be a serious risk of real prejudice that would lead to injustice if Nomura had to respond any earlier. The SFC lodged an appeal to the Court of Appeal, which was heard in April 1998. Nomura also applied to vary the order, so as to prohibit the SFC from proceeding further until judgment is given in the Australian court proceedings.

The Court of Appeal decided that Nomura had not established sufficient evidence of a real risk of prejudice.

In delivering their judgments, each member of the Court made reference to the considerable importance of the functions which the SFC is discharging in particular as they relate to the integrity of the Hong Kong securities industry. The judgments made it clear that the SFC should not be prevented from carrying out its statutory and public duties unless there are clear and strong ground for doing so. The Court considered that the effectiveness of disciplinary proceedings is undoubtedly diminished if delay occurs.
