The Securities and Futures Commission and the German Federal Securities Supervisory Office (Bundesaufsichtsamt fur den Wertpapierhandel, Germany, or BAWe) signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) yesterday in Hong Kong. The BAWe is an independent federal authority which began its regulatory activity on 1 January 1995. It is responsible for ensuring the proper functioning of the securities markets in Germany and has amongst its objectives investor protection, market transparency and market integrity.

The MOU establishes a framework for mutual assistance and facilitates the exchange of information between the two authorities. The scope of this MOU includes the enforcement of laws, rules and regulations relating to securities and futures markets; clearing and settlement activities; takeovers and mergers; the disclosure of information relevant to investors; the prevention of insider dealing, market manipulation and other fraudulent practices; and the fitness and properness and conduct of business standards of advisers, traders and other financial market practitioners. The BAWe possesses powers to provide investigatory assistance, similar to section 59A of the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance, which they are willing to exercise on behalf of the SFC.

The MOU was signed by Mr Anthony Neoh, Chairman of SFC and Mr Georg Wittich, Chairman of BAWe. Mr Wittich is in Hong Kong attending the meetings of the Technical Committee of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO). Mr Neoh is the Chairman of the Technical Committee.

Mr Neoh said: "We are delighted to be able to enhance our working relationship with our counterparts in Germany. This Memorandum, together with the Declaration of Co-operation on Supervision of Cross-border Investment Management Activity signed on 30 December 1997, establish effective co-operative arrangements between the financial regulators in Germany and the SFC in Hong Kong which in turn greatly enhances the regulators' capabilities in the protection of investors and promoting the integrity of their financial markets."

Mr Wittich said: "The exchange of confidential information is of great importance for the national securities supervisory authorities. Regulators must be in a position to exercise cross-border supervision of internationally active financial markets and market participants. With this Memorandum of Understanding the BAWe consistently extends its co-operation with foreign supervisory authorities."

With the signature of this MOU with the BAWe, the total number of MOUs and similar co-operative arrangements entered into between the SFC and overseas regulators has reached 29.
