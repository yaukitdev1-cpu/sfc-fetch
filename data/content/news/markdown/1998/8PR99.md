The Securities and Futures Commission has approved at a meeting today the Hong Kong Futures Exchange’s proposal to extend the trading hours of its Hang Seng index futures and options markets from 20 November 1998.

In arriving at its decision, the Commission has considered the regulatory implications of, and all relevant arguments for or against the proposed extension.

The Commission noted that after-hours trading would allow market participants to observe the sentiment of the close of the cash market to decide on their hedging or trading strategies, while early trading hours would provide a price discovery mechanism for the cash market by reflecting the overnight trading in overseas markets. It was suggested that the proposal was consistent with practices in major markets where trading hours for the futures market are usually longer than the cash market.

The Commission also noted concerns about possible market manipulation which might occur due to the difference in closing times of the cash and futures markets.

The Commission is of the view that even if the cash and futures markets in Hong Kong were to have the same trading hours, manipulative activities, if any, could occur in overseas markets. Hence, disallowing Hong Kong Futures Exchange’s proposed extension of trading hours would not entirely remove the possibility of manipulation. The Commission believes that it would be in a better position to detect any such manipulation in our own market.

In this connection, the Hong Kong Futures Exchange has been asked to consider, and to report to the SFC, additional surveillance measures for any malpractices during the extended trading hours.

The Commission considered that it was also a commercial issue for the Hong Kong Futures Exchange to extend its trading hours and therefore approved the requested extension.
