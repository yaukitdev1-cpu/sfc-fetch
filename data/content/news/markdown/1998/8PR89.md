The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (SEHK) and Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) announced today new rules requiring SEHK Members and persons registered with the SFC to obtain and record information about the ultimate originators and beneficiaries of securities and futures transactions before such transactions are processed.

The rules have been introduced as part of the Government's 30-point plan to strengthen Hong Kong's securities and futures markets, and are consistent with rules applying in major developed markets. They will not affect the confidentiality of client information because this information would only be disclosed to the SEHK or the SFC upon request by either of the two organisations.

The SEHK and SFC appreciate that it may take industry participants may need some time to time to adjust their business systems to fully comply with the new rules, and will take a pragmatic approach to the enforcement of the new rules. Therefore, both organisations will take a pragmatic approach to introduction of these rules, and also appreciate that the new rules may have some unintended consequences, and will review their operation in the light of practical experience.

The rules will be incorporated into the SEHK's Rules of the Exchange and its Code of Conduct Regulations and the SFC's Code of Conduct for Persons Registered with the SFC respectively. The SFC rule supplements the existing "Know Your Client" obligations in the Code of Conduct.

In relation to discretionary accounts and investment funds, the rules will only require that the name of the discretionary account or investment fund be recorded, together with that of the manager of that account or fund. However, if the investment discretion of a manager of a discretionary account or investment fund is overridden by the instructions of a beneficiary of that account or investment fund, the name of the beneficiary must be recorded.

SEHK Chief Executive Mr Alec Tsui, said "the implementation of the new rules will strengthen the orderly operation of our market." SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng said the new rules would significantly enhance the ability of the SEHK and the SFC to monitor trading in Hong Kong's markets.
