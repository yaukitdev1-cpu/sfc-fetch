The Securities and Futures Commission announced today that it has notified approximately 100 licensed intermediaries that disciplinary action may be initiated against them unless they report promptly to the SFC on the status of their Year 2000 readiness measures. The SFC had previously notified intermediaries of their Year 2000 compliance obligations, provided guidance notes and sample methodologies for dealing with the problem, and given a deadline for reporting.

"This action underscores the seriousness with which the SFC regards the Year 2000 issue," said Mr Andrew Procter, SFC Executive Director for the Intermediaries and Investment Products Division. "An intermediary's failure to protect his business from possible failures resulting from Year 2000 computer problems not only threatens the survival of that business, but could also have serious consequences for investor protection and market integrity."

The Code of Conduct for Persons Registered with the SFC as well as the Management, Supervision and Internal Control Guidelines require intermediaries to have policies and procedures in place to protect their operations and clients from financial loss. Failure to take adequate steps to address the Year 2000 problem could result in non-compliance with these requirements. An intermediary's failure to report to the SFC on its Year 2000 compliance measures might therefore result in disciplinary action, including suspension of registration.

From March - April 1998, the SFC conducted a survey of securities, futures and leveraged foreign exchange intermediaries in Hong Kong (other than members of The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and Hong Kong Futures Exchange) to determine their readiness to address the Year 2000 problem. The survey results indicated that an overwhelming majority of licensed or exempt intermediaries was aware of the Year 2000 issue. However, it also revealed that considerable work is still required for many intermediaries to adequately address the problem.

Subsequent to the survey, the SFC commenced and will continue carrying out on-site inspections on licensed businesses to ensure they are devoting sufficient efforts and resources to address the Year 2000 issue properly. In discharging its regulatory functions, the SFC also considers it necessary to require intermediaries to report the progress of their Year 2000 project on a periodic basis. The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and Hong Kong Futures Exchange impose similar reporting and compliance requirements on their members.

To raise awareness of the Year 2000 problem and to help intermediaries ensure their businesses are Year 2000-compliant, the SFC has established a Year 2000 section on its website, located at [http://www.hksfc.org.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/). The Year 2000 section includes SFC letters to intermediaries, guidance notes, recommended methodologies, testing scenarios, reporting forms, the SFC's own monthly progress reports, and links to other Year 2000 resources on the Internet.
