The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC*)* today issues a consultation paper on certain proposed amendments to the Securities (Disclosure of Interests) Ordinance (the Ordinance). The public is invited to comment on the proposals before 31 August 1998.

The objective of the Ordinance is to provide the market with information about interests in shares held by substantial shareholders and directors of listed companies so as to reduce insider dealing and improve transparency of the market place. The Ordinance was introduced in 1991 and has not been revised since then. With the significant development of the international and Hong Kong markets and the proliferation in the use of derivatives and other innovative financial products in recent years, the SFC believes that the Ordinance should be updated and improved to provide more transparency to the market place and to ensure that Hong Kong's disclosure regime meets international and regional disclosure standards.

Mrs. Laura Cha, Deputy Chairman and Executive Director of the SFC, said: "The proposals in the Consultation Paper are intended to increase market transparency in order to maintain Hong Kong's competitiveness as an international financial centre. However, we are also mindful that this objective must be balanced with the need to minimise the compliance burden of the market. Therefore, our proposals also include areas where burdensome requirements are to be removed. The SFC's proposals were the subject of informal consultation with a number of market groups in the past years."

The Consultation Paper seeks comments on 14 areas. The principal proposals for reform are summarised as follows:

(1) To ensure that Hong Kong's disclosure regime keeps pace with international and regional standards, the SFC recommends:

(a) reduction of the initial substantial shareholding disclosure threshold from 10% to 5%; and 

(b) shortening of the notification period from 5 calendar days to 2 business days.

(2) To remove unnecessary and unduly burdensome requirements under the current Ordinance and reduce compliance costs, the SFC proposes that:

(a) the existing requirement to disclose details of registered shareholders and their shareholdings be removed;

(b) provisions be introduced to exempt substantial shareholders from the obligations to disclose when their interests in shares fluctuate by a de minimis amount; and

(c) multiple filings of notification forms be reduced by allowing disclosures to be made on a group basis. A draft notification form is attached to the Consultation Paper for public comment.

(3) The SFC proposes to improve the extent of information available on price, securities dealings and persons having interests in shares in the following areas:

(a) It is proposed that substantial shareholders be required in the same manner as listed company directors to disclose the consideration in acquiring or disposing of interests in shares. Substantial shareholders and directors will also be required to disclose terms of agreements relating to off-exchange transactions that give rise to a disclosure obligation. 

(b) In order to improve transparency on corporate shareholders, it is proposed that an unlisted corporate substantial shareholder be required to disclose details of any person holding 10% or more of its issued share capital.

(c) To provide the market with information on the ultimate owners of a listed company, it is proposed that a trustee of a discretionary trust be required to disclose the identity of any "settlor" of the trust. Further, a "settlor" of a discretionary trust will be deemed to be interested in the shares held by the trust and may, as a result, be under a separate duty of disclosure. A proposed definition of "settlor" is included for comment in the Consultation Paper.

(d) In light of recent development of financial products in the market, the SFC considers it necessary to review the extent to which derivative products should be subject to disclosure under the Ordinance. The circumstances under which a person may be required to disclose derivatives and the details to be disclosed are discussed in detail in the Consultation Paper.

(e) It is also proposed that the exemption currently made available to Hong Kong registered investment managers and trust companies under the Securities (Disclosure of Interests)(Exclusions) Regulations be removed.
