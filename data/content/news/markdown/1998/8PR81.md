The Securities and Futures Commission today issued for public consultation proposals for new investor compensation arrangements for clients of members of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong.

In developing the proposals, a number of objectives have been set:

- To enhance the existing investor compensation arrangement and increase investor confidence while keeping costs commensurate with benefits. It is also important to avoid creating moral hazards;
- To provide a secure per investor level of compensation for retail investors under a formal and transparent structure that is easy to understand;
- To provide the compensation arrangement through a new independent entity that includes industry and public interest representatives and is subject to checks and balances;
- To protect and leverage existing compensation fund assets, including use of insurance, while minimizing any additional cost to the industry;
- To employ market-based commercial risk management mechanisms and incentives within the arrangement; and
- To initially provide a new compensation arrangement in relation to members of the Stock Exchange, but within a flexible structure that will allow additional arrangements to be developed for other segments of the market (such as securities margin finance company dealers and members of the Hong Kong Futures Exchange).

Under the proposals, new legislation would define an investor compensation company, which would be recognised and regulated by the SFC, for facilitating compensation arrangements relating to transactions in securities or futures contracts.

The company would be independent, with its board comprising representatives from the SFC and the Stock Exchange, Financial Secretary appointees to represent the public interest, and a director with insurance and risk management expertise. The corporate structure proposed and subject to discussion with the Stock Exchange is that of a company limited by guarantee with the SFC and the Stock Exchange being members and providing undertakings to contribute to the company in the event of its winding-up.

The substantive and procedural requirements for claiming compensation would be contained in the company's rules, which would require SFC approval.

A main feature would be the introduction of a per investor level of compensation. It is estimated that the level would be between $100,000 and $250,000 using existing compensation fund assets and subject to negotiation with insurance underwriters.

Under the proposed risk financing structure, no new funds would likely be required from Stock Exchange members or investors. However, a regular funding source would be considered if assets fall below a prudential level. On the other hand, the structure would be sufficiently flexible to provide higher per investor levels of compensation if risk exposures decrease or available funding increases.

A specified amount of risk would be retained by the company with certain of the excess risk covered by insurance. Specialist underwriters would assist in quantifying risk and in recommending ways to improve client asset protection mechanisms.

The implementation of planned regulatory measures to reduce risk to client assets should reduce the costs of the new arrangements or enable higher investor compensation limits in future. These include proposals to regulate share margin financing and changes to the Financial Resources Rules.

Mr Anthony Neoh, Chairman of the SFC, said: "The structure should not create significant moral hazards. By this we mean the danger that brokers and investors might lower their attention to risk due to an over-reliance on the compensation arrangement."

"We believe our focus on retail investors coupled with the existing funding constraints and related levels of compensation achievable mitigate against moral hazards," he said.

Introduction of the new arrangements would require extensive legislative amendments, which will be the key factor determining when the arrangements can be put in place.
