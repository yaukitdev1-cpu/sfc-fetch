The members of the Asia Pacific Regional Committee (APRC) of the International Organisation of Securities Commissions (IOSCO), representing securities regulators from 16 regulatory bodies (see Attendance List), met today in Hong Kong as a follow up to their meeting in Nairobi, Kenya, in September, in conjunction with the IOSCO Annual Conference, to review their approach to the recent market turbulence in the Asian region. The meeting was chaired by Mr D R Mehta, Chairman of the Securities and Exchange Board of India.

The members of the Committee heard reports on recent market developments, and considered issues such as :-

- The operations of hedge funds.
- Corporate governance initiatives in the region.
- Proposals for multilateral training.
- Co-operation between them with respect to cross border market surveillance.
- Recent reports issued by global groupings such as the G22, with respect to the global financial architecture.
- The role played by international rating agencies in the period leading up to, and during, the market turbulence.
- Y2K readiness efforts in regional markets.
The members of the Committee reaffirmed their commitment to high levels of transparency and disclosure in financial markets and decided :-

1. To set up a working party to gather information on hedge funds and to examine the issues of regulation and better risk management of highly leveraged activities. The working party will support and contribute to similar studies within IOSCO and other forums.
2. To strengthen the dialogue with banking regulators in the region to improve the effectiveness of financial market regulation in the region generally.
3. To arrange participation by their staff in training activities being conducted by members of the APRC and take up proposals from the Asian Development Bank for assistance for those and other multilateral training activities.
4. To encourage its members to take active steps to support the development and adoption of high standards of corporate governance, particularly in listed companies and in the small and medium size enterprise sector; and to establish a dialogue group to be led by Malaysia and Australia to monitor developments in this area and report at future meetings of the Committee.
5. To improve regulatory co-operation, including information exchange, in order to strengthen market surveillance and maintain overall market transparency and integrity.
6. To note that a regional meeting to deal with the Y2K issue had been held in Sydney in October 1998.
7. To continue their project on competitive policy and securities market regulation: Towards a Framework for Cost Benefit Analysis.


Mr Mehta on behalf of the member delegates warmly thanked the Hong Kong Securities and Futures Commission for graciously hosting the meeting.
