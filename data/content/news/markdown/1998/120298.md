The Securities and Futures Commission today issues a consultation paper on a review of the Hong Kong Code on Takeovers and Mergers. The public is invited to comment on the proposals set out in the paper.

The Code was first introduced in 1975, and was reviewed in detail between 1989 and 1991, resulting in the current version being published in 1992. Since then the only major amendment was Rule 2.10 regarding privatisation by way of scheme of arrangement, which was introduced in 1993. It has now been five years since the last version was published, and the SFC believes a review of the Code is necessary.

The review was carried out by the Corporate Finance Division of the SFC, with participation of the Takeovers and Mergers Panel and assistance of the Special Adviser to the SFC, Mr Christopher de Boer, a former Chairman of the Panel. A total of eight Panel meetings were conducted between October and December 1997. The paper and the proposed amendments to the Code were prepared in light of the discussions and decisions of the Panel.

Mrs Laura Cha, SFC Deputy Chairman and Executive Director of Corporate Finance Division, said, "Since the Code is considered to be sound in its basic objective to protect public shareholders when there is a change of control in public companies, no changes are proposed to the administration of the Code or its voluntary nature. However, the latter aspect will be kept under continuing review."

"This present review aims to refine the Code and to provide additional interpretive guidance based primarily on the relevant experience in Hong Kong over the last 5 years," she said.

Some of the principal areas reviewed and changes recommended are as follows:

Suitability of financial advisers in takeover matters

A new paragraph is added to Section 1.7 of the Code to emphasize that financial advisers must be able to satisfy the Takeovers Executive that they have the professional competence and adequate resources to fulfil their role. This follows a Court judgment last year in which it was held that the Executive was entitled to be satisfied on these points.

*Privatisations by way of scheme of arrangement*

One of the significant issues under review is Rule 2.10 on privatisations by scheme of arrangement. In light of the experience since 1993 - in particular the results of two privatisation attempts last year which narrowly failed to achieve the required level of approval, several options have been proposed for consultation:

(i) maintaining the Rule in its present form, ie keeping the required level of independent shareholders' approval at 90%;

(ii) reducing the level to 75%;

(iii) changing the Rule so that in addition to any legal requirements, a scheme can only be blocked by (a) more than 10% of the independent shareholders voting in person or by proxy against the proposal *and* (b) those shareholders holding more than 2.5% of the total voting rights; and

(iv) removing the requirement that the terms of the scheme must be fair and reasonable in the opinion of an independent financial adviser.

The market's views are sought on any aspects of the above options. Mrs. Cha indicated that the SFC is sympathetic with the argument that presently a very small minority can block a fair and reasonable proposal and therefore considers option (iii) to be viable.

*Guidance note for whitewash transactions*

Apart from the proposed removal of the application of Rule 5 (regarding withdrawal of an offer) and Rule 22 (immediate disclosure of dealings during an offer period), there are no major changes to the requirements currently applied by the Takeovers Executive. The Guidance Note (Schedule VI of the Code) is essentially a clarification of the Executive's practice since 1992.

*Change of leadership within a concert group*

Currently if a member of a concert group increases his shareholding and becomes the leader of the group, he may incur a general offer obligation. It is proposed to clarify the application of the basic provisions of Rule 26.1 in such circumstances and to address the possible avoidance of a general offer obligation by arrangements between the vendor and purchaser of voting rights.

There is currently no proposal to change the general offer threshold, which would remain at 35%, and the "creeper" level, which would remain at 5% for any 12-month period. Based on the discussions conducted with the Panel, these two issues will be reviewed again later this year.

The Share Repurchase Code, introduced in November 1992 and currently printed together with the Code on Takeovers and Mergers, is not the subject of the present review. It is intended a full review would be undertaken later this year.
