Regulators should carefully study the pros and cons of the emergence of alternative trading venues including dark pools before making any policy changes, the Chief Executive Officer of the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), Mr Martin Wheatley said.

Speaking today at the Trade Tech Asia 2009 conference in Singapore, Mr Wheatley noted the proliferation of dark pools has not only raised concerns over transparency and fairness of alternative institutional trading venues to other market participants, but also potential difficulties for regulators to conduct effective market surveillance.

On the other hand, overseas experience shows that alternative trading venues provide benefits to the market such as reducing trading costs, enhancing overall market liquidity, providing innovative trading tools and allowing faster execution, Mr Wheatley said.

“I think when it comes to regulating alternative trading venue, it’s crucial to strike a proper balance between realising the potential benefits offered by alternative trading venues and reducing their potential risks,” said Mr Wheatley.

Mr Wheatley said global regulators have been paying more attention to the regulatory implications that dark pools may have on the integrity of the market.

He also pointed out dark pools raised the issues of fragmentation of pricing data and of price discovery function currently performed by stock exchanges.

While dark pools have gained significant footholds in the US and Europe, Mr Wheatley said they remain at an infancy stage in Asia.

In Hong Kong, off-exchange transactions reported to the exchange accounted for only about 3% of total market turnover on most trading days (Note 1). On a few occasions involving index rebalancing, off-exchange transactions reported to the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong could exceed 10% of the total market turnover.

“This illustrates that there may be a case for the presence of alternative trading venues to meet specific trading needs in the market,” said Mr Wheatley.

The SFC is currently studying the market structure and dark liquidity, with a view to identifying appropriate ways for alternative trading venues to develop in Hong Kong, he added.
