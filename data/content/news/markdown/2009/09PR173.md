In its [Quarterly Report](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/quartely/jul_sep_09.pdf) for July to September 2009, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) notes that it has made a number of proposals to enhance investor protection based on issues identified in its various investigations into the sales of Minibond products.

The report also highlighted a major success in combating insider dealing.

Using powers under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, the SFC had helped arrange the repurchase scheme for 16 Minibond distributing banks in the best interests of the investing public. As of the end of the reporting period, the scheme had received a high level of investor acceptance (Note 1).

Two months after announcing the repurchase scheme, the SFC began a three-month consultation to solicit public comments on proposals to fine-tune regulations governing the sale of retail investment products to the public. The co-ordinated set of proposals cover each stage of the investment life cycle, including pre-sale documentation, disclosure at the point of sale, selling practices, on-going disclosure during the term of investment, and a post-sale cooling-off period.

During the reporting period, a District Court imposed its maximum possible jail sentence of seven years on the former director of a major international investment bank for insider dealing, reinforcing the SFC's message that such misconduct will not be tolerated and will be tackled aggressively.

The SFC has also launched the first phase of its On-line Portal which allows more than 36,000 licensees, registrants and associated entities the option of submitting many licensing-related documents electronically and to receive SFC messages via the Internet.

In line with using mass media to reach as many of the public as possible, the SFC launched a mini television series and infomercials to promote the do's and don'ts of investing.

The latest Quarterly Report is available on the SFC website at [www.sfc.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/index.html).
