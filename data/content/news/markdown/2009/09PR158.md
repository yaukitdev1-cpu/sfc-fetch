The Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), Dr Eddy Fong, has called on market practitioners and investors to respond to proposals recently announced by the SFC on how to enhance investor protection regarding the sale of investment products.

Speaking today at the Financial Planning Conference 2009 organised by the Institute of Financial Planners of Hong Kong, Dr Fong said there is a need to rebuild the trust of investors after the financial crisis so that the market can continue to function efficiently.

“It is important for financial intermediaries to realise there is no going back to ‘business as normal’. It’s therefore vital for both policy makers and market participants to work together for a more resilient financial system and this may mean adjusting to a ‘new normal’,” Dr Fong told the conference.

The proposals, contained in an SFC consultation paper, comprise three major components: enhanced disclosure of product risks and features, a revised code to govern the conduct of intermediaries and a new concept of a cooling-off period for long-term illiquid products.

Dr Fong said while the proposed measures are not a panacea for all the problems, they should be able to adequately tackle most of the concerns that have surfaced.

“For any proposal or financial reform to work, the efforts of the regulator alone are not sufficient. We need the participation and co-operation of industry to make it work,” Dr Fong said. “Our intention is to put in place a system which strikes a good balance that will provide enough flexibility for market players and sufficient investor protection.”

Dr Fong called on market practitioners in the frontline to make their views heard in their organisations, adding their feedback would be invaluable to the SFC and the way forward on enhancing the financial regime and investor protection.
