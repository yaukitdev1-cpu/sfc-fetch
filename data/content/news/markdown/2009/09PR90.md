As it looks back over the 20 years since its formation, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) is also looking ahead to consider the regulatory changes required to address that the many issues that the financial crisis has raised over the past 12 months.

In its 2008-09 Annual Report, published today, the SFC considers the difficulties experienced by major financial markets around the world as an opportunity to review regulatory structures both globally and in Hong Kong. However, it cautions that any review must be done carefully and thoughtfully to ensure that any changes made bring benefit to the various stakeholders in our financial markets.

“Regulatory reform is not merely more but better regulation. All stakeholders will have a role to play in maintaining a well-functioning and robust financial system,” says SFC Chairman, Mr Eddy Fong.

The financial crisis last year has highlighted the need for closer international co-operation between regulators to identify and manage macro-prudential risk across the global financial system.

SFC Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley, says that the SFC will closely monitor and observe these international efforts to implement regulatory reforms. However, in Hong Kong, the SFC also has a set of priorities focusing on further enhancing investor protection.

“Close supervision and firm enforcement will continue,” says Mr Wheatley. “We will be increasing our resources in these areas in order to combat and minimise market misconduct and encourage regulatory compliance. Upgrading and increasing programmes that deliver investor education will complement these efforts.”

The SFC gave an account of the steps it took in managing the effects of the financial tsunami on Hong Kong, including discussions on maintaining the standards of intermediaries and the conclusion of the first two Minibond investigations. Against the backdrop of an economic downturn, its investor education programme has focused on explaining risk management skills, promoting a proper investment attitude, and preparing investors for market volatility.

To mark the SFC’s 20th anniversary, the report carries a special chapter featuring interviews with former SFC chairmen, alumni and 20-year staff members.
