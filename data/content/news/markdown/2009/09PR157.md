The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today signed a deal with the Securities Commission of Malaysia (SC) that will foster the further development of Islamic fund management in Hong Kong and strengthen co-operation between the two regulators.

The Declaration on Mutual Cooperation on Development of Islamic Capital Market and Islamic Collective Investment Schemes provides for the mutual recognition of Islamic collective investment schemes (CISs) between the two jurisdictions. This will facilitate the cross-border offering of these schemes, which will be subject to a streamlined vetting process, to investors in the two markets (Notes 1 and 2).

"I am delighted at the signing of the Declaration. This not only marks a milestone in the regulatory co-operation with our Malaysian counterpart, but also contributes to the government's objective to promote Islamic finance in Hong Kong," the SFC’s Chairman, Dr Eddy Fong, said at the signing ceremony.

"Investors in both markets will enjoy a wider choice of investments with the cross-border offering of Islamic CISs. We look forward to seeing the growth of Islamic investment expertise in Hong Kong along with expanded business opportunities," Dr Fong added.

“The linkages with leading financial centres such as Hong Kong will raise the international profile of the Islamic capital market and will benefit our respective intermediaries and investors,” said SC Chairman Tan Sri Zarinah Anwar.

“This initiative will also support the Malaysian government’s agenda towards promoting Malaysia as a major hub for Islamic finance, including Islamic fund intermediation,” she added.

The Declaration also promotes co-operation and information sharing between the SFC and the SC in relation to Islamic CISs, as well as the exchange of regulatory experience relating to Shariah principles governing these schemes.

The signing ceremony, which took place in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, was also witnessed by the Permanent Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury (Financial Services), Miss Au King Chi, JP. In her address, Miss Au shared Hong Kong's aspirations and efforts in developing Islamic finance, as well as its vision in furthering regional co-operation.

Notes:

1. SC-approved Islamic CISs (excluding hedge funds, wholesale funds and real estate investment trusts) primarily regulated by the SC and managed by SC licensees seeking authorisation from the SFC will be subject to a streamlined vetting process.
2. SFC-authorized Islamic CISs (excluding hedge funds, leveraged funds and real estate investment trusts) primarily regulated by the SFC, managed by SFC licensees and domiciled in either (i) Hong Kong or (ii) jurisdictions which are signatories of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding and considered by the SC to have broadly implemented IOSCO Principles 17 to 20, will be subject to a streamlined vetting process in their application for authorisation by the SC.
