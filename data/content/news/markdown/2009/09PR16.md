Many investors are attracted to guaranteed funds as relatively safe investments. However, they are not completely risk-free and investors must understand how the guarantee works before making a commitment.

In his latest column entitled [“When is a guarantee not a guarantee?”](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2009articles/guaran_feb09.html), Dr Wise reminds investors that the guarantee in a guaranteed fund is not equivalent to principal protection. The guarantee is conditional and like other investments, a guaranteed fund exposes an investor to some risk.

The guarantee of a guaranteed fund works by paying back investors a pre-determined percentage of the invested capital, which may or may not be 100%, if the realised value of the underlying investment is less than the guaranteed amount on the fund’s maturity. After fees are deducted, a portion of the subscription money is invested in a portfolio of investments designed to produce an amount equal to the guaranteed amount on the fund’s maturity. The balance is placed in other assets, which may include structured notes, to achieve a yield over and above the guaranteed amount. If the realised value of the underlying investment is less than the guaranteed amount at maturity, the guarantor will cover the shortfall.

However, Dr Wise notes that pre-determined conditions must be met for the guarantee to function. These conditions often will include requiring investors to hold the investment until a specified date, usually the maturity date, and ensuring that the same fund manager manages the fund until maturity. In addition, investors are exposed to the credit risk of the guarantor. If the conditions for the guarantee are not met, or the guarantor itself fails, then investors are exposed to all the risks of the underlying investments and could end up recovering an amount that is less, or even significantly less, than the principal.

“Guaranteed funds can provide investors with a less risky option than an equivalent unprotected fund. However, investors need to recognise that a guaranteed fund is not the same as a principal-protected instrument,” Dr Wise says. Before investing in a guaranteed fund, he advises investors to find out and understand the conditions of the guarantee.
