In his latest column entitled “[Are all SFC-authorised products suitable for everyone](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2009articles/auth_mar09.html),” Dr Wise reminds investors that authorisation is only the first of two levels in the Securities and Futures Commission’s (SFC) regulatory regime of providing investors with a degree of protection.

With authorisation, the SFC reviews the documentation, and in some cases the structural features of an investment, to see if legal requirements and certain impartial benchmarks are met, but more importantly, whether the required information is disclosed. However, this authorisation does not equate to a recommendation or endorsement of the product nor does it mean that the product is suitable for everyone.

The second level of protection lies with the Code of Conduct for intermediaries. Intermediaries who sell the product to investors must ensure the suitability of the investment for each individual, taking into account his or her particular circumstances. On their part, investors should not just take the intermediaries’ recommendations at face value but should also ask relevant questions to ensure that the investment meets their needs.

“Investors should note that marketing materials only contains highlights. Before making a commitment, investors should check the product’s offering document, then evaluate the intermediary’s recommendation and the product risks against their own circumstances,” Dr Wise advises.
