In his latest column entitled “[Beyond bonds: Learn about bond funds](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2009articles/fund_sep09.html),” Dr Wise advises investors not to regard bond funds as a direct proxy for bonds.

Bond funds have different characteristics from single bonds, including their risk profiles, which may vary widely from fund to fund, Dr Wise explains. Although bond funds invest in bonds, they can also invest in other debt securities and are actively managed as a portfolio by professional managers according to prescribed investment objectives and policies. Dr Wise offers investors an overview of the pros and cons of the two products (see also appendix):


- For investors seeking broad exposure, bond funds may offer an advantage as they allow investors exposure to a diversified portfolio for a relatively small investment. Individual bonds, however, are often sold in large denominations so a much bigger commitment is needed to achieve similar diversification.
- Those counting on fixed income should take care with bond funds. While most bonds pay interest regularly in the form of coupon payment, bond funds may not distribute dividends regularly.
- Unlike bonds, bond funds are not obliged to repay the principal to investors on a set maturity date. In fact, they do not have maturity dates and are usually open-ended. While a bond held to maturity will return the principal invested, a bond fund will expose the holder to fluctuations in market price as underlying investments are constantly re-valued.
- In terms of liquidity, bond funds are more appealing. Many bonds will not have an active secondary market but units in most bond funds can be bought or sold in daily dealing.
Despite the differences, both bond funds and bonds share some common risks associated with investing in bonds: the risk profile/credit risk of the bond issuers, the liquidity of the underlying debt securities, the exposure to interest rate fluctuations, currency fluctuation and inflation risk. And if the price of a bond fund drops drastically, investors may lose money even if they still receive dividends from it.

“Before deciding on a bond fund, please understand your own investment profile and objectives to make sure that the investment meets your individual needs,” Dr Wise reminds investors, adding that they should also consider the types of bonds/assets underlying a bond fund as they will affect a bond fund’s performance.
