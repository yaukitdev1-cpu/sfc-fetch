The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today issued a new set of questions and answers providing further guidance to broker firms in relation to their securities margin financing business.

The SFC has stepped up monitoring of broker firms’ securities margin financing activities since 2008. A number of risk reminders have been issued to relevant broker firms and five training sessions on margin controls have been provided to broker firms as well as their auditors over the past 12 months. The SFC has also engaged an international accounting firm, KPMG, to assist in a special review of 10 selected broker firms focusing on their financial soundness and margin controls amidst deteriorating market conditions.

The special review identified some common margin control and risk management issues, including:


- insufficient documentation of margin call follow-up actions;
- inadequate controls over stock collateral and client concentration;
- a lack of proper justification for significant deviation from established margin policy, including allowing further purchases by clients when the clients have yet to meet outstanding margin calls; and
- a lack of formal stress testing procedures.

In light of these findings, a new set of questions and answers in respect of securities margin financing is now posted on the SFC website ([www.sfc.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/faqs/super/faq_ml_240809_eng.pdf)). Broker firms are reminded to pay attention to the guidance provided in the questions and answers as well as the requirements in the Code of Conduct (Note 1) and Internal Control Guidelines (Note 2) in developing their margin policies and operating their securities margin financing business.

“We note that total margin loans outstanding have increased to $26 billion at the end of June 2009 versus $17 billion at the end of 2008,” said Mr Martin Wheatley, the Chief Executive Officer of the SFC. “Senior management of broker firms are reminded to maintain a strong risk management and control system to rigorously manage securities margin financing risks. Broker firms should remain vigilant and conduct regular stress tests to assess the adequacy of their financial resources in view of changing market conditions.”
