Recent events have led many investors to view compensation as key part of investor protection. However, the key to guarding investors’ interests actually lies in a coordinated set of regulations that covers the entire investment life cycle – from the pre-sale stage through the sale process and then in the post-sale follow-up. Investors also have a responsibility for their own decisions and they must carefully consider the information and explanations given to them.

In his latest column entitled “[Your opinion counts](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2009articles/protection_oct09.html)”, Dr Wise highlights some important proposals by the Securities and Futures Commission that aim to enhance investor protection. These proposals are set out in a public consultation document and Dr Wise urges investors to “help the regulator help them” by providing feedback on these suggestions. He highlights some of the key proposals which include:


- Product summaries – Product issuers should be required to prepare “Key Facts Statements” that summarise the terms and details of the investment product being offered, in an easy-to-understand way, within just a few pages. These statements would form part of the much lengthier offering documents.
- Transparency of commissions and benefits – Selling intermediaries would have to disclose to their clients, at the pre-sale stage, any commissions, fees or other benefits that they would earn from the product issuer.
- Conditional cooling-off period – The proposed cooling-off period would be for longer-term, illiquid investment products so that investors do not get locked into long term products that they don’t want. There would also be some costs associated with an investor exercising this option.

Dr Wise urges investors to “Share your thoughts – they really make a difference.” From now until 31 December, members of the public should take the opportunity to voice their views on the proposed changes which are designed to help improve the regulations that protect them when making investments.

Through his column, Dr Wise, a fictitious character created by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), shares with the public his wisdom on investing and his thoughts on regulatory issues.
