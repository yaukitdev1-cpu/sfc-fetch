Online trading is gaining popularity among retail investors as technology advances, but it may not be suitable for all investor types.

In his latest column, “[How to get the most out of online trading](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2009articles/online_dec09.html),” Dr Wise cautions investors about the do-it-yourself nature of online trading and offers pointers on choosing an online trading site to match investment needs.

Dr Wise reminds investors that when trading via Internet, they have to be responsible for their own actions. Trading on line can be convenient, cost-effective and efficient because it allows access to information and input of orders anytime, anywhere. But precisely because it is self service, one wrong stroke on the keyboard may make a difference between gain and loss. Hence investors should familiarise themselves with the navigation of the trading site, its trade confirmation procedures, alternative means of placing orders in case of system outage, and its security measures, before opening an online trading account.

“Online trading may not be suitable for everyone,” Dr Wise says. “If you are not computer-savvy or not comfortable with handling the relevant trading procedures, stay with a conventional brokerage.”

Dr Wise further offers tips on how to safeguard against hackers, including inputting the site address directly into a web browser, steering clear of public computers, changing passwords from time to time, and installing security software.
Through his column, Dr Wise, a fictitious character created by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), shares with the public his wisdom on investing and his thoughts on regulatory issues.

Dr Wise’s column is published on the last working Tuesday of every month. The column is posted under the “Dr Wise” section of the SFC’s InvestEd website. Investors are welcome to send their feedback and questions to Dr Wise by email to InvestEd@sfc.hk.
