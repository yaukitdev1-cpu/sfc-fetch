Fraudsters are becoming increasingly sophisticated with some even creating fake regulator websites to confirm their “legitimacy” as a regulated intermediary, Dr Wise warns.

In his latest column “[Wise up to new tricks](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2009articles/fake_nov09.html),” Dr Wise unveils this new tactic of boiler rooms and highlights some warning signs of fraud. His tips to protect investors from scams include:


- Verify the entities’ authenticity from independent sources – Check both the brokerage’s credentials and the regulator’s authenticity using independent and reliable sources. Do not rely on the information provided by the party doing the selling.
- Beware of “very attractive” deals – Fraudsters always prey on greed to entice investors with deals that are too good to be true.
- Ignore cold calls – Most regulatory regimes in major markets ban cold calling by intermediaries so all cold calls should be treated with extreme caution. Cold calling is the usual way boiler room operators (note 1) approach investors.
- Be extra careful with offshore entities – Investors should be extra careful when dealing with overseas intermediaries, especially those from unfamiliar markets. It is by operating across borders and via multiple jurisdictions that boiler rooms get away with their scams, leaving victims with nowhere to turn to seek recourse.

“Keeping your guard up is your best protection against scams,” Dr Wise reminds investors. “Do not send money to any third party unless you are absolutely sure of their credentials.”
