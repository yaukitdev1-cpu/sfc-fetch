Investors must be aware of the downside risks of the investment they make in the midst of the current financial crisis.

The warning was issued by the Securities and Futures Commission’s Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley while speaking at the Funds and Securities Automation Summit 2009.

Citing hedging activities as an example, Mr Wheatley pointed out that many of the investors - be it individual, corporate or government – had suffered huge losses because “they did not think about the downside risks or that they believed the downside risks would be very limited”.

“Many believed they were safely making that bet because they assumed that the downside risks would not lead to them paying out on the put option,” Mr Wheatley said. “These are not hedges, these are speculations,” he added.

Investors also need to pay attention to the counterparty risks of the products which they have bought into. Product issuers in Hong Kong have already been asked to provide more upfront disclosure of risks, in particular, counterparty risks.

Mr Wheatley said the current financial crisis will inevitably bring about regulatory changes, but he warned against knee-jerk reactions.

“Hong Kong will take a very strong approach to bring in forward changes that we think will benefit the market. But we will think about them very carefully and will not rush into changes where the pendulum swings too far in the opposite direction,” he said.
