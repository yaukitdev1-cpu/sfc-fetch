In the current environment of low interest rates, some investors may be tempted to borrow to invest as a way to raise their potential returns. However, this strategy of leveraging can cost investors more than they can afford.

In his latest column entitled “[Think twice before you borrow to invest](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2009articles/borrow_may09.html),” Dr Wise explains to investors with illustrative examples and a real-life case that leverage is a double-edged sword; it can increase potential returns but it also exposes investors to extra risks.

Some investors borrow to increase their investment capital so that the potential return can be magnified. This is known as “leverage”. However, leverage can compound both profit and loss. When borrowing to invest, an investor is also subject to a lender’s “margin call” if the value of his collateral falls below a prescribed level. Failure to meet a margin call will allow the lender to liquidate some or all of the investor’s assets so as to repay some of the lender’s exposure. In extreme cases an investor can lose more than their initial capital investment.

“Taking out a loan to invest is only suitable for those who can accommodate the additional risks that it brings,” Dr Wise says. “As leveraging will increase risks, you must be sure that leveraging suits your own financial profile, investment objectives and risk tolerance level before borrowing to invest.”
