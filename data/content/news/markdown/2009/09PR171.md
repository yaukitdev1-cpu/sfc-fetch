In a forum attended by over 200 industry participants today, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) drew on overseas experience to discuss implementation issues regarding certain of the investor protection measures proposed in its recent consultation.

Ms Deborah Koromilas, Senior Executive Leader, Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC), and Ms Sheila Nicoll, Director of Conduct Policy, Financial Services Authority (FSA) of the United Kingdom, were invited to the forum to share insights into how similar measures were implemented in their own markets.

“Our consultation to enhance investor protection contains a number of measures that have already been introduced in major overseas markets,” said Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer. “However, we have been very conscious of the need to ensure that we do not simply copy what others have done but carefully consider what makes sense for Hong Kong. As has been demonstrated today, our proposals are actually modest by comparison with other developed markets.”

Mr Wheatley pointed out that over 40 soft consultation sessions with stakeholders have already taken place and more are planned before the end of the consultation process on 31 December. While some concerns had been raised, the SFC found that overall the proposed measures have received in principle support. The forum held today was to explain the practical aspects of certain proposals, drawing reference from markets that already have similar arrangements in place.

“We understand that some concerns have been expressed by the industry about the proposals we have made. However, while we are very conscious of the need for balance, our response to recent events requires a focus on investor protection measures. What I hope everybody understands and agrees is that it is not possible to just return to business as usual,” Mr Wheatley said.

“We appreciate the concerns that can arise whenever regulatory change is considered and are very pleased to be able to assist the SFC in explaining how commission disclosure and cooling off work in the Australian market. We hope that the information we have shared will help provide some insight into how these measures can actually work in practice”, said Ms Koromilas.

Ms Nicoll said, “While the specific rules will vary according to the product and circumstances, the concepts of disclosure of commissions and non-monetary benefits on selling financial products to retail investors, and of cancellation rights, have existed in the UK for many years."

SFC officials explained during the forum that the proposed measures have been designed as pragmatic solutions reflecting the specific circumstances of the Hong Kong market and are deliberately more limited than the equivalent regulations in other developed markets.

The SFC issued a consultation paper in September 2009 on how to enhance investor protection in relation to the sale of retail investment products. The proposals include a key facts statement which summarises the features and risks of a product, on-going disclosure of material information concerning investments, and a cooling-off period applicable only to longer-term products with a limited secondary market.

On commission disclosure, the SFC is seeking views on a number of options that would best suit the situation in Hong Kong.

The consultation closes on 31 December 2009. The [consultation paper](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/consult/q3_consultation.pdf) is available on the [SFC website](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/) (www.sfc.hk).
