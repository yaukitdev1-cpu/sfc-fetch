A more consistent international regulatory approach to short selling would help minimise regulatory arbitrage and simplify the compliance process, said the Chief Executive Officer of the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), Mr Martin Wheatley.

Speaking at the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) Summer School 2009 in Sydney, Australia, Mr Wheatley, who heads the task force on short selling under the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) Technical Committee, said the task force has sought market views and received many useful submissions from various industry groups in major markets.

The task force was set up to examine the regulatory issues concerning short selling to help restore and maintain investor confidence under the current financial crisis.

Mr Wheatley said the issues that the task force has discussed included: whether naked short selling should be banned outright; the measures that should be taken to deter or prevent abusive market conduct that may be used in conjunction with short selling; and the extent to which short selling positions should be reported to the authorities and the public.

The task force report will be released shortly for market consultation.

Mr Wheatley also cautioned investors to prepare for continued market volatility given the possibility of further deterioration in the global economies and financial markets. Investors should stay vigilant and up-to-date on crucial product and market risks, he added.
