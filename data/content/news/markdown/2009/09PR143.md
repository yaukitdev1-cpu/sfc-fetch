Hong Kong’s hedge fund industry has remained strong despite the global economic downturn, according to a survey report released today by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC).

The ["Report of the Survey on Hedge Fund Activities of SFC-licensed Managers/Advisors”](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/surveys/09/Hedge%20Fund%20Survey%20Report%202009_ENG.pdf) (Note 1) shows the total assets under management/advisory (AUM) stood at US55.3 billion as of 31 March 2009.

Below is a summary of the major findings:


- The number of hedge funds (Note 2) managed by the SFC-licensed hedge fund managers in Hong Kong increased to 542 as at 31 March 2009, an increase of 11% over 2008 and nearly five times the level in 2004, the earliest year covered in similar SFC surveys.
- The hedge funds invested mainly in Asia Pacific using equities long/short and multi-strategies. As at 31 March 2009, 59.1% of the total AUM was invested in the Asia Pacific markets.
- As expected, AUM has dropped from 2008 levels consistent with falls in the overall financial markets and the need for liquidity by many investors. However, total AUM has expanded significantly in recent years from US$9.1 billion in 2004 to the peak of US$90.1 billion in 2008. At 31 March 2009, AUM stood at US$55.3 billion, representing six times the level in 2004, although the recovery of global and regional financial markets will have driven an increase from this post-crisis level.
- The industry contains a good mix of local and international hedge fund managers. About 60% of the SFC-licensed hedge fund managers were locally controlled, with the remainder controlled from overseas.
- Overseas institutional investors dominated, with 98.1% of the investors being from overseas, mainly the Americas and Europe. In terms of type of investors, funds of funds constituted about 40% of the investor base as at 31 March 2009.
- Following the financial crisis, SFC-licensed hedge fund managers adopted relatively conservative investment approaches. As at 31 March 2009:
-  28.5% of total AUM was held in cash, deposits or money market instruments.
-  68.8% of their hedge funds had limited leverage.
-  52.5% hedge fund managers were using more than one prime broker – up from 39.6% in 2006, reflecting a higher awareness of counterparty risks.
The report also outlines various initiatives taken by the SFC in recent years in response to the development of Hong Kong’s hedge fund industry. Licensing procedures have been streamlined and clarified to help shorten the average processing time by about 40% and proactive guidance is being provided through sharing inspection findings.

“Pressure to tighten hedge fund regulation has been on the rise,” the SFC's Chief Executive Officer Mr Martin Wheatley said. "However, we will continue to look for a balance between appropriate regulatory controls, ensuring adequate protection for investors, and allowing room for industry development and growth.”

The full survey report is available on the SFC website at [www.sfc.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/index.html).

Notes:
1. Questionnaires were sent to 231 licensed corporations that were SFC-licensed hedge fund managers as at 31 March 2009. They were asked to provide information regarding the general operations of their hedge fund business as well as the hedge funds they managed as at 31 March 2009. Some data was also collected at the snapshots of 31 March 2007 and 2008 for comparison and trend analysis purposes. A total of 225 replies were received, representing a response rate of 97.4%.

2. There is no formal definition of the term “hedge funds.” For the purpose of this survey, funds, including managed portfolios, that use alternative investment strategies, pursue absolute returns, charge performance-based fees in addition to a management fee, and adopt investment mandates that give managers more flexibility to shift strategies have been regarded as hedge funds.
