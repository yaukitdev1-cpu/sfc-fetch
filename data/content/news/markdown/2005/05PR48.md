The SFC issues this statement to update investors on the migration status of intermediaries licensed under the pre-Securities and Futures Ordinance regime. These intermediaries are permitted by the Securities and Futures Ordinance to carry on regulated activities until 31 March 2005 and are commonly referred to as deemed licensed corporations.

As at 28 February 2005, 85 deemed licensed corporations (comprising 36 brokers, 45 investment advisers, and four securities margin financiers) had yet to submit migration applications to the SFC.

According to our records, 23 of these deemed licensed corporations hold client assets. They have either ceased business or do not intend to migrate to the new regime. For protection of investors, the SFC is closely monitoring their return of those assets to clients.

The SFC is also in close dialogue with the rest of the deemed licensed corporations on their individual migration plans. These corporations do not hold client assets. However, they may have relationships with and may be providing other services to clients.

Deemed licensed corporations that have not lodged applications to migrate to the new regime by 31 March 2005 will cease to be so deemed and must discontinue all regulated activities. If they wish to continue carrying on regulated activities from 1 April 2005, they should lodge their migration applications as soon as possible to ensure no interruptions to their businesses and to their services to clients.

To safeguard your own interests, you may wish to check the Public Register on the SFC website to see whether your intermediary has applied for migration to the new regime. For a corporation that has yet to lodge its application, there is a remark denoting "*Licensed under transitional arrangement (migration application not yet lodged)*" in the corporation’s record.

Investors who are clients of a deemed licensed corporation that has not yet lodged its migration application, may wish to enquire with that corporation about its migration plan, and if necessary, make alternative arrangements for their assets held or the services provided by that corporation.
