SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, Mrs Alexa Lam, today gave the opening address at the 8th Annual Hedge Funds World Asia.

Mrs Lam said that hedge funds were welcomed to set up in Hong Kong. The SFC did not discriminate against any firms, big or small. However, the SFC must be satisfied that the manager is well qualified and experienced in his field, that the fund must have good governance, and that the fund must have stringent risk and internal management controls.

“These requirements do not impose additional costs on the hedge funds because all institutional investors will demand no less. These requirements will not be lowered either,” she said.

Mrs Lam said that, according to AsiaHedge figures, in the first half of 2005, some US$3.5 billion of new hedge funds had come to Asia Pacific for investment, of which US$1.3 billion to Hong Kong, ahead of Japan and Singapore.

Mrs Lam said since March this year, there had been a number of high profile hedge fund losses and failures and international regulatory focus on hedge funds. As a result, certain concerns had been highlighted.


- Conflicts of interest – Hedge fund managers had been signing side letters with preferred investors, so that they are given the most favoured nation clauses.
- Valuation – the issue is whether valuation is done fairly, especially in relation to bespoke, sophisticated and complex instruments that are highly illiquid.
- Documentation – a lot of over-the-counter contracts were not properly executed or documented.

Mrs Lam said that as part of SFC’s ongoing supervision programme, it had in the past nine months done a theme inspection on hedge fund managers based in Hong Kong, covering funds representing 45% of the total hedge funds assets under management in Hong Kong.

The SFC looked at several key areas such as their strategies, leverage, governance, systems and controls.

“We also held in-depth discussions with the prime brokers, probably the ones who know hedge funds much better than anybody else,” she said.

“We believe in active dialogues with our industry, so that we can better understand their circumstances, and that we can put together a better macro picture. From that picture, we hope to be able to detect issues that the market is concerned about and which we will bring back to the industry, to alert them,” she said.

The inspection found that (a) most of the Hong Kong based hedge funds had simple equity long short strategies; (b) the external leverage of those hedge funds was relatively modest; and (c) the major prime brokers appeared to take a conservative approach to risk and understood that complex bespoke products posed major challenges in risk management and monitoring.

Mrs Lam said we would take follow up action on misconduct that was detected.

“We will not tolerate misconduct. But on the other hand, we are very committed to creating a friendly environment for properly run and managed hedge funds,” she said.
