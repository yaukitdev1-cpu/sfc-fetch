SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, today called on the Hong Kong hedge fund industry to adopt best practices for their operations.

In a keynote speech at a hedge fund conference, Mr Wheatley said the revaluation of Renminbi had triggered waves of inflows into Asia and more hedge fund activity in Asian stocks was expected.

The most popular country for new Asian hedge funds to choose as their bases was Hong Kong, followed by Australia, Japan and the United States, he said at the Hong Kong: Hedge Fund Hub of Asia - Conference 2005. The conference is hosted by the Hong Kong Chapter of the Alternative Investment Management Association.

Mr Wheatley said hedge funds were a major source of liquidity and could significantly enhance market efficiency. They played a valuable arbitrage role in reducing or eliminating mispricing in financial markets.

“Hedge funds, like other institutional investors, can contribute to or may adversely impact financial stability. As a regulator, the SFC believes that instead of prohibiting hedge fund activities altogether, we should spare more resources and effort to understand the industry, to understand where the risk areas are and how it can facilitate the growth of the economy,” he said.

Mr Wheatley said: “We are fully aware that the SFC cannot work on its own. We believe in building partnership with other regulators, whether local or overseas, and the industry to build a fair and transparent regulatory framework with the primary objective to safeguard investor interests, which can also facilitate market and product development, and maintain market confidence and promote high standards.”

“In line with this, we maintain close dialogue with overseas regulators such as the Financial Services Authority in the UK and the Securities and Exchange Commission in the United States, with whom we are currently discussing initiatives in relation to hedge fund activities,” he said.

Mr Wheatley said a more discerning and pro-active approach by investors in hedge funds should help reduce the risk of failure by a major fund.

“Some of the recent hedge fund frauds indicate up-front due diligence is important,” he said. “Institutional investors should be encouraged to press for more information and transparency from hedge funds to ensure that they understand the factors contributing to investment returns and portfolio risks.”

Mr Wheatley said: “Similarly we believe it would be in the best interest of the hedge fund industry to more broadly and proactively encourage increased transparency, particularly as it grows and matures. We believe that market participants should also intensify their efforts to improve the underlying infrastructure of the markets in which hedge funds are important.”

Mr Wheatley welcomed the initiatives of industry bodies and practitioners, such as the Managed Funds Association (MFA), the Alternative Investment Management Association (AIMA) and the Gerald Corrigan report, to provide guiding principles and recommendations of best practices to hedge fund managers on their operations to strengthen their risk management, to satisfy their responsibilities to investors and to address unexpected market events (Note 1).

“These reports and best practices made sensible recommendations about upgrading internal controls, transparency and documentation. It is crucial that such practices permeate throughout the hedge fund community,” he said.

“We would appreciate the Hong Kong hedge fund industry shares the same view with us,” he said.
