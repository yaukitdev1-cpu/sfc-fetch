The SFC is pleased to announce that it has signed a [Letter of Intent](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/aboutsfc/cooperation/loi_sec_sri_lanka_050406.pdf) with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC Sri Lanka) on the enhancement of regulatory co-operation between the SEC Sri Lanka and the SFC (Note 1).

The two regulators aim to work towards the mutual recognition of investment products authorised in their respective jurisdictions for cross-border distribution.
 
This Letter of Intent is an important step for both the SEC Sri Lanka and the SFC in the area of cross-border regulation of investment products. Under the Letter of Intent, both authorities agree to strengthen co-operation in matters pertaining to cross-border trading and the supervision of regulated funds. It is hoped that with enhanced communication and harmonisation of the respective regulatory regimes, both Hong Kong and Sri Lanka will be able to facilitate the development of deeper and broader investment markets through the trading in each other's markets of mutually acceptable investment products.
 
Both the SEC Sri Lanka and the SFC are members of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) and signatories of the IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding (Note 2).  This Letter of Intent is signed in the spirit of mutual co-operation of member securities regulators fostered by IOSCO.
 
The Letter of Intent was signed today by Dr Dayanath C. Jayasuriya, Chairman of the SEC Sri Lanka, and Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC, in Colombo, Sri Lanka, on the occasion of the opening ceremony of the IOSCO 2005 Annual Conference. The signing was witnessed by Dr. Sarath Amunugama, Minister of Finance of Sri Lanka.
 
Both authorities have also established a bilateral working group which has begun working towards the achievement of the objectives set out in the Letter of Intent.
 
Mr Sheng said: “The SFC is committed to strengthening regional financial market co-operation in order to raise the quality of the Asian markets.  By signing this Letter of Intent, the SFC joins hands with the SEC Sri Lanka in a demonstration of our mutual intention to enhance the level of regulatory co-operation between our respective markets in line with the objectives and standards of IOSCO.  Our ultimate goal is to open up regional fund markets through the cross-border trading of financial products in our respective jurisdictions.  As securities regulators, we will work together to remove unnecessary hurdles to cross-border trading, without compromising the level of investor protection in either of our jurisdictions.”

Dr Dayanath Jayasuriya said: "As we move towards one 'Global Village', the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka encourages local as well as foreign business entities to increase their investments and to expand their operations. In this context, it is important that capital market regulators facilitate cross-border listings and operations, whilst maintaining the capacity to ensure that the applicable laws and regulations are being complied with. This Letter of Intent provides the basis for the regulators in Sri Lanka and Hong Kong to enter into a new phase of greater co-operation that would eventually benefit all market participants and the public."

Notes:

1. The Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka was founded upon the promulgation of the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka Act No. 36 of 1987, as amended by Acts Nos. 26 of 1991 and 18 of 2003, with the mission to promote, develop and maintain a securities market that is fair, efficient, orderly and transparent. The power and functions of the SEC Sri Lanka include the implementation, enforcement, and administration of the Act, its Rules and Regulations prescribed thereunder, to ensure proper disclosure of information in the public offering of securities, financial stability and proper conduct of securities companies, proper performance of various self-regulatory agencies and other institutions related to securities businesses, public compliance with takeover codes, as well as integrity and fairness of the Sri Lankan securities market.

2. The IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding Concerning Consultation and Co-operation and The Exchange of Information (IOSCO MMOU), of which there are currently 26 full signatories and a further 5 that have committed to making the legislative and other changes necessary to meet the requirements of the IOSCO MMOU. Signatories of the IOSCO MMOU have the legal authority, the resources and the commitment to initiate or receive requests for regulatory assistance to or from every other signatory; providing seamless cross-border regulatory co-operation and enforcement assistance.

3. The SFC signed similar Letters of Intent with Indonesia’s Ministry of Finance Capital Market Supervisory Agency (BAPEPAM) in May 2004 and Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC Thailand) in November 2004.
