The SFC today publishes the Autumn 2005 issue (No.61) of the [*SFC Quarterly Bulletin*](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/speeches/public/bulletin/sfc_bulletin/autumn_05.htm).

*A Survey on the Retail Structured Notes Market in Hong Kong* shows that the retail structured notes market in Hong Kong has grown significantly in recent years, with the majority of the notes being equity linked notes. It also remarks that one of the factors leading to an overall decline in market volatility in Hong Kong is the growth in the structured notes market in recent years.

In another article,* Proportion of Derivative Warrants Expiring In-the-money and Out-of-the-money,* it is estimated that of all derivative warrants that were issued after January 2002 and expired before the end of August 2005, about 45% expired in-the-money. Furthermore, the proportion seems to be associated with the performance of the market as well as the underlying stocks.

*Trading of Hong Kong Stocks by US and Japanese Residents* reveals that the trading of Hong Kong stocks by US residents has been accounting for about 11% to 35% of the Hong Kong market turnover since 2000, whilst the corresponding figure for Japanese residents has been less than 2%. It also examines the relationship between the trading pattern and market sentiment.

This issue also includes the *Quarterly Review of the Securities Market* and the *Quarterly Financial Review of the Securities Industry.* The former reviews the performance of the local and regional stock markets during the third quarter of 2005, whilst the latter analyses the financial performance of the securities industry over the same period. The reports found that:


- Hong Kong’s securities market advanced further following the gains in the first half of 2005; and
- overall profitability of the securities industry improved in the third quarter of 2005 with the increase in market turnover.
