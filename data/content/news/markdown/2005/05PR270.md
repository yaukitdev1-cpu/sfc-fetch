The SFC is pleased to release its second *Quarterly Report* for the financial year to 31 March 2006 (Note 1).

During July - September, the SFC concluded four consultations. We proposed changes to keep the rules and codes up-to-date with market developments and international practice, to increase investor protection, and to combat money laundering. The SFC also continued to strengthen its co-operation with other regulators across Asia and Europe.

In light of market concerns over the derivative warrants market, we commenced a review of the regulatory framework of the derivative warrants market in Hong Kong during the quarter, and a report is expected by the end of November. We also stepped up our investor education on warrants through the Dr Wise’s Column and newspaper articles.

Work Progress

During the quarter, the SFC:


- proposed some changes to the definitions of certain regulated activities;
- continued to review the responses to the consultation on proposed amendments to the Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules;
- amended the Securities and Futures (Investor Compensation – Levy) Rules for an automatic levy triggering and suspension mechanism;
- consulted on proposed reforms to the regime governing the public offering of shares and debentures;
- concluded a review of the Codes on Takeovers and Mergers and Share Repurchases;
- concluded the revisions to the Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Guidance Note;
- completed a thematic review of hedge fund managers;
- approved a Listing Rules amendment to enable Exchange Participants to participate in share placement activities and rights issues of a suspended company;
- released the findings of the Fund Management Activities Survey 2004;
- revised the Hedge Fund Guidelines;
- approved HKEx’s rule amendments for the rollout of Phase I of SDNet;
- held workshops to promote the development of eXtensible Business Reporting Language (XBRL) in Hong Kong;
- worked closely with the other parties to prepare for contingency events during the Sixth Ministerial Conference (MC6) of the World Trade Organization;
- prosecuted 15 entities and disciplined 29 licensees for various regulatory breaches;
- sent an inspection team to three licensed corporations to monitor their financial position and ensure proper safeguarding of client assets;
- released findings of the first Public Perception Survey;
- improved its corporate website and the Electronic Investor Resources Centre (eIRC);
- partnered with Lingnan University to launch two credit-based investor education
- courses; and
- signed Letters of Intent with Malaysia’s Securities Commission and Jersey’s Financial Services Commission.

Surplus recorded

The SFC recorded a surplus of $87 million for the second quarter. A surplus of $136 million was recorded for the first six months of the financial year, compared to the projected surplus of $8 million in the approved budget.

Total revenue was $208 million, 26% higher than the previous quarter. Levy income increased 37% as the average daily stock market turnover rose. Total expenditure (including depreciation) amounted to $121 million, against $116 million recorded in the last quarter.

At the end of September, our reserves stood at $996 million, equivalent to 1.96 times the approved annual operating expenditure (including depreciation) of $508 million.

SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said that based on the current information and by managing the budget prudently, a surplus was expected in the third quarter.
