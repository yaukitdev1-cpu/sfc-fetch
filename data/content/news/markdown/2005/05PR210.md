The SFC today publishes the [Consultation Conclusions for the Consultation Paper on Proposed Amendments to Schedule 5 to the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO)](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/conclusion?refNo=05CP3).

The Consultation Paper was published on 23 February 2005 proposing amendments to the legal definitions of certain regulated activities set out in Part 2 of Schedule 5 to the SFO (Note 1). These amendments reflected the latest development of the industry and comments received from market participants on the licensing regime.

The proposed amendments included:

(a) Extending the definition of “asset management” to include management of real estate investment trusts.

(b) Amending the definition of “dealing in securities” so that:

(i) in a case where all parties to the dealing concerned are authorised financial institutions and the dealing is by an approved money broker who represents each of the parties concerned, the dealing is to be excluded from the definition; and

(ii) any person who, as principal, disposes of securities other than to a professional investor, is not to be excluded from the definition.

(c) Amending the definitions of “advising on securities” and “advising on futures contracts” so that the giving of advice by a licensed asset manager, solely for the purposes of carrying on fund management activities pertaining to collective investment schemes under his management, is to be excluded from the definitions.

The SFC received 13 submissions on the Consultation Paper. They generally welcomed the proposals, although some parties also raised certain issues in relation to (b)(ii) above. After careful deliberation, the SFC has decided not to implement that particular amendment for the time being. Instead, it will be considered in a separate consultation exercise. Whilst seeking to enhance investor protection, the SFC is mindful to avoid any unintentional effect that the proposal may bring. Subject to the legislative process, the other proposed amendments will become effective on 9 December 2005.

The Consultation Conclusions paper summarises the comments received and sets out the SFC’s responses. It is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website.
