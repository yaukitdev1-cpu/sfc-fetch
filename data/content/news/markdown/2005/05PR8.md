The SFC today publishes a Consultation Paper on the Review of the Disclosure of Interests Regime under Part XV of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) for public comment.

Part XV requires a shareholder who has an interest in 5% or more of the voting shares of a listed corporation (a substantial shareholder) to give notice of such interests in specified circumstances. It also requires a director or a chief executive, who is interested in the shares or debentures of a listed corporation (of which he is a director or chief executive) or any of its associated corporations, to give notice in specified circumstances. 

Before the commencement of the SFO, the Administration committed to review the Part XV regime at an appropriate time. The SFC has been reviewing the regime by gathering market comments and has also discussed with market participants its effectiveness in meeting its objectives, and any need to update it in line with market developments.

Generally, commentators welcome the greater transparency Part XV has brought about and consider that its objectives have largely been achieved. Most comments were technical or practical in nature. These involve matters such as filling in or filing the statutory forms or regarding the interpretation of the law. There are also requests for changes in areas of interest to particular segments of the market.

The Consultation Paper sets out issues identified in the course of the review for public comment. In some areas the SFC has set out proposals for change to address these issues.  The SFC will also revise the Outline of Part XV, a guidance publication, to assist understanding of the provisions. Some changes may need to be effected through legislative changes (e.g. through amendments to statutory forms for disclosures or by amending Part XV).

In other areas, the SFC is seeking further views of the public before proposing change. Some of the more significant matters of general application on which the SFC is seeking further views are on (a) how to make filings easier; (b) disclosure of share pledges; (c) the *de minimis* exception; and (d) change in nature of interest.

Make filings easier

A significant number of comments on Part XV involve practical difficulties with filing notifications and understanding them. Accordingly, the Consultation Paper lists a set of additional codes to describe the events leading to the notification or capacity in which the interest is held, and invites further suggestions for useful codes.

Disclosure of “security interests”

The SFC is also consulting on the disclosure of “security interests” which would include share pledges by controlling shareholders. The Consultation Paper considers various suggested ways of dealing with the question and discusses the various pros and cons of requiring disclosures. It looks at the difficulties in balancing the interest of investors in view of the information value of disclosure of security interest, the business burden, the liquidity of the underlying stock at the time of a forced sale and the timing of disclosure. The Consultation Paper notes considerable market disagreement over the appropriate solution on the disclosure of share pledges.

The SFC knows of no other jurisdiction that requires the disclosure of share pledges with qualified lenders. However the SFC appreciates that the circumstances of Hong Kong might call for a different approach. One possible way forward for Hong Kong may be to impose disclosure obligations on substantial shareholders and lenders to report immediately the fact that the lender is taking steps to enforce a security interest.

“de minimis” exception

The *de minimis* exception was originally intended to exclude disclosures by substantial shareholders for small changes (0.5%) around a whole percentage level. However the law was drafted such that the exception operates in a very complex manner, and does not reflect the original policy intention.

The Consultation Paper:

(i) suggests a way to make the operation of the *de minimis* exception much simpler and  easier to apply and understand; or

(ii) alternatively discusses how to change the disclosure regime altogether so that the *de minimis* exception is not necessary.

Change in nature of interest

Another area of practical difficulty is when a discloseable “change in nature” of an interest may have taken place. The Consultation Paper proposes a list of circumstances to define exhaustively a “change in nature” so that it is easier to determine when this disclosure obligation arises.

Mr Mark Dickens, SFC’s Executive Director of Supervision of Markets, said: “In coming up with proposals to address market concerns and to make the Ordinance more user-friendly, we have kept in mind the need to minimise undue compliance burdens while preserving market transparency, and the need to keep the regime in line with market developments.”
