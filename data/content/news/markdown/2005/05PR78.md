The SFC today publishes the March issue of Dr Wise’s Column, [*Look Before You Lock*](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/features/doctor/features_87_226_316.htm).

In this issue, Dr Wise explains that both locking and closing out money-losing leveraged foreign exchange positions involve taking offsetting transactions to an outstanding position, and the resulting trading loss from the pair of offsetting positions is the same. This means that the locking strategy cannot change the fact that a trading loss has been incurred and fixed. Whether or not an investor can recover the trading loss by further trading attempts is unrelated to the locked positions, but to whether the market moves as expected.

On the other hand, once a leveraged foreign exchange trading position is locked, it has to be unwound. Therefore, investors always incur additional commission for and extra transaction costs from the bid and offer spread when unwinding their positions. Moreover, carrying locked positions entails interest expenses, which normally involve an interest spread of 3%. Thus, interest expenses could become very substantial if the locked positions are kept for a long period of time.
