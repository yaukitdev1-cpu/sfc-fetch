The SFC today releases the Investor Survey on Sponsors.

Conducted by the Social Sciences Research Centre of the University of Hong Kong between October and November 2004, the Survey collected the investing public’s views on the subject of sponsor regulation.  The Survey asked investors about their understanding of a sponsor’s role in the listing process, their views on current sponsor standards and possible means to enhance the standards of sponsors.

Background

The Survey is part of the second stage of the Commission’s initiatives to enhance sponsor standards, as outlined in the Consultation Conclusions Paper on the Regulation of Sponsors and Independent Financial Advisers released in October 2004. 

The Survey interviewed 1,623 Hong Kong adults, of which 765 (47.1%) were identified as retail investors, 172 (10.6%) as potential investors, and 686 (42.3%) as non-investors (Note 1).  The Survey also covered 10 institutional investors, which comprise local and international fund management companies.  These 10 fund management companies together accounted for approximately 85% of Hong Kong’s retail fund market and 58% of the pension fund market, including Mandatory Provident Funds (MPFs) (Note 2).

Key findings

The Survey findings show that the respondents have a fair understanding of a sponsor’s role in the listing process.

- 83.9% of retail investors and 100% of institutional investors are aware that a listing applicant must appoint a sponsor to provide corporate finance advice during the listing application. 
- 88.5% of retail investors, 89.9% of potential investors, and 100% of institutional investors identify due diligence work as one of the sponsor’s duties.

Respondents are in unison in their views regarding local sponsor standards.  95% of retail investors, 94.2% of potential investors, and 100% of institutional investors agree that there is a need to raise sponsor standards in Hong Kong.  

In addition, over 90% of the retail and institutional investors are of the view that in enhancing sponsor standards, the following four aspects should be considered:

- Relevant IPO sponsor experience and competence of firms and professional staff;
- Firm’s internal system and control to oversee the quality of sponsor work;
- Sufficient human and organisational resources of a firm in carrying out sponsor work; and
- Sufficient financial resources of a firm including professional indemnity of sponsor firms.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “We note that there are two major findings of the Survey and the responses to the Joint Consultation on Sponsor Regulation: the investing public take the view that sponsor standards need to be raised; and the market supports the SFC to be the single statutory regulator responsible for the regulatory oversight and discipline of sponsors.

“The SFC will carry out an action plan for the enhancement of sponsor standards. The SFC will review the existing licensing regime and formulate a specific set of eligibility criteria for sponsors and independent financial advisers (IFAs) under the licensing regime.  The SFC will also review the existing conduct rules applicable to sponsors and IFAs, such as the Code of Conduct for Licensed Persons and the Corporate Finance Adviser Code of Conduct, and make revisions to the codes where necessary.

“Furthermore, the SFC plans to conduct a theme inspection in relation to sponsor’s compliance with the recently revised Listing Rules and the newly implemented practice notes on due diligence regarding sponsor work.” 

Way forward

The SFC will prepare for a public consultation on a specific set of eligibility criteria for sponsors and IFAs in the first half of 2005.  In the second half of 2005, the SFC will conduct a theme inspection on sponsors’ compliance with the recently implemented amendments to the Listing Rules and practice notes.

The Survey is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website under ["Speeches and Publications" - "Publications" - "Surveys and Reports"](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/surveys/investor_survey_on_sponsors_050302.pdf).

Notes:

1. In the Survey:

*“Retail investors”* include respondents who had traded Hong Kong stocks during the past five years;

*“Potential investors”* include respondents who had not traded any Hong Kong stocks during the past five years, but indicated that they would trade in the future;

*“Non-investors”* include respondents who had not traded any Hong Kong stocks during the past five years and did not plan to trade in the future.

2. The percentages are based on the total asset under management of these 10 fund managers obtained via SFC’s Fund Management Activities Survey 2003.
