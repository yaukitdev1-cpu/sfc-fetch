The SFC yesterday submitted its views to the Government on its Consultation Paper on Proposed Amendments to the Securities and Futures Ordinance to Give Statutory Backing to Major Listing Requirements.

At a meeting on 21 February 2005, the Board of the Commission decided on the SFC's response document and its submission to the Government.

The Commission supports the Government’s proposals, which would provide an effective legal framework under which the SFC may operate.

In particular, the Commission agrees with the Government’s proposal for introducing a power to impose a range of civil sanctions on issuers, directors and other corporate officers for breaches of the statutory listing rules. Civil sanctions are essential for effective enforcement of statutory disclosure and transaction rules. It is also essential to have the power to impose civil fines, to enable regulatory action to be tailored proportionately to misconduct. Without fines being available, there is no middle ground between public reprimands for lesser infractions and disqualification of officers of corporations for more serious breaches.

The Commission also believes that providing the SFC with the power to impose fines would enable it to take swift action to uphold its regulatory objectives of maintaining a fair and transparent market and protecting members of the public investing in or holding securities. It would also serve to protect and enhance the reputation of the Hong Kong financial market.

On the other hand, the Commission is of the view that the amounts of fines proposed, a maximum of $5 million for the SFC and up to $8 million for the Market Misconduct Tribunal (MMT), may be able to cater for the majority of cases but do not address really serious cases adequately. The Commission believes that the more realistic fining levels to underpin the new civil regime for listing regulation would be up to $10 million for the SFC (i.e. the same maximum level of fines as may be imposed on regulated persons already under Part IX of the SFO) and unlimited fines for the MMT (as in the UK), which would enable the SFC to deal with the less serious cases and the MMT to deal with the more serious cases effectively.

The Commission would also like to stress that the right to apply as of right for a review by the Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal (made up of Government-appointed market practitioners and chaired by a High Court judge) is an important safeguard on SFC enforcement actions. However, the Commission will study further enhancements to the enforcement decision-making process in the light of submissions received from the market on this question.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said: "Giving statutory backing to certain of the Listing Rules will significantly enhance our listing regulatory regime and the quality of our market. It will also bring the regulatory regime in Hong Kong further into line with international markets."

"The regime for enforcement of breaches of the Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules needs to be effective and able to be responsive to the dynamic listed sector. A balance must be struck between the need for effectiveness and the desire for accountability," he said.

"We look forward to the Consultation Conclusions and the decision of the Government on this matter," Mr Sheng said.
