The SFC today releases proposals for relaxing the geographical restriction on investments by SFC-authorised REITs (the REITs) and seeks comments on the proposals and the related provisions on regulating overseas investments by REITs.

The proposals are divided into three key aspects:


- To lift the geographical restrictions so that REITs may invest in real estate anywhere in the world;
- To issue a Practice Note focusing on the obligations of REIT managers when investing in overseas investments; and
- To consider a series of initiatives to further facilitate the development of REITs as an investment product.

Introduction of a Practice Note

Given the different risks involved in overseas property investments, the proposed Practice Note provides concrete guidance on the application of the provisions in the Code on REITs when investing in overseas properties, and supplements the principles outlined in the Code on REITs.

The draft Practice Note advocates that:

(a) all REITs are regulated on the same regulatory principles; there will be no separate sets of rules for different jurisdictions;
(b) the REIT manager must have the requisite competence and conduct proper due diligence;
(c) each of the parties involved in a REIT (manager, trustee, valuer, auditor, etc) has its duties and responsibilities; and
(d) there must be clear and proper disclosures of risks in investing overseas.

Other initiatives

In addition to the lifting of geographical restriction and the issuance of a Practice Note, the SFC also invites the public to comment on a series of initiatives for the REIT regime, including:


- recognition of property portfolio management expertise directly for REIT managers’ licences;
- review of the gearing ratio; and
- acceptance of special product features, such as payment of management fees by the way of issuance of units, provided that there are proper safeguards in place.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "After 18 months of intensive discussions and research by the Taskforce on Overseas Real Estate Investments by REITs, we believe this is the right time to lift the geographic restriction."

"The most important ingredients in the management of a REIT are the expertise and integrity of the manager and its ability to conduct proper due diligence in light of the risks relevant to the jurisdiction(s) that a REIT invests in. The draft Practice Note emphasises not only the REIT manager’s responsibility to conduct due diligence, but also its duty to assess, mitigate, manage and disclose all relevant risks in relevant overseas property investments," Mrs Lam said.

The one-month consultation will end on 30 April 2005. Following the consultation, the SFC expects to launch the new REITs regime in June 2005. Interested parties may now approach the SFC to start discussions on their business plans and proposals.

The Consultation Paper is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website at [www.sfc.hk](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=05CP5).
