SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, today gave the keynote address at the Opening Ceremony of the Summer Academic Program of the Asia Institute for Political Economy, University of Hong Kong.

Mr Sheng’s speech, entitled "Free Markets and Property Rights Infrastructure: Lessons from Hong Kong", examines how free markets helped to make Hong Kong successful and the implications for emerging market reforms, including China’s move toward the market economy.  It also looks at some world events in the last two decades which led to recent re-thinking of the meaning of free markets, and the question whether there is a need for a "Free Market II" philosophy to replace the "positive non-interventionism" practised in Hong Kong.

"Success in economic development does not only befall countries richly endowed with natural resources, capital or even cheap labour," Mr Sheng said. "Success is the result of competition between institutions of governance – the higher the quality, the more prosperous."

Mr Sheng said that the emergence of information technology in recent years had led to a better understanding of how systems or institutions function.  In an IT system, the hardware is only as good as the software. Similarly the proper functioning of markets also depends on a very important software, the "property rights infrastructure" (PRI).

The essential elements of a modern PRI include the following institutions grouped by three broad categories:

1. Institutions for delineation of property rights: central registry of property rights; and accounting and legal process to define and verify the property rights

2. Institutions for exchange of property rights: trading process (such as stock exchange trading platform); clearing, settlement and payment infrastructure; and regulated intermediaries

3. Institutions for enforcement and fine-tuning of property rights: norms, standards, codes, regulations, and law that help protect the property rights of participants against abuses; the regulators; robust media and disclosure regime; and independent and transparent judiciary

According to Mr Sheng, the PRI in Hong Kong is a sophisticated system that works well together to protect and transact property rights.

"Just as providing water, sewage and road facilities (hard infrastructure) can address poverty and provide opportunity, it must be just as important that governments pay attention to PRI (soft infrastructure) in making markets work better," Mr Sheng said.  

He concludes: "Since Hong Kong is already an international financial centre with a PRI that is both competitive and transparent by world standards, Hong Kong can bring in a wealth of market experience in building the PRI to facilitate the Mainland’s transition to a fully competitive market economy that operates on global standards."
