Five more members of the public have been given the Smart Investor Awards for reporting boiler rooms and Internet financial scams to the SFC. They received their awards at a presentation ceremony yesterday (Note 1).

Three of the award winners were cold-called by suspect boiler room operators, who purported to be based in Argentina, South Korea and Switzerland respectively and offered brokerage services for overseas stocks. All the winners were smart enough to turn down the offers of the unknown callers and reported the matters to the SFC.

Another award winner received two emails allegedly from an overseas bank and a financial institution respectively, which were typical phishes (emails) to entice recipients to disclose their personal data. Both emails requested customers of the two institutions to click on the embedded links to enter their personal information for activating the new security feature or system upgrade. Yet the winner himself is not a customer of these two institutions.

The other award winner is an SFC licensee who reported to the SFC a suspicious fake website. Purporting to have operations in Hong Kong and overseas, the website operator uses company names resembling both current and former SFC licensees. It claims to provide asset management services and offer investment funds to investors through numerous offices or sister companies worldwide. In addition, that website is hyperlinked to another suspicious fake website carrying a bank’s name.

As a result of the five award winners’ reports, the names of the seven dubious entities have been added on the [Alert List](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/alert.htm) on the SFC-operated Electronic Investor Resources Centre (eIRC) located at www.eirc.hk. At present, there are 224 entities on the Alert List (Note 2).

Presenting the Awards yesterday, Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Executive Director and Chief Operating Officer, said: "To complement our enforcement efforts, the SFC introduced the Smart Investor Awards in February 2004. Responses from both the general public and the market are encouraging. Altogether, 12 members of the public, including both investors and SFC licensees, have won 13 awards *.* awards. This shows that the public can actively assist in enforcement and regulatory actions to prevent financial frauds by alerting the regulators and the police to any suspicious activities."

In 2004, the SFC received 132 complaints of boiler rooms and Internet financial scams involving reported losses by investors of $11.7 million. Seven of the victims were Hong Kong investors who had suffered a total loss of approximately $2.5 million.

In addition to the Alert List, to facilitate investors to find out more information about financial scams, the SFC has recently built a [dedicated page](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/boiler_room_overseas_regulators.htm) on the eIRC that comprises hyperlinks to the investor alert lists of other overseas regulators (Note 3).

The public should also be aware that fraudsters may use various tricks to dupe unwary investors. Some adopt names similar to those legitimate household names to confuse investors; others claim to have offices in different countries to create a global and professional image. When boiler room operators learn that they have been named by regulators, they may change their company names or change the base of operation to another country. Some of them may hit the victims again by posing as a new company, offering to help the victims sell the stocks previously bought from the boiler room operators or other investment opportunities.

Investors should always stay vigilant and do their homework. The SFC has made available various educational materials about financial scams on the "Investor Alert" section of the eIRC. The SFC has also created [two sample fake websites](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/scam_websites.htm) in that section to help investors better understand the typical pitfalls of such scams.

Members of the public are welcome to make reports of suspicious activities for consideration of the Smart Investor Awards.

Notes to Editor:

1. The Smart Investor Awards were introduced in February 2004 to encourage the public to report financial scams. Eight awards have previously been given out. Each winner received an SFC corporate watch and a certificate of recognition. Details of the Awards and photographs of yesterday’s (6 January 2004) presentation ceremony are available on the eIRC under the section of ["Investor Alert" - "Smart Investor Awards"](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/smart_investor_awards.htm).

2. The [Alert List](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/alert.htm) was first set up in November 2000 when boiler room activities became active. As the scope and tactics of fraudulent activities grow and evolve with time, the Alert List has been expanded to include scam websites (e.g. copycat websites) and phishing scams to caution investors. The SFC keeps updating the Alert List when new entities and information come to our attention.

3. The webpage is available under the section of ["Investor Alert" – "Boiler Room Operation"](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/boiler_room.htm) of the eIRC. At present, this page is linked to the investor alert lists of 12 overseas regulators.
