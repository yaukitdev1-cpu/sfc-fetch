The two-year transitional period for intermediaries and individuals under the pre-Securities and Futures Ordinance licensing regime to migrate to the new regime has ended today (Note 1).  

A successful programme – high percentage of migration

A total of 1,124 deemed licensed corporations, 75 deemed registered institutions and 15,253 deemed licensed individuals have lodged their migration applications by the end of the transitional period, representing 96%, 83% and 99% of the respective population.  They may continue to provide dealing or advisory services to clients.

Investor protection

Only 43 deemed licensed corporations (comprising 22 brokers, 17 investment advisors and 4 securities margin financiers) and 15 deemed registered institutions (authorised financial institutions) did not migrate to the new regime under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO). 

The licences and registrations of those deemed licensed persons that had not lodged a migration application by today will have now automatically lapsed by operation of law. They must thereafter cease all regulated activities for which they were previously licensed or registered.

The SFC has arranged with those licensed corporations that hold client assets to notify their clients and make arrangements with their clients for the return of their assets.  It should be noted that a corporation that is no longer deemed licensed still owes a fiduciary duty to its clients after 1 April 2005 in respect of client assets that it continues to hold.  Clients of these corporations are advised to arrange for the return of their assets if they have not already done so.

Investors are reminded that these persons will no longer appear on the Public Register on the SFC website.  Only the persons listed on the Public Register may carry on the designated regulated activities.

A rigorous exercise – what we have done

The SFC had taken extensive measures even before the commencement of the SFO and had continued throughout the transitional period to encourage deemed licensed persons to migrate as early as possible.  Such measures included:

- publishing *Guidelines on Transitional Arrangements;*
- devising simple migration application forms that require only the checking of appropriate boxes;
- posting the migration application forms on the SFC website together with sample completed migration application forms and FAQs on the transitional arrangements;
- providing a hotline for  enquiries on migration procedures;
- providing an ‘early bird’ discount on annual fees for migrating in the first year of the transitional period and other fee waivers;
- liaising with various industry bodies regularly to enlist their participation in the migration exercise and to answer their queries;
- publicising the migration progress via press releases and informative articles; and
- identifying deemed licensed corporations which hold client assets, requesting them to provide bi-weekly status reports on their client asset positions and, where necessary, imposing conditions on their licences and arranging with them the timely return of client assets.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “Today we have closed the final chapter on the former licensing regime.  The migration of intermediaries to the new regime under the SFO progressed smoothly and in an orderly manner.  The nine types of regulated activities under the single licensing system of the SFO facilitate the industry to carry on different regulated activities in a capital efficient way.  This new regime represents the culmination of nearly a decade of hard work and ensures Hong Kong’s market regulation is on par with international best practice, affording appropriate investor protection on the one hand, while leaving enough flexibility for market development on the other.”

“Such a high percentage of deemed licensed persons lodging their migration applications before the end of the transitional period would not have been possible without the co-operation of the industry.  In that respect, we extend our gratitude to the industry.” Mrs Lam said.
