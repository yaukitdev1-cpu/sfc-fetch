The SFC today releases a consultation paper on proposals to introduce specific regulations for sponsors and help raise their standards.

Building on the existing licensing regime for corporate finance advisers under Type 6 Regulated Activity of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO), the SFC proposes a set of additional requirements imposing specific entry criteria and ongoing compliance obligations on sponsors.

The Consultation is the second part of a two-stage initiative adopted by the SFC and HKEx to raise sponsor standards in Hong Kong. HKEx concluded the first stage of the initiative with amendments of the Listing Rules and the introduction of the Practice Notes on Due Diligence by sponsors in respect of initial listing applications, both of which became effective on 1 January 2005.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The SFC’s proposals aim to raise the overall standards of sponsors. Sponsors must take responsibility for their work. They are expected to improve the quality of their work through tighter internal supervision, the engagement of skilled and experienced individuals and better employment of resources in carrying out sponsor work."

"Upon completion of the second stage, we will have a comprehensive regulatory regime specific for sponsors. We will have a set of clear benchmarks in place to measure the compliance standards of sponsors. At the same time, the SFC is minded to utilize a broad range of regulatory tools available under the SFO to enforce against the sponsors who fall short of the standards expected of them." Mrs. Lam said.

***The Proposals***

The proposals focus on the following four aspects:

1. Organisational structure, internal controls and resources
2. Management responsibility
3. Formation of transaction teams and appointment of Principals
4. Ongoing obligations

*Organisational structure, internal controls and resources*

Sponsors should ensure that they maintain effective systems and internal controls within the firm, including effective reporting lines between staff and the management. They should also ensure that there are sufficient resources to handle the sponsor work they undertake.

*Management responsibility*

The management of a sponsor has the ultimate responsibility for the supervision of the sponsor work. It also bears the overall responsibility of ensuring that each listing application is advised on by a transaction team which is at all times adequately supported by the firm’s resources and staffed with the right mix of skills, experience and expertise to advise on the transaction in question.

*Formation of transaction teams and appointment of Principals*

A transaction team should be set up for each sponsor engagement. The management should appoint at least one Principal as the supervisor and leader of each transaction team. In order to qualify as a Principal, an individual should be a Responsible Officer licensed to carry on Type 6 Regulated Activity. In terms of experience, the Principal should: (i) possess five years of corporate finance experience; and (ii) have played a substantial role in at least two completed IPOs during the five-year period immediately before he/she is appointed as a Principal. The management should also ensure there are sufficient Principals in carrying out sponsor work.

*Ongoing compliance obligations*

A sponsor should keep proper books and records, as well as proper audit trail for its work. It will be required to conduct annual self-assessment of their internal controls and systems. It is also proposed that sponsors should maintain a minimum capital of $10 million.

Proposed Transitional Arrangements

The proposed effective date of the new requirements will be 12 months from the publication of the consultation conclusions. Licensed corporate finance firms will therefore have 12 months to prepare for the new requirements.

The SFC estimates that there are about 70 licensed corporate finance firms that are active in conducting sponsor work in the past five years. We expect that, generally, these firms should be able to transit smoothly.

As for licensed corporate finance firms that have no track record in conducting sponsor work, they may also transit if they make the necessary arrangements, for example, appointing at least two Principals, in compliance with the new requirements on the effective date.

Mrs Lam said: "Sponsors play a critical role in Hong Kong’s financial markets. These proposals aim to enhance investor protection through raising sponsor standards. We believe that standards expected of sponsors should not be compromised if Hong Kong is to maintain its position as one of the leading capital-raising centres in the world."

The two-month consultation will end on 31 August 2005. The [Consultation Paper](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=05CP8) is available at the SFC office and on the SFC website at www.sfc.hk.
