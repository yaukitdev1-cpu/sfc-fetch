The SFC today publishes the January issue of the *SFC Enforcement Reporter*, summarising the SFC’s enforcement action in the month of December 2004 and discussing the compliance issues highlighted by recent cases.

In December, the SFC successfully prosecuted two people for unlicensed foreign exchange trading and breaching the disclosure of interests law respectively.

The SFC disciplined 11 licensees during the month for account opening failures, liquid capital breaches, making of unsolicited calls and internal control failings.

We also settled with three licensees in December. In one case, a licensed corporation was removed by HKEx from its approved list of sponsors under the GEM for withholding from HKEx material information relating to a listing applicant. The licensed corporation also agreed not to act as a sponsor under the Main Board for five months. Its Principal Supervisor voluntarily withdrew from acting in that capacity for nine months and undertook not to perform any activities for any GEM or Main Board listing applicant or companies for the same period. The SFC agreed not to take further disciplinary action against the corporation and the individual given their respective undertakings not to carry out sponsor and compliance adviser functions during the same respective periods as agreed with HKEx subject to the same exceptions. The SFC decided the settlement was in the public interest as it achieved its primary aim of protecting investors.

In another case, the sole responsible officer of a firm charged cash clients’ securities to a bank as security for an overdraft facility and gave the SFC documents containing inaccurate information during investigation. He appealed the SFC’s decision to revoke his licence to the Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal. He subsequently ceased business, surrendered his licence and undertook never to reapply for a licence or similar regulatory approval from the SFC and withdrew his appeal. The SFC therefore decided not to pursue the matter.

Since 1 April 2004, the SFC has successfully prosecuted 62 entities. We have withdrawn summonses issued against eight persons and a further five persons were acquitted after trial. The SFC has also disciplined 58 licensees for various regulatory breaches and entered into settlements with voluntary payments with three licensees. Disciplinary proceedings have been taken against 18 licensees, which were concluded with no formal sanction imposed, although 15 of them received private warnings. Such proceedings were also commenced and discontinued against four deemed licensees who left their firms before the conclusion of the action.
