The SFC has stepped up its investor education in universities in Hong Kong with a series of initiatives in the 2005/06 academic year. These initiatives, including credit-based courses, lectures and group discussion sessions, are introduced to help younger people establish a proper sense of and attitude towards financial management.

Credit-based courses

The SFC has again partnered with Lingnan University to launch two credit-based investor education courses with expanded and more customised contents:

*Foundations of Financial Investing* – To be offered in October 2005, the five-session course focuses on the fundamental concepts of stock and fund investments, consumerism in using securities services and the principles of personal financial planning. A visit to a brokerage house will be organised for students for a better understanding of stock trading and settlement.

*The Wisdom of Investing* – To be offered in March 2006, the five-session course covers investor activism, corporate activities of listed companies and alternative investment vehicles such as hedge funds, derivatives and structured products.  There will be a visit to a fund house. 

Students can earn 10 Integrated Learning Programme (ILP) units by attending all the sessions of each course (Note 1).

Guest lectures

In November 2005, SFC representatives will give guest lectures on the theme of “Facts and Fallacies of the Financial Market” in elective finance courses for non-business-major students at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Hong Kong Baptist University.

Public lecture series

The SFC will jointly organise with The Open University of Hong Kong a new series of six investor education lectures for the public in November and December 2005.  Prominent speakers and market practitioners will speak on a wide range of interesting investment topics.  More details and enrolment procedures will be announced soon.  

“Meet-the-SFC” sessions

The SFC has scheduled “Meet-the-SFC” sessions for various universities’ finance or investment societies during the fourth quarter of 2005. Through direct dialogues with the undergraduates, SFC representatives will explain the SFC’s work and steps that novice investors should take to prepare themselves for smart financial investing. 

Mr Ian Johnston, Adviser to Chairman’s Office, said:  “Preventive education for students and young adults is an important part of the SFC’s investor education efforts.  Given the language of investment can be complex and confusing, simply learning through experience can be painful, bumpy and costly.  Learning to invest wisely is indeed a lifelong process. The sooner our young generation starts to learn about investing, the higher their financial literacy will be.”

The SFC will continue to explore further opportunities to collaborate with local universities.

Notes:

1. Since the academic year 2001/02, Lingnan University has introduced Integrated Learning Programme (ILP) to complement its formal curriculum. It aims at enriching students’ learning experiences, enhancing their exposure and inspiring creative thinking. All undergraduates are required to complete 75 ILP units in their three-year of study at the University.  The courses “Foundations of Financial Investing” and “The Wisdom of Investing” are offered under Civic Education in the ILP.
