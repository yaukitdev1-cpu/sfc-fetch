SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, gave a speech today at a Seminar on Board Operation Practice and Risk Management. The seminar, co-hosted by the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission of the State Council and Ernst & Young, is held in Hong Kong and attended by senior management of State-owned enterprises (SOEs).

Mr Sheng’s speech is entitled "State-owned Enterprises Reform and Governance". It discusses how companies might improve their corporate governance structure and the checks and balances system of corporate governance, namely through self-discipline, regulatory discipline and market discipline. Mr Sheng said leadership of the SOEs in the Mainland faced significant challenges in the face of global competition and increasing expectation of transparency and accountability.

Mr Sheng outlined the Swedish Government’s corporate governance requirements of the SOEs in the country as an illustration. Back at home, Mr Sheng said that the SFC, as a statutory regulator of the market, was subject to at least nine aspects of checks and balances. Its performance could be measured by comparing the growth of the organisation with the development of the market, as well as comparing its growth with that of its regulatory counterparts in other jurisdictions, he said. These indicators showed that the SFC was an efficient and accountable regulator.
