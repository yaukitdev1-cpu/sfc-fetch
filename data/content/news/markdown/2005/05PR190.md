The SFC today publishes the [Summer 2005 issue (No.60) of the *SFC Quarterly Bulletin*](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/speeches/public/bulletin/sfc_bulletin/summer_05.htm).

The first feature article *The Asian Bond Fund Project 2 – A New Asset Class For Hong Kong Investors* discusses the development of bond index tracking exchange traded funds in Asia and the initiatives from both investment and regulatory perspectives.

The speech* A Pan-Asian Bond Market: How Do We Get There?* was delivered by the SFC Chairman at the 2nd Annual Asia Pacific Bond Congress in Hong Kong.  It addresses the historical development of the bond market in Asia.  It also emphasises the desires for cooperation among regulators to facilitate the convergence of the bond markets in the Asian region.

The third article* A Re-Examination of the Disclosure of Discretionary Earnings Forecasts: The Case of Initial Public Offerings in Hong Kong* is contributed by Professor Paul B. McGuinness, Professor of the Department of Finance at the Chinese University of Hong Kong.  It examines the general characteristics surrounding the voluntary release of prospectus earnings forecasts of recently organised initial public offerings in Hong Kong.

*Fund Management Activities Survey 2004* summarises the findings of a survey on fund management activities in Hong Kong.  It aims at enabling a better understanding of the local fund management industry and to identify possible trends.

This issue also includes the *Half-Yearly Review of the Securities Market* and the *Half-Yearly Financial Review of the Securities Industry.*  The former reviews the performance of the local and regional stock markets during the first half of 2005, whilst the latter analyses the financial performance of SEHK participants over the same period.  The papers point out that:

- Hong Kong’s securities market slightly retreated following the rally in the second half of 2004; and
- overall profitability of the securities industry reduced as comparing with the first half of 2004.  The drop was primarily due to increase in total overheads and interest expense.
