The SFC wishes to alert investors to a boiler room operator named "Bernard Simpson Consultants", which actively targets the Hong Kong public and has recently changed its camouflage.

The SFC first received a complaint against Bernard Simpson Consultants in November 2003 and immediately put it on the [Alert List](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/alert.htm) (Note 1). At the time, it held out to have offices in Malaysia and Costa Rica.

The SFC has received reports that the company might have changed identities. It now purports to operate from Costa Rica and South Korea. It has also changed its website URL from "www.bernardsimpson.net" to "www.bscmail.net".

As a typical boiler room scam, Bernard Simpson Consultants mainly cold calls investors by phone to lure them to buy U.S. company stocks. It also uses the tactic of "forced invoicing", where it issues invoices to investors and asks them to pay for stocks that it claims have been bought on their behalf, even though the investors have never agreed to the purchase.

The SFC has so far received 10 complaints against the company, making it one of the most dubious entities on the Alert List. The majority of the complaints to the SFC have been from Hong Kong investors. In two of the worst cases, the victims lost around $540,000 in total. Both victims were cold called by the company to buy overseas stocks and they paid to the company’s bank account in Hungary or its office in Malaysia.

The SFC wishes to remind investors that self-protection is the best and first line defence against scams. The following are useful tips to help investors avoid falling victim to boiler room scams:

- Reputable brokers do not cold call people who are not their clients to offer investment opportunities. Cold calling is a crime in many places including Hong Kong;
- Stay vigilant. Verify the legitimacy of the persons or companies you are dealing with from a reliable source of information.
- Beware of too-good-to-be-true investment offers.
- Don’t invest based solely on a telephone sales pitch or an unsolicited e-mail, and don’t make a decision hastily.
- Be firm and refuse to pay for a purchase to which you have not agreed.

Since January 2005, the SFC has received 43 complaints of boiler rooms involving reported losses by investors of about $4.6 million. During the same period, 32 suspected boiler rooms were added to the SFC Alert List to warn the investing public.

As part of its investor education work, the SFC has made available educational materials about typical pitfalls of boiler room scams on the "[Investor Alert](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/boiler_room.htm)" section of the SFC-operated Electronic Investor Resources Centre (eIRC) (www.eirc.hk). Besides, the SFC has created [a sample boiler room website](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/alert/boiler_room_website.htm) in that section of the eIRC to show how fraudsters make use of a website to boost their legitimacy.

Moreover, the SFC introduced the Smart Investors Awards (Note 2) in February 2004 to raise public awareness of the modus operandi of various financial scams. A total of 13 awards have been given out. The public are welcome to report suspected boiler rooms or other financial scams for consideration of awards.
