The SFC is pleased to announce that it has signed a Statement of Intent (SOI) concerning co-operation, consultation and the exchange of information with the Financial Services Agency of Japan (FSA) (Note 1).

The SOI establishes a framework for mutual assistance and facilitates the exchange of information between the SFC and the FSA to enable better co-operation and enforcement of the securities laws and regulations of Hong Kong and Japan.

The SOI was signed today by Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC, and Mr Akira Shichijo, Senior Vice Minister for Financial Services of Japan, at the SFC’s office in Hong Kong.

Mr Sheng welcomes the signing of the SOI between the SFC and the FSA. He said "The growing globalisation of financial markets requires securities regulators worldwide to closely co-operate with one another to ensure securities markets are protected from illegal and improper cross border activities and securities laws and regulations can be enforced. The SOI between Hong Kong and Japan, one of the Asia’s most important markets, will greatly enhance the SFC’s and FSA’s abilities in this regard. I am very pleased with this enhanced co-operation."

Notes to Editor:

1. The securities law of Japan requires that an initial agreement concerning co-operation with foreign securities regulators for enforcing foreign securities laws only be made by the Government of Japan with the Government of that foreign jurisdiction. In this instance the Consulate-General of Japan to Hong Kong and the Government of Hong Kong SAR have recently exchanged such an agreement (Note Verbale) concerning the co-operation between the SFC and the FSA. The SOI, setting out the details of the co-operation between the SFC and the FSA, is made pursuant to the Note Verbale. The FSA is the capital market regulator of Japan.

2. Including this SOI, the SFC has signed 35 similar co-operative agreements or MOUs with securities and futures regulators around the world. In addition, SFC has begun to sign Letters of Intent (LOIs) with regional jurisdictions to facilitate greater regulatory harmonisation in line with IOSCO standards. SFC has signed LOIs with Indonesia, Thailand and Sri Lanka. Negotiations are currently on going with several other jurisdictions. These bilateral LOIs will facilitate more Asian financial products (beginning with mutual funds) to be traded within Asia. Hong Kong is willing to work with other jurisdictions to cross-trade each other’s financial products.
