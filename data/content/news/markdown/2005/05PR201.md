The SFC notes that there have been comments in the market and the news media regarding the derivative warrants market. The SFC wishes to make the following statement:

The SFC has commenced a review of the regulatory framework of the derivative warrants market in Hong Kong. The review seeks to establish whether the system and measures adopted in the previous reform of the market, which took place in 2002 after thorough market consultation, can ensure that there is a fair and orderly market for the issuance and trading of derivative warrants. Any proposed changes would be the subject of consultation and the SFC will work with Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Ltd.

The SFC believes that there is a strong need for investor education. The SFC reminds investors that they must understand the nature of derivative warrants and the risks involved. In particular:


- Investors should compare the valuation of different warrants on the same underlying when choosing a warrant;
- Investors need to understand how warrant prices move in relation to factors which affect them; and
- Investors need to beware that short dated and deeply out-of-the-money warrants may not closely track the movement of their underlying assets.

The SFC is stepping up its investor education on derivative warrants. The SFC will in the near future launch a focused campaign to explain some general misconceptions about warrants.

The SFC is investigating specific instances of suspicious activities relating to the trading of derivative warrants on the stock exchange. The SFC cannot be more specific about the details because of confidentiality requirements. The SFC has received some complaints about derivative warrants and will handle them in accordance with our established procedures.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, Acting Chairman of the SFC, said: "We take the views of the market very seriously. We believe it is time to review the derivative warrants market in light of its rapid development. We will not hesitate to crack down on malpractices and consider investor protection a top priority."

"However, it would be premature to draw any conclusion at such an early stage of the review. We will keep the market informed of the result of our study," Mr Au-Yang said.

"In the meantime, investors must make sure they understand the products and the risks involved before investing. When in doubt, investors should seek professional advice," he added.
