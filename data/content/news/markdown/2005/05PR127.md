The SFC is pleased to announce that it has signed a Letter of Intent with India’s Securities and Exchange Board (SEBI) on the enhancement of regulatory co-operation between the SEBI and the SFC (Note 1).

The two regulators aim to work towards the mutual recognition of investment funds authorised in their respective jurisdictions for cross-border distribution.

This Letter of Intent, the fourth to be signed by the SFC, is an important step for both the SEBI and the SFC in the area of cross-border regulation of funds and the development of a cohesive Asian market for investment products denominated in regional currencies, including mutual funds (Note 2).

Under the Letter of Intent, both authorities agree to strengthen co-operation, particularly in matters relating to cross-border trading and the supervision of investment products. It is hoped that with enhanced communication and regulatory equivalence of the respective regulatory regimes, both Hong Kong and India will be able to facilitate the development of deeper and broader investment markets through the trading in each other's markets of mutually recognised funds that are acceptable to both authorities.

Both the SEBI and the SFC are members of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO) and signatories of the IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding (Note 3). This Letter of Intent is signed in the spirit of mutual assistance and co-operation among member securities regulators fostered by IOSCO.

The Letter of Intent was signed today by Mr M. Damodaran, Chairman of the SEBI, and Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC, in Mumbai, India.

Both authorities have also established a bilateral working group which has begun working towards the achievement of the objectives set out in the Letter of Intent.

Mr Sheng said: "The SFC is committed to strengthening regional financial market co-operation in order to raise the quality of the Asian markets and promote investment in financial products originating in the region. By signing this Letter of Intent, the SFC enters into a partnership with the SEBI in a demonstration of our mutual intention to enhance the level of regulatory co-operation between our respective markets in line with the objectives and standards of IOSCO. India and Hong Kong are important securities markets in Asia. We hope to open up regional fund markets through the cross-border trading of financial products in our respective jurisdictions. As securities regulators, we will explore together how to remove unnecessary hurdles to cross-border trading, without compromising the level of investor protection in either of our jurisdictions."

Mr Damodaran said: "SEBI, as a dynamic regulator with a global vision, has been constantly endeavoring to align Indian securities markets with international standards. A signal move in this direction has been to enter into MOUs with various jurisdictions which cover, inter alia, co-operation in information sharing and training. The Letter of Intent with the Securities and Futures Commission is the precursor for forging long term partnership with the underpinning of shared common objectives of harmonising the cross border trading in a slew of investment products with the supportive regulatory framework. I am delighted at the eventful and momentous start in our signing the LOI with the SFC, Hong Kong. I am confident that this Letter of Intent would go a long way to benefit the investing community of our two significant markets in a proper growth trajectory."

Notes to Editor:

1. The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) was established vide SEBI Act of 1992 with an object to protect the interest of investors in securities and to promote the development of, and to regulate Indian securities market. The power and functions of SEBI include the formulation of the securities market policies, regulations and guidelines and implementation, administration and enforcement of the mandate of SEBI Act and rules and regulations made thereunder by such measures as it may deem fit. Such measures inter-alia include ensuring adequate disclosures in the public offering of securities in the prescribed manner, proper conduct of securities market intermediaries, proper performance of various self regulatory organisations/agencies related with securities market, compliance of public announcements and offers under takeover code as well as ensuring the maintenance of integrity and fairness of the Indian securities market.

2. The SFC signed similar Letters of Intent with Indonesia’s Ministry of Finance Capital Market Supervisory Agency (BAPEPAM) in May 2004, with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Thailand (SEC Thailand) in November 2004 and with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC Sri Lanka) in April this year.

3. There are currently 27 signatories of the IOSCO Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding Concerning Consultation and Co-operation and the Exchange of Information (IOSCO MMOU), which is the internationally recognised benchmark for enforcement co-operation between securities regulators. Each signatory of the IOSCO MMOU has the legal authority, the resources and the commitment to initiate or receive and act upon requests for regulatory assistance to or from every other signatory. This facilitates seamless, timely and effective cross-border regulatory co-operation and enforcement assistance.
