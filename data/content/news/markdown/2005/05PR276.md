The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

In the three months from July to September 2005, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 36 listing applications. This compares to 23 listing applications received in the previous quarter (Note 1). Of the 36 applications, we issued comments on 17 cases. Our average response time was seven working days. In the previous quarter we commented on 12 cases of the 23 applications received.

As reported previously (Note 2), in the three months from January to March 2005 we commented on 19 out of 30 applications received. Of those 19 cases, three cases lapsed due to the expiry of the six-month application period, one was withdrawn, and 13 have been listed or have been approved by the Stock Exchange to be listed. Two applications are still outstanding.

Quality of disclosure

The SFC noted two serious disclosure deficiencies common to several cases. Firstly, in a number of cases, neither the business model of the listing applicant nor the commercial rationale of its key business transactions was clearly explained, making it difficult for potential investors to see the genuine business substance of the applicant. This is typically observed where the listing applicant’s operations involve close business arrangements with other, often related, parties. In one case reviewed, the applicant operates a business substantially similar to that of a related company and contracted all material aspects of its business out to this related company during the track record period. It did not have any staff or own any principal operating assets until these were acquired apparently from the same related party towards the end of the track record period. In another case, the listing applicant claims to process products sourced from a third party and sell the processed products back to this supplier for distribution to the ultimate customer in circumstances where the value added by the listing applicant is unclear. Until the rationales of these apparently unusual arrangements were disclosed, investors would not be able to make an informed assessment of the listing applicants’ businesses.

In the second group of cases, disclosure of the relationship with the controlling shareholders is often incomplete. In one of the cases reviewed, the listing applicant relies heavily on its controlling shareholders to distribute its products whereas the relevant shareholder actually has no experience or expertise in the distribution business. The pricing of the distribution arrangement also cannot be reliably verified against market prices. The commercial rationale of the distribution arrangements with the controlling shareholders is unclear.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: “Some companies’ business models involve unusual relationships with related parties or other third parties. It is important that all material aspects of a listing applicant’s operations and business relationships are disclosed in a way that enables potential investors to understand and assess fully the business fundamentals of the company.”

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
