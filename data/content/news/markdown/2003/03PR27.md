The SFC is pleased to announce the appointment of Mr Albert T. da Rosa, Jr. and Professor Charles D. Booth, and the re-appointment of Professor Stephen Y. L. Cheung and Professor Paul B. McGuinness to its AAAC for a term of 2 years.

Mr da Rosa is a legal adviser and a member of the Solicitors Disciplinary Tribunal Panel.

Professor Booth is the Director, Asian Institute of International Financial Law of the University of Hong Kong and a member of the Law Society of Hong Kong CPD Accreditation Sub-Committee.

Professor Cheung and Professor McGuinness have been members of the AAAC since 2001.

"I welcome Mr da Rosa and Professor Booth, and the re-appointment of Professor Cheung and Professor McGuinness, who will bring their professional expertise and valuable experience to the AAAC," said Alexa Lam, Chairman of the AAAC and Executive Director of the SFC.

Mrs Lam also paid tribute to Ms Patricia Shih and Professor Dennis Chan, who have retired from the AAAC, for their valuable contribution.

The AAAC was established under section 6 of the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance to advise the SFC on any policy matters regarding competence and continuous professional training requirements for the industry. It plays a crucial role in raising and promoting high quality industry professionalism among market intermediaries, thereby boosting investor confidence in our market intermediaries.

During the past year, the AAAC approved three industry qualifications (including the new licensing exam under the Securities and Futures Ordinance) and four recognised institutions for providing continuous professional training, and decided on having a sole exam body for running securities and futures exams. The AAAC also endorsed the Guidelines for Exemptions from certain competence requirements, which simplified the process for qualified persons when they seek to enter the industry or take up new regulated activities.

Industry practitioners recognise that it is in their interest to add value to their services. To help intermediaries gain relevant skills and product knowledge to diversify their businesses and remain competitive, the AAAC fully supports the collaborative efforts of the Hong Kong Securities Institute and the Hong Kong Institute of Bankers in developing exams and training programmes on financial planning.

In the year ahead, the AAAC will continue its effort in identifying training and re-training needs of the industry and enhancing professional standard among intermediaries, thereby facilitating access to other financial markets locally and in other jurisdictions.
