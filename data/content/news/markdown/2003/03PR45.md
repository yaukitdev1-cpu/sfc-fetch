The SFC has gazetted a number of codes, guidelines and forms etc that will become effective upon implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance on 1 April 2003.

These items are part of a considerable body of regulatory codes and guidelines that are to be published by the SFC in the run up to 1 April.

Many SFC codes and guidelines are being updated to rationalise their contents with the SFO. New items have also been prepared to assist the market in understanding and complying with the regulatory requirements where applicable.

Most of the revisions do not involve policy changes. Where substantive changes are made, they generally have been made in response to market requests or following consultation with the market.

The 10 items that are gazetted today and included in this release are:

***Code made under section 169, SFO***

*1. Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission*


The revised Code basically follows the current Code; changes introduced are primarily to rationalise the revised Code with the SFO and its rules and to result in a uniform code of conduct applicable to all intermediaries and their representatives.


***Guidelines published under section 399, SFO***

*2.* *Suggested Control Techniques and Procedures for Enhancing a Firm's Ability to Comply with the Securities and Futures (Client Securities) Rules and the Securities and Futures (Client Money) Rules*
 

This guideline is prepared in response to requests from the market and industry associations for guidance on suggested internal control techniques and procedures that are generally expected of:

- a licensed corporation, a registered institution and any associated entity of either of them in complying with the Securities and Futures (Client Securities) Rules that are made under section 148 of the SFO; and
- a licensed corporation and any associated entity of such corporation in complying with the Securities and Futures (Client Money) Rules that are made under section 149 of the SFO.

*3. Debt Collection Guidelines for Licensed Corporations*


The SFC issued the Debt Collection Practice Guidelines in November 2001 owing to concerns over the approach adopted by debt collection agencies in recovering debts owed to registered persons. Apart from the addition of three explanatory notes to facilitate compliance, no material changes have been made in the guidelines now being re-issued. The notes concern:

- to discharge its duty of monitoring and controlling the operation of its debt collection agencies, licensed corporations should bring these guidelines to the attention of the agencies, and require them to undertake that they shall abide by the requirements set out in the guidelines;
- the guidelines are not intended to undermine the licensed corporations’ legitimate business activities or to prejudice their contractual rights; and
- the guidelines should not impose any additional regulatory burden on licensed corporations as they merely outline in writing the minimum debt collection practice standards that licensed corporations have been expected to adopt.

*4.* * Management, Supervision and Internal Control Guidelines for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission*

*5.* *Core Operational and Financial Risk Management Controls for Over-the-Counter Derivatives Activities of Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission*


These are re-makes of the existing version. Only minimal changes have been effected in order to extend their application to registered institutions to ensure a level playing field and to rationalise them with the provisions of the SFO.


*6.* *Client Identity Rule Policy*


The SFC issued the Client Identity Rule Policy under paragraph 5.4 of the Code of Conduct for Persons Registered with the SFC in March 1999. The Rule Policy requires a registered or licensed person, before executing a transaction, to obtain and record details of the ultimate beneficiary of the transaction and the person who originates instructions in relation to that transaction. Only minor amendments have been made to the Rule Policy now being revised to rationalise its content with the revised Code of Conduct and the provisions of the SFO. No changes to the underlying policy or new requirements have been introduced.


***Forms specified under section 402, SFO***

*7.* *Form under the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules*


For the purposes of compliance with the requirements of section 145 of the SFO as amplified in section 56 of the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules, the SFC has developed the Financial Return, which is required to be submitted by licensed corporations regularly. The SFC has designed the Financial Return modeled on the existing forms required to be submitted by intermediaries.


*8.* *Forms under the Securities and Futures (Accounts and Audit) Rules*


For the purposes of compliance with the requirements under section 156(1)(a) of the SFO as amplified in section 3(1) and (3) of the Securities and Futures (Accounts and Audit) Rules, the SFC has developed the Business and Risk Management Questionnaire (BRMQ), Account Disclosure Document (ADD) and Analysis of Client Assets (ACA).

The BRMQ is a new requirement which is designed to obtain information regarding the business activities and risk management measures of licensed corporations and associated entities. In addition, it can be used by all licensed corporations and associated entities as a self-assessment tool to understand their business operation and assess the relevant risk management measures and internal controls.

The ADD sets out additional financial information to be disclosed in the accounts of licensed corporations. It has been drawn up primarily based on the existing rules applicable to intermediaries.

The ACA is a new reporting requirement to obtain information regarding the amount and location of the assets held for the clients of intermediaries by associated entities.


***Others***

*9. Publication of the names of overseas regulatory authorities*


For the purposes of sections 186(5) and 378(6) of the SFO, the SFC has published the names of all the overseas regulatory authorities with whom the SFC may co-operate and share non-public information. Each of these overseas bodies performs any function similar to a function of the SFC or the Registrar of Companies, or regulates, supervises or investigates banking, insurance or other financial services or the affairs of corporations, and is subject to adequate secrecy provisions.


*10.* *Disciplinary Fining Guidelines*


Under the SFO, the SFC may impose a fine on a regulated entity up to the higher of $10 million or three times the amount of profit made or loss avoided, if the regulated entity is guilty of misconduct or is not fit and proper. The SFO requires the SFC to publish Disciplinary Fining Guidelines under section 199 and consider them when it fines.

These Guidelines provide a list of flexible considerations that the SFC will take into account when deciding whether to fine and how to fine. The more serious the improper conduct, the greater the likelihood that the SFC will impose a fine and the size of the fine will be larger. In making such a decision, the SFC will consider all the circumstances of each individual case.
