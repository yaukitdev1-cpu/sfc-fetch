The SFC issues the following update on certain matters concerning the implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO).

Licensees migrating into the new regime

Migration to the new regime is continuing apace.  Around 4,600 intermediaries, representing 25% of all those migrating, had either migrated or lodged their migration applications as at 30 September 2003.  More than 2,600 have already received their new licences.

Licence applications from new entrants

Since 1 April 2003, 1,703 licence applications from new entrants have been received.  32 were from corporations and 1,671 from representatives.  32% of the representatives opted for the fast track provisional licence to commence conducting regulated activities sooner. 

Business consolidation to move towards single licence

Under the single licensing regime, a corporation may be granted a licence to carry on one or more regulated activities.  It is noted that increasingly there is a trend for related licensed corporations to merge their regulated business to benefit (in the form of lower compliance and operational costs) from the new regime.  This process of business consolidation and rationalisation usually takes the form of licensed stockbrokers seeking permission to additionally conduct futures brokerage business.  Some of them also seek to diversify their businesses to include leveraged foreign exchange trading.

Compliance with the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules

The six-month transitional period as provided under the Rules has now expired.  Licensed corporations which were exempt persons (not being authorised financial institutions) or registered investment advisers under the former regime are reminded of their statutory obligations to comply with the financial resources requirements regarding, for instance, paid-up share capital, liquid capital and submission of financial resources returns under the Rules, where applicable. 

Failure to comply with the Rules will amount to misconduct and may render the licensees concerned subject to immediate suspension of trading and/or other possible disciplinary action.  This will also impugn their fitness and properness to remain licensed with the SFC.

Offers of investments

The transition from the old regime under the Protection of Investors Ordinance to the new regime under Part IV of the SFO has generally been smooth.  The new regime has allowed the SFC more flexibility in dealing with rapidly changing market needs while ensuring proper protection for investors.

Selected useful publications

Code on Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

The SFC has published the Code on REITs and the related Consultation Conclusions Paper.  The new Code will regulate all publicly offered REITs in Hong Kong.  It sets out, among other things, the licensing and competence requirements applicable to management companies of REITs (Note 1). 

*Licensing Information Booklet*

To assist the public to gain a better understanding of the new licensing regime, the SFC has also published a Licensing Information Booklet setting out in plain language useful information concerning licensing and related requirements under the SFO (Note 1). 

The SFC will regularly keep the public informed of developments relating to financial intermediaries, investment products or other matters under the SFO regime.
