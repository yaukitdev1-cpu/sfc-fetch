The SFC notes that there was a newspaper article today suggesting that the processing of licensed representative applications had been prolonged after the transfer of the representative licensing function from the Stock Exchange to the SFC. In response, the SFC issues the following statement.

The SFC has not assumed the representative licensing function from the Stock Exchange. It has been carrying out this function since its establishment in 1989. To reduce compliance costs to the industry, the SFC worked with the Stock Exchange to abolish the Exchange’s registration regime for Exchange Participants’ sales representatives on 1 April 2003.

With regard to the processing time of a representative application, the SFC has pledged to shorten the period from 10 weeks to eight weeks since August 2003. Applicants may also apply for a fast track provisional licence (a facilitative measure under the Securities and Futures Ordinance), which is usually granted within seven business days.

Without going into the details of any specific case, the SFC would like to stress that applications for provisional licences would normally be refused only on competence or other conduct grounds. The prime consideration in processing a licence application, whether it is a provisional licence or a full licence application, is whether the applicant is fit and proper to act as a licensed intermediary.

The SFC will continue to facilitate the provision of investment services by licensed intermediaries whilst at the same time ensuring investor interests and market integrity are not prejudiced.
