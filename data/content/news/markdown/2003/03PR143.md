The SFC issues the following statement after the meeting of the LegCo FA Panel today:

In March 2003 the Expert Group to Review the Operation of the Securities and Futures Market Regulatory Structure, chaired by Mr Alan Cameron, published its report. The Expert Group’s key recommendations involved removal of the listing function from Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx) to a new division of the SFC, to be known as the Hong Kong Listing Authority, giving the Listing Rules statutory backing to ensure their effectiveness and allowing HKEx, relieved of it regulatory burden, to operate as a commercial entity with minimal Government influence and less SFC involvement in its day to day operations.

Today the LegCo Panel on Financial Affairs met to discuss the Expert Group’s Report. Mr Cameron and Dr Raymond Ch’ien (two of the three members of the Expert Group) were present. Mr Ashley Alder, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, represented the Commission.

Following the Panel’s meeting, the SFC believes that it is appropriate to reiterate its views on the issues dealt with by the Expert Group.

First, the SFC’s views are not focussed on which organisation carries out the job of listed company regulation. The fundamental issue is to determine whether there are significant problems with the current regulatory structure and, if there are, which solutions will keep Hong Kong competitive and enhance market quality. Our aim should be to achieve a system that works best for Hong Kong. It is clear to the SFC that our present listing system has insufficient enforcement "teeth" to deal adequately with those who breach the rules. In addition, Listing Rules regulation currently involves a large amount of administration and bureaucracy devoted to non-enforcement matters; the bulk of regulatory effort is focussed on prevetting of prospectuses, announcements and circulars. Lack of regulatory teeth in the form of credible sanctions means that there is a relatively low level of regulatory incentive for the market to do quality work, to comply with both the letter and the spirit of the Rules and to adhere to standards that ensure fairness for all shareholders, particularly minorities. Enforcement of the Rules needs to be toughened to ensure that directors and intermediaries have a real incentive to comply, and to discourage conduct that can damage the reputation of the whole market.

We also believe that problems of duplication and lack of clear accountability arise from the fact that two market regulators have split responsibilities for regulation of listed companies. In addition, as the Expert Group points out, there are clear difficulties faced by HKEx in managing its conflicts of interest in listing regulation.

The Commission believes that Hong Kong’s current regulatory structure is out of step with those in other leading financial centres, and that there is an urgent need to build on the achievements of the Securities and Futures Ordinance to ensure that our system is world-class.

Hong Kong needs to fashion a regulatory system which ensures that corporates and their advisers have real and appropriate responsibilities, achieved through effective enforcement of our market rules. Enhanced enforcement capability and credible sanctions should be accompanied by significantly reduced regulatory process and bureaucracy. The goal is to ensure that the majority who play by the rules and play fair with their shareholders will face a lighter regulatory burden. Those who break the rules will find that they face sanctions which operate as real deterrents. This is the international financial centre standard. The goal is to enhance Hong Kong’s competitive position, and raise quality by incentivising appropriate behaviour and compliance at both large and small companies and intermediaries.

The SFC therefore agrees with the Government’s view that the general direction recommended by the Expert Group is appropriate and will enhance the quality of the market.

The SFC also believes that there is a clear community consensus on the need for significant change. This is evident from the very extensive consultation undertaken by the Expert Group, as well as the more recent views of the Standing Committee on Company Law Reform, which on Wednesday released its second phase consultation in which it called for statutory backing to be given to the Listing Rules and recommended that reforms should not be delayed without sound reason. HKEx has also called for reform. It has made clear its view that market quality is a genuine issue, and has made proposals for statutory effect to be given to Listing Rules and a significantly enhanced role for the SFC.

There is across the board agreement that change is needed. All advocates for reform, ranging from the Expert Group itself to HKEx, have identified serious weaknesses with our current listing regulatory system. We must therefore move quickly.

The policy decision is for the Government, and the SFC is committed to carrying out Government policy as effectively as possible and in the best interests of the market. The next phase of research and consultation on these important issues should be conducted so as to enable the Government to make the best decisions on how to take forward the work of the Expert Group as quickly as possible, and in particular to determine the details of the implementation process.

We will spare no efforts to work with the Government and HKEx on this vital project.
