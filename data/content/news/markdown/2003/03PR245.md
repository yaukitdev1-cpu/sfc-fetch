At a meeting of the Working Group on Review of Financial Regulatory Framework for Intermediaries today, members noted that there had been press speculation about its work. Members were concerned that the public might have been led to believe that certain proposals had been made by the Working Group.

The SFC issues the following statement on behalf of the Working Group to give an update to the market.

Background

In 2002, the SFC introduced two amendments to the Financial Resources Rules to manage down the risks of margin financing. Members of the Legislative Council’s Panel on Financial Affairs asked the SFC at that time to review the arrangement for brokers to pool and re-pledge clients’ securities as well as the need to tailor regulatory capital requirements based on the line of business of brokers. The SFC undertook to the Panel that it would form a working group with industry participants to review the whole matter and to report back to the Panel.

Since inception in 2002, the Working Group on Review of Financial Regulatory Framework for Intermediaries has held many meetings to examine the risks in the business operations of brokers in the industry with a view to facilitating the long term healthy growth of the industry, enhancing investor protection, and strengthening the internal risk control system of brokers.

The Working Group comprises a broadly based group of market practitioners (including representatives of brokerages of different sizes), the Consumer Council and academics.

The Working Group is unanimously of the view that a main concern relates to imprudent margin lending of a small minority of brokers. Members agree that brokers who borrow from banks by re-pledging margin clients’ securities collateral to re-finance imprudent lending to clients pose high risks as securities collateral of all clients of a broker are normally pooled in the course of re-pledging. The loss suffered by margin clients in the failure of C.A. Pacific Group is a case in point.

The general consensus of the Working Group is that measures need to be taken to tackle imprudent margin financing practices for the protection of investors as well as the healthy growth of the industry.

Options under consideration

The Working Group is considering a number of options that could go towards meeting the above objectives. For every option, its effectiveness in ameliorating the relevant issues is carefully weighed against its market implications, and any other relevant factors.

The Working Group is acutely aware that each option has its own pros and cons, and as such, none of the options under consideration should be taken as conclusive or definitive. Rather, it thinks that the various options, together with their respective pros and cons, should be fully exposed for public consultation so as to seek further views from the industry and the investing public.

The SFC plans to conduct public consultation when the Working Group is ready to make proposals. Any options put forward by the Working Group will be explained in detail in order to gauge the views of the market, which will ultimately decide the best option to be taken to address the share margin financing risks identified by the Working Group.
