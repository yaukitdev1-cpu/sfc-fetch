The SFC today publishes the July issue of *SFC Enforcement Reporter*, summarising the Commission’s enforcement action in the month of June 2003 and discussing the compliance issues highlighted by recent cases.

In June, the SFC successfully prosecuted five persons for breaking securities laws. A market manipulator was given suspended prison sentence while two company directors were fined for non-disclosure of securities interests. Two other persons were also fined for issuing unauthorised investment advertisements and advising on futures without a licence.

The reprimand of an analyst for selective disclosure of non-public price sensitive information of a listed company highlighted the importance of properly handling such information. If an analyst receives non-public price sensitive information, the only safe course is not to disclose it. It is for the company itself to disclose the information to the investing public as a whole according to the Listing Rules. The SFC recommends HKEx’s Guide on Disclosure of Price-Sensitive Information to market practitioners for further guidance.

During the month, the SFC also announced that it would refine its disciplinary priorities with a two-focus approach. The focus on industry sectors would be extended from brokerages to cover banks’ capital markets departments, corporate finance advisers, fund managers and investment advisers. With regard to the types of misconduct, our new enforcement focus would be on dishonesty, conflicts of interest and serious internal control and management failings that expose investors to actual or possible loss.

Since April 2003, the SFC has successfully prosecuted 16 entities and disciplined 19 licensees for various regulatory breaches.
