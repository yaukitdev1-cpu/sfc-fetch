The SFC has noted that a wide range of structured notes, such as equity-linked notes (ELNs) and credit-linked notes (CLNs), are available in the retail market.  In light of their sophisticated nature, the SFC considers it appropriate to issue the following general investor advice.

Generally speaking, ELNs are debt instruments where the coupon or repayment of principal, whether in cash or physical delivery of the reference shares, is conditional on the performance of a single stock or a basket of stocks.  CLNs are debt instruments under which payments of interest, principal or both are affected by the occurrence of “credit events” of a single company or a group of companies.  A “credit event” refers to specified situations relating to the reference company’s credit position, such as where the reference company becomes insolvent or fails to repay its loans of a certain aggregate amount. 

Structured notes are sophisticated instruments.  Before making any investment in such notes, you as an investor should:

- note that there are different types of retail bonds or notes in the market, many of which have unique and distinctive features.  Not all retail bonds, including ELNs and CLNs, are capital protected.   As the market for structured notes develops, novel features are likely to evolve. 
- read the contents of the offer document or the term sheet to understand the pay-off mechanism and other terms and conditions of a structured note, e.g. whether the note is callable or its maturity date is extendable at the option of the issuer.
- check the creditworthiness of the issuer. An issuer may be a thinly capitalised special purpose vehicle not affiliated with the large international financial institution acting as an arranger for the offering of the note. 
- note limitations on realisation of the investment. This is because there may not be a secondary market for the structured note.  Many factors, such as prevailing interest rates, interest rate expectations, the price of the reference stock, the perceived credit quality of the issuer, time remaining to maturity and the market for similar products, etc. can affect the price of a note traded in the secondary market. 
- when in doubt, consult your financial adviser.  Under the Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the SFC, distributors of structured products are required to ensure that the structured product is suitable for you and ensure that you understand the nature and risks of the structured product.

To help investors learn more about structured notes, the SFC has posted two new educational articles, *Explaining Structured Notes* and *Evaluating Structured Notes*, under the “Getting into Bonds” article series in the “Features” section of the Electronic Investor Resources Centre (eIRC) at www .hkeirc.org.  

The articles do not seek to cover all risks relating to investment in structured notes and will not be able to do so.  Investors should therefore read carefully the contents of the offer document or the term sheet and consult their financial advisers as to the suitability of the note when in doubt.
