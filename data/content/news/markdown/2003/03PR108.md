The SFC issues the following update on licensing matters following the implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) on 1 April 2003.

Migration of licensees to the new regime

On the back of thorough preparation by the SFC and the industry, intermediaries are migrating smoothly to the new regime.

Under the SFO, intermediaries may during the two-year transitional period (1 April 2003 through 31 March 2005) convert their licences that were obtained under the old regime. Four firms and 166 individuals have applied for conversion. As of 30 April, 121 existing licensees have successfully obtained new SFO licences – including 7 licences granted on 1 April.

The SFC urges intermediaries to lodge their applications early, and preferably before the anniversary date of their existing licence. Applicants who apply during the first year will enjoy a 5% early bird discount off their annual fees until the end of the transitional period.

The SFC also wishes to remind deemed licensed representatives that those intending to change employment during the transitional period should migrate to the new regime before resigning from their current principals. Otherwise, their deemed licence would lapse upon their leaving their accredited principals. In this regard, the SFC issued a circular to licensed firms on 23 April 2003 outlining the procedures to be taken (Note 1).

Applications brought over from the former regime

A total of 437 licence applicationsreceived under the old regime remained outstanding on 1 April 2003. These cases have been treated as applications under the new single licence regime. Each of the applicants has been requested to specify one or more of the nine types of regulated activities that the applicant wishes to engage in (Note 2).

Under the SFO transitional provisions, a person who was licensed under the repealed ordinances before the implementation of the SFO is deemed to be licensed for two years. The deeming provisions are generous. For example, a securities dealer before 1 April 2003 is deemed to be licensed to carry out four types of SFO regulated activities: dealing in securities, advising on securities, advising on corporate finance and asset management.

However, intermediaries should consider whether all of the regulated activities are necessary to their business. Fewer regulated activities mean savings on annual fees in the future and easier compliance with fewer continuous professional training units.

New applicants for SFO licences

During April 2003, the SFC received 273 applications for SFO licences from new applicants (2 from corporations and 271 from representatives).

Of these representatives, 11% opted for the fast track provisional licence, a novel feature of the new regime. All of them (except for a few with complications) were licensed within a week.

We have not received any application for a temporary licence.

Obtaining application forms from the SFC website and Infoline

To further facilitate intermediaries’ operation, the SFC has posted forms for migration, other applications, notification and annual return on its website under the "Forms Specified by the Commission" (www.hksfc.org.hk/eng/bills/html/sfo-form.htm).

The SFC endeavours to ensure a smooth migration of about 27,000 licences to the new regime. To that end, circulars will be issued from time to time to guide intermediaries. For example, a circular was sent to associated entities of intermediaries on 15 April 2003 requesting for certain relevant particulars required under the new regime. Intermediaries are therefore encouraged to check their FinNet mailbox frequently, and to look up the "Frequently Asked Questions" posted under the SFO section of the SFC website (www.hksfc.org.hk/eng/bills/html/sfo-faq.htm).
