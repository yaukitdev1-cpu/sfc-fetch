The SFC has gazetted three licensing guidelines today that will become effective under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) when it commences on 1 April 2003.  The Guidelines are published under section 399 of the SFO.

1. *Fit and Proper Guidelines*

These Guidelines outline the areas that the SFC will consider in determining a person’s fitness and properness to be licensed or registered.  They replace the Fit and Proper Criteria published in December 2000 and are more reader-friendly with more examples under each heading.

2. *Guidelines on Competence*

Replacing the Guidance Note on Competence published in June 2001, these Guidelines set out the competence requirements on the academic or industry qualifications, regulatory knowledge, industry and management experience expected of an individual applying for licence.  In respect of a corporation, these Guidelines set out the expected business structures and personnel resources.  Apart from relaxing a few requirements to facilitate individuals enter into the industry and to aid intermediaries diversify into other product areas, there has been no change in the basic policies.  The relaxations are in the following areas:

- Exempting a highly experienced representative from the recognised industry qualification (RIQ) requirement when applying for approval as a responsible officer;
- Exempting a highly experienced representative from the local regulatory framework examination (LRF) requirement, subject to acquiring five additional CPT hours, when applying for approval as a responsible officer;
- Exempting a highly experienced representative/responsible officer from both the RIQ and LRF requirements, subject to acquiring 10 additional CPT hours and business restrictions, when applying for approval to engage in each additional regulated activity;
- Exempting a representative applicant with sufficient compliance or regulatory experience from the LRF requirement; and
- Exempting an itinerant professional (Note 1) from the LRF requirement if his cumulative duration of regulated activities in Hong Kong is not more than 30 days in each calendar year.

3. *Guidelines on CPT*

These Guidelines replace the Guidance Note on CPT published in June 2001.  They set out the requirements for licensed representatives to continuously update their knowledge and skills through continuous professional training, and for licensed corporations to maintain an appropriate continuing education programme for their representatives.  Likewise, the fundamental requirements remain.  There are relaxations in the following areas:

- Multiple counting of CPT units – A representative could multiple count his/her same CPT activities in respect of his/her various regulated activities that have the same competence requirements (Note 2).  Thus a representative licensed to deal in securities as well as advise on securities would only require five CPT hours annually.  (This relaxation is made in response to market representations to the Working Group on the Business Environment of the Stockbroking Industry.);
- Licensed representatives and corporations will no longer be required to annually report on their CPT compliance.  However, records of CPT programmes will still have to be kept and subject to inspection by SFC staff; and
- Time spent by licensed representatives in meetings or activities as members of the SFC’s regulatory committees or official working groups would be recognised as CPT units.

The three Guidelines can be downloaded free of charge from the SFC website under “Licensing Related Matters” of the Intermediaries, Licensing and Investment Products section.  Printed copies will be available for sale at the SFC’s office at $20 each.

Notes:

1. Itinerant professional is an individual from overseas who needs to repeatedly visit Hong Kong on business for a short period each time.

2. Groups of regulated activities with the same competence requirements:

Type 1 - dealing in securities
Type 4 - advising on securities
Type 8 - securities margin financing

Type 2 - dealing in futures contracts
Type 5 - advising on futures contracts

Type 3 - leveraged foreign exchange trading

Type 6 - advising on corporate finance

Type 9 - asset management
