The SFC has relaxed its licensing competence requirements to facilitate re-entry into the securities, futures and leveraged foreign exchange markets by former practitioners.

The relaxation is in response to a recommendation made by the Working Group on the Business Environment of the Stockbroking Industry to facilitate re-entry into the industry by former practitioners (Note 1).

The SFC is pleased to announce that its Academic and Accreditation Advisory Committee (AAAC) (Note 2) has decided that former practitioners who had left the industry for between three and eight years may be exempted from the relevant competence examinations when they re-apply for a licence to practise in the same regulated activity and role as previously licensed. Hitherto only former practitioners who had been licensed within the past three years were exempted.

To be eligible for the new exemption, applicants must also meet at least one of the following requirements:


- Completion of five Continuous Professional Training (CPT) hours for each year of absence from the industry. Regulatory training must make up at least 50% of the CPT activities;
- Attendance at a specialised course conducted by the Hong Kong Securities Institute in respect of securities or futures, or the Vocational Training Council in respect of leveraged foreign exchange. Course details will be announced by the two organisations in due course.

Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The SFC will continue to work with the Academic and Accreditation Advisory Committee and the industry to promote high quality professionalism among market intermediaries, thereby boosting investor confidence in the market."

Notes :

1. The Working Group - comprising representatives from the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau, SFC and Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Ltd - published its recommendations in April 2003.

2. The AAAC comprises representatives from the academia and the industry. It advises the SFC on any policy matters regarding competence and continuous professional training requirements for the industry.
