The SFC is today pleased to introduce a new investor education column and its host, Dr Wise.

Dr Wise is a fictitious character. He represents the collective wisdom and knowledge of market specialists and experts at the SFC. Dr Wise will, through his monthly column, talk to investors about the key issues of investing. He will also explain in plain language various regulatory issues in the increasingly complex financial market.

Dr Wise’s Column is written by various staff of the SFC, but SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, will personally write many of the articles.

In the preface, Mr Sheng explains the purpose of the column: "Investing is a lifetime matter. All of us need to invest to have funds for the future, our children’s education, our retirement and for a rainy day. Because we are investing our savings, we must understand what we are buying, why we are buying, what risks we face and what our investment objectives are."

In the inaugural issue, *From Hiking to Financial Planning*, Dr Wise shares with readers the basic idea of financial planning. On a country trail on the Lantau Island, Dr Wise discusses with his friend the importance to determine one’s investment objective, time horizon and assess one’s financial resources as well as risk appetite. With these in mind, a person has to study and choose carefully the type of investment that is most suitable to him or her.

The article sets the stage for the coming issues on risk management, characteristics of different investment products, how to assess an investment and choose an investment adviser.

Dr Wise’s Column is published on the last Tuesday of every month, starting from December 2003. The column is posted on the SFC website under the "Advice to Investors" section. Investors are welcome to send their feedback and questions to Dr Wise by email at investor.info@hksfc.org.hk.

**Dr Wise’s Column – Preface by Andrew Sheng, SFC Chairman**

Investing is a lifetime matter. All of us need to invest to have funds for the future, our children’s education, our retirement and for a rainy day. Because we are investing our savings, we must understand what we are buying, why we are buying, what risks we face and what our investment objectives are.

In fact, we all make savings and investment decisions all the time: whether to make a bank deposit, buy insurance, buy a house, or buy stocks, bonds, or unit trusts. Because we can face loss if we make the wrong decision, we have to be careful when making each decision. In making investments, it is easy to make mistakes. But not investing for the long term can also be a mistake, where one could face retirement without sufficient savings. Hence, we must all learn to invest wisely.

We in the SFC believe strongly that the quality of our market depends on the quality of our investors. And "learning by doing" is probably the best form of investor education. Good investor education will help retail investors invest wisely and protect themselves as markets inevitably move up and down.

Today, I am writing to introduce to you a new investor education column hosted by "Dr Wise". Dr Wise is not a single person and different people within the SFC will write the column each month. We hope, through this column, to chat with you in plain language about key issues of investing and to explain regulatory issues in increasingly complex markets.

I will personally write many of the columns, so why do we use Dr Wise? The reason is that the SFC is an organization and not a single person. We have many market specialists who can bring you different market perspectives. Hence, Dr Wise stands for a collective wisdom in helping you to make more sense of investing and to invest wisely.

As a regulator, we cannot give investment advice on when and where to invest, or how much you ought to set aside for investing. This is the job of investment professionals. However, we can offer generic advice, help you get the key facts, and share with you the pros and cons of different financial products and services.

We cannot normally comment on individual or specific cases because we are subject to secrecy laws. However, we will monitor trends and draw your attention to potential issues. We appreciate your feedback, and when you write to Dr Wise, we will try to answer you through this column – not every single letter, but where the question is of wide interest.

You are always welcome to write to Dr Wise by email at investor.info@hksfc.org.hk.

**Dr Wise’s Column December 2003**

***From Hiking To Financial Planning***

This is the best time of the year for hiking in Hong Kong.

Chris and I are long-time friends, and we share a passion for hiking. Last Sunday, we decided to go for a short hiking trip on Lantau Island. From Wong Lung Hang, via Sunset Peak, towards Cheung Sha Beach, the whole trip would take us about three hours. That day, the weather was fair and most pleasant.

The Wong Lung Hang Country Trail is famous for its stony path and big steps. From the bottom of the hill to Sunset Peak, there are roughly 2,000 steps rising over 600 metres. When Chris and I reached Lantau Camp near Sunset Peak, we were both catching our breath. But the feeling was good. The light breeze, the gentle sunlight and the beautiful scenery encouraged us to move on.

Chris is a lawyer in his late thirties. Like most people of his age, he has a young family and a passion for his career, but little time for financial planning. "Business has been getting a bit better in the last six months, but it is still unstable," Chris said. "If there is anything I have learnt from the economic recession in the last few years, Doctor, it is that I have to be smarter with my investment and be more prepared for rainy days."

I listened to him carefully as we walked towards the deserted camps on the Trail. "Financial planning and investment is very much like hiking," I said. "You have to do it step by step; understand what you want to achieve, within what time frame, how much food and water to carry, and how much risk you can take." I paused. "And of course, you will have to choose the most suitable path and study your map carefully. Sometimes, the weather may not be as perfect as you wish, and that is why contingency planning and risk management are so important."

Chris was at first slightly puzzled, but soon smiled and said "Umm... Doctor, I think I understand. I need to determine my investment objective, time horizon and assess my financial resources as well as my risk appetite. Then I have to study, and choose carefully the type of investment that is most suitable to me given the investment objective that I have in mind."

I looked at Chris and said "Yes, and there are always many ways to achieve the same result. You can approach Sunset Peak from Nam Shan or by Wong Lung Hang, but the physical strength and time each demand of you is different. You cannot rush, you can only do it at your own pace." As we chatted, we were already climbing towards the trigonometrical station at Sunset Peak.

We sat at the summit for a while. Facing Tung Chung Bay, we had the magnificent Lantau Peak on our left hand side. Chris stretched his back, and said "It feels so good to be up here. On a fine day, you can see so clearly and so far." I smiled. We could feel the gentle sunlight on our backs. "When I invest, I always like to think that I am standing at the highest point of a mountain. I always want to be able to look far into the distance, take a balanced perspective, and understand the possible ups and downs that I may have to go through on the path that I choose. Only with that, will I be able to plan ahead and not to lose sight of my goal." Chris looked at me and added "And it is like understanding the bear market as well. If we look far enough, we may be able to detect signs of a storm. Even though we will not be able to do anything about it, at least we can leave earlier for a safer place, and avoid being caught in a storm!" I laughed. "You have learnt well! Let’s go, it is time for lunch."

It was almost noon when we walked down Sunset Peak. As we were descending the hilly path, Chris looked at the map and said "Doctor, here is a short cut to Pak Kung Au. I reckon that we may be able to save 15 to 20 minutes if we take that path. What do you think?" I looked at the rocky path and assessed the altitude. "Well, at my age, I don’t think I will be able to do that and take the risk. I will take the longer path, even though we will be 20 minutes later." Chris smiled. "My pleasure too! I guess that as with investment, we need to assess our tolerance for risk and then choose carefully the path that is most suitable to us..."
