The Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) will come into operation on 1 April 2003, marking the beginning of an important chapter of Hong Kong’s securities and futures regulation.

SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, said at a press conference today that the SFC would do its best to implement and enforce the law, improve quality of the market, and protect investors.

“The Ordinance sets Hong Kong firmly on the path to reinforce its position as an international financial centre, but success depends on the co-operation of all market participants – intermediaries, professional services providers, issuers, investors and other interested parties,” he said.

“The SFC is ready for the challenges that the responsibilities and powers under the SFO will bring about. Likewise, the industry has been actively preparing for the new legislation,” Mr Sheng said.

The SFO was enacted on 13 March 2002. Forty pieces of subsidiary legislation that are necessary for the implementation of the Ordinance have been made. Certain background information and major features of the SFO are in Note 1.

The SFC has organised or taken part in some 60 seminars to familiarise more than 13,500 practitioners from all relevant sectors with the SFO requirements. Mr Sheng today unveiled initiatives to further assist intermediaries in complying with the law and enable investors to get useful information to protect their interests. These include:

1.     *A revamped SFC Infoline service* *(tel: 2840 9393)* – an interactive telephone enquiry service for the public with specific focus on matters relating to intermediaries licensing and supervision, and investment products authorisation. This has upgraded the enquiry service which was first introduced in 1996, and complements the Investor Hotline (tel: 2840 9333) whose dedicated team of staff deal with investors’ questions and complaints;

2.     *A new investor booklet* *“SFO & You”* – this 16-page publication looks at the changes and new features of the SFO and in particular how they may affect individual investors. Other market participants may also find it a handy guide to the SFO in plain language. This free booklet is available at the SFC office. A more detailed version is posted on the SFC website and the eIRC (the Electronic Investor Resources Centre: www.hkeirc.org);

3.     *An updated investor leaflet “**When and How to Make a Complaint**”* – this explains in detail how an investor can make a complaint in order to protect his or her own interests. It is available free of charge at the SFC office and posted on the websites;

4.     *A comprehensive public Register of intermediaries* – a new public register on the SFC website (under the section “Intermediaries, Licensing & Investment Products”) will enhance the transparency of the intermediaries that investors deal with. Apart from the licensing status and conditions of all companies and individuals licensed with the SFC, the complaint channels of the firms and all public disciplinary actions that have been taken against the intermediaries in the past five years will be shown;

5.     *Second edition of bilingual glossary “An English-Chinese Glossary of Securities, Futures and Financial Terms”*  – an expanded and updated version of the popular bilingual glossary originally published by the SFC in April 1999. This 800-plus-page revision contains Chinese translations commonly used in the Greater China region for over 13,800 English financial terms and phrases – nearly doubling the collection in the first edition. The glossary is available for sale for $100 each at the SFC office. A 20% discount is available for certain groups (Note 2). An electronic version can be downloaded free of charge from the SFC website. Look for this icon  on the SFC homepage; and

6.     *Updated Regulatory Handbook* – the electronic depository of SFC codes and guidelines has been updated in light of the SFO. The Regulatory Handbook can be found on the SFC website, under the section of “Bills, Legislation and Codes”.

The SFC website has a dedicated, comprehensive section on the SFO, which includes links to the full text of the Ordinance and subsidiary legislation, certain useful guidelines (including those on disclosure of interests), and many training and background documents. Look for this icon on the SFC homepage.

Mr Sheng took the opportunity to pay tribute to Legislative Council members, the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau, the Department of Justice, market participants who gave valuable comments during the consultation process, and all the others who have contributed to the enactment and implementation of the SFO and its subsidiary legislation.

*There are significant regulatory improvements in the SFO in the following areas:*


- Greater accountability of the SFC

- For the first time the **regulatory objectives** of the SFC are enshrined in legislation. (Part II)

- Replacement of the existing part-time Securities and Futures Appeals Panel with an **independent full-time Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal** to be chaired by a judge. (Part XI)


- Facilitating market innovation

- The emergence of electronic trading networks has been addressed in the SFO – providers of **automated trading services** which are similar to an exchange-trading network may apply for authorisation, (Part III) while those whose activities are more reminiscent of broking may seek a licence. (Part V)

- A new term “**collective investment scheme”** is introduced to embrace existing financial products but be flexible enough to capture new products and ensure they are properly regulated too. (Part IV)


- A streamlined licensing regime

- A **simplified licensing regime** offers more convenience to brokers, investment advisers etc, who need only have one licence to carry on all the regulated activities that they are eligible to conduct and facilitates simplified compliance systems. Banks are required to be registered to carry on most regulated activities (i.e. not securities margin financing or leveraged forex trading since these are core banking functions regulated under the Banking Ordinance). (Part V)

- Greater flexibility in prescribing **financial and operational requirements** for intermediaries (including the securities arms of banks), and business conduct rules and codes. (Parts VI & VII)


- Combating market misconduct

- Significant reforms and more effective powers relating to **inquiries into listed companies**, supervisory inspections of intermediaries, and investigations into possible grounds for disciplinary action. (Part VIII)

- An expanded civil regime for all types of market misconduct, not just insider dealing as under existing law and establishment of a **Market Misconduct Tribunal** to deal with these, armed with a wider range of sanctions than those available to the current Insider Dealing Tribunal. (Part XIII)

- The criminal regime for market misconduct is expanded to include insider dealing; a **maximum jail sentence of 10 years** and fines of up to $10 million are provided for. (Part XIV)

- **Auditors** of listed companies are granted immunity from civil liability for reporting suspected fraud to the SFC. (Part XVI)


- New disciplinary sanctions to enhance proportionality

- **New and intermediate sanctions** are available. The SFC can penalise improper conduct more effectively and fairly by tailoring sanctions more precisely to the misconduct involved. Full application of the disciplinary regime to banks and their employees. (Part IX)


- New and improved investor compensation arrangements

- A new **Investor Compensation Fund** is established to cover defaults by a wider range of intermediaries, and with a transparent limit of compensation per investor. (Part XII)

-  Protecting investors’ interests

- New power to seek the court’s assistance in **compelling compliance** with certain restrictive requirements - intended to protect investors’ interests. (Part X)

- A **private right of civil action** for any person who suffers pecuniary loss as a result of market misconduct (Part XIII) or by relying on false or misleading public statements concerning securities or futures (Part XVI).

-  More transparent market disclosure

- **Dual filing** requirements establish the SFC as a statutory regulator of listed company disclosure. (Part III and the Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules made by the SFC under it)

- **Tighter threshold for disclosure of interests** in listed companies (a shareholder owning 5%, down from 10%, must disclose), more timely notification and more notifiable items. (Part XV) The SFC has some flexibility in taking a decision to prosecute a breach of the reporting obligations under Part XV and in the first few months after commencement, a warning letter will usually be issued before a prosecution is initiated.
