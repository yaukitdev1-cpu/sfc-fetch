The SFC is inviting secondary school teachers to enrol in its new series of investor education workshops which will be held from 29 September to 11 November 2003.

Teachers workshops are part of the SFC’s investor education initiatives to help secondary school teachers of Economics, Commerce and related subjects understand more about different investment products and the latest market developments so as to facilitate their teaching work.

A total of 12 workshops will be held. They are divided into six modules, of which three are new modules introduced this year. There are two advanced modules covering *Stocks* and *Funds* designed for those teachers who have already participated in previous workshops and would like to enhance their knowledge in these areas, such as different types of corporate activities, specialised funds like hedge funds and real estate investment trusts. Besides, in view of the increasing popularity of bonds, warrants and equity linked instruments, a new module *Alternative Investments* has also been added to provide an in-depth discussion on these products.

The elementary modules covering *Stocks*, *Funds*, and *Options & Futures* are still available for those who have not attended the workshops before.

Participating teachers will receive free teaching kits and SFC-produced investor education materials to facilitate their discussions with students in class.

Since 2000, the SFC has organised workshops for secondary school teachers to encourage them to share with their students the key concepts of investing. A total of 42 workshops were held in the past three years, reaching over 1,700 participants. A "Teachers Corner" has also been set up on the SFC website to provide an easy access to the PowerPoint presentation materials, case studies and exercises used in the workshops.

The workshops are now open for free enrolment. Invitation letters have been sent to all secondary schools in Hong Kong. Interested teachers can also visit the "Teachers Corner" on the SFC website or contact the Investor Education and Communications Department at 2842 7711.
