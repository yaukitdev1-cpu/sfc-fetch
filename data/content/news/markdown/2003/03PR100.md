The SFC will actively take forward the recommendations in a report published today by the Government on measures to improve the business environment of the stockbroking industry.

The SFC welcomes the report by the Working Group on the Business Environment of the Stockbroking Industry. The SFC is a member of the Working Group.

The Working Group has made recommendations in these main areas: enhance the transparency of brokerage commissions and other service fees; minimise compliance burden and rationalise fees; upgrade market infrastructure; and improve training opportunities for brokers.

The SFC will work closely with the Administration, Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx), and the industry to implement the recommendations. The industry’s support and co-operation is vital to successful and timely implementation.

Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "The SFC is committed to working with the industry as a whole. Small and medium sized operators are particularly hard hit in the present business environment. We believe the recommended measures will help strengthen their competitiveness and enhance the industry’s services to investors."

Please see below for an action plan for recommendations in which the SFC has a principal or significant role:



**Recommendation****SFC action and target**Enhance transparency of fees and charges by brokers and banksThe SFC and the HKMA will continue to monitor disclosure.
* Target: Ongoing

The SFC will as a priority form a working group with industry representatives to classify the fees and charges on securities transaction services into broad and standardised categories. As step two, the SFC will work with the HKMA and, through HKMA, the banking sector to solicit the views of banks with respect to these proposed categorisations.
* Target: First meeting of working group in early May 2003; market-wide consultation in third quarter of 2003. Aim to implement across the industry before year-end.

The SFC will step up education to enable investors to understand the services and compare the fees and charges. There will be joint publicity efforts with the Consumer Council and newspapers.
* Target: SFC educational articles in newspapers from 14 April 2003; a series of three articles in the Consumer Council’s *Choice* magazine (starting in the April issue published on 15 April 2003); continuous updates on SFC websiteLevel the playing field between brokers and banksThe SFC and the HKMA will work closely to ensure effective enforcement of cold calling restrictions and consistency in the regulatory approach towards banks and brokers. Consistency in regulatory approach is underpinned by a MOU between SFC and HKMA.
* Target: Ongoing

The SFC will explore with the HKMA the possibility of rationalising the relevant regulatory fees imposed on banks for conducting securities business, with a view to introducing a sliding scale of fees reflecting the size of the securities business of a bank.
* Target: SFC and HKMA to meet in May to discuss further way forwardReview of fees inherited by HKEx from the pre-merger entitiesThe SFC and HKEx will as a priority review the fees, with a view to rationalising and simplifying them for exchange users.
* Target: OngoingMinimise compliance burden on small and medium sized brokersThe SFC has conducted some 60 seminars on the Securities and Futures Ordinance for 13,500 participants.

The SFC is prepared to consider on a case-by-case basis applications to modify or waive any requirements where an intermediary has genuine compliance difficulty, provided investor protection is not compromised. The SFC takes a facilitative approach in vetting such applications.
* Target: Ongoing

The SFC is adopting a flexible approach to regulatory action against brokers on minor, inadvertent or technical first-time breaches of new requirements under the SFO.

The SFC began last year a regular discussion forum with the industry associations. In light of market feedback on SFO requirements, the SFC has issued public statements to clarify and ease the concern of practitioners on fees and CPT requirements. The SFC will continue this exchange.
* Target: Monthly meetings and continuous feedback

The SFC will consider and work with the Hong Kong Securities Institute (HKSI) on alternative means, including enhanced training, to satisfy re-entry requirements.
* Target: End of 2003. Meanwhile, re-entry seekers can make use of the conditional approval for licence provided for in the Guidelines for Competence.Upgrade market infrastructureThe SFC chairs an IP Account Task Force comprising brokers, HKEx and SFC representatives to study and design an enhanced, user-friendly and cost effective investor participant account model, which HKEx intends to put to the new HKEx board for endorsement. This model will remove from small and medium sized brokers custody and settlement risks and help them to better compete with banks and larger brokers. It will also provide enhanced protection to investors.

The SFC has conducted several meetings with small and medium sized brokers to canvass their views on the model and engage their buy-in. It will continue to spread the message across the brokerage community with a view to helping the Task Force to develop a model that is attractive to investors and brokers.
* Target: Pending HKEx Board discussion

In tandem, the Working Group on Review of the Financial Regulatory Framework for Intermediaries, set up by the SFC last year, is taking forward its discussions on, among other things, a lower capital requirement for brokers who do not hold client assets.
* Target: Public consultation in third quarter of 2003Enable brokers to diversify their product rangeA joint working team has been set up between SFC and HKEx to consider streamlining the Listing Rules for SFC authorised funds under Chapter 20, so that they get straight through listing processing. This aims to facilitate listing of products like Exchange Traded Funds and Real Estate Investment Trusts. The SFC made proposals in March to HKEx for comments.
* Target: ETFs and REITs consultation conclusions expected in third quarter of 2003.

On a longer term, we aim to streamline listing of closed end investment companies that are not meant for public offering.

The SFC has been actively taking part in the Administration’s effort to facilitate public offering of bonds. Guidelines and proposed Companies Ordinance changes have been published, which is supported by our investor education campaign.Improve training opportunitiesThe SFC will continue its close liaison with the HKSI in fulfilling the training needs of the industry.
