The Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau (FSTB) and the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) released today (18 November) the consultation conclusions on the proposal to empower SFC to initiate a derivative action.

FSTB and SFC released a consultation paper on 27 May 2003 on the proposal to empower SFC to initiate, without Court approval, a derivative action against wrongdoers in relation to a listed company on grounds including fraud, negligence, default in relation to any legislation, or breach of fiduciary or statutory duty. Any damages awarded by the Court will go to the concerned company and not the aggrieved shareholders. The Proposal is one of the recommendations made by the Standing Committee on Company Law Reform, as part of its Phase I Review of Corporate Governance Review.

A total of 31 submissions have been received, 21 from market regulators and operators, professional organisations and trade bodies, and ten from individuals or firms.

"We have considered the public comments carefully against the overall picture of the measures to enhance corporate governance," a spokesman for FSTB said.

Of the submissions received, four of them do not express any preference; seven give their support to the proposal; eight suggest the proposal be held in abeyance for the time being and 12 are against the proposal.

In view of the limited support for the proposal and the numerous reservations expressed by the vast majority of respondents, both FSTB and SFC have agreed that the proposal would be held in abeyance for the time being, pending –

(a) the passage, enactment and implementation of provisions relating to shareholder remedies under the Companies (Amendment) Bill 2003 which is currently being scrutinized by the Legislative Council;
(b) the accumulation of further experience in implementing the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) which has enhanced SFC’s powers in protecting shareholder interests; and
(c) the conclusion of the Civil Justice Reform.

"FSTB and SFC will keep the implementation of SFO and improvements to the Companies Ordinance under review in light of market needs and public comments, and seek to enhance investor protection and facilitate market development through appropriate measures," the FSTB spokesman said.

"SFC will also continue its efforts in investor education with the aim of enhancing investors’ understanding of corporate activities and of their rights as shareholders. Minority shareholders will be encouraged to participate more actively in the way companies are run through the exercise of their voting rights at general meetings," an SFC spokesman said.

"In the event that the provisions of the Companies (Amendment) Bill 2003 relating to shareholder remedies are enacted, SFC will play its part in increasing investors’ awareness of their new statutory rights of redress as aggrieved shareholders," the SFC spokesman added.

The consultation conclusions are available on the websites of FSTB ([www.info.gov.hk/fstb](http://www.info.gov.hk/fstb)) and of SFC (www.hksfc.org.hk).

A derivative action refers to civil proceedings brought by a minority of company members (shareholders) in their own names seeking a remedy for the company in respect of a wrong done to it. Any damages awarded by the Court would go to the company, instead of to the members initiating the derivation action. The proposal to empower SFC to initiate, without Court approval, a derivative action involves the use of SFC’s resources to initiate such action where the company is unwilling or unable to do so, and where the exercise of the power is both in the public interest and in the interest of the company concerned.
