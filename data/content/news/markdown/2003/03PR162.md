The SFC issues the following update on licensing matters after the implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO).

Licensees migrating into the new regime

During the first 100 days of the new regime (1 April to 9 July 2003), 20 corporations and 1,062 representatives (together representing about 4% of the industry total) have successfully converted their old licences to the new SFO licences.  Another 60 corporations and some 500 representatives have also submitted their migration applications and will get their new licences soon. 

As a continuous effort to facilitate the process, the SFC has posted some sample completed migration application forms on its website (under “Forms specified by the Commission” of the SFO section) to help licensed corporations and their representatives to fill out the forms.  In addition, the SFC has simplified the procedures to transfer licensing conditions to the new licences in order to expedite the migration process.

Applications from new entrants

Since 1 April 2003, the SFC has received 1,189 applications from new industry entrants (16 from corporations and 1,173 from individuals).  Whilst Type 1 regulated activity (i.e. dealing in securities) is the most popular for individual applicants, Type 4 (i.e. advising on securities) is the most popular for corporate applicants.  Amongst the applications submitted by the individuals, about 16% also applied for a provisional licence in order to commence conducting regulated activities earlier.

Fidelity insurance for stock exchange participants fully operational

Following the development of a new fidelity insurance scheme for the new regime, a master insurance policy covering stock exchange participants for the year ending 31 March 2004 has been approved by the SFC and is now fully operational.  Under the Securities and Futures (Insurance) Rules, participation in this master insurance policy is compulsory for stock exchange participants licensed for Type 1 regulated activity (i.e. dealing in securities). 

The insurance policy provides protection to the licensed corporations concerned against the risks of losses of client assets due to robbery, theft, forgery and fraudulent acts by employees, etc.  It serves as a continuation of the Brokers’ Fidelity Insurance (BFI) Scheme previously arranged by the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited for its participants.  Both the insurance limit and deductible level of the new policy follow the BFI.  Each participant of the scheme is insured for a maximum indemnity limit of $15 million per scheme year with a deductible amount of $3 million per claim/loss (Note 1).

Competence examinations also in full swing

In tandem with the introduction of the new licensing regime, the Hong Kong Securities Institute rolled out its new examination programme on 14 June 2003.  It now offers examination modules covering various regulated activities such as dealing in and advising on securities and futures contracts, advising on corporate finance, securities margin financing and asset management.

The Academic and Accreditation Advisory Committee of the SFC has approved the Vocational Training Council to continue to administer the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Examination programme. Applicants who have completed the programme are considered having met the competence examination requirements under the new licensing regime.

Notes:

1.     “Deductible amount” in relation to a policy of insurance means the amount of any loss or claim that an insured person must bear himself.
