The SFC has been approving subordinated loans or loan facilities obtained by licensed brokers, which enable them to meet the minimum liquid capital requirement under the Financial Resources Rules (FRR). This helps facilitate brokers’ business operations amid several popular Initial Public Offerings (IPOs), without compromising investor protection and market integrity. 

Over the past few weeks, there have been a number of very successful IPOs in Hong Kong that have been many times oversubscribed.  To facilitate brokers in offering IPO financing to their clients, the SFC has, from early November 2003 to date, approved 10 IPO-related subordinated loans or subordinated loan facilities aggregating HK$1,325 million.  The SFC took, on average, less than four business days to process each of the applications for approval.  In straightforward cases where the documentation met all requirements, approval was granted within one to two business days.

Brokers are urged to ensure that they submit full details and documentation that meet all requirements as that will expedite the approval process.

Treatment of IPO under the FRR

Under the FRR, the scale of IPO financing that a broker can provide is governed by the size of its regulatory capital (being the sum of its shareholders’ funds and approved subordinated loans).

(a) Firstly, bank borrowings will increase the required liquid capital of a broker.   If a broker is to borrow $200 million bank financing over an IPO, its required liquid capital will increase by 5% of that amount, i.e. $10 million. 

The objective of setting the required liquid capital is to provide a capital buffer to provide for any non-specific risk arising from an operation. 

For the avoidance of doubt, bank loans and IPO client receivables must be stated at gross and not netted off each other.

(b) Secondly, a broker is required to collect at least 10% of the subscription money from its clients, failing which the broker will have to fund the shortfall with its own regulatory capital.

Compliance with the FRR

Brokers are encouraged to estimate the volume of business they may want to be engaged in for each IPO as early as possible and to update this estimate as frequently as possible.  If they envisage a very high volume of IPO business, they must take all appropriate measures well ahead of time to avoid breaching the FRR at any time.  Further, they should discuss any likely problems they might have with the SFC at the earliest opportunity. 

In view of current market development, we shall explore with the market additional or further measures to facilitate IPO activities without compromising investor protection.
