The SFC notes that the fund management industry is concerned that it may be incurring higher licensing fees and subject to additional Continuous Professional Training (CPT) and financial resources requirements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO).  To enhance regulatory clarity and ease the market’s concerns, the SFC issues the following statement:
**Licensing fees** 

Industry practitioners are concerned that they may need to pay higher licensing fees because they will be deemed to be licensed for more than one regulated activity (see Note).  The industry therefore suggested that fund managers should be allowed to carry out incidental regulated activities (e.g. giving investment advice, issuing research reports, etc) with a licence for Type 9 (asset management) regulated activity.

The SFC wishes to state that in implementing the SFO, it will carefully consider any suggestion and, where appropriate, fine-tune the provisions of the legislation.  To this end, the SFC has been actively considering the submission made by the industry.

Meanwhile, it should be noted that although fund managers will be deemed to be licensed for more than one regulated activities under the SFO during the two-year transitional period, they will not incur any more licensing fees than what they are now paying.  In this regard, the SFC has provided various fee waivers applicable to existing intermediaries.  For instance, during the transitional period, an investment adviser needs only pay an annual fee for one regulated activity (i.e. $4,740) instead of three deemed regulated activities.  

Continuous Professional Training

The industry is concerned that fund managers may be required to complete more CPT hours on the basis that each licensee is required to complete five CPT hours per calendar year for each regulated activity.  It should be noted that special arrangements are provided during the transitional period such that existing licensees only need to complete the number of CPT hours that is currently applicable to them.

Capital Requirements

Industry practitioners are concerned that more regulatory capital may be required as they will be deemed to be licensed for more than one regulated activities during the transitional period.  The SFC wishes to stress that the capital requirements applicable to Type 4 (advising on securities), Type 5 (advising on futures contracts) and Type 9 (asset management) regulated activities under the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules are all the same. Besides, the capital requirements that are applicable to a corporation licensed for more than one type of regulated activity is simply the higher (or the highest) of the respective capital requirements that are applicable to each of the regulated activities it is licensed for.

To illustrate, a corporation that is licensed for Type 1 (dealing in securities), Type 3 (leveraged foreign exchange trading) and Type 9 regulated activities is only required to maintain the highest liquid capital requirement (i.e. $15 million which is applicable to Type 3 regulated activity), although the individual liquid capital requirements for Type 1, Type 3 and Type 9 regulated activities are $3 million, $15 million and $3 million respectively.
