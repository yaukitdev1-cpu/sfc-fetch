In response to market enquiries on the launch of Real Estates Investment Trusts (REITs), the SFC would like to highlight certain licensing matters relating to fund management companies for REITs.

Licensing requirements

Basically, a management company for REITs is required to be licensed for Type 9 regulated activity (asset management). Whether it needs to be licensed for other types of regulated activity depends on its mode of operation.

For instance, if it intends to undertake matters relating to the listing application of REITs without appointing a listing agent, it will need to be licensed for Type 6 regulated activity (advising on corporate finance). If it wishes to distribute REITs or other securities, it will need to be licensed for Type 1 regulated activity (dealing in securities).

There are currently a large number of management companies licensed for Type 9 regulated activity. They can act as a REIT manager by:
(i) employing in-house property managers (individuals) with requisite experience and whose roles are properly supervised; or
(ii) delegating or contracting out the property management functions to external property management companies, subject to proper on-going supervision being in place.

Alternatively, a new management company can be set up specifically to manage REITs with all necessary staff to provide both investment and property management as a "one-stop" service. The new management company has to apply for a new licence with the SFC for asset management and it will take approximately 15 weeks to process the company’s licence application after it has submitted all required information to the SFC for consideration. It should be noted that such licence application is different from the authorisation application for the REIT concerned. The latter application relates to the authorisation of the REIT itself (rather than the management company) and will take approximately 30 to 35 business days to process depending on the complexity of the product, quality of the application and barring any unforeseen circumstances. Both types of application can however be processed in tandem.

Parties interested in launching REITs (whether or not they are already licensed by the SFC) should approach the SFC as soon as possible to discuss related licensing and authorisation requirements before submitting formal applications.

Key personnel of management companies

Like other licensed corporations, each management company for REITs shall appoint at least two responsible officers to directly supervise its business. Each of them has to possess not less than five years’ experience in managing collective investment schemes including private funds apart from complying with the general competence requirements for the purposes of licensing. It is desirable that new management companies recruit their key personnel from the existing pool of fund management experts who are already approved by the SFC as responsible officers to carry on Type 9 regulated activity.

FAQs posted on SFC website

The SFC has prepared FAQs containing relevant licensing information to assist market entrants in setting up management companies for REITs. These FAQs are available on the SFC website at www.hksfc.org.hk (under "Securities and Futures Ordinance" – "FAQs" – "Licensing").

Separately, the SFC will issue a checklist to facilitate applications for authorisation of REITs. The FAQs on the SFC website will also be updated from time to time to clarify issues commonly raised by market practitioners relating to their interpretation of the Code on REITs and applications for authorisation of REITs.
