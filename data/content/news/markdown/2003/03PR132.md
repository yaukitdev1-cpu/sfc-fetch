The SFC issues the following update on licensing matters following the implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) on 1 April 2003.

Licensees migrating into the new regime

More licensees are seeking to convert their old licences to the new SFO licences.  In the last month, 583 licensees (including 26 corporations and 557 individuals) submitted their migration applications to the SFC.  This number is more than three times that of April (Note 1).  To date, 346 licensees have successfully obtained their new SFO licenses.

Applications from new entrants

The SFC received 267 new licence applications in May 2003.  Among the 258 applications submitted by individuals, 26% also applied for a provisional licence so that they could conduct regulated activities earlier (i.e. before the grant of their normal representative licences) (Note 2).

SFC Infoline revamped

In light of the commencement of the new regulatory regime, the SFC has revamped its 24-hour Infoline which is accessible by the public at 2840 9393.  The new Infoline provides useful information about licensing and supervision of intermediaries and authorisation of investment products.  Through the Infoline’s fax request, intermediaries and licence applicants can obtain licence application forms, notification forms and annual return forms.  The SFC would like to encourage intermediaries to make good use of resources available in the Infoline.

Public register of licensed persons and registered institutions

Under the SFO, the SFC is required to maintain a register of licensed persons and registered intermediaries.  Members of the public may access the register through the SFC website ( prsfo. hksfc.org.hk/sfcprd/eng/pr/html/PR002.jsp).  Some of the information contained in the register and available for public inspection include:

- Licensee’s name;
- Effective date of a licence;
- Type(s) of regulated activity that the licensee is permitted to conduct;
- Condition(s) imposed on the licence;
- Licensee’s business addresses;
- Record of each public disciplinary action taken by the SFC against the licensee in the past five years; and
- Contact information of complaints officers.

The SFC considers that the public register would be particularly valuable to investors as they could know more about an intermediary before dealing with it.

**Updated FAQs on the SFC website** 

As part of its efforts to help market participants understand the new regulatory regime, the SFC from time to time posts on its website answers to those most commonly raised questions on licensing and other matters.  Intermediaries are encouraged to look up the FAQs under the SFO section and subscribe our Website Update e-mail Alert service, which is free of charge, for the most up-to-date information.

Ends

Notes:

1.     In April 2003, four corporations and 166 individuals submitted their migration applications.

2.     “Provisional licence” is a new licence category under the new licensing regime.  An individual applying to be a representative may choose to obtain a provisional licence upon payment of an additional application fee of $800.  It takes about one week to grant a provisional licence as compared with about 10 weeks for a normal representative licence.  The provisional licence is deemed to be revoked when the SFC subsequently approves or refuses the normal representative licence application.
