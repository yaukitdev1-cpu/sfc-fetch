The SFC is surveying investors’ views on securities investment research activities in Hong Kong, particularly on the possible conflicts of interest of securities analysts.  This exercise follows an earlier industry survey on securities firms’ investment research practices.

Investors often make references to the information or opinions provided by securities analysts or brokerage firms during the investment process.  Over the past year, there has been considerable debate over the role of analysts in the investment process.  Concerns have been raised that analysts may face conflicts of interests. 

The SFC has been actively monitoring the developments both in Hong Kong and on the international front.  To obtain further empirical data and a more comprehensive view on these important issues, the SFC is conducting a survey among retail and institutional investors.

The SFC is pleased to have obtained the assistance of the Consumer Council in designing the questionnaire and distributing it through its April 2003 issue of the *Choice* magazine, which is published today.

Interested investors may also complete and return the questionnaire online through the SFC website (http:// eapp01.hksfc.org.hk/apps/lc/investor_survey.nsf/GenerateIntroEng?openagent).  A hyperlink to the questionnaire has been established with the Electronic Investor Resources Centre ([www.hkeirc.org](http://www.eirc.hk/eirc/html/EN/index.htm)).  Information could also be found on the website of the Consumer Council ([www.consumer.org.hk](http://www.consumer.org.hk)).

The questionnaire covers areas including investors’ use of and observations on research reports, issues relating to disclosure of interest and conflicts of interest, and investors’ views on credit ratings.

The questionnaire is also being distributed through investors’ associations. The SFC encourages investors to express their views. No personal information is requested when filling in the questionnaire.

The survey on investment research practices was completed in March.  The SFC has received more than 360 replies and is studying them.

An SFC spokesman said: “We will take into consideration the results of these surveys and other factors in formulating policy proposals most suitable for Hong Kong.  The public will be consulted on any proposed regulatory measures that may be formulated.”
