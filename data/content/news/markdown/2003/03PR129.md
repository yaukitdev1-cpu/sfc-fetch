Operation of the dual filing arrangement under the Securities and Futures Ordinance has been smooth.

The SFC has reviewed 27 new applications for listing on the stock exchange up to the end of April 2003 (Note 1), and has commented on 10 of them. In four cases, there were fundamental problems.

Experience to date is that Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx) also agreed with the SFC’s comments. The SFC will continue to work in full co-operation with HKEx.  In fact, dual filing is part of an overall shift in regulatory approach, which is shared by HKEx.

The SFC’s average response time for the 10 cases was seven working days. The shortest time was one day.

So far, the SFC has not exercised its reserve power to object to any new listing applications.  Since serious issues are identified at the beginning of the process, and the SFC and HKEx are working together, a statutory objection at the end of the process would not normally be necessary.

SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, Mr Ashley Alder, said the dual filing arrangement had not caused any additional compliance burden on issuers who had prepared adequate draft prospectuses.

“The Memorandum of Understanding signed between the SFC and HKEx in January sets out the arrangements to ensure that dual filing doesn’t give rise to additional burden or cost to companies and market practitioners,” he said today at a briefing to the media.

“As we’ve planned, the SFC has remained in the background and has not added any time to the overall listing process,” Mr Alder said.

The SFC undertakes a preliminary review of each listing application under dual filing. Where there are apparent matters of concern about disclosure, a more thorough review will be conducted.

“As we have emphasised before, the regulatory focus should move from pre-vetting towards enforcement. This implies a better incentive structure and is the international standard. Those who comply with the rules will have more flexibility and freedom to do business; those who break the rules will face regulatory consequences. This would be good for both investors and the market.  Both the SFC and HKEx are working actively on this approach,” Mr Alder said.

The SFC gives comments only on substantive issues. It seeks to ensure that the overall disclosure in a prospectus enables investors to understand the business of a company and to make informed investment decisions. In cases where the applicants needed to do more work, the SFC discussed the issues with the Listing Division of HKEx and problems are pointed out to the applicants and their sponsors.

The SFC has observed some common problems where disclosure tends to be inadequate:

- Related party transactions that have been a large part of the company’s business;
- Businesses of a controlling shareholder that potentially compete with the company;
- Business model - R&D, manufacturing and/or marketing;
- Very impressive growth or performance, including very high margins;
- Industry overview and competition.

Statements of disclosure on these aspects must be supported by sufficient background and explanations. Listing sponsors must carry out proper due diligence to understand their clients’ businesses, the industry, etc and to help draft the disclosure to enable investors to get the same understanding. 

If a sponsor does this before filing an application, there would be very few issues outstanding and an application should sail through. In this respect, the forthcoming SFC-HKEx consultation on the regulation of sponsors is important.

Mr Alder concluded: “I want to stress that we want this to be part of a more market-friendly listing application process that also more effectively protects the interest of investors. Both the SFC and HKEx are moving in the same direction with greater mutual understanding. Applications with good disclosure should encounter fewer hurdles than before. Bad disclosure will encounter more.”

Note:

1.  Under the Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules of the SFO, which became effective on 1 April 2003, listing application materials to the HKEx are also filed with the SFC. The SFC has the powers to make comments and object to a listing application. The SFC can also exercise its statutory powers to take action against false or intentionally misleading disclosure. To enable a smooth transition, the SFC made arrangements with HKEx to start receiving listing materials from HKEx from 1 March 2003. The 27 cases were those received in March and April 2003.
