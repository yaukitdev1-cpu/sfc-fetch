The initiative to update the prospectus regulatory regime received broad public support, according to the Consultation Conclusions on "Proposed Amendments to the Companies Ordinance (CO) to facilitate Offers of Shares and Debentures" released by the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau (FSTB) and the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today (November 25).

Sixteen submissions were received from financial institutions, professional bodies, market practitioners and organisations representing investors on proposals related to the amendments to the CO contained in the joint consultation paper published in March 2003. The submissions helped to shape the Companies (Amendment) Bill 2003 introduced to the Legislative Council on June 25, 2003.

The major legislative proposals to update the prospectus regime include -

(a) excluding from the prospectus regime offering documentation related to specific types of offers - such as small-scale offers, offers to "professional investors" and offers to not more than 50 persons;

(b) allowing more flexible operation of the prospectus regime by providing the SFC with an additional criterion to grant exemptions from full compliance with certain prospectus requirements - that the exemption would not be prejudicial to the interests of the investing public;

(c) providing a statutory basis for proposals to permit awareness advertisements and to introduce an alternative "dual prospectus" structure to facilitate "programme" offers, as originally spelled out in guidelines issued by the SFC in February 2003.

The proposed amendments to the CO are Phase II of a three-phase exercise designed to facilitate market development by overhauling the existing regulatory framework for offers of shares and debentures.

Under Phase I, the SFC in February 2003 issued three guidelines permitting awareness advertisements, an alternative dual prospectus structure, and relaxation of procedural requirements for registration of prospectuses. The SFC also issued two class exemptions related to prospectuses for offers of debentures.

Phase III of the exercise will involve a comprehensive review of the securities offering regime by the SFC.

"We are pleased to note that facilitative measures implemented in Phase I are bearing fruit - a number of companies have tapped the procedural relaxation and issued retail bonds under the new guidelines promulgated by SFC in February 2003 and the class exemption notice which came into effect in May 2003," said Mr Frederick Ma, the Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury.

"We believe that the Phase II measures, if implemented, will further boost the development of the bond market," he added.

Since the SFC issued its guidelines on the dual prospectus structure in February 2003, five issuers have taken advantage of the guidelines to launch "programme" debenture offers. Two of the issuers made further debenture issues on the basis of their initial programme prospectuses.

"We will continue to engage market practitioners in the Phase III review and aim to put forward proposals for public consultation by September 2004," said the Executive Director of the SFC, Mr Ashley Alder.

The Companies (Amendment) Bill 2003 is currently under the scrutiny of the Bills Committee of the Legislative Council. Examination of prospectus-related amendments by the Bills Committee is near completion.

The consultation conclusions are available on the websites of FSTB ([www.info.gov.hk/fstb](http://www.info.gov.hk/fstb)) and of SFC (www.hksfc.org.hk).
