SFC Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng, today outlined the priorities of the Commission’s work in 2003.

The top priority is to ensure a seamless transition to the new regulatory framework under the Securities and Futures Ordinance. The new framework represents significant improvements in many areas of securities and futures regulation, including more effective measures for investor protection.

The SFO was enacted in March last year and is expected to come into operation on 1 April 2003. The Commission will be busy putting the new law into operation.

Secondly, Mr Sheng said the SFC would toughen its enforcement activities to tackle listed corporate crime and misconduct in the securities and futures area.

"We will enforce the law to protect the investors," he said at a meeting with media representatives.

"There will likely be more listed company investigations and enforcement action arising from dual filing. The SFC will take stern action against IPO sponsors whose work falls short of the high standards expected of them. There will also be a tougher disclosure of listed company interest regime under the SFO," Mr Sheng said.

In addition to enforcement, the SFC will continue to work on other areas of work including investor education, enhancement of market quality, market development facilitation, and infrastructure building. (Please refer to the attached document for more information and the progress made in these areas in 2002.)

In the current economic environment, the SFC has kept its expenditures in check. The accumulated deficit for the nine months to the end of 2002 was $35.3 million, 19% lower than the $43.6 million a year earlier. (Note 1)

The SFC will continue to exercise strict cost controls. Annual operating expenditure for the fiscal year ending 31 March 2003 is projected to be 5% lower than that of last year. Projected full year deficit is $69 million.

Mr Sheng said a major undertaking this year would be the relocation of the SFC office at the end of June.

"No decision has been made on the new premises yet. Our primary objective is to keep the rental down and at a level commensurate with the current economic environment," he said.
