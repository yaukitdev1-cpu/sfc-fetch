The SFC has today published the Code on Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) and the related Consultation Conclusions Paper. The new Code will regulate all publicly offered REITs in Hong Kong.

The SFC issued a Consultation Paper on the Draft Code on REITs on 7 March 2003 for an initial period of four weeks. At the request of market participants and industry practitioners, the consultation was extended by two weeks and ended on 23 April 2003.

A total of 52 submissions were received from a diverse group of professionals including investment bankers, fund managers, lawyers, property managers/developers, surveyors and accountants.

The market welcomed the issuance of a code to authorise REITs for retail investors. Respondents generally requested changes to the regulatory provisions of the draft Code governing REITs operations and investments to allow more flexibility in the development of the REITs market.

The SFC has discussed the issues with respondents and consequently, the SFC has decided to revise a broad range of key requirements while maintaining other regulations so as to strike a balanced approach towards maintaining robust investor protection and catering to industry development.

REITs are a new product that have unique characteristics. A REIT combines the features of an investment fund with that of a listed property company. As such, the hybrid nature of REITs requires a dynamic regulatory framework. The new Code incorporates key listed company corporate governance principles such as the disclosure of material information to unit holders, connected party transaction protection and public announcements.

There are four broad areas where the relevant revisions to the Code on REITs have been made. Highlights of the major revisions include:

Use of Special Purpose Vehicles
The Code has been revised to allow REITs to invest in real estate through Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs). This will enable REITs to benefit from the same tax treatment as property companies. To ensure sufficient transparency, the SFC will mandate all SPVs to be 100% owned by the REITs and there should be no more than two layers of SPVs.

Operational Issues
The SFC has agreed to modify the restrictions as follows:

- the management company may choose between internal management and external management;
- the borrowing limit of a REIT will be increased to 35% of the value of gross assets (original proposal was 35% of the value of net assets);
- investments in hotels and recreation parks are permitted;
- no limit on cash holdings;
- dividend payout ratio will be reduced to 90% of net income after tax (original threshold was 100%); and
- valuation frequency will be once a year (original proposal was quarterly).

Listing and Corporate Governance
The SFC has reached a general understanding with Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx) on our respective regulatory roles: the SFC will be the responsible authority for authorising REITs as collective investment schemes, including the vetting of constitutional and offering documents, and the monitoring of on-going compliance with the Code. HKEx will be responsible for the listing of REITs on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong, including the vetting of documents in respect of listing issues not covered by the Code, supervision over the listing process and the monitoring of on-going compliance with the Listing Rules.

Geographical Restrictions
The SFC will maintain the restriction that SFC-authorised REITs should only invest in Hong Kong property. This decision is made in line with the SFC’s primary objective to give investors an additional product through which they can participate in investment in the Hong Kong real estate market. Hong Kong investors are already familiar with the local property market, which has in place a clear and robust legal framework for property ownership.

In response to the feedback received, the SFC will set up a task force to examine the minimum benchmarks that REITs should set for themselves when they invest in overseas real estate. The SFC believes that as the REITs market develops, the geographical restriction could be removed.

Alexa Lam, Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: "We are grateful to the industry for their helpful and constructive feedback to the consultation. With the new revisions implemented, we believe that the Code has achieved a good balance at this initial stage. To maintain this balance and ensure a good standard of corporate governance, we intend to keep the Code under review and will continue to work closely with the market and investors as the market evolves."

The SFC has also unveiled a new investor education pamphlet explaining the nature of REITs and the investment risks involved. As the underlying assets of REITs comprise real estate, their value could be affected by property, interest rate and rental cycles, and the economic and political outlook at a given period of time.

Copies of the Code, the Consultation Conclusions Paper and the investor education pamphlet are available at the office of the SFC and on the SFC website at www.hksfc.org.hk.
