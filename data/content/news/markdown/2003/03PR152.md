The SFC will take a serious view on non-disclosure and false or misleading disclosure of interests in Hong Kong listed companies.

Hong Kong’s statutory requirements on disclosure of interests are set out under Part XV of the Securities and Futures Ordinance. They aim to ensure greater transparency of dealings by substantial shareholders and corporate insiders so that there is a level playing field for all investors.

The SFO has set a tighter threshold for disclosure (a shareholder owning 5%, down from 10%, must disclose), and has prescribed more timely notification (three business days as compared to five days previously) and more notifiable items.

Upon the implementation of the SFO on 1 April 2003, the SFC stated that it had some flexibility in taking a decision to prosecute a breach of the reporting obligations under Part XV. It indicated that, in the first three months after commencement, a warning letter would usually be issued before a prosecution was initiated.

Between 1 April and 25 June, Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx) received 9,732 disclosure notices. So far, more than 1,360 notices that were late or not completed properly have been referred to the SFC.

The SFC has issued six warning letters in relation to such failings, but it is expected that warning letters will soon be issued in substantially more cases, although not necessarily in all the cases referred by HKEx.

The large volume of filings in the initial months after commencement was anticipated owing to the tighter disclosure requirements, in particular the 5% initial reporting threshold. The SFC does not expect the influx of filings to continue at the same level in the future.

The SFC wishes to state that, from 1 July 2003, its prosecution efforts on Part XV will focus on, in order of priority, the following:


- Non-disclosure of interests
- False or misleading disclosure
- Late disclosure (late disclosure could amount to non-disclosure in many instances)

Mr Alan Linning, SFC’s Executive Director of Enforcement, said: "Three months after the commencement of the new regime, the market has become more familiar with the reporting obligations. The SFC wishes to remind market participants that the SFC adopts a serious view on any non-compliance with the disclosure law due to dishonesty."

"It is possible that some participants may still experience difficulties in filling in disclosure notices. The SFC will continue to exercise flexibility in action against inadvertent and minor breaches," he added.
