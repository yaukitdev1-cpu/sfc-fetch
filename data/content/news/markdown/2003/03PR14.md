The SFC today publishes for public consultation revised guidelines to facilitate securities offerings on the Internet. 

The proposed revisions to the Guidelines for Registered Persons Using the Internet to Collect Applications for Securities in an Initial Public Offering (eIPO Guidelines) include its renaming to the Guidelines for Persons Using the Internet to Collect Applications for Securities in a Public Offering (ePO Guidelines).  

The eIPO Guidelines was published by the SFC in July 2000 and sets out the responsibilities of a person registered with the SFC who uses the Internet to collect applications from its clients in an initial public offering. Since its publication, different forms of electronic public offerings (ePOs) have evolved which make use of the Internet  (and other electronic means) to distribute prospectuses, enable the downloading of application forms or the collection of application information.  

The draft ePO Guidelines provides guidance on ePOs which have evolved since the publication of the eIPO Guidelines in 2000. It also extends its application to all persons who take part in an ePO, including issuers, sponsors, share registrars as well as intermediaries.  Its scope is also extended to cover both initial public offerings and follow on offerings. In addition, the draft ePO Guidelines addresses issues raised by industry participants relating to electronic prospectuses and hyperlinking to electronic prospectuses on websites of third parties.  

The draft ePO Guidelines aims to provide guidance to industry participants so that they may develop a greater variety of ePO services. This will give investors more choice and flexibility in the methods by which they can apply for securities in a public offering.

The SFC proposes to issue the ePO Guidelines under section 399 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) after the public consultation.
