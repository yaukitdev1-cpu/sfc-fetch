The SFC is surveying securities investment research activities in Hong Kong to assess the state of the industry, particularly with regard to the possible conflicts of interest of securities analysts. 

Over the past year, there has been considerable debate over the role of securities analysts in the investment process.  In particular, regulatory actions in the United States have brought into question the independence and objectivity of analysts in investment firms. 

The SFC has been actively monitoring the developments in this area, both in the domestic market and on the international front.  To obtain further empirical data and a more comprehensive view from practitioners in the local context, the SFC is conducting a survey on the securities firms’ investment research activities undertaken for publication. 

The SFC has asked all securities dealing and securities advising firms (which would include all firms engaging in securities investment research) to respond to a questionnaire.  The questionnaire covers various areas, including the scope of a firm’s investment research operations, its existing practices, and its views on the issues involved. 

In tandem with this exercise, the SFC will also conduct another survey to seek the views of investors on the issue of analysts’ conflicts of interest. 

An SFC spokesman said: “The SFC will take into consideration the results of these surveys and other factors in formulating policy proposals most suitable for Hong Kong.  We will consult the public on any proposed regulatory measures that may be formulated.”
