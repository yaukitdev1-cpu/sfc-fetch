The SFC today publishes for public consultation proposed revisions to the existing Guidance Note on Money Laundering.  

The main purposes of the revisions are:-

(a) to extend the application of the Guidance Note to all associated entities(except those which are authorised financial institutions);

(b) to rationalise the Guidance Note with the Securities and Futures Ordinance and its rules;

(c) to update the Guidance Note to include the legal requirements imposed under the newly enacted anti-terrorist financing law which, among other things, criminalises the supply of funds and making funds or financial (or related) services available to terrorists or terrorist associates and the new offences created under the anti-money laundering laws with respect to dealing in properties that are subject to restraint orders and charging orders; and

(d) to provide further guidance in identifying suspicious transactions and detecting terrorist financing with steps recommended by the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (Note 1) and Financial Action Task Force (FATF) (Note 2).

Meanwhile, the FATF is currently reviewing its 40 anti-money laundering Recommendations. Upon completion of the review, the Guidance Note may need to be further revised to reflect new standards of control measures to fight money laundering and combat terrorist financing for financial institutions.  

The review by FATF is expected to be completed soon.  As such, only minimal changes are proposed in the consultation document in the meantime.

The SFC has sent copies of the consultation document to intermediaries via the FinNet communication network.  In addition, the public may obtain copies of the consultation document free of charge at the SFC’s office and on the SFC’s Internet website at www.hksfc.org.hk.

Notes:

1. The Joint Financial Intelligence Unit was set up in 1989 to receive reports about suspicious financial activity made under the provisions of the Drug Trafficking (Recovery of Proceeds) Ordinance and, since 1995, the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance. The Unit is jointly run by the Hong Kong Police Force and the Hong Kong Customs & Excise Department.

2. FATF is an inter-governmental body tasked with the development and promotion of policies, both at national and international levels, to combat money laundering. FATF currently consists of 29 jurisdictions and two international organizations. Hong Kong is one of its members. For more information on FATF, please visit its website at [www.fatf-gafi.org](http://www.fatf-gafi.org).
