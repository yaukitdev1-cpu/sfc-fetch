The SFC has noticed recent press reports on brokers’ policies of allowing their cash clients to satisfy payments for purchases of securities up to seven or eight days after the transaction was carried out. It is to be noted that in the case of Hong Kong stocks, brokers are required to settle with the clearing house two days after the transaction in accordance with the rules of the Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited. Such policy of prolonged period for client settlement may put the firm at risk.

The SFC believes that those market participants who adopt prolonged settlement terms are fully aware of the need to carry out prudent credit policy to protect their own financial stability and the integrity of the market. Clearly it is their duty to prudently manage the risk exposure of their business. Nevertheless, the SFC is mindful of the fact that there may be aggressive players in the market who may not have taken the necessary risk management measures.

Prolonged settlement period increases the risk of default. Should a client default on his payment for the securities purchased, the broker concerned would have to absorb any loss that might occur. A broker might not always recover the purchase cost by selling the securities afterwards, for instance if the share price were to fall after the client had defaulted. The broker might even encounter difficulties in selling the securities if they are relatively illiquid. Besides, the broker should consider whether the cash client agreement contains adequate provisions to afford the firm sufficient protection against client default, as many standard agreements currently used only gives the firm a right of lien over the securities purchased pursuant to the prolonged settlement terms. This might restrict the firm’s ability to seek recourse against the client’s other stock holding with the firm. In such cases, the broker firm might not have sufficient protection against credit risks of clients on prolonged settlement terms.

The SFC reminds all licensed and registered persons that they are required by the *Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission* to employ adequate resources for running their business. Extending credits to clients without capital that is commensurate with the risk arising from such credits would not be acceptable. In addition, the *Management, Supervision and Internal Control Guidelines* expects licensed and registered persons to institute proper credit policies so as to contain credit or default risk at an acceptable and appropriate level. Non-compliance with these requirements by licensed and registered persons posing undue risk to investors or the integrity of the market would not be tolerated.

Brokers are also reminded that, to protect their own interests, they should vigorously observe the "Know Your Client" requirement under the *Code of Conduct* and the *Internal Control Guidelines*’ suggested measures for credit assessment, and exercise caution when assessing the creditworthiness of clients.

Brokers must also carefully evaluate the suitability of prolonged settlement periods for clients to ensure that such settlement terms and their consequential leverage effect are suitable in light of a client’s investment objective and financial situation. In addition, the firm must observe the requirement under the *Code of Conduct* to draw to clients’ attention the risks arising from trading on such terms. Existing risk disclosure on securities dealing alone may not be adequate for informing clients the risk associated with the leverage effect of the prolonged settlement period.

Investors are also reminded to study, and ensure they understand, any conditions that may underlie the granting of any prolonged settlement period by their brokers. In addition, they should watch out for the risk of over-trading (i.e. trading for such amounts that they do not have readily available funds to settle) under such settlement period, and take into account their financial ability in trading on such terms.
