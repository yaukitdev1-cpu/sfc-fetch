The SFC notes that there have been recent newspaper articles suggesting that several fund management companies had withdrawn their applications for authorisation of hedge funds due to poor market conditions and lengthy and complicated authorisation process. In response to media enquiries, the SFC issues the following statements.

Since the promulgation of the Guidelines on Hedge Funds in May 2002, there have been withdrawals of hedge fund applications from four fund management companies (not five as reported in the papers).

For three of the cases, the applications have been withdrawn after their failure to comply with the necessary regulatory requirements: insufficient experience of the hedge fund managers and/or failure to meet structural requirements.

For the remaining case, the fund management company has withdrawn one fund application but continues to pursue authorisation for another because of a change in its marketing strategy. The company has not cited stringent authorisation criteria as the reason for withdrawal.

It should be noted that the hedge fund guidelines were promulgated after thorough discussion with the market and public consultation. They are regarded by the majority of industry participants as the appropriate regulatory yardstick for hedge funds that are to be sold to the public.

Ms Tina So, SFC’s Director of Investment Products, said:  “The SFC would not be serving the interests of the public if issues of non-compliance were ignored when processing fund applications. Hedge funds are a new asset class for the retail public.  Hedge fund managers who are used to serving the private or professional market must be prepared to modify their products to meet the regulatory requirements when targeting the retail public.”

Authorisation processing time depends largely on the complexity of the product and the completeness and compliance of the application materials. Applications by hedge funds and other products that are fully compliant with the regulations should not experience any delays in authorisation.

"The SFC is committed to accommodating market changes and facilitating investors’ access to a greater diversity of investment products. The SFC encourages the industry to discuss with it matters of compliance and other market development issues,” Ms So said.

While the SFC from time to time receives media enquiries about the number of outstanding hedge funds applications and their types, it regrets that disclosure of specific information about a particular fund category is considered not appropriate because of commercial sensitivity.

However, there has been continuous growth in the funds area despite the current stock market conditions.

There were 1,965 authorised unit trusts and mutual funds as at end of March 2003, 4% higher than a year ago. Total net asset value of authorised funds (as of December 31 2002) was US$342 billion, an increase of 20% over the year.

In April 2003, the number of applications for authorisation rose 41% to 31, from 22 in April 2002. The number of authorisations also increased by 21% from 14 to 17.
