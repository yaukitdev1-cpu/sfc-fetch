**The Implementation of the Securities and Futures Ordinance**

29 March 2003

Dear Matthew,

Thank you for your letter, which I received two days ago.  I understand that while you are in the United States, you nevertheless notice that my colleagues in the SFC and I have been extremely busy getting ready for the implementation of the new Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO), which will come into force soon.

As a matter of fact, the SFO, which comes into effect on 1 April, is the most important and comprehensive securities legislation in Hong Kong.  It consolidates and modernises 10 existing relevant ordinances, thereby providing us with more flexibility to effectively regulate the market with standards that are comparable to best international practice.  I would like to express my heartfelt gratitude to each and everyone who have made a valuable contribution to the process during the past years.

The new ordinance allows ample room for market development and accords a greater degree of protection to investors.

First of all, to ensure that investors are given accurate and timely information on listed securities so as to make informed investment decisions, a “dual filing” system will be implemented.  Under the new system, the SFC will become the statutory regulator of listed company disclosures.  Listed companies and listing applicants are required to file announcements or listing documents to both Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited and the SFC.  Anyone who makes false or misleading disclosure will be subject to our investigation and, where appropriate, criminal prosecution.  The SFC may object to a listing application if the applicant provides insufficient or misleading information in its listing documents.

Moreover, the disclosure threshold for substantial shareholders of listed companies has been reduced from 10% to 5% and the notification period from 5 days to 3 days.  These requirements enable investors to gain better and timelier access to information about the changes in the shareholding of substantial shareholders and directors.  Such information is essential for investors to evaluate their investment strategies.

Under the new legislation, we have been given more effective tools to combat market misconduct.  The new Market Misconduct Tribunal will handle cases like insider dealing, market manipulation, dissemination of false and misleading information and other forms of misconduct on a civil route.  It will decide on cases using a less stringent civil standard of proof and is able to impose a wide range of deterrent sanctions, such as disgorgement of profits.  Criminal prosecution may also be brought against persons involved in market misconduct, with maximum penalty of 10 years’ imprisonment or a fine of $10 million.

If investors suffer financial loss as a result of market misconduct or false information, they will have a new statutory right of action to claim against people who commit the misconduct or issue such information.  Findings of the Tribunal will be admissible in evidence in a private civil action.  In fact, the SFC is equipped with enhanced investigatory powers in relation to listed companies involved in market misconduct.

As I have mentioned on several occasions, one of our top priorities this year is to step up our enforcement efforts. In the past two weeks, we have begun investigation into two corporate misconduct cases, taking the total number of new cases over the past year to six.  We will also strengthen our co-operation with the Commercial Crime Bureau of the Police and the ICAC.

Regarding the regulation of intermediaries, we have introduced a new single licensing system which brings greater cost effectiveness to intermediaries. On the other hand, the new SFO provides the SFC with greater flexibility in disciplinary sanctions concerning intermediary misconduct.  Apart from partial suspension or revocation of licences, we can impose a fine of up to $10 million.  In addition, frontline and supervisory staff of banks engaging in securities activity and who commit misconduct are also subject to our disciplinary actions.

A new single investor compensation fund will replace the existing compensation schemes.  The new regime is based on a per-investor compensation limit of $150,000 in respect of trading in securities and futures contracts.  Therefore, an investor can ascertain in advance the compensation amount in the event of intermediary default.  Coverage is also extended to both exchange and non-exchange participants, banks and securities margin financiers.

To promote the public’s understanding of the impact of the SFO on investors, we will issue a booklet entitled “SFO and You” to explain the major changes and implications brought about by the new legislation.  The brochure will be published and posted on our website on 1 April 2003.  However, I would like to remind investors to have a thorough understanding of the investment products they are interested in and study carefully the risks involved before making any investment decisions.

Apart from enhancing the SFC’s various powers in the discharge of its duties, the SFO also attaches greater importance to proper checks and balances.  The establishment of an independent full-time Securities and Futures Appeals Tribunal, to be chaired by a judge, will further ensure that we are accountable for the functions we perform.

You may be aware that the Expert Group to Review the Operation of the Securities and Futures Market Regulatory Structure published its report last week. It recommended the transfer of listing functions to the SFC in a bid to step up the regulation of listed companies.  In fact, one of the important objectives of the SFO is to improve the quality of the market and offer greater investor protection.  We will work closely with the Administration and related parties, particularly HKEx, to ensure that we perform our new functions effectively and efficiently.

My colleagues and I are looking forward to witnessing a new chapter in the history of the regulation of the securities and futures market in Hong Kong.  I hope we can achieve our fundamental objective as reflected in the following mantra: “New Regulatory Framework, Better Quality of Market – Enforce the Law, Protect Investors”.

Yours sincerely,

Andrew
