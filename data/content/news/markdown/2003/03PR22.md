The Securities and Futures Commission today releases its *Quarterly Report* for the financial period 1 October to 31 December 2002.

Accumulated deficit down 19% from a year ago

During the quarter, the SFC recorded a total operating expenditure of $97 million, $2 million below that for July to September. Total income dropped to $81.5 million from $85.8 million owing to lower market turnover. As a result, the SFC registered a quarterly deficit of $15.5 million, $2.5 million higher than the previous quarter’s.

Revenue for the three quarters was 6% lower than last year, at $258.3 million. The fall was mainly the result of the reduction of the average daily market turnover from $8.1 billion to $7.1 billion. But the SFC’s stringent cost control measures, including salary and headcount freeze (other than new recruits for the dual filing initiatives) paid off by reducing the operating expenses (including depreciation) by 8% from a year ago to $293.6 million.

The accumulated deficit for the three quarters to the end of 2002 was $35 million, well below the projected deficit of $87 million in the approved budget. It was 19% lower than the $43.6 million deficit for the same period last year.

The total number of staff at the end of December 2002 was 402, compared to 415 at the end of March 2002.

At the end of December 2002, the SFC's reserves stood at $588.7 million, equivalent to 1.31 times the approved annual operating expenditure estimate of $450 million.

SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng expected a further decline in revenue. He stressed that the SFC would use its resources effectively. By keeping a tight rein on expenditure, he expected that the Commission would be able to keep the deficit for the full year within budget projection.

Work Progress

During the quarter, the SFC:


- signed a new MOU with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority on the regulation of banks’ securities business;
- continued its SFO related training for intermediaries and market participants;
- amended the Financial Resources Rules to help intermediaries manage their risks;
- requested a brokerage to make arrangement to return assets to clients, after it failed to meet the minimum liquid capital requirement and ceased business;
- announced the authorisation of the first retail hedge funds and published hedge funds reporting requirements;
- published exemption guidelines to facilitate entry to the industry;
- published the Report of the Steering Committee on the Enhancement of Financial Infrastructure II;
- directed HKEx to suspend trading in a stock due to inadequate information disclosure;
- successfully prosecuted 11 entities and disciplined 17 intermediaries for regulatory breaches;
- started publishing a monthly newsletter on its enforcement action;
- began a monthly forum with brokers;
- organised 11 educational workshops for secondary school teachers; and
- hosted a two-day meeting of the IOSCO Standing Committee 1 in Hong Kong.

The *Quarterly Report* is accessible on the SFC website at www.hksfc.org.hk. Printed copies are also available at the SFC's office.
