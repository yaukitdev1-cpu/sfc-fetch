The renminbi’s uptrend may have sparked interest in renminbi bonds, but retail investors really should find out more about the local bond market before entering it.

In the latest issue of [InvestEd Intelligence](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/focus/jan11/sounding.html), the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) provides an overview of the retail bond market and explains a number of topical investment issues, including how Hong Kong’s disclosure-based regulatory regime helps guard investor interests.

“Every investment involves risks and so do bonds,” says Mr Brian Ho, the SFC’s Executive Director, Corporate Finance. “No matter which currency a bond is denominated in, investors should always understand how it works in both the primary and secondary markets and critically assess the associated risks before investing.”

In the “Sounding Board” column, Mr Ho urges investors to get the most out of the disclosure-based market, “Timely disclosure of accurate information is key to upholding a fair and orderly market. Having said that, it’s equally important that investors make prompt use of information available to them to make informed investment decisions.”

In the “Words of Wisdom” column, Dr Wise further explains how the SFC and The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Ltd work hand in hand to enforce disclosure requirements before and after the listing of securities.

The newsletter also previews how the “mixed media offer” that combines the use of a paper application form with an electronic prospectus stands to facilitate the take-up of initial public offerings. Further, it cautions the public against investment solicitations in the form of financial courses.

InvestEd Intelligence is a bimonthly newsletter for investors providing a diverse range of educational information about investing. Topical market issues, product development information and regulatory updates are among topics to be covered regularly. The newsletter is published at the SFC’s InvestEd site on the 20th day of every odd month, or the day after, if the 20th falls on a Saturday or a public holiday.
