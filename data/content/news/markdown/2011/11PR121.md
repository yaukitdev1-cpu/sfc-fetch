The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) reminds investors who engage in leveraged or margin trading to closely monitor their positions amid high levels of market volatility as margin calls by intermediaries are not obligatory and forced liquidation is possible (Note 1).

Large fluctuations in financial markets around the world have increased investment risks, especially for leveraged or margin trading. Investors engaged in leveraged or margin trading may face margin calls on their leveraged or margin positions and forced closing out of such positions as the market moves against them.

“Investors should prudently evaluate the risks before engaging in leveraged or margin trading and pay due regard to their own financial capability, investment experience and investment objectives,” said Mr Ashley Alder, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer.

“Besides assuming the responsibility for prompt settlement of all margin calls, investors should appreciate that in extreme market conditions, their positions might be closed out mandatorily. They should therefore constantly keep an eye on market condition and reassess their ability to maintain their positions,” he added.

Investors should read their client agreement carefully and fully understand the terms and conditions of leveraged or margin trading, for example, interest charges, margin calls, and the circumstances under which a client’s positions may be closed without the client’s consent. If there is any doubt on the terms, they should seek clarification from the intermediary or advice from professional advisers.
