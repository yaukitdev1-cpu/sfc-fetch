The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) reminds investors that when trading renminbi securities (Note 1) in the secondary market, they must find out from their intermediaries details regarding settlement methods, fees and charges and other processes for their transactions.

To begin with, investors interested in trading renminbi securities on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (SEHK) should first check the readiness of their intermediary. A list of participants who have confirmed their readiness for renminbi securities services has been published on the website of Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx). If their brokerage or bank is not on that list, investors should check if that intermediary is trading and clearing the renminbi securities via other participants which have confirmed to be ready.

Just as with other stock transactions, investors trading renminbi securities in the secondary market have to pay stamp duty, the SFC levy and SEHK trading fees. Although the transactions are in renminbi, these fees and charges are collected through SEHK from intermediaries in Hong Kong dollars, using the exchange rate determined by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority on the day of the trade and posted on the HKEx website by 11am on each trading day.

Investors should check with their intermediary the settlement currency of their renminbi transactions, how the exchange rate is set if the trade is to be settled in a currency other than renminbi, and the payment method. Finding out about the intermediary’s policy on settlement defaults is also a must.
