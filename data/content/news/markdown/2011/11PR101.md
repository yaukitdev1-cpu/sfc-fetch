The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today announced additional measures to enhance the level of the collateral and transparency of domestic synthetic Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) in an ongoing effort to strengthen protection for investors (Note 1 & 2).

The relevant synthetic ETF managers have been required to top-up the collateral level for each of the domestic synthetic ETFs to achieve at least 100% collateralization to ensure there is no uncollateralized counterparty risk exposure arising from the use of financial derivatives to replicate index performance.

The relevant synthetic ETF managers have also been required to put in place a prudent haircut policy, in particular, where the collateral taken is in the form of equity securities, the market value of such equity collateral must be equivalent to at least 120% of the related gross counterparty risk exposure.

Each domestic synthetic ETF will start to increase the collateral level two weeks after the related announcement is made by the manager, and full collateralization must be achieved as soon as practicable and no later than 31 October 2011.

Should market condition require, the relevant synthetic ETF managers may, in their professional judgement, accelerate the implementation timetable to achieve full collateralization ahead of 31 October 2011.

"Building on our continuing effort in strengthening protection for investors, we believe the increase in collateral levels will better mitigate the counterparty risks faced by the domestic synthetic ETFs,” the SFC’s Acting Chief Executive Officer, Mrs Alexa Lam said.

“However, as with any investment products, investors should always exercise caution when making investment decisions, particularly during volatile market situation,” she added.

ETF managers are currently required to make the overall collateralization levels and other information about the collateral available on ETFs’ websites for investors’ reference. To further improve transparency, domestic synthetic ETF managers will be required to publish the latest collateral management policy on the ETFs’ websites on an ongoing basis.

Investors should pay attention to announcements made by the relevant synthetic ETF managers for details on the changes to the collateral management policy, as well as the corresponding impact on the domestic synthetic ETFs. They should contact the relevant synthetic ETF managers if they have doubts on the new collateral management policy.

Today’s announcement followed a series of measures already introduced to enhance investor protection and the transparency of synthetic ETFs. Last year, a series of structural requirements were codified in the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds to address the risks associated with synthetic ETFs (Note 3).

To help investors identify synthetic ETFs more easily from traditional physical ETFs, all synthetic ETFs have also been required to add a marker to their stock short names since 22 November 2010 and an annotation to their fund names since 16 January 2011 (Note 4).
