The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) reminds investors that, commencing 25 June 2011, all SFC-authorized funds and investment-linked assurance schemes (ILAS) that are marketed to the public must provide investors with Product Key Facts Statements (KFS) and updated offering documents that satisfy a number of additional disclosure requirements.

Launched in phases since June 2010, these disclosure requirements are part of the SFC’s package of new measures to enhance protection for the investing public (Note 1).

“It is important that investors understand a product in order for them to make an informed and responsible investment decision,” said Mrs Alexa Lam, the SFC’s acting Chief Executive Officer. “To play their part, they should always use the KFS, and more importantly, the offering document, as primary sources to understand how a product works and to learn about associated risks before investing.”

In plain language with graphic illustrations at times, a KFS is designed to serve the objectives of brevity, clarity and comparability in explaining an investment product. It summarises the key features, associated risks, and fees and charges related to investing in a product. A KFS is a summary about a product, which should help investors when they navigate the offering document containing detailed information (Note 2).

The SFC further notes that some of the 2,200 SFC-authorized funds and ILAS existing in the market have decided not to be marketed to the public from 25 June 2011. Such products will not issue any KFS nor any revised offering documents satisfying the additional disclosure requirements. Investors are reminded to consult the relevant product distributors or issuers when in doubt.

Meanwhile, the SFC has also reminded distributors to check with fund managers and ILAS issuers to ensure that the SFC-authorized funds and ILAS they offer have issued KFS and revised offering documents satisfying the additional disclosure requirements. If in doubt as to the disclosure in those two documents, distributors are to consult the relevant product issuers.
