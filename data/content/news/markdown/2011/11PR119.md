The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has today begun a [consultation](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/doc?refNo=11CP5) to solicit public comments on proposals for a new set of guidelines on anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing (the guidelines) (Note 1).

The guidelines seek to provide guidance to the industry, among others, relating to the operation of the provisions of Schedule 2 to the enacted Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing (Financial Institutions) Ordinance (AMLO), which shall come into effect on 1 April 2012. Schedule 2 of AMLO aims to bring customer due diligence and record keeping requirements in line with latest international standards.

The key objective of the proposed guidelines is to assist licensed corporations in designing and implementing appropriate and effective policies, procedures and controls so as to comply with the requirements of the AMLO and other applicable anti-money laundering/counter-terrorist financing legislation and regulatory requirements.

The proposed guidelines also include some examples of industry-specific suspicious transactions that may warrant enhanced scrutiny and reporting to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit by licensed corporations.

“We welcome the enhanced anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing regime as that would help uphold the integrity of Hong Kong’s securities markets and reinforce Hong Kong’s position as an international financial centre,” said Dr Eddy Fong, the SFC’s Chairman.

The SFC intends to publish the consultation conclusions and a final version of the guidelines in early 2012 (Note 2).

The public is invited to submit comments on the proposed guidelines to the SFC on or before 18 November 2011. Written comments may be submitted on line via the SFC website ([www.sfc.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/)), by email to [aml_guideline@sfc.hk](mailto:aml_guideline@sfc.hk), by post or by fax to 2284 4660.

Notes:
1. The consultation paper seeks comments from the public on two proposed guidelines: (1) the Proposed Guideline on Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing (the Guideline) and (2) the Proposed Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Guideline Issued by the Securities and Futures Commission for Associated Entities (the Guideline for AEs).

An associated entity is required under the Guideline for AEs to implement anti-money laundering/counter-terrorist financing policies, procedures and controls similar to those in the Guideline. The term “associated entity” refers to a company that is in a controlling entity relationship with an intermediary and receives or holds in Hong Kong client assets of the intermediary.

2. The guidelines will replace the existing Prevention on Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Guidance Note published by the SFC.
