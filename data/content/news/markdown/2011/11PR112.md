With the tough economic environment in Europe and the United States driving up volatility in major markets, even local investors without direct overseas exposure could be affected.

In the latest issue of [*InvestEd Intelligence*](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/focus/sep11/sounding.html), the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) urges investors to understand the product structures and monitor closely the credit risks of product issuers as well as their counterparties, especially during dramatic market fluctuations.

“With sovereign debt problems, weakening economic prospects and other uncertainties in Western economies, international financial institutions could be particularly vulnerable,” said the SFC’s Chairman Dr Eddy Fong. “Investors must monitor the market situation and understand what implications it has on their investments, such as whether the credit rating of any product they’ve invested in is declining.”

Penning this issue’s “Sounding Board” column, Dr Fong also reminds investors to assess if they need to adjust their portfolios or investment strategies in response to the changing economic, financial or political developments in local and overseas markets.

Dr Wise further explains how investors can track their trading activities and safeguard their investments using the contract notes and account statements. Meanwhile, the “Case in Point” column features a sample case of leveraged foreign exchange investing, illustrating that an intermediary may close a client’s margin positions – without forewarning the client – if the level of funds in the account is no longer sufficient. In addition, this issue also discusses what liabilities may bind investors who trade securities on line and provides a number of precautions against common misuse of these services.

September’s “IE Activities Planner” announces the issuance of the booklet entitled, “[Know Your Rights in the Sales Process](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/images/publications/salesprocess2011.pdf).” In lively dialogues and photo arrangement, the booklet helps investors understand the SFC’s new investor protection measures, which aim to raise standards of selling practices. Investors are encouraged to download an e-copy from the SFC’s [InvestEd website](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/index.html).

InvestEd Intelligence is a bimonthly newsletter for investors, which provides a diverse range of educational information about investing. Topical market issues, product development information and regulatory updates are among topics to be covered regularly. The newsletter is published at the SFC’s InvestEd site on the 20th day of every odd month, or the business day after, if the 20th falls on a Saturday or a public holiday.
