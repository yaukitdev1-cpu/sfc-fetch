In its latest edition of the [Dual Filing Update](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/dual/jan11.pdf), the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) reminds sponsors to exercise due care and diligence to understand their listing applicants’ businesses and to ensure that the information provided to the regulators and eventually – investors – is accurate and complete.

This issue describes a number of cases in which sponsors failed to identify material errors or omissions in the draft listing documents and other supporting materials submitted. In one case, the estimated capital value of the listing applicant’s unsold property units disclosed in the initial draft prospectus was overstated by about three times, having erroneously included the value of the units already sold. In another case, enquiries by regulators revealed that a major customer initially said to have been dealing with the applicant for six years was incorporated only five years prior and did not place its first order with the applicant until about one year earlier.

The issue also highlights various common disclosure deficiencies in lapsed or withdrawn applications in relation to issues fundamental to the listing applications, which called into question whether the sponsors were aware of the fundamental issues or had properly appraised their implications before submitting the applications.

“Sponsors are expected to identify issues that are fundamental to the listing applications as part of their due diligence and to properly address the issues before submitting listing applications,” said Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer. “Even if an application does not proceed to listing because of lapse or withdrawal, provision of false or misleading information in a material particular could constitute an offence and lead to disciplinary action on the sponsor under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.”
