The SFC's inspection of investment advisers has found that deficiencies in selling practice continued in the industry (Note 1).

In late 2006, the SFC completed the inspection of 10 investment advisers to assess the standard of SFC licensed investment advisers and whether any improvement had been made since an earlier round of inspection two years earlier. In the 2004 inspection, the SFC inspected 15 firms and found various issues and deficiencies (Note 2).

The latest inspection findings, published today in the Report on Findings of Second Round of Thematic Inspection of Licensed Investment Advisers, revealed similar issues and deficiencies. These included insufficient knowledge of clients, lack of proper due diligence on the products sold, lack of justification to illustrate suitability of advice, poor documentation and non-compliance with licensing requirements etc.

Firms inspected have been asked to rectify the deficiencies identified. Investigation has already commenced on the more serious breaches and deficiencies noted in this second round of inspection.

To raise the standard of the industry, the SFC issued guidance on 8 May 2007 on requirements relating to the suitability of advice in the Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the SFC.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “The need for wealth management continuously grows and investment products become more complex and diverse. Investment advisers are increasingly playing a key role in helping investors make right investment decisions. The SFC expects investment advisers to discharge their responsibilities honestly and competently. We will monitor the investment advisory industry to ensure that they comply with the regulatory requirements and meet the standards expected of them to protect the investing public. Rigorous regulatory action will be taken against those who committed serious infractions of the law.”

“It is also equally important for investors to take action to protect their own interests. In light of the recent market volatility, investors should thoroughly understand the risk and return profile of financial products recommended by their investment advisers and cautiously select those products that meet their investment objectives and risk profiles. Meanwhile, we will continue our efforts in investor education,” Mrs Lam added.
