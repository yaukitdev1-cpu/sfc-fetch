Hong Kong’s stock options market has had the highest growth of all major markets in 2007 and become the most active in Asia. This remarkable growth can in large part be attributable to factors that are unique to Hong Kong, according to an SFC research paper.

During the first nine months of 2007, the average daily trading volume was 168,392 contracts, 129% higher than 2006 and Hong Kong has overtaken Australia to become the most active stock options market in Asia.



The rapid growth in trading of stock options market over the past two years can be attributable to:


- the growth in the trading of the cash market, particularly an increase in the listing and trading of Mainland stocks in Hong Kong;
- the growth in the derivative warrants market;
- the narrowing of trading spreads on stocks; and
- the relaxation of position limits on stock options.

Some factors that have influenced the growth of the Hong Kong stock options market are unique to Hong Kong which may help explain why the growth was so much higher than other markets. For instance, Hong Kong has a large number of Mainland enterprises listed on HKEx and the narrowing of trading spreads and the relaxation of position limits were initiatives taken in the local market.

Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, said: “The measures introduced in recent years to facilitate growth of the market have been successful. We should continue to explore measures to further enhance our markets.”

The report notes that the stock options market in Hong Kong is largely a professional and institutional market. Also notwithstanding the significant growth, the size of the stock options market remains small when compared to the US and European levels, suggesting that considerable potential for further growth exists.

The [research paper](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/research/research/rs%20paper%2038.pdf) is available on the SFC website under the section “Research and Statistics”.
