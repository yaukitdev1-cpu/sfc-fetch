The April issue of the [SFC Enforcement Reporter](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/enforcement/07/apr_07.pdf), published today, reminds brokers they should not execute orders that are obviously tainted by a manipulative intention, such as “marking the close” (Note 1).

The SFC has a number of current investigations involving marking the close. In some of these cases, the SFC is examining whether the brokers involved in executing the client orders knew the instructions would result in fixing an artificial closing price. Brokers should not execute clients’ orders if it is doubtful that the orders may mislead the market or create a false price, and they have not satisfied themselves that the orders are bona fide. In March, the SFC suspended a licensee for three months for placing orders for a client who intended to artificially raise the closing prices of a stock.

In other action this month, the SFC started disqualification proceedings against five former directors of a failed listed company for having recklessly entered into transactions totaling $35 million which ultimately caused losses to the company.
