The SFC is reviewing the Enforcement Reporter, a monthly summary of enforcement actions, and seeks comments from the public regarding the type of information and other improvements they would like to see.

The review is part of the SFC’s ongoing effort to enhance the transparency of its enforcement process. The public may send feedback to [enfreporter@sfc.hk](mailto:enfreporter@sfc.hk).

Mr Mark Steward, SFC’s Executive Director of Enforcement, said: “The SFC seeks a proactive, constructive and positive relationship with securities and futures markets, intermediaries and the investing public and the SFC Enforcement Reporter is a part of that relationship. The SFC Enforcement Reporter can play a strong role in communicating current regulatory problems, stimulating debate and helping compliance. We want to improve the SFC Enforcement Reporter and I encourage all our stakeholders to tell us what they would like to read about. ”

The [March issue of the SFC Enforcement Reporter](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/enforcement/07/mar_07.pdf) is published today. It warns that persons who misappropriate client assets will face prison sentences. In February, three ex-licensees found to have misappropriated client assets were jailed by the Court for 14 to 17 months. The misconduct was uncovered in 2002 in a circularisation exercise conducted at the request of the SFC following an inspection. The SFC will continue to take appropriate action against those who have abused the trust of their clients. Investors are also reminded to co-operate with audit confirmation requests promptly and accurately to protect their interests and assure the integrity of their accounts.

During the month, the SFC also banned a licensee for life for failing to account for client assets in suspicious circumstances and another for misappropriating client assets and concealing the misappropriation. The SFC also banned an ex- licensee from the industry for four months for making false declaration in a client’s account opening documents.

Copies of the March issue of the SFC Enforcement Reporter, which covers enforcement outcomes in the previous month, are available at the SFC office. All issues are also available on the SFC website under [“Speeches, Publications & Consultations” – “Publications”](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/speeches/public/enforcement/enforcement.html).

The public are able to subscribe to the newsletter by registering for the Update Alert service on the SFC website. They will then be able to receive the newsletter via email.
