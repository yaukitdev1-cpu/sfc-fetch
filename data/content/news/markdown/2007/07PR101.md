The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

Cases handled

In the 12 months from April 2006 to March 2007, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 95 listing applications. The SFC received 86 listing applications from April 2005 to March 2006. In last year, there was a noticeable increase in the number of applications with planned offer sizes exceeding $1 billion (40 such cases as compared to 28 cases for the previous 12 months).

Of the 95 listing applications, we raised comments on 58 cases, of which 10 have lapsed or been withdrawn or rejected by the Stock Exchange, 38 have been listed or approved for listing and 10 remain active as at the date of this release.

In the three months from January to March 2007, the SFC received 23 listing applications. This compares to 22 listing applications in the three months from October to December 2006 (Note 1). Of the 23 applications, we commented on 13 cases. Our average response time was seven working days. In the previous quarter, we commented on 13 cases out of the 22 cases received.

As reported previously (Note 2), in the three months from July to September 2006 we commented on 22 out of 34 applications received. Of those 22 cases, two lapsed due to expiry of the six-month application period, one was withdrawn and 19 have been listed.

Quality of disclosure

The proposed use of listing proceeds gives important insights into the future business development of the issuer and how the issuer’s management intends to deploy resources to implement stated business strategies. In several cases, the SFC noted the draft prospectuses failed to provide meaningful details of the use of proceeds to enable a proper understanding of how the intended use of proceeds ties in with the business development of the listing applicant.

In various cases reviewed, the listing applicants gave a general description in the draft prospectuses to the effect that a substantial portion of the listing proceeds will be used to expand their production capacities. However, the draft prospectuses provided no quantitative information about the existing production capacity, the actual production volume, utilisation rates or other relevant information to provide a basis for the investors to assess the extent of expansion in production capacities and how such scale of expansion is justified.

In another case, the listing applicant disclosed plans to use over two-thirds of the listing proceeds to expand its production facilities for one line of products. The proposed investment is more than double the net book value of the listing applicant’s existing property, plant and equipment. Despite the significant investment size, the draft prospectus contained insufficient details about this expansion plan for investors to understand how and to what extent the new facilities would enhance the listing applicant’s production capabilities, and how this is in line with its business development strategies.

Mr Brian Ho, the SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: “Listing applicants intending to tap the market for funds should articulate clearly how they intend to make use of the funding for the benefit of their business. Investors need sufficient relevant information before making their investment decision.”

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime and to promote proper disclosure in listing applications.
