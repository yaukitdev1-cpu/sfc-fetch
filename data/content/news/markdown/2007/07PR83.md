The SFC aims to strike an appropriate balance between effective regulation and market facilitation as its contribution to Hong Kong’s development as a world-class financial centre, Mr Martin Wheatley, SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, said today.

Speaking at the World Pension Forum Conference, Mr Wheatley cited recent surveys indicating increased global recognition of Hong Kong’s regulatory standards. He noted that a competitiveness study on leading financial centres published earlier this year by the City of London Corporation, Hong Kong was regarded as “a real contender to become a genuinely global financial centre” and was ranked third in their league of international financial centres, following London and New York. The report highlighted the regulatory environment as a key reason for the success of a financial centre, he added (Note 1).

In expounding the SFC’s regulatory philosophy, Mr Wheatley said: “We are mindful that the key roles of the SFC include not only regulation and enforcement, but also market facilitation and investor education. The SFC is not just here to regulate and police the financial market, but also to actively help enhance its quality and status.”

In addition to the more widely recognised enforcement role, the SFC aims to conduct its activities in a manner that facilitates enhancement of the breadth and depth of our market so that investors have access to a wide range of quality products and services.

Mr Wheatley noted that Hong Kong is one of the very first jurisdictions globally to allow the sale of hedge funds to the retail public and Hong Kong’s exchange traded funds (ETF) market is the largest in Asia ex Japan in terms of market value and turnover (Notes 2 and 3).

He also said that Hong Kong’s deeply rooted Mainland connections made it an attractive platform for international players to invest in the Mainland. Hong Kong was also able to act as a conduit for channeling investment from the Mainland to the international market, he added.

The SFC has signed a MOU with the China Banking Regulatory Commission (CBRC), following which CBRC announced a notice widening the scope of investment allowed under the overseas wealth management business of Mainland commercial banks on behalf of their clients (Note 4). Mainland commercial banks’ wealth management businesses may now invest in a wider range of asset classes, including Hong Kong equities and equity funds authorised by the SFC, Mr Wheatley said (Note 5).

Mr Wheatley’s speech, [“Raising the Bar on Regulating Hong Kong’s Financial Markets”](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/speeches/07/mw_20070517_wpfc.pdf), is attached.
