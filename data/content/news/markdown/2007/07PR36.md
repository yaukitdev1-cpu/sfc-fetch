The SFC is pleased to note the finding of a new survey of financial centres that Hong Kong ranks third, after London and New York, according to SFC’s Chairman, Mr Eddy Fong.

At a Hong Kong Securities Institute luncheon, Mr Fong referred to the Global Financial Centre Index report, published by the City of London last week, which concluded that Hong Kong performed well in all of the key competitive areas, especially in regulation. Furthermore, Hong Kong was a real contender to become a genuinely global financial centre.

Mr Fong said it was telling that the world’s two largest and leading markets, London and New York, considered it necessary to review their competitiveness, and Hong Kong, despite its many achievements in recent years, could also not afford to be complacent.

He said Hong Kong could reinforce its competitive edge by capitalising on the Mainland connection, and enhancing the quality of the market. He also said the SFC could enhance Hong Kong’s quality as an international financial centre by implementing appropriate policies and practices, in particular, strengthening Hong Kong’s market infrastructure and regulatory regime so that they were on a par with global developments.

Mr Fong noted that, given the robust market environment in the Mainland, there were concerns as to whether Mainland enterprises would continue to list in Hong Kong. He believed that Hong Kong’s strong fundamentals, such as skills, experience, products and services, etc, would continue to make it an attractive place for Mainland enterprises to list. He also noted that a Hong Kong listing helped improve the regulatory and corporate governance standards of a Mainland enterprise which would help attract international investors.

While it is important to facilitate market development, Mr Fong stressed that the SFC must not lose sight of the importance of investor education. He said the SFC has proposed establishing an investor education body to improve investor education across the whole financial services sector. The SFC has received initial support from the Government and other financial regulators, and the relevant parties are forming a working group to take the proposal forward.

Mr Fong concluded that maintaining Hong Kong’s competitiveness was a shared responsibility. “The SFC will continue to work with its stakeholders – including the Government, fellow regulators, the Exchange, market professionals and participants, and of course, the Mainland authorities - to achieve this goal,” he said.
