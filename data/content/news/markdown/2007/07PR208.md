Hong Kong plays a unique role in Mainland’s capital market reforms and the experience is both challenging and rewarding, according to Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer.

In a speech at the International Regulators Seminar 2007 organised by the Financial Services Authority in London, Mr Wheatley said that Hong Kong had benefited significantly from being both the premier fund raising centre for Mainland enterprises and as a platform for the distribution and channelling of Mainland savings to overseas markets. This facilitation of the Mainland financial market development by Hong Kong had been beneficial to both Hong Kong and the Mainland.

He told more than 100 senior banking, securities and insurance regulators from all over the world that Hong Kong was well-positioned to help the Mainland capital markets integrate with the rest of the world.

However, Mr Wheatley added: “With further integration and linkages with Mainland markets, we anticipate that the Hong Kong market may itself become more volatile. This is an area that we, as the market regulator, are giving increased attention.”

Looking forward, Mr Wheatley said market participants in Hong Kong were well placed to meet the specific needs of Mainland enterprises and investors and to act as a platform through which they could access both a wide variety of financial products and also access global financial markets. He believed that the demand for financial services from Mainland investors was large enough for both Hong Kong and Shanghai to serve their specific markets.
