The SFC cautions investors against a suspected boiler room operator, Dongyin Futures Brokerage Company Limited, which claimed in a notice on its website that the SFC and Police had raided its offices and frozen its bank accounts.

The Dongyin notice is false. It was published by Dongyin after the SFC issued a warning to the investing public on 17 July 2007 that Dongyin is a suspected boiler room operation (Note 1). The SFC’s inquiries into Dongyin are continuing. The SFC would like to hear from any investors who have been in contact with Dongyin or who have opened an account with Dongyin. Investors can contact the SFC by telephone at 2840 9333 or by email to [complaint@sfc.hk](mailto:complaint@sfc.hk).

Dongyin does not appear to have any physical presence in Hong Kong other than services provided by three local secretarial companies. This is typical of many boiler room operations which usually have no real business location in the jurisdiction they appear to operate from.

Dongyin has never been licensed by or registered with the SFC in any capacity. Dongyin claimed on its website that it is a member of the Hong Kong Derivatives Exchange (Note 2). There is no such exchange in Hong Kong and the use of this name could have violated section 34 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

The SFC wishes to take this opportunity to remind investors how to guard against boiler rooms. If investors receive unsolicited inducements to purchase or trade shares and open an account having never met the operator, there is a high chance of them falling victim to an illegal share dealing operator. Boiler room operators may not have a physical location that authorities can raid.

Based on our experience, the modus operandi of boiler room fraud is as follows: investors could be called by someone claiming to be in Country A, but who is really in Country B, asking them to invest in a stock listed in Country C; investors are asked to send money to an account in Country D and by the time they realise the whole thing is a con, their money has gone. Often boiler room operators will transfer money received from their victims from an account in one jurisdiction to an account in another jurisdiction almost as soon as it has been received. By the time the boiler room scam has been discovered the money has therefore disappeared (Note 3).

Invariably bank accounts are emptied before the authorities can get to them. Where boiler room operators are suspected to be associated with Hong Kong, the SFC will investigate and liaise with other regulatory organisations where appropriate, but investors should not expect to recover their investments and the SFC cannot assist them in this regard.

The SFC also wishes to remind investors to only deal with licensed persons in order to be afforded protection under the current regulatory regime.
