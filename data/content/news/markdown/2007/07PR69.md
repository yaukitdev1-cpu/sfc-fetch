The SFC strives to put in place regulation of world-class standards in the face of challenges and opportunities brought by globalisation of the markets, SFC Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley, said today.

Globalisation could generate and sustain domestic economic development, but rapid growth of cross-border trading and investment, and the growing integration and correlation between markets also increased the risks and enhanced the vulnerabilities of local markets from international capital flows, he said at a conference in Hong Kong.

Regulators might also find it harder to detect and combat poor market conduct, Mr Wheatley said, adding that the proliferation of increasingly complex financial products, traded overseas or over-the-counter (OTC), gave rise to further difficulties in market abuse investigation.

“What all this means is that national securities regulators need to extend their concerns well beyond their respective geographical boundaries,” he said. “Securities regulators need to work together to promote and protect the integrity and stability of our own markets which in turn will ensure the global capital market will operate with integrity and stability.”

Mr Wheatley said the SFC had been actively participating in the work of the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO), and had recently also strengthened co-operation with the China Securities Regulatory Commission. The SFC worked with the stock exchange to ensure there was robust risk management while within the SFC a special team kept a close eye on the market situations, he said.

Mr Wheatley emphasised, however, that the SFC welcomed market developments from participants and was always mindful of the need to strike a proper balance between market development and market stability when exercising its regulatory functions.
