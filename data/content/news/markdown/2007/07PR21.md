The Government and the SFC will brief the Financial Affairs Panel of the Legislative Council on 2 March 2007 regarding proposals to adopt a revised approach to giving statutory backing to major listing requirements. The revised approach is described in a Consultation Conclusions paper released today by the SFC, and is aimed at ensuring that the regulatory regime is flexible, effective and clear.

The proposals

In January 2005, the Government began a consultation regarding proposals to extend the market misconduct sanctioning regime to cover breaches of new statutory listing requirements. At the same time, the SFC published for consultation proposed amendments to the Securities and Futures (Stock Market Listing) Rules (SMLR Amendments) with a view to establishing those requirements by codifying certain important requirements of the Stock Exchange’s Listing Rules as subsidiary legislation.

The Government and the SFC have received general market support for those proposals. However, there were concerns that the initially proposed statutory rules were unduly detailed and a breach of the minor detailed requirements might attract statutory sanctions. The Government has discussed those concerns extensively with the SFC, the Stock Exchange and market participants, leading to the development of a revised approach which is discussed in the Consultation Conclusions on the SMLR Amendments, released today by the SFC.

The revised approach which the Government and the SFC now propose has the following main features:


- A set of general principles will be enshrined in the SFO. These are fundamental obligations to be complied with by listed issuers, being statements of standards expected by the market and the spirit behind the existing listing requirements;
- A new schedule to the SFO will explain the general principles, including the definitions and factors in determining compliance;
- A non-statutory Listing Code will be issued by the SFC to set out the more detailed and technical provisions to assist compliance. It is intended to be substantially similar to provisions of the SMLR Amendments (and hence the existing Listing Rules of the Stock Exchange), but with revisions to take into account market comments received.

The listing requirements covered are essentially the three core areas identified previously, namely (i) periodic financial reporting, (ii) disclosure of price sensitive information and (iii) notifiable transactions and connected transactions which require shareholders’ approval. These areas will be deleted from the Listing Rules to avoid duplication.

It is intended that the SFC will administer the new regime and will work with the Government and the Stock Exchange to ensure a smooth transition.

SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “The proposals aim to strengthen Hong Kong’s position as a leading international financial centre by reinforcing our listing regulatory regime. This will enhance both investor confidence in the Hong Kong market and the market’s competitiveness.”

Transition and way forward

The SFC’s approach is to encourage and assist compliance through education, policy, guidance, rulings and other practical assistance. The SFC will, at appropriate times, publish clear guidelines regarding the criteria for assessing the significance and seriousness of breaches, and for deciding which enforcement route to take.

The Government aims to introduce a Securities and Futures (Amendment) Bill into the Legislative Council to set out the general principles and other important listing disciplines to be enshrined in the SFO (including the schedule) as soon as practicable. The SFC will formally publish a draft Code for public consultation.

Mr Wheatley said: The proposals seek to address the insufficient investigation powers of the Stock Exchange and the limited sanctions available under the existing non-statutory Listing Rules. The revised approach follows an international trend towards principles-based regulation and enables the regulator to address serious breaches that cause significant harm to the market, while minimising issuers’ risk of incurring statutory liability due to minor breaches. This represents the right balance between creating an effective deterrent and avoiding over-regulation.”

“The SFC will continue to work closely with the Government and the Stock Exchange on implementation arrangements, subject to legislative approval, and in particular will seek to ensure a smooth transition to the new regime,” he said.
