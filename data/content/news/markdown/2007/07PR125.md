Hong Kong continued to lead the UK and US in the trading of stocks and their options in these markets, according to an SFC Research Paper.

As of end 2006, 53 stocks (including 35 H-shares and red chip stocks and 18 Non-Mainland HSI constituent stocks) were available for trading in these three markets. Excluding HSBC, Hong Kong accounted for 82% of the total turnover of these stocks in these three markets. It is also noted that the growth in trading of Mainland stocks was much higher than that in non-Mainland stocks in all the three markets.

For stock options, Hong Kong accounted for at least 75% of the total trading. The market shares for Hong Kong in the trading of stocks and their options have been largely stable in recent years. Hong Kong’s share in the trading of HSBC options in particular, had grown significantly.

There were five ETFs traded in Hong Kong, the US and Europe with the HSCEI or the FTSE/Xinhua China 25 Index as the underlying. Trading in Hong Kong was less active than those in the US and Europe where ETFs are popular. The ETFs market in the US is much deeper and hence more liquid, while transaction costs in Hong Kong are higher as a result of stamp duty.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Acting Chief Executive Officer, said: “Hong Kong remained the leading market in the trading of stocks and options traded in these markets. Nevertheless, we should continue to further deepen and widen our ETF market and reduce transaction costs to enhance competitiveness. The SFC will study major ETF markets to draw on their experience and examine factors leading to a successful ETF market.”

The [Research Paper](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/research/research/rs%20paper%2037.pdf) is available on the SFC website under the section [“Research and Statistics”](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/research/research/research.html).
