The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

Cases handled

In the three months from October to December 2006, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 22 listing applications. This compares to 34 listing applications in the three months from July to September 2006 (Note 1). Of the 22 applications, we commented on 13 cases. Our average response time was seven working days. In the previous quarter, we commented on 22 cases out of the 34 cases received.

As reported previously (Note 2), in the three months from April to June 2006 we commented on 10 out of 16 applications received. Of those 10 cases, two lapsed due to the expiry of the six-month application period, two were withdrawn, two were rejected by the Stock Exchange and four have been listed.

Quality of disclosure

Understanding the business prospects of an issuer is as important as understanding its historical performance in making an informed investment decision. In several cases, the SFC noted the draft prospectuses failed to present a balanced view, in particular where the listing applicant is involved in more than one line of business or where the continuation of the principal business is open to question.

In one case reviewed, the listing applicant started investing in listed companies engaging in similar businesses during the track record period to expand its size. These investments grew to represent a significant portion of the listing applicant’s assets at the end of the track record period and the associated revaluation surplus exceeded the listing applicant’s profits from its operational activities. The listing applicant planned to continue to deploy substantial resources on these investments after listing. However, the draft prospectus failed to disclose such investment activities prominently as part of the applicant’s principal activities, and disclose sufficient details to enable investors to appreciate the potential financial impact on the group of conducting such investment activities in addition to its operational activities.

In some other cases, we note that the continuation of the listing applicant’s principal business was open to question, but the listing applicant did not make prominent disclosure of the potential impact on its financial condition and sustainability.

In one case reviewed, while the listing applicant is engaged in three separate business lines, substantially all of its profits during the track record period was derived from one line of business which requires a licence that is due to expire in the near future with no certainty of renewal. The listing applicant placed great emphasis on the other businesses and played down the significance of the possibility of not being able to continue with its principal business and the possible resulting change in its business characteristics.

In another case reviewed, the listing applicant’s financial condition depended very much on the success of a new project which had yet to obtain all the necessary permits and approvals from regulators. However, the listing applicant failed to make prominent disclosure of the risks in relation to the potential impact on its financial position should there be any delay or difficulties in the development of the new project.

Mr Brian Ho, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: “Investors need to understand fully the listing applicant’s business and its prospects before making an investment decision. It is important that the prospectus not only contains the full story but also presents the information in a fair and accurate manner to enable the investors to understand and assess the listing applicant’s business properly.”

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
