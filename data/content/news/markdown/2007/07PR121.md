The combined fund management business in Hong Kong recorded a 36.0% year-on-year increase to $6,154 billion at the end of 2006, according to the latest SFC Fund Management Activities Survey (FMAS).

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “These survey results underscore the strength of Hong Kong’s asset management business, which is the product of the hard work and entrepreneurship of asset managers who have chosen Hong Kong as their platform. We will continue our efforts to ensure that Hong Kong captures the growth opportunities in wealth management in the region. To this end, we will work in partnership with the industry to develop new platforms and introduce new products.”

Background

The FMAS is an annual survey conducted by the SFC to collect information and data on the general state of affairs of the fund management industry in Hong Kong. It helps the SFC plan its policies and operations. The survey covers the fund management activities of two types of firms in Hong Kong, namely:

(1) corporations that are licensed by the SFC and engage in asset management and advisory businesses (collectively “licensed corporations”); and

(2) banks that engage in asset management and other private banking activities (collectively referred to as “registered institutions”), and whose fund management activities are subject to the same regulatory regime i.e. the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO) as licensed corporations.



The combined fund management business (Note 1) amounted to $6,154 billion at the end of 2006, 36% higher than the $4,526 billion in 2005. Compared to 2004, the combined fund management business registered a two-year growth of 70%.

(2) **All sectors of the combined fund management business** **reported growth during the year**

Asset management, which accounted for the largest share of the combined fund management business, recorded an impressive growth rate of 27.5% in 2006. Advisory business and other private banking activities showed growth of 67.1% and 54.5% respectively, up from a lower base of comparison but importantly, indicating a broadening in the range of fund management activities conducted in Hong Kong.

(3) **Flow of non-Hong Kong investment capital consistently remains strong at over 60% of the fund management business**

In 2006, of the total $6,101 billion of non REIT fund management business, 62.1% ($3,786 billion) was sourced from non-Hong Kong investors. This is achieved on the back of the presence of a large number of internationally renowned financial institutions, the quality expertise of the investment professionals and the established platform that Hong Kong provides for making investments in the growing economies of the region.

(4)** The proportion of assets managed in Hong Kong has experienced steady growth over the years**

A total of $4,134 billion worth of assets were managed in Hong Kong, accounting for 56% of the asset management business in 2006, up from 53% in 2003. With the significant opportunities offered by the Qualified Domestic Institutional Investors (QDII) programmes, the funds managed in Hong Kong are expected to show significant growth, as licensed corporations and registered institutions are well-positioned to capture fund management business in this area, given their established networks in the Mainland.

The combined fund management industry at HK$6,154 billion represents 4.2 times the gross domestic product of Hong Kong, compared with 3.3 times in 2005. As at the end of June 2007, there were 2,001 SFC-authorised funds, compared with 1,968 at the end of June 2006. The breadth of the market has also increased, with the introduction of new products like the *first* open-ended China A-share fund that substantially invests directly in A shares through Qualified Foreign Institutional Investors quota, the *first* exchange traded fund (ETF) that tracks the performance of the Indian stock market and the *first* ETF that tracks an index on commodities futures.

Outlook of Hong Kong as an international financial centre

Hong Kong is well-placed to capture the tremendous opportunities provided under the Mainland QDII programmes. Enjoying close economic ties with the Mainland, Hong Kong has a well-established, deep and liquid market, a world class regulatory regime and a critical mass of financial talent with international exposure and Mainland expertise.

The recent signing of the Supplement IV to the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement, which allows qualified Mainland fund management companies to set up businesses in Hong Kong, represents another major breakthrough for Hong Kong’s asset management business. As Mainland asset managers begin to establish and operate in Hong Kong, they will broaden Hong Kong’s financial intermediary base and further strengthen Hong Kong’s lead as a key financial and asset management hub.

Mrs Lam added: “Hong Kong will strive to maintain its position as both the hub for global capital and the centre for management of the wealth that the Mainland has started to export in search of overseas investment opportunities and risk diversification. The SFC will continue to work in close partnership with the Mainland regulators to ensure that Hong Kong provides the Mainland with the best wealth management platform. In short, our aim is for Hong Kong to become the one-stop supermarket where Mainland investors can shop for the best wealth management service and products the world has to offer.”

Notes :

1. “Combined fund management business” comprises fund management business and SFC-authorised real estate investment trusts management business.

“Fund management business” comprises asset management, advisory business and other private banking activities.

“Asset management” refers to the provision of services which constitutes Type 9 regulated activity as defined in Schedule 5 of the SFO, but excludes REIT management, and “assets under management” shall be construed in the same manner.

“Advisory business” refers to the provision of pure investment advisory services on funds/portfolios and does not include the provision of research. It constitutes Type 4 and/or Type 5 regulated activity as defined in Schedule 5 of the SFO. Such service is typically provided by the respondent (or its delegate) to overseas managers who seek expert advice on the Hong Kong or Asian portion of their global investment portfolio.

“Other private banking activities” are defined as the provision of financial services to private banking clients other than by means of Type 9 regulated activity. They include providing the service of managing clients’ portfolio of securities and/or futures contracts wholly incidental to the carrying on of Type 1 and/or Type 2 regulated activity.
