I. Background
1. The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority
(HKMA) conducted an annual joint survey on the sale of non-exchange-traded investment
products by licensed corporations (LCs) and registered institutions (RIs) to non-professional
investor clients, individual professional investors (PIs)1 and certain corporate PIs for whom
intermediaries cannot make use of a waiver of the suitability obligation2. The survey covered
the sale of investment products from 1 January to 31 December 2025 (the reporting period).
2. The survey was designed to collect information about the types and value of investment
products sold. The information collected helps the SFC and the HKMA better understand
market trends, identify risks associated with the selling activities of intermediaries, and
coordinate their responses to address areas of common concern. It is believed that this
information would also be useful to market participants.

II. Scope
3. The survey covered transactions of investors who opened accounts with LCs, RIs or related
companies of LCs or RIs outside Hong Kong and to whom investment products were sold by
licensed representatives (for LCs) or relevant individuals (for RIs) in Hong Kong regardless of
where the transactions were booked. The survey excluded sales to institutional PIs, and
corporate PIs for whom intermediaries can rely on a waiver of the suitability obligation.
4. The survey covered the sale of non-exchange-traded investment products, such as collective
investment schemes (CIS), debt securities, structured products, swaps and repos. The survey
excluded leveraged foreign exchange products that were not structured products, insurance
products, mandatory provident funds, pooled retirement funds, exchange-traded funds, futures
contracts, listed securities, exchange-traded derivatives, paper gold schemes and certificates
of deposit. Currency-linked instruments, interest rate-linked instruments, and currency and
interest rate-linked instruments issued by authorized financial institutions as defined in section
1 of Part 1 of Schedule 1 to the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap 571) and sold by RIs
were also excluded.
5. LCs and RIs with respective total transactions amounting to $1 billion or more and $30 billion
or more during the reporting period (collectively, large firms) were requested to provide
further details about the investment products sold, such as their transaction amounts by types
of investors and details of the top five products ranked by transaction amount.
6. The survey was sent to 2,611 firms, including 2,502 LCs and 109 RIs, licensed or registered
for Type 1 (dealing in securities), Type 4 (advising on securities) or both regulated activities.
Over 99% of the firms responded (respondent firms). A total of 385 LCs and 67 RIs (2024:
349 LCs and 65 RIs) reported that they had sold investment products to investors during the
reporting period. These firms included retail, private and corporate banks, investment advisors,
securities brokers and international financial conglomerates. Among these firms, 106 LCs and
22 RIs (2024: 81 LCs and 20 RIs) were regarded as large firms for the purposes of this survey.
The large firms accounted for 97% (2024: 96%) of the aggregate transaction amount of all
firms.

III. Key findings
This section presents the key findings from the survey. Further details are set out in Part IV of
this report and relevant 2020-2025 annual figures are set out in the Appendix.
A. Sales and market participation continued to make new highs
7. Sales of non-exchange-traded investment products hit a record level in 2025. The total
transaction amount3 reported by respondent firms increased by 63% year-on-year to $9.9
trillion, reflecting strong investor engagement amid favourable market conditions. LCs and RIs
accounted for 38% and 62% of the total transaction amount, respectively. About half of the
respondent firms more than doubled their sales in 2025. The top 20 firms continued to
dominate total sales, accounting for 77% of the aggregate transaction amount.
8. CIS and structured products were primary drivers for transaction growth. CIS sales
increased by 85% year-on-year to $4.1 trillion, while structured product sales rose by 53% to
$3.9 trillion. Together, these product categories accounted for over 80% of total transaction
activity during the year.
9. Market and investor participation broadened further to record highs. The number of firms
engaged in investment product distribution increased by 9% to 452 in 2025, while the number
of large firms rose by 27% to 128. Investor participation also expanded significantly, with the
number of clients completing at least one transaction increasing by 33% to over 1.6 million.
These developments indicated both deeper client engagement and broader distribution activity
across the market.
B. Fixed-income, currency and commodity (FICC)-related products powered investor activity and
market growth
10. Investors maintained significant allocations to FICC-related products in 2025. Demand
remained strong for such products related to FICC, including money market funds, bond funds,
debt securities, currency-linked and commodity-linked structured products, currency swaps,
etc., even amidst improving risk appetite and robust equity market performance. The sustained
demand for these products underscored the sustained importance of fixed income, cashmanagement, currency-related and commodity-related strategies in investors’ asset
allocations.
11. Demand for liquidity-management instruments reached unprecedented levels. CIS sales
increased by 85% year-on-year to a record $4.1 trillion in 2025, making CIS the largest
product category sold by respondent firms for the first time since this survey commenced in
2020. In particular, money market funds accounted for 88% of the top five CIS sales reported
by large firms, underscoring the continued importance of cash-management strategies in
investor portfolios.

12. Investors continued to favour high-quality fixed income assets in 2025. The sales of debt
securities eased slightly from the level reported in 2024 but remained 43% higher than in 2022
at $929 billion. Growth over the past three years was driven primarily by sovereign bonds4 and
investment-grade corporate bonds, which increased by 138% and 43% respectively. Chinese
Mainland-related issuers accounted for the largest share of the transactions of the top five
corporate bonds5 in 2025, while Hong Kong government bonds remained an important
component of sovereign bond activity. These underscored the enduring appeal of Chinese
Mainland and Hong Kong fixed income markets to investors.
C. Equity-linked products fuelled structured product growth
13. Improving market sentiment supported strong demand for equity-linked structured
products in 2025. Structured product sales increased by 53% year-on-year to a record $3.9
trillion in 2025, with equity-linked products remaining the dominant category. Sales of equitylinked products rose by 58% to $2.7 trillion and accounted for 70% of all structured product
transactions. Among the sales of the top five structured products reported by large firms,
underlying equities in the technology, internet and automotive sectors together made up over
80%.
D. Digital distribution kept gaining traction
14. Online channels continued to gain share of investment product distribution in 2025. The
number of respondent firms distributing investment products through online platforms
increased by 17% to 122, while online sales accounted for 21% of total transaction activity, up
from 17% in 2024 and continuing the long-term uptrend. CIS remained the dominant product
category sold online, representing 84% of online sales, and the proportion of CIS investors
conducting transactions through online platforms increased to 83% from 78% in 2024. These
developments highlighted the ongoing digitalisation of investment product distribution which
broadened investor access.

B. FICC-related products powered investor activity and market growth
Investors maintained significant allocations to FICC-related products in 2025
29. In 2025, investors continued to maintain substantial allocations to such products related to
FICC, reflecting their enduring appeal in diversified portfolios. This category includes money
market funds, bond funds, debt securities, currency-linked and commodity-linked structured
products, currency swaps, etc., all of which saw robust demand throughout the year.
30. The demand for such FICC-related products remained strong while risk appetite improved and
equity markets performed well. The sustained demand for these products highlighted the
critical role of fixed income, cash-management, currency-related and commodity-related
strategies in shaping investors’ asset allocation decisions, ensuring balanced portfolios that
can navigate various market conditions.
Demand for liquidity-management instruments reached unprecedented levels
CIS became the top-selling product
31. Investor demand for liquidity-management solutions surged to a new high in 2025. Sales of
CIS climbed by 85% year-on-year, reaching an all-time high of $4.1 trillion. For the first time
since the launch of the survey, CIS sales surpassed those of structured products, representing
42% of the aggregate transaction amount in 2025. LCs and RIs accounted for 51% and 49%
of the total CIS sales, respectively; the top 20 sellers of CIS contributed 85% (2024: 84%).
32. The number of clients of LCs and RIs investing in CIS totalled 735,701 and 505,632
respectively in 2025. Most of them were non-PI clients purchasing authorised CIS. The overall
sales growth of CIS in 2025 was driven by a 99% surge in authorised CIS sales to $2.8 trillion,
and a 61% increase in non-authorised CIS sales to $1.4 trillion.
Money market funds dominated CIS sales, underscoring the importance of cash-management
strategies in investor portfolios
33. Based on the top five CIS reported by the large firms, money market funds remained the most
popular fund type. The proportion of money market funds continued to increase to 88% in
2025, up from 80% in 2024. This signalled investors’ ongoing focus on cash management in
portfolio construction.
