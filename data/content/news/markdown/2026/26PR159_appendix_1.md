## Explanatory Note (September 2026) Malaysia-incorporated Company's Compliance with Core Shareholder Protection Standards and Other Matters

The purpose of this note (the ' Note ') is to give an overview of the regulatory regime applicable to  a  Malaysia-incorporated  company  (' Malaysian  Company ')  in  seeking  a  primary,  dual primary or secondary listing 1 on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited (the ' Exchange ') 2 and  explain  how  a  Malaysian  Company  complies  with  the  core  shareholder  protection standards under Appendix A1 (the ' Core Shareholder Protection Standards ') of the Listing Rules and other matters.

Important note: This Note does not override the Rules and is not a substitute for advice from qualified professional advisers with respect to any particular listing applicant. If there is any conflict or inconsistency between this Note and the Listing Rules, the Listing Rules prevail. You may consult the Listing Division on a confidential basis for an interpretation of the Listing Rules or this Note. The information contained in this Note on foreign laws, regulations and market practices is based on information provided by legal counsel qualified to practise in Malaysia. We have neither separately verified this information nor updated this information since  its  receipt.  A  potential  applicant/an  applicant  should  inform  the  'Exchange  of  any changes in the laws, regulations and market practices described in this Note.

Reference should also be made to the guidance materials published by the Exchange on overseas issuers from time to time.

## A. Overview of applicable regulatory regime

## 1. Regulatory regime and regulators

- 1.1 The regulatory framework applicable to a Malaysian Company (whether private or public, and whether the shares are traded on a local or foreign stock exchange) is primarily set out in the Companies Act 2016 (the ' CA 2016 ').  Regulation of the CA 2016 falls within the purview of the Companies Commission of Malaysia (' CCM ').
- 1.2 Malaysian securities law is regulated by several national rules and regulations, including the Capital Markets and Services Act 2007 (' CMSA '),  the  Malaysian  Code on TakeOvers  and  Mergers  2016  (' Malaysian  Take-Overs  Code '),  the  Malaysian  Rules  on Take-overs,  Mergers  and  Compulsory  Acquisitions  2016  (' Malaysian  Take-Overs Rules ')  and Main Market Listing Requirements of  Bursa Malaysia Securities Berhad (' Bursa  Securities '),  the  stock  exchange  of  Malaysia  (' Bursa  LR ').  Securities Commission Malaysia (' SC ') regulates and supervises compliance with the securities laws.

- 1.3 For a company incorporated under the CA 2016 to be eligible to seek a listing of its shares, it would need to be a public company. 3 Accordingly, a private company seeking a listing must first convert to a public company.

## 2. Regulatory Co-operation between Hong Kong and Malaysia

- 2.1 Hong Kong Listing Rules 4 state that  each of the statutory securities regulators of an issuer's jurisdiction of incorporation and the statutory securities regulator of the place of central  management  and  control  must  be  a  full  signatory  to  the  IOSCO  Multilateral Memorandum  of  Understanding  Concerning  Consultation  and  Cooperation  and  the Exchange of Information (' IOSCO MMOU ').
- 2.2 This requirement is met for a Malaysian Company as the SC is a full signatory of the IOSCO MMOU. In addition, the SC has signed a memorandum of understanding 5 with the SFC to provide mutual assistance on exchange of information in relation to securities matters with the SFC.

## 3. Overseas listings

- 3.1 For  a  Malaysian  Company  whose  shares  are  listed  on  the  Main  Market  of  Bursa Securities, approval of Bursa Securities is required for the listing on the Main Market of Bursa Securities of the new shares to be issued pursuant to an initial public offering under Chapter 6 of the Bursa LR (where such new shares are to be transmissible for trading on Bursa Securities). The approval does not need to be obtained prior to the filing of the listing application with the Exchange .
- 3.2 Generally, no regulatory approval is required for a Malaysian Company to list its shares on an exchange outside Malaysia. 6

## B. Compliance with Core Shareholder Protection Standards

- 5.1 A Malaysian Company is required to comply with, among other requirements, the Core Shareholder Protection Standards. It must demonstrate how the domestic laws, rules and regulations to which it is subject and its constitutional documents, in combination (' Domestic Standards ') provide the Core Shareholder Protection Standards.
- 5.2 It is noted that certain Domestic Standards in Malaysia: (a) do not fully meet the Core Shareholder Protection Standards; or (b) align with the Core Shareholder Protection Standards but are subject to contrary provisions of a Malaysian Company's constitutional  documents. These  differences,  together  with  the  listing  conditions  and recommendations a Malaysian Company is expected to comply with to address these differences, are summarised in Appendix I .

- 6.2 There  are  differences  between  the  provisions  of  the  HK  Takeovers  Code  and  the Malaysian Take-Overs Code (See Appendix II ). A Malaysian Company to which both HK Takeovers Code and Malaysian Take-Overs Code apply must include the following disclosures in its listing document:
- (a) that  the  Malaysian  Company,  its  shareholders  and  investors  are  required  to comply with the requirements of both the Malaysian Take-Overs Code and the HK Takeovers Code;
- (b) where there are conflicts between the provisions of the Malaysian Take-Overs Code and the HK Takeovers Code and the Malaysian Company, shareholders and investors will need to comply with the stricter of the requirements under both codes;
- (c) any potential offeror must not acquire any shares or voting rights in the Malaysian Company that would give rise to a requirement to make a mandatory general offer under the Malaysian Take-Overs Code or the HK Takeovers Code unless it is satisfied that the making or implementation of such an offer would comply with the provisions of the Malaysian Take-Overs Code and the HK Takeovers Code;
- (d) any potential offeror should be aware that any dispensation(s) under the Malaysian Take-Overs Code and the HK Takeovers Code will be granted only in exceptional cases and there is no assurance that such dispensation(s) would be granted; and
- (e) any potential offeror should consult the SC and the SFC at the earliest and in any event before a general offer is triggered under the Malaysian Take-overs Code or the HK Takeovers Code.
- 6.2 A Malaysian Company should also comply with the stricter of the requirements under the CA 2016, Bursa LR (where applicable), the Malaysian Take-Overs Code, the HK Takeovers Code in relation to any conflicts as regards the requirements for share buybacks.

## Stamp Duty

- 9.3 No Malaysian stamp duty is chargeable on any transfer of shares if the transfer is put into effect solely by way of a transfer amongst CCASS stock accounts or otherwise using a transfer form or other instrument(s) executed outside Malaysia. 24

## 10 Simplified listing framework for dual listings in Hong Kong and Malaysia

- 10.1 The SFC and the SC have established a simplified listing framework for a Malaysian Company simultaneously seeking a primary listing in one jurisdiction and a secondary listing in the other, either on the Main Board of the Exchange or the Main Market of Bursa Securities.
- 10.2 The framework adopts a single submission model, under which all required application materials are submitted through the regulator of the primary listing. It also provides for a coordinated review process, including a single channel for regulatory comments and applicants' responses arising from reviews conducted by the relevant authorities.
- 10.3 Appendix  III sets out the streamlined application,  prospectus  and  submission requirements under the simplified listing framework.
