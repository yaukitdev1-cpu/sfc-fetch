Synthetic exchange-traded funds (ETFs), which use financial derivatives to replicate index performance, may be a cost-effective platform to tap a restricted market but they also expose investors to counterparty risks.

In its just-released issue of [*InvestEd Intelligence*](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/focus/nov10/sounding.html)*,* the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) reminds investors that in addition to the market risks of securities they replicate, synthetic ETFs also carry credit risks of the counterparties issuing the derivatives.

“Synthetic ETFs are like a two-edged sword,” says Mrs Alexa Lam, the SFC’s Deputy CEO and Executive Director, Policy, China and Investment Products. “While they may be keen to access an otherwise restricted market, such as Mainland China’s A-share market, investors should understand the risks associated with counterparties and securities replication before making a commitment.”

Also penning the “Sounding Board” column, Mrs Alexa Lam reiterates how the product key fact statements serve the purpose of helping issuers explain investment products with “brevity, clarity and comparability.”

The November issue of the newsletter also explains common misconception about the price quoting mechanism of derivative warrants and the extension of regulatory oversight to credit rating agencies operating in Hong Kong.

Meanwhile, Dr Wise alerts investors to issues that may arise if they are tempted to cut corners to obtain shares in hot initial public offerings.
