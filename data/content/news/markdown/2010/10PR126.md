Respondents to a public consultation conducted by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) (Note 1) overwhelmingly indicated support for a series of proposals to establish a regulatory regime for credit rating agencies (CRAs) that do business in Hong Kong, as the [consultation conclusions](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/conclusion?refNo=10CP4) released today show.

The SFC’s proposals anticipate the creation of a new type of regulated activity under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO), Type 10: “providing credit rating services.” The implementation of this change is expected in the first quarter of 2011, with Hong Kong CRAs, and their Hong Kong based rating analysts, being required to obtain Type 10 licences by June 2011.

“Establishing a regulatory regime for CRAs is an important move for Hong Kong and necessary in order to ensure that regulation in Hong Kong in this area keeps pace with international initiatives”, said Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer. “As the regulation of CRAs internationally is still evolving, the SFC will closely monitor overseas developments with a view to the local regulatory regime being brought appropriately in line with those that are taking shape in other major jurisdictions.”

In tandem with the new regulatory move, some refinements to the Code of Conduct for Persons Providing Credit Rating Services are being made. Details of the submissions from the 21 respondents and the SFC’s responses to them are summarised in the conclusion paper.

To help implement these proposals, the SFC will work with the Government to introduce necessary amendments to the SFO. The legislative amendment process is expected to be completed in the first quarter 2011, with a view towards commencing immediately afterwards the licensing process for the CRAs operating in Hong Kong and for their rating analysts.
