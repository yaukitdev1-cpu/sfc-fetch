A retail investor pleaded guilty at the Eastern Magistracy today to nine counts of manipulating the price of four derivative warrants on Industrial and Commercial Bank of China Ltd, China Mobile Ltd, China Huiyuan Juice Group Ltd and Bank of China Ltd during the continuous trading session between 11 June and 29 July 2008 (Note 1).

An investigation by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) revealed that on nine trading days during that period, {X} initially placed a large size ask order on the market followed by a small size bid order at a slightly lower price for each of the warrants. Within a minute or two, he cancelled the previous ask order and placed another large size ask order at a higher price. He then replaced his previous bid order with another small bid order at a higher price.

{X}&rsquo;s peculiar orders caused the nominal price of each of the warrants to rise to a very high level, ranging from 5.5 times to 39.9 times of their theoretical values. {X} was then able to make an illegal profit by disposing his holdings in the warrants at higher prices.

&ldquo;The SFC will continue to prosecute cases of misconduct where there is harm and damage to the market and investing public,&rdquo; said Mr Mark Steward, the SFC&rsquo;s Executive Director of Enforcement.

The Court adjourned {X}&rsquo;s sentencing until 19 January 2010 pending background and community service reports.
