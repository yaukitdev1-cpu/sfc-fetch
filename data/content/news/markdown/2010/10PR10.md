Some investors are drawn to leveraged foreign exchange (LFX) trading, by the opportunity to profitably magnify minute price movements in currencies, without fully realising that leverage can work both ways.

In his latest column, “[Leveraged Foreign Exchange, a Double Edged Sword](http://www.invested.hk/invested/en/html/section/dr_wise/2010articles/forex_jan10.html),” Dr Wise explains to investors the complexity and risks of trading LFX.

Leverage can amplify both profits and losses, Dr Wise notes. The real leverage investors apply (i.e. the value of the transaction divided by the trading capital) directly impacts the risk of their LFX investment. The higher the real gearing, the higher the risk. They may also lose a large part of their initial trading capital if they fail to meet margin calls as their positions may be compulsorily liquidated crystallising losses.

He further reminds investors that LFX contracts are not traded on established exchanges and as counterparties, LFX traders often take positions against those of their clients. LFX trading is not physically settled and there is no delivery of the currency purchased or sold. Given the complexity of the investment, he advises investors to deal only with licensed LFX traders.


“LFX is a double-edged sword,” Dr Wise says. “Try it only if you are prepared to take on these risks.”
