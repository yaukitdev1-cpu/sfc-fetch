The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) encourages major investment banks to rigorously adopt the Principles for Sound Compensation Practices and its implementation standards published by the Financial Stability Board (FSB).

“We call on major investment banks that are part of an internationally active financial conglomerate to review their remuneration practices by reference to the FSB recommendations. They should ensure their incentive structures are consistent with prudent risk management,” said Mr Martin Wheatley, the SFC’s Chief Executive Officer.

In a letter to these major investment banks, the SFC highlighted the FSB recommendations regarding effective governance of remuneration and the alignment of employees’ incentives with the long-term profitability of a firm. In addition, remuneration should be adjusted for all types of risk and be sensitive to the time horizon of risks.

“The FSB implementation standards aim at enhancing the stability and robustness of the financial system, which is increasingly globalised. It is important that significant financial institutions worldwide sign up to these standards and see to their effective implementation,” Mr Wheatley added.

The SFC acknowledges that the Hong Kong Monetary Authority has also released a guideline on sound remuneration system applicable to all authorized institutions.

The implementation standards are available on the FSB website ([www.financialstabilityboard.org](http://www.financialstabilityboard.org/)).
