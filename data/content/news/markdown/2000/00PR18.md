The Securities and Futures Commission today issued two Consultation Papers seeking public comment on:


- proposals for assessing the competence of an applicant for registration; and
- the introduction of a continuous professional training requirement.

In its June 1999 consultation paper “Review of Licensing Regime”, the SFC proposed the setting out of competence standards for registrants and the establishment of a continuous training requirement as an on-going obligation for licensed persons. The SFC also proposed a requirement that licensed corporations must have a training policy to provide continuous training to their accredited representatives. These proposals were generally welcomed by the industry.

Against this background, the two Consultation Papers issued today call for comments on the more detailed proposals on competence and training. Following consultation, the additional guidance on competence and training will be incorporated into the **“Fit and Proper Criteria”** – the SFC’s general guide to how it exercises its licensing powers.

The competence of a corporate applicant will be assessed by reference to the particular service it intends to provide and its ability to address all attendant business risk. A corporate applicant will be expected to have proper infrastructure, adequate internal control and risk management systems and qualified personnel to supervise and operate the business. An individual applicant will generally be required to have passed approved industry courses and to have relevant experience.

Once licensed, a licensee will be expected to keep up to date with key industry developments. Corporate registrants will be required to provide or facilitate continuous training for their staff. An individual registrant will be required to complete a minimum number of continuous professional training hours annually depending on their function and level of responsibility.
