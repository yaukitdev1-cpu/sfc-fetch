The Securities and Futures Commission today issued a [consultation paper](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/consult/consultation%20paper%20on%20incidental%20advice.pdf) seeking public comments on its proposed practice note on when investment advice given by solicitors and professional accountants can properly be considered incidental to the practice of their profession.

People who carry on the business of giving advice on securities should be licensed as investment advisers. However, solicitors and professional accountants are exempt from such requirement where the advice is given wholly incidental to the practice of their profession (“incidental advice exemption”).

In its June 1999 consultation paper “Review of Licensing Regime”, the SFC expressed a concern that some investment advice, especially in the corporate finance area, provided by solicitors and accountants had gone beyond what might reasonably be regarded as wholly incidental to the practice of their profession.

In the practice note, the SFC proposes that a solicitor or an accountant can rely on the incidental advice exemption and will not be required to be licensed if all of the following requirements are satisfied:


- the advice given on securities forms a normal or a wholly incidental part of the overall services provided; and
- no discrete fee is charged for the wholly incidental advice given as a part of the general practice of the solicitor or accountant.

In considering whether the provision of advice on securities forms a wholly incidental part of the overall services offered by the solicitor or accountant, the SFC is generally of the view that if a solicitor or an accountant, in the course of providing his professional services, gives investment advice which is:


- subordinate to the main purpose for which those services are provided, and in that sense is incidental to the giving of legal or accounting advice;
- general in nature in the sense that such advice would not result in the solicitor or accountant making specific recommendations to the client on a particular stock or investment product; and
- given on an irregular and non-repetitive basis,

such advice will form a wholly incidental part to the overall services offered by the solicitor or accountant.
