The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today publishes the [findings of a survey on the provision of online trading services by registered dealers in Hong Kong](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/consult/online%20survey.pdf) as at 30 April 2000.

The Online Trading Survey aims to measure the extent on use of online facilities for trading and advisory services, to identify potential regulatory issues, and to assist in planning for deployment of regulatory resources. The SFC has surveyed all 844 registered securities and commodities dealers. These companies were asked to indicate whether they have current online trading operations, the nature of these operations, and whether they are planning to provide online trading services in the next 12 months.

Reflecting a global trend, use of online trading facilities is growing rapidly in Hong Kong. The SFC is monitoring closely development in this area.

“The use of the Internet by Hong Kong brokers is growing very fast. The wider choice of services on offer is good for investors and good for the industry. Our regulation of the industry will facilitate that growth. At the same time we are very closely watching developments. We want investors to be protected and we want to assist brokers to understand the new challenges especially in the areas of Internet security and systems development and maintenance,” said Mr Andrew Procter, Executive Director of the Intermediaries and Investment Products Division.

“As a follow up to the Online Trading Survey we shall publish guidance to the industry on the provision of Internet based services and we shall be intensifying our efforts to educate investors on their rights and responsibilities,” he added.

Key findings of the survey are as follows:

- Use of online trading facilities and provision of online services is increasing rapidly in Hong Kong. In the seven months since the last survey in September 1999, the number of online brokers, the number of client accounts and online trading transaction volume have all increased two to three fold.
- Of the 844 registered dealers, 28 are providing online trading facilities, with a total of 37,382 online trading accounts, up from 10 online brokers and 8,876 accounts in September 1999. Another 242 dealers indicated an intention to launch online trading facilities within the next 12 months. Most online brokers are participants of the SEHK and the HKFE.
- Among the existing online brokers, 22 are providing online trading facilities in Hong Kong listed securities, two in overseas (predominantly US) listed securities, two in both Hong Kong and overseas securities, and two in futures contracts.
- Online trading is still in its infancy: turnover constituted only 1.3% of the total market volume for Hong Kong securities in April 2000, and online brokers represented less than 4% of the total number of registered dealers.
- Clients trading online are generally individuals and residents in Hong Kong.
- More local stockbrokers are forming strategic alliance with each other to provide online services.
- Most of the online brokers are using Internet and telephones (touch-tone and mobile) to communicate with clients. In addition, most indicated that they are planning to use WAP phones to facilitate communications. Some are considering the use of Personal Data Assistants (“PDAs”). Further innovative development of online trading facilities is expected.
The survey also confirms several important policy matters that the SFC has previously identified. To aid in its supervision of intermediaries providing online trading services, the Commission is engaged in a Special Inspection Programme aimed at addressing a number of technology-related issues. Online brokers should ensure that their systems have adequate capacity, integrity, and security protection. 

The SFC is also keenly aware of the need for investors to have the necessary knowledge on issues about online trading. The SFC has recently launched the Electronic Investor Resources Centre on the Internet and in consultation with the Investor Education Advisory Committee will continue to promote investor education in this area.

Intermediaries would also benefit from more comprehensive guidelines on their online activities, particularly in the following aspects:

- account opening and digital signature,
- suitability of financial products offered to clients, and
- risk disclosure to clients who use online trading facilities.
These issues are being taken up in the Online Trading Working Group, which brings together industry participants under the Chairmanship of the SFC.
