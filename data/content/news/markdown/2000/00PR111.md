Mr Andrew Sheng, the Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), addressed the HKSI today on the development of the S & F Bill at a seminar at the SFC.

“The purpose of the Bill is to ensure that the regulatory framework delivers a level playing field in our market which is comparable to other major markets,” said Mr Sheng. As an international financial centre, Hong Kong must benchmark its regulatory regime with international best practice. “This does not mean that our regulatory framework must be the best in the world, but that we must be on a par with generally accepted regulatory practices in other major markets,” he added.

Mr Sheng reported that consequent upon public consultation and discussions with market participants, changes will be made to the Bill to ensure that, while maintaining its objectives of meeting international standards and ensuring a level playing field and transparent markets, the Bill is practical and does not impose too heavy a burden on the market. “But the principal features of the Bill remain basically unchanged,” he said.

The three major areas of the Bill where changes will be introduced as a result of consultations with the market are:

- Disclosure of Interests - the intention is to enhance market transparency. The SFC is now working with market experts to minimise the burden of disclosure. Possible new solutions to address this problem include reducing the amount of information that must be disclosed, introducing improved standard forms and enabling the electronic filing of returns;
- Market Misconduct - there has been general support for the proposed regime. Relevant provisions will now be fine tuned so that the mental element for market misconduct is clearly spelt out. The onus of proving the offence will generally rest with the prosecution;
- Management Responsibility - this reinforces good practice and ensures that those in management act responsibly. However, liability will be based on actual fault and participation. The onus of proof of fault will be on the prosecution. Disparities in penalties will be eliminated.
Mr Sheng reiterated that the SFC’s priority must be, first and foremost, to protect the investor. The draft Bill seeks to strike a good balance in providing sufficient flexibility for market innovation and at the same time increase investor protection. The SFC has, in close consultation with the market players and the Government, reached a positive and pragmatic compromise in response to the feedback from the consultation exercise. 

Mr Sheng gave his personal assurance that the SFC aims to regulate firmly, fairly and with as light a touch as possible.
