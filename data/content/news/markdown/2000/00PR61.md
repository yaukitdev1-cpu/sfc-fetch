Some 150 leading representatives of stock exchanges, securities exchange regulators, public sector officials, investors, companies, academics and international financial institutions will meet in Hong Kong on 31 May – 2 June to discuss issues of corporate governance in Asia, with special emphasis on the role of disclosure of corporate information. 

This second meeting of the Asian Roundtable of Corporate Governance, a partnership between the OECD, the World Bank, and the Asian Development Bank and local institutions, has as its title "the Role of Disclosure in Strengthening Corporate Governance and Accountability". Co-hosted by the Hong Kong Society of Accountants, the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong and the Securities and Futures Commission of Hong Kong, it will takeis going to take place at the Island Shangri-La Hotel.

The Asian Roundtable on Corporate Governance forms part of ongoing efforts by the OECD and the World Bank to improve corporate governance world wide, using the OECD Principles of Corporate Governance as a reference point. Specifically, the Asian Roundtable aims to improve the understanding of corporate governance and assist in developing public policy responses; promote an ongoing dialogue between the private and public sectors; monitor and evaluate developments in the region; identify needs for technical assistance; and inform the international community about reform initiatives in Asia.

At its first meeting in Seoul, participants concluded that disclosure is one of the keystones of good governance. While much has been achieved since the Asian crisis, they agreed, there are still areas that require further attention. With that in mind, participants decided to draw up a broad set of recommendations on aspects of governance ranging from disclosure to shareholder rights, the equitable treatment of minority shareholders, boards of directors and the role of stakeholders in governance.

This second meeting will assist in the formulation of these recommendations. Their purpose will be to draw attention to priority areas where improved corporate governance can enhance investment, the development of robust capital markets and the welfare of Asian societies. There will be a news conference at 2.00 p.m. Hong Kong Time on Friday 2 June. Journalists will not be admitted to the discussions but will have an opportunity to contact participants during the course of the three-day session. A third meeting of the Roundtable is scheduled for March 2001, to be hosted by the Monetary Authority of Singapore.
