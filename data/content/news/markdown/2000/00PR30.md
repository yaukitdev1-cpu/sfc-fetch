The Securities and Futures Commission welcomes the passage of the Securities (Margin Financing) (Amendment) Bill (Bill) by the LegCo on 15 March 2000.

Following the Bill’s enactment, the SFC will submit the necessary subsidiary legislation to LegCo for negative vetting. The subsidiary legislation will flesh out the details of the regulatory framework and the licensing regime provided for in the Bill. The subsidiary legislation will be published in the Gazette and will be subject to negative vetting by LegCo. The effective date for the Bill is expected to be 12 June, 2000. The SFC will issue a further reminder notice to the market closer to the effective date.

The subsidiary legislation will cover elements of the regulatory structure such as:

- Application requirements
- Annual returns
- Notification requirements
- Fees
- Additional deposit requirements, and
- the financial resources rules
Securities dealers and securities margin financiers must now decide whether they wish to continue providing securities margin financing and, if they do, they must apply for a licence or transfer the business to a licensed securities dealer.

The time lag between the making of the subsidiary legislation and the effective date of the Bill will enable securities margin financiers to complete any restructuring or recapitalisation that may be needed in order to meet the new requirements. Once the Bill is in effect, there is a 30-day time limit for submitting licence applications to the SFC. After that 30-day grace period, all securities margin financiers who have not submitted licence applications must cease to provide securities margin financing. For those who have submitted licence applications within the transitional period, they are allowed to continue to provide securities margin financing pending the determination of the application by the SFC, or if the SFC subsequently refuses the application, for another 14 days or such longer period as may be specified by SFC after being notified of the refusal.

Investors are reminded to take special precautions during the implementation and transitional period of the regime for regulating securities margin financiers. Margin financiers who were carrying on securities margin financing immediately prior to the commencement of the Bill and have submitted an application for licence with the SFC are permitted by law under the transitional arrangement to continue to provide securities margin financing without the necessary licence. This does not however mean their licence applications will necessarily be approved by the SFC. Should the SFC refuse the licence applications, it will be illegal for such margin financiers to continue business after 14 days or such longer period as may be specified by the SFC. Investors are therefore advised to exercise extreme caution in doing business with these financiers.

Andrew Procter, Executive Director and Member of the Commission, warned investors, "It should be made clear that, while the SFC considers their application for a licence, margin financiers are permitted to carry on business but they may not comply with the requirements of the new legislation. Investors may not be offered the level of protection that will be available under the new regulatory regime."
