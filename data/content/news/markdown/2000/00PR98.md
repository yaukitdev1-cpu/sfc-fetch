The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today issues a Guidance Note on Incidental Investment Advice Provided by Solicitors and Accountants (Guidance Note). The Guidance Note sets out the circumstances in which the Commission would regard investment advice provided by solicitors and accountants as incidental to the practice of their professions (incidental advice). The Guidance Note is provided as assistance to solicitors and accountants.

People who carry on the business of giving advice on securities are required to be licensed as investment advisers. However, solicitors and professional accountants are exempt from that requirement where the advice is given wholly incidental to the practice of their respective professions (incidental advice exemption).

In April this year, the SFC issued a consultation draft to seek comments from the public, professional bodies and trade organizations on the incidental advice exemption.

A total of nine interested parties responded during the one-month consultation period. In general, the respondents expressed support for the SFC to issue Guidance Note to make clear its views as to what constitutes the giving of incidental advice by solicitors and accountants. There were no major differences of views on the circumstances suggesting that the incidental advice exemption is available.

The SFC considers that a solicitor or an accountant will generally be able to rely on the incidental advice exemption and is not required to be licensed in the following circumstances:

- the advice given on securities forms a normal or a wholly incidental part of the overall services provided; and
- no discrete billing of fee is charged for the wholly incidental advice given as a part of the general practice of the solicitor or accountant.
In considering whether the provision of advice on securities forms a wholly incidental part of the overall services offered by the solicitor or accountant, the SFC is generally of the view that advice will form a wholly incidental part of the overall services offered by the solicitor or accountant if the investment advice is:

- subordinate to the main purpose for which those services are provided, and in that sense is incidental to the giving of legal or accounting advice;
- general in nature in the sense that such advice would not result in the solicitor or accountant making specific recommendations to the client on a particular stock or investment product; and
