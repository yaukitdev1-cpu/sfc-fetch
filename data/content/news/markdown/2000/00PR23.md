Mr Andrew Sheng’s remarks to the Legislative Council Financial Affairs Panel today, 13 March 2000 are attached for your information.


**Finance Panel, Legislative Council**

 


**SUBSCRIPTION ARRANGEMENTS**

Mr Chairman,

1. I am honoured to make our report on the above. First of all, as the public has been inconvenienced by the incident, I believe that all parties involved should bear responsibility and express regret and apologies for the inconvenience caused to the public.
2. Allow me to summarize the key facts. 

- Both BNPPP and HSBC are respectively very experienced sponsor and receiving bank;
- The decision was made much earlier in February to have only 10 receiving bank branches to handle the Tom.com IPO;
- On 17 February, Mr. Francis Leung, Chairman of BNP Prime Peregrine (BNPPP) was quoted by the press that the response to Tom.com’s IPO would be even better than that of Beijing Enterprises. However, in his meeting with us on 10 March, Mr. Leung claimed that he was only referring to the response to the private placement and not the public subscription tranche;
- On 18 February, the Commission asked BNPPP to ensure that there were sufficient prospectuses and forms available to the public;
- On 22 February, more than 1.7 million forms were issued. On the same day, the Commission contacted SEHK to make sure that the sponsor would ensure the orderly submission of forms and smooth processing. We understand that SEHK had contacted the sponsor for this purpose;
- On 23 February, Mr. Lawrence Fok (SEHK) and Mr. David Stannard (SFC) made the decision to allow those investors who were in the queue before 12:00 noon to be allowed to submit their forms after 12:00 noon.
3. Because of Mr. Leung’s statement that the response generated by Tom.com would be better than that of Beijing Enterprises, we have compared the results of Beijing Enterprises with those of Tom.com, and concluded that the results were better, especially at the retail level. Considering that the price for each board lot of Tom.com shares was lower than that of Beijing Enterprises, the sponsor should have been aware of the high retail interest. Moreover, from the way the public was responding in terms of return of forms, there was every indication that Tom.com’s was going to be of high retail interest.
4. On the allocation of responsibility for the smooth handling of the IPO, because both the sponsor and the receiving bank gave different accounts, we cannot determine whether BNPPP or HSBC made the decision on increasing or not increasing the number of receiving bank branches.
5. It is the expectation of the market and the regulator that the sponsor should –

- Act as the overall manager of an IPO;
- Advise the issuer on pricing and gauging the likely reception of the IPO by the investing public;
- Be responsible for managing and organising the whole issue process to ensure that it is conducted smoothly and in a fair and orderly manner;
- Be ultimately responsible for advising the issuer to ensure that its agents and services providers are competent and have the capacity to perform any outsourced or delegated functions in connection with the IPO. One such agent or service provider is the receiving bank which distributes prospectuses and application forms, and receives and processes completed application forms. The responsibility of putting in place a satisfactory subscription arrangement therefore rested with BNPPP.
6. After reviewing all the facts available to it, the Commission does not accept BNPPP’s arguments that –

- BNPPP could not have appreciated the nature of the problem being the unforeseeable response by small investors and the extent to which this exceeded all historical records.
- BNPPP had taken all reasonable steps and that it was entitled to rely on HSBC’s experience.
- BNPPP had taken all reasonable steps to accommodate the unforeseeable nature of demand as it became apparent.
7. The Way Forward

- The Commission notes that insofar as regulatory action by the Commission is concerned, there is no specific provision in the current legislation or the Code of Conduct for Persons Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission for the Commission to impose sanction on any parties in this case. The Commission however has already recorded its disappointment to the sponsor concerned. However, it is important to understand the lessons to be learned from this incident, and to take immediate remedial steps to ensure that the problems will not occur again.
- The way forward is therefore to, first, consider specifically incorporating the relevant requirements of sponsors in the proposed Corporate Finance Practitioners Code of Conduct so that -
- The responsibility of the sponsor in the subscription process is clearly spelt out; and
- It is made very clear that a sponsor’s ability to discharge this responsibility in a satisfactory manner will be taken into account in assessing its fitness and properness for continued registration.
- In addition, it is important that market participants also understand the issues, and co-operate in improving the existing practice in an IPO. To this end, the Commission will work closely with the HKMA, SEHK, Hong Kong Association of Banks and relevant parties to review the IPO procedures and set down appropriate guidelines for smooth operations in the IPO process. Specifically,
- In the forthcoming GEM Listing Rules review, it is recommended that consideration should be given to including relevant provisions on a sponsor’s responsibilities in handling public offers.
- The same will also be considered for the Main Board Listing Rules review to be conducted later this year.
- The Commission will work with the SEHK towards a better understanding among sponsors of the expectation of the regulators and the sponsors’ responsibility to have in place a fair and orderly subscription process. In fact, this process has already commenced and, in this connection, the Commission noted that the IPOs in the past week, while heavily oversubscribed remained largely orderly.

WAIVER ISSUES

1. The GEM Listing Rules were adopted in July 1999 after public consultation, but shortly afterwards concern was expressed by the SEHK that some relaxation was necessary because of the special nature of technology companies. The Commission agreed to have a formal review within six months from the launch of the GEM to take into account experience gained.
2. The GEM was launched in November, 1999. Since then, however, the inflow of IPO applications to the GEM has slowed down due to market activities on the Main Board.
3. Public concerns have focused on four aspects of the approval process for the IPO:- 

- “Fast track” processing of the listing application
- Non-compliance with the requirements of the Companies Ordinance
- Allocation of a specific Stock Code; and
- Grant of waivers
4. The SFC is aware of the market’s concern that considerable uncertainty and room for abuse will be created if the GEM Listing Rules provide for one set of criteria, whilst a different set of criteria is allowed to be built up by a separate body of waivers. The SFC and the Exchange have discussed these issues at length and have agreed that there should be a review of the GEM Listing Rules (with public consultation) as soon as possible, ahead of the originally scheduled six month period.
5. In the meantime, limited relaxation of the requirements on the moratorium for management shareholders, active business pursuits, accountants' report and share option provisions have been announced on 11 March in the joint Press Release of the SFC and the SEHK. Pending finalization of rule review no further relaxation or waiver will be granted.
6. Members of the Panel should note that GEM has taken a lead in development compared with other Asian emerging markets. The SFC fully supports the GEM but we must strike a balance between market development and investor protection.
7. The SFC is concerned, however, that the market should be aware that selective comparison of individual features of one market with another may lead to regulatory arbitrage without a full appreciation of important regulatory differences in different markets. For example, it is well known that the regulatory regime in the US is significantly different from that in Hong Kong. One of the principal checks and balances in the US is the shareholder class action or liability suit against listed companies and their directors and advisers (coupled with the availability of a contingency legal fee system), a feature which is unavailable in Hong Kong. Such a structure is one of the principal tools for the enforcement of corporate governance and investor protection in the US. In the absence of such a structure in Hong Kong, the Commission felt that it might be necessary for the Listing Rules and other regulations to impose greater requirements in order to seek to achieve the same ends.
8. Compared with the GEM, NASDAQ is a relatively established market with substantial participation of professional investors, whereas the GEM is still at its infancy. In contrast, many of the investors in the GEM are retail investors. All these differences should be taken into account in the forthcoming rule review.


Thank you.
