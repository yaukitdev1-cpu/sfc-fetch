The need for rapid development of technology in Hong Kong's financial markets within an appropriate regulatory framework is the key theme of the Securities and Futures Commission's (SFC) eleventh [Annual Report](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/speeches/public/annual/rep00/99-00ar.html) published today. 

"Technology has changed securities markets profoundly in character, depth and liquidity," said Mr Andrew L T Sheng in his Chairman's Statement in the Annual Report. "Hong Kong's securities markets must use technology to offer quality services at competitive prices."

Strategic alliances will be crucial for Hong Kong to maintain its status as an international financial centre as the boundaries of global financial markets disappear. "To promote such alliances, our regulatory framework must be on par with international standards and our financial technology infrastructure must be ready for connection with the rest of the world," Mr Sheng said.

The challenges presented by rapid advances in technology and sweeping market reforms and the need to contain costs were successfully met during the year under review.

The major market developments during the year 1999-2000 were:

- The Securities and Futures Bill to consolidate 10 existing securities ordinances was completed and launched as a White Bill for consultation. The Bill will establish a regulatory framework which will facilitate technology innovation and market development, enhance investor protection and reduce market malpractice.
- The exchanges and clearing houses were officially merged under the umbrella of the Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited.
- Work began on the implementation of the blueprint for upgrading the financial infrastructure of the securities and futures markets to enhance Hong Kong's competitive position as a premier international financial centre.
1999-2000 was also the year when technology and the New Economy began to grip Hong Kong, bringing challenges and advances.

The potential disruption that might have accompanied the Y2K transition was avoided, following an extensive review of all operating software and hardware, as well as contingency procedures in the securities and futures industry.

The technology, media and telecommunications sector of the stock market grew to 42% of the total as supply grew to meet strong investor demand. Interest was further stimulated by the launch of the Growth Enterprise Market, where 61% of the companies listed had high technology content.

Preparations for online trading in Hong Kong have begun. This will be introduced through the implementation of the Automatic Order Matching and Execution System (AMS/3). The Automated Trading System (HKATS) of the Hong Kong Futures Exchange was under extensive industry-wide tests last year.

Despite the major developments which had to be accommodated, the SFC continued to offer good value for money. Costs were successfully contained without impairing standards of service, and this resulted in a return to a budget surplus for the year.

The future

The current year will continue the crucial process of modernizing markets and providing a fair and efficient regulatory structure. Among the key tasks to be tackled will be:

- the development of online trading and electronic initial public offerings,
- the development of guidelines for the approval of automated trading systems,
- an upgrading of clearing arrangements, to include scripless trading and straight-through processing for securities and futures markets,
- the creation of an Electronic Investor Resources Centre (eIRC) to aid investor education,
- the implementation of AMS/3 and HKATS,
- the introduction of the Securities and Futures Bill into the Legislative Council, and
- a review of the GEM listing rules, to reflect experience of actual operations.
Looking ahead, Mr Sheng said that the SFC must prepare and upgrade for the New Economy. The Commission is applying technology to improve efficiency. This will introduce a more orderly workflow and allow the provision of online information management and information sharing within the Commission. 

The SFC believes that investment in its people is vital, and to keep staff abreast of key changes affecting the markets, in-house training has been strengthened and opportunities for overseas training further developed.
