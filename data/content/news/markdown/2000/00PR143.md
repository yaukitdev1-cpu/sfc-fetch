The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has today tabled a [report](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/general/general/press_release/00/mtrrep.pdf) to the Legislative Council Panel on Financial Affairs regarding the distribution of share certificates in relation to the initial public offering of MTR Corporation Limited.

The report identified that two main errors were made. The first was an operational error made under time pressure, which resulted in the printing and issue of duplicate certificates. The other was inadequate public communications on how the duplicate certificate matter would be resolved, which resulted in confusion and inconvenience to investors.

After reviewing reports from Central Registration, MTRC, the Joint Global Coordinators and HKEx, the SFC is satisfied that Central Registration has taken concrete steps to resolve problems for affected investors at the time and has put forward a number of measures to prevent the recurrence of the incident. Central Registration has commissioned an overall review of its systems and procedures. Central Registration intends to appoint a person with experience of working at a senior level in registry businesses as its executive chairman, and will provide public communication and crisis management training to its senior staff.

The SFC also noted that Central Registration deeply regrets that duplicate share certificates were issued and has apologised unreservedly to the Government, MTRC, regulatory authorities, investors and other affected parties for any embarrassment caused.

For the way forward, the SFC will review and consult with the market, the industry and the Government on whether the current self-regulatory system of share registrars should be refined or replaced with a more statutory regulatory model.

“Compliance by the registrar industry with a non-statutory code of standards, with appropriate monitoring and reporting to an oversight regulator, could be an appropriate way forward,” said SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng.

“The SFC will work with the industry, the Government and the public to take these proposals forward. We welcome comment from the public on these issues,” Mr Sheng said.

The SFC will reaffirm the role of the sponsor as coordinator and manager of the entire IPO share issue process. The SFC will also continue to work closely with the market to refine and further enhance the eIPO mechanism and the integration of eIPO into the existing framework for public offers in Hong Kong.

The SFC considers it important to move towards a scripless trading, clearing and settlement environment, where the possibility of duplicate paper certificates would be minimised. The SFC notes that the Financial Secretary has reconvened the Steering Committee on the Enhancement of Financial Infrastructure to accelerate the introduction of scripless trading in Hong Kong. The SFC will continue to work with the Government and the industry to improve Hong Kong’s financial infrastructure.
