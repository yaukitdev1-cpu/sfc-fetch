On 28 March 2000, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) took part in an international Internet Surf Day arranged by IOSCO, of which it is a member. The aim of the surf day was for members of IOSCO throughout the world to simultaneously surf the internet so as to identify possible illegal activities relating to the securities and futures markets.

The surf day was also to demonstrate that regulators worldwide recognise the importance of monitoring the internet so as to detect illegal activities. They recognise that the internet is a medium that has international penetration and it is a means to disseminate information to an ever growing number of the world's population.

The SFC has an internet surveillance program which involves the daily monitoring of web-sites, chat-rooms and bulletin boards. The purpose of this program is to detect any activities which target Hong Kong and which may infringe the legislation that the SFC enforces. In deciding whether or not Hong Kong is a target, the SFC will consider the nature of the business activity, as well as other factors, including whether a local distribution agent is used, reference is made to Hong Kong dollars and the use of the Chinese language. If during the SFC's surveillance of the internet, potentially illegal activities aimed at another jurisdiction are detected, information concerning these activities will normally be passed to the regulator in that jurisdiction. To date, the program has resulted in the SFC prosecuting one individual for carrying on a business of leveraged foreign exchange trading without being licensed, and taking disciplinary action against a registered intermediary for attempting to recruit introducing agents.

Commenting on the IOSCO Surf Day and the SFC's internet surveillance program, Mr Paul Bailey, Executive Director of Enforcement said "Investors who surf the internet looking for good investments should keep a close eye out for sharks. The internet is good news for investors. It allows almost instant access to opportunity but the power of the internet can be and has been abused by people who seek to prey on the unsuspecting or unsophisticated investors. The surf day was aimed at increasing public awareness of the potential use of internet for illicit activities and that regulators worldwide are not only aware of this possibility but are working together to combat such. This has been facilitated by the increased international co-operation over the years, which is essential given internet has no boundaries. The SFC currently has co-operative arrangements with regulators in 19 countries. But there is a limit to what regulators can do to protect investors from those who use the internet for fraud. Investors must also take care to protect their own interests."

Investors should always take care in investing in any scheme including understanding what the scheme entails, who is behind it and, if an intermediary is involved, that it is properly licensed or authorised in the jurisdiction where it operates. Many regulators offer investor access to their list of licensed intermediaries. In Hong Kong, the "Registry Section" on the SFC’s web site (http: //www.hksfc.org.hk) provides a full list of registrants and authorised investment products.

If you are considering an investment opportunity found on the internet, think twice:

- do some research, get hold of an annual report, check out that the company really exists;
- be aware that what looks genuine and substantial on the internet may just be clever marketing;
- be on your guard if there is a promise of "privileged" information, any encouragement to "hurry" or where there is a promise of unusually high returns and low risk; and
- if in doubt, seek professional advice.
Moreover, investors should take great care when considering investing based on unconfirmed information that may be available on the internet, in particular, through chat rooms and on bulletin boards. Any investment decision should be based on information that can be verified. 

The public are invited to report any suspicious activities they come across, whether it be identified in advertisements, web sites, chat rooms or bulletin boards, pertaining to the securities and futures market in Hong Kong. This can be done by contacting the SFC's hotline on 2840-9333, completing the complaint form in our web-site or alternatively e-mailing the SFC on "investor.info@hksfc.org.hk". All such reports will be treated with the utmost confidence.
