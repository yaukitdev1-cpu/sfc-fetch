The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today releases the findings of its [Fund Management Activities Survey 1999](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/speeches/public/consult/consultation/fmas.html) (FMAS 1999).

The survey collects statistics on fund management activities by SFC registered and exempt persons. It provides a better understanding of the state of the industry, and is a first step to building a comprehensive industry database for regulatory and market facilitation purposes. The survey will be conducted annually.

The survey was developed by the SFC in consultation with the Hong Kong Investment Funds Association. All SFC registered intermediaries or exempt persons were asked to respond if their primary investment advisory business during the year ended 31 December 1999 was management of funds or portfolios. In all, 142 registered intermediaries and eight exempt persons responded.

[Key findings of the survey are in the attached summary](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/speeches/public/consult/consultation/fmas.html). Some main aggregate figures are as follows:

- Total assets under management by the 150 respondents amounted to HK$3,501.4 billion. Of these, $749.4 billion was directly managed by them in Hong Kong, while the remainder of $2,752 billion was sub-contracted or delegated to other offices or third parties either in Hong Kong ($13.7 billion) or overseas ($2,738.2 billion) for management.
- Total assets managed in Hong Kong were $763.1 billion, representing 21.8% of all assets under management by the respondents. Of total assets managed in Hong Kong, those directly managed by the respondents ($749.4 billion) accounted for 98.2% with the remainder sub-contracted or delegated to other offices or third parties in Hong Kong for management.
Mr Andrew Procter, Executive Director of the Intermediaries and Investment Products Division, said information gathered in this survey and future polls would help the SFC focus its regulatory efforts and maintain an environment conducive to business development.

"Fund management is an important industry in Hong Kong. There is increasing competition to lure the industry to other regional centres. It is vital that we are in a position to reinforce the strengths of Hong Kong as a major asset management centre. The FMAS will help identify the issues that are crucial to Hong Kong's competitiveness," Mr Procter said.
