In response to one of the Government’s 30 measures announced by the Financial Secretary in September 1998 to strengthen the regulatory regime of short selling, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) worked closely with the Financial Services Bureau on the Securities (Amendment) Ordinance 2000 (the Ordinance), which was enacted on 26 May 2000. The Ordinance will increase the penalties for naked short selling and introduce new criminal offences for unreporting short selling.

The maximum penalties for naked short selling will be increased from a fine of $10,000 and an imprisonment for 6 months to a fine of $100,000 and an imprisonment for 2 years. The new sections introduced in the Ordinance require a person who intends to sell short on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited informs his brokers that the order which he places is a short selling order. If the short selling transaction is to be settled by securities borrowed under a securities borrowing and lending arrangement, the person needs to confirm that the lender from whom he obtains the securities has the securities available to lend to him to settle the transaction. The maximum penalties for committing an offence under the new sections are a fine of $50,000 and an imprisonment for 1 year.

The Ordinance also empowers the SFC to make rules to require stock lenders to keep records of their lending activities. The SFC made the Securities (Stock Lending) Rules (the Rules) on 29 May 2000. The Rules set forth the requirements that lenders keep records of their lending activities for one year and provide such records to the Commission upon its request.

The operation of the Ordinance and the Rules will create proper audit trail for and strengthen the regulation of short selling.

The Rules were gazetted on 30 May 2000. Both the Ordinance and the Rules are scheduled to take effect on 3 July 2000.
