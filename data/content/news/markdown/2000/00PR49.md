In its continuous effort to promote investor education, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has enhanced its website database by introducing a “Questions Corner”. It seeks to answer typical questions raised by retail investors on stock, derivative and fund investments, helps them to assess the risks of different financial products and make informed investment decisions. 

The “Questions Corner” contains the following topics:

Answers to Top Questions – focus on more specific issues investors may be faced with, e.g. new share offerings, discretionary accounts, risks of buying unauthorised funds and black-market index futures.

Shareholders’ Basics – explain issues such as share consolidation and subdivision, procedures for share registration, collection of unclaimed benefits and issuance of replacement certificates.

Investment-Linked Assurance Schemes – help investors understand the nature and risks of this type of investments and how they are regulated.

Investor Compensation Schemes – explain the operation of the two statutory compensation funds for stock and futures trading, including the eligibility criteria.

This subsection complements the existing Investor Education materials on the website, which includes full contents of all the investor publications, articles of fictional accounts of investor experiences with different scenarios and advice to investors how to handle similar circumstances, fact sheets about Internet investing and day trading.

In addition, there is an “Interactive Kiosk” that test investors’ awareness of how to make informed investment decisions, and protect themselves against potential pitfalls.

“The SFC considers investor education the first important step to investor protection. The use of the Internet medium to disseminate its key messages is most appropriate in this age of cyber-based information flow. We shall proactively enhance the Investor Education section on the website to help improve public understanding of the opportunities and risks associated with different types of investment activities,” an SFC spokesman said.
