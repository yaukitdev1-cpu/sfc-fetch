The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today announced that registrants may now submit returns on Financial Resources Rules (FRR) electronically via the newly launched e-FRRR system. 

By introducing the e-FRRR system, the Commission aims to make the submission of returns more convenient, accurate, secure and more timely than that through the current paper-based channel.

The e-FRRR system has been tested and used by nine pilot registrants for submitting the June 2000 returns. The SFC is satisfied with the testing results and has decided to make the system available to all registrants on 11 August 2000 for submitting FRR returns electronically.

"This is a major breakthrough in electronification of data submission for the securities and futures markets in Hong Kong. I am most encouraged to see that three small-sized brokers were amongst the nine pilot registrants who submitted their June FRR returns electronically. They have demonstrated the ability and creativity of the brokers in Hong Kong to make use of new technology to enhance efficiency and competitiveness," said Mr Andrew Sheng, the Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission.

To assist registrants in using the system, the Commission is prepared to provide hands-on technical assistance to the first 50 registrants who intend to make use of e-FRRR to submit July 2000 FRR returns electronically. In addition, the SFC will hold a series of briefings on the use of the system. Details of these are provided on SFC's website: www.hksfc.org.hk under "Updates for Intermediaries". The e-FRRR User Guide and other relevant information are also available on the web site.

The e-FRRR system has adopted the latest digital signature technology. Returns signed with electronic certificate (e-cert) issued by the Hong Kong Post Office can do away with paper copies.

"The e-FRRR system is the first application on the Securities and Derivatives Communication Network (SDNet), which is a part of the blueprint envisaged by the Steering Committee on the Enhancement of Financial Infrastructure for upgrading Hong Kong's financial infrastructure. We plan to introduce electronic submission for annual reports and corporate finance documents in the coming months. The SFC will work closely with the industry users in implementing full electronification of data submission through the SDNet," Mr Sheng said.
