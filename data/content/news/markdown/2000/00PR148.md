The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) Chairman, Mr Andrew Sheng announced today that the new Securities and Futures Bill aims to be on a par with internationally accepted regulatory practices in New York and London and will present Hong Kong with a modern regime compatible with the New Economy and other global financial markets. The Government will introduce the Bill before the Legislative Council on 29 November 2000.

“The Bill strikes a good balance between giving the market sufficient flexibility for innovation and competition, and providing adequate investor protection. It is a practical law which encourages an open, transparent, fair and efficient securities and futures market in Hong Kong,” Mr Sheng said.

“We have worked closely with market participants, international firms and stockbrokers associations to create solutions that further refine and improve the Bill. This dialogue with the industry is very important to ensure effective implementation of the Bill and smooth market transition to the new regime,” Mr Sheng said.

Mr Sheng declared that as a public body, the SFC will continue to be transparent and accountable in administering the Bill when it is passed.

“We take the trust the public has placed on us very seriously,”Mr Sheng said.

He pointed out that the Commission is already subject to various checks and balances that ensure the SFC carries out its duties in a fair and proportionate manner. He said that these measures will be further enhanced with the creation of an independent Process Review Panel and a wider scope of appeal against SFC decisions. The PRP should not be seen as a curb on the SFC exercising its powers. It is a mechanism which SFC welcomes to give the Commission public credibility that it exercises its powers fairly and in full accordance with due process.
