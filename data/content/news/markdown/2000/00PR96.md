The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has today issued [the Consultation Conclusions Report (the Report) on the Consultation Papers on Competence and Continuous Professional Training](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/conclusion?refNo=00CP1).

In March 2000, the SFC invited public comments on its proposals to introduce competence standards for market entrants and continuous professional training requirements for existing registrants.

The consultation period ended on 31 March 2000 and 22 submissions were received from various market participants and professional institutions. The Report summarizes the result of the consultation and the response of the SFC.

The revised proposals on the Consultation Paper on Competence requirements are summarized as follows:

Corporate applicants must be competent to manage the risk of their business. However, the Commission will accept suitable arrangements, whether in Hong Kong or elsewhere, at local company level or group level, as long as the arrangement can achieve the same objective of ensuring competent management of business risk, the exercise of proper control of and the efficient running of the business.

For individual applicants:

a. work experience and industry qualification gained either in Hong Kong or overseas will be recognized provided that it is relevant to the functions to be performed by the applicant upon the granting of registration;
b. applicants with substantive relevant overseas but little or no local experience will generally be required to pass a recognised examination on the local regulatory framework to familiarize themselves with the local regulatory and legislative environment;
c. in appropriate cases, an applicant may be granted a registration conditional upon the passing of a local regulatory framework paper within 3 months of obtaining registration;
d. the passing of recognised exams and the obtaining of relevant experience should be within 3 years of the date of application;
e. recognised educational or industrial qualifications gained more than three years ago will be considered if applicants have adequate relevant working experience and have remained in the industry (either in Hong Kong or overseas), or can prove recent registration with relevant overseas regulators;
f. the list of recognized examinations will be expanded and reviewed regularly;
g. director applicants will ordinarily be required to pass English or Chinese and Mathematics of a standard equivalent to School Certificate examination; and
h. applicants for registration may have to demonstrate that they have the expected level of industry and regulatory knowledge by passing a recognised course whereas existing registrants are expected to remain competent in discharging their functions.

The revised proposals on the Consultation Paper on Continuous Professional Training (CPT) requirements are summarized as follows:

a. the onus is on business entity registrants to design and implement a continuous education programme which best suits the needs of registered directors and representatives;
b. the SFC will continue to liaise with the Hong Kong Securities Institute and other professional / tertiary bodies to develop more courses for meeting the CPT requirements and minimizing any financial burden on registrants;
c. the minimum CPT hours requirement for directors and representatives will be adjusted to 5 hours per calendar year;
d. internally organized training course will be recognized for CPT purposes if the training topics are relevant and beneficial to the registrants in the performance of their functions; and
e. the SFC’s Academic Advisory Committee represented by representatives from regulators, academic institutions, Securities Institute, Consumer Council, tertiary bodies and market practitioners will assume the additional function of advising the SFC on courses and examinations suitable for recognition for the purposes of the competence requirement and to endorse courses for the CPT requirement.

Mr. Andrew Procter, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products Division said: “The proposals in the earlier Consultation Papers were generally well supported. Highly skilled intermediaries are an important part of Hong Kong’s competitive advantage. In reaching the current revised proposals, the SFC has taken into account public comments and struck a balance between protection of investors and the cost to registrants in meeting these competence standards. These new requirements are scheduled to come into effect on 1 January 2001. We believe that the proposal will affirm the professional standard of financial intermediaries in Hong Kong and further enhance Hong Kong’s competitive position as an international financial centre. We also take this opportunity to thank the market for providing us invaluable and constructive comments.”
