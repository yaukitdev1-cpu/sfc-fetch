The Securities and Futures Commission ("SFC") and the Securities Commission of New Zealand ("NZSC) signed a Memorandum of Understanding for regulatory cooperation in Montreal, Canada yesterday (17 September).

The purpose of the Memorandum is to enhance the protection of investors and to promote the integrity of the financial markets of Hong Kong and New Zealand by providing a framework for mutual assistance and the exchange of information to the extent permitted by the laws and practices of the respective jurisdictions.

The signatories to the Memorandum were Mr Anthony Neoh, QC, Chairman of the SFC, and Mr Euan H Abernethy, Chairman of the NZSC. The NZSC is a body corporate established under section 9 of the Securities Act 1978. The NZSC is responsible for keeping under review practices relating to securities and for exercising securities and futures jurisdictions in
particular cases in New Zealand.

In a joint statement Mr Neoh and Mr Abernethy said, "This Memorandum recognizes the increasing international activity in the securities, futures and options markets and the corresponding need for mutual cooperation between the relevant national authorities. By providing the framework for future cooperation, the Memorandum will allow Hong Kong and New Zealand regulatory authorities to establish a united front to protect investors and more effectively regulate the marketplace."

The scope of the Memorandum includes the enforcement of laws, rules and regulations relating to securities and futures markets; clearing and settlement activities; takeovers and mergers; the disclosure of information relevant to investors; the prevention of insider dealing, market manipulation and other fraudulent practices; and the fitness and properness and conduct of business standards of advisers, traders and other financial market practitioners.

To the extent permitted by laws and practices, each authority will provide the authority with any information that is discovered which gives rise to a suspicion of a breach, or anticipated breach, of the rules or laws of the other authority. The information supplied will be used solely for the purposes of securing compliance with or enforcement of the rules or laws, and taking regulatory action or imposing regulatory requirements.

The Memorandum does not modify or supersede any laws or regulatory requirements in Hong Kong or New Zealand. Under the requirements of the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance, the SFC can only disclose information to qualified overseas regulators if it is in the public interest to do so.

The signing of the Memorandum coincides with the 21st Annual IOSCO Conference which is currently being held in Montreal. In addition to this Memorandum with the NZSC, the SFC also has Memoranda of Understanding with the securities regulators in the United Kingdom, China, Australia, Thailand, Malaysia, France, Indonesia, the United States, Taipei, British Columbia, and Spain.

The Securities and Futures Commission ("SFC") and the Comision Nacional del Mercado de Valores ("CNMV") of Spain signed a Memorandum of Understanding for regulatory cooperation in Montreal, Canada yesterday (17 September ).

The purpose of the Memorandum is to enhance the protection of investors and to promote the integrity of the financial markets of Hong Kong and Spain by providing a framework for cooperation, including channels for communication, exchange of information and investigative assistance.

The signatories to the Memorandum were Mr Anthony Neoh, QC, Chairman of the SFC, and Mr Luis Carlos Croissier Batista, President of the CNMV. The CNMV is a public agency responsible for the surveillance and supervision of the securities markets in Spain.

When signing the Memorandum, Mr Neoh said, "This Memorandum with the CNMV furthers the SFC's objective of establishing cooperation between financial market regulators around the world. We look forward to working more closely with the CNMV to ensure the integrity and development of our respective markets."

Mr Croissier Batista of the CNMV added, "This agreement between the SFC and the CNMV will provide both authorities with a safe and efficient tool to carry out their corresponding supervisory duties within an open financial scenario where the globalization of trading is becoming its most significant feature."

The two authorities have agreed to ensure that mutual assistance is provided by the exchange of information in relation to the enforcement of laws and regulations relating to securities, futures contracts and other investment products and the prevention of insider trading, market manipulation and other fraudulent and manipulative practices.

To the extent permitted by laws and practices, each authority will provide the other authority with any information that is discovered which gives rise to a suspicion of a breach, or anticipated breach, of the rules or laws of the other authority. The information supplied will be used solely for the purposes of securing compliance with or enforcement of the rules or laws, and taking regulatory action or imposing regulatory requirements.

The Memorandum does not modify or supersede any laws or regulatory requirements in Hong Kong or Spain. Under the requirements of the Securities and Futures Commission Ordinance, the SFC can only disclose information to qualified overseas regulators if it is in the public interest to do so.

The signing of the Memorandum coincides with the 21st Annual IOSCO Conference which is currently being held in Montreal. In addition to this Memorandum with the CNMV, the SFC also has Memoranda of Understanding with the securities regulators in the United Kingdom, China, Australia, Thailand, Malaysia, France, Indonesia, the United States, Taipei, British Columbia, and New Zealand.
