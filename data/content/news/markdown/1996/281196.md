The Securities and Futures Commission (“SFC") today published a consultation document containing proposed guidelines on “Management, Supervision and Internal Controls".

The proposed guidelines have been developed in an effort to provide guidance to registered persons and licensed traders with regard to the SFC’s expectations in relation to the manner in which these persons establish and maintain internal management and control systems with respect to their businesses. The individual codes of conduct issued by the Stock and Futures Exchanges and the SFC require registered persons and licensed traders to maintain satisfactory internal control systems.

Satisfactory internal control systems are established and maintained to provide reasonable assurance that:

- the assets of a firm and its clients are safeguarded against unauthorised use or disposition;
- thorough and reliable accounting and financial records and other information used within and
- published by the business, are properly maintained; and
- the firm complies with all applicable laws and regulatory requirements.
Due to the significant differences that exist in the nature, size, and activities of various registered persons/licensed traders, the adequacy and appropriateness of a firm’s internal control system can only be effectively determined based on an objective and thorough analysis of the firm’s individual structure, operations, and needs.

The senior management of a firm should take it upon itself to perform this analysis in order to ensure that the firm’s internal control system is appropriate for the firm’s particular circumstances.

The SFC will adopt a pragmatic approach in evaluating the adequacy of a firm’s internal control system by taking into account each firm’s particular circumstances.

The registered person/licensed trader and the firm’s senior management bear ultimate responsibility for the adequacy and effectiveness of the internal management and control systems implemented.

The proposed guidelines are divided into eight sections, each of which identifies a specific objective, followed by control guidelines, which, if implemented, would assist a firm in achieving the specific objectives.

The proposed guidelines are also supplemented by an Appendix, which sets out suggested control techniques and procedures relating to areas of operational controls and risk management.

The headings of the eight sections are:

- Management and Supervision
- Segregation of Duties and Functions
- Personnel and Training
- Information Management
- Compliance
- Audit
- Operational Controls
- Risk Management
It is proposed that the guidelines will apply to persons registered under the Securities Ordinance (Cap. 333) or the Commodities Trading Ordinance (Cap. 250) as dealers, dealing partnerships, investment advisers, investment advisers' partnerships, and persons licensed under the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading Ordinance as licensed leveraged foreign exchange traders, including where applicable the representatives of such persons.

Although it is not proposed that the guidelines have the force of law, a failure to comply could reflect adversely on the fitness and properness of the registered person/licensed trader to continue to be registered/licensed.

Mr Anthony Neoh, Chairman of the SFC, said: "The need to ensure that the business structures and operations of financial professionals exist in an environment in which adequate internal controls are present has become abundantly clear due to the events of the past several years."

"The risks posed to both professionals and their clients are complex and significant, and thus warrant a fresh examination of the methods used to ensure financial and operational integrity. In the coming months, the Commission staff will organise briefings on the proposed guidelines with the stock and futures exchanges, leveraged foreign exchange traders and other concerned industry stakeholders," Mr Neoh said.

"We hope that we can work with the industry to produce a set of guidelines which will enhance the market integrity and also the professionalism of industry participants," he added.

Industry representatives and other interested parties are invited to submit comments on the proposed guidelines by 31 January 1997. It is hoped that the guidelines can be introduced in April 1997.
