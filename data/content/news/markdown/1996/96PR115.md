Speaking at the conclusion of the 21st Annual Conference of the International Organisation of Securities Commissions ("IOSCO"), in Montreal, Canada, the Chairman of the Securities and Futures Commission, Mr Anthony Neoh, welcomed the challenge to support the continued strong growth in the Chinese economy.

Responding to an acknowledgment by Mr Zhou Daojiong, Chairman of the China Securities Regulatory Commission that "China and Hong Kong had a lot to learn from each other", Mr Neoh said "The challenge for Hong Kong is to use our open markets and internationally oriented and respected systems, to continue to prosper and to help China to prosper."

Mr Neoh added "Looking to the future, Hong Kong's markets will remain the first choice of corporations in China for capital raising and investment. We in Hong Kong offer great potential to the world, and in particular the region, as an international financial hub, in the same way London has provided a hub to Europe".

In a speech to IOSCO members, Mr Zhou encouraged Hong Kong to take on this important role, noting that it would benefit both Hong Kong and China. Mr Zhou emphasised the important role of Hong Kong as an international financial sector in assisting China's financial sector to tap the international market and stressed that the Hong Kong market will continue to be the main place for Chinese enterprises seeking overseas listings.

"In order to promote the co-prosperity of securities markets in Hong Kong and the Mainland, we will speed up the listing of state-owned enterprises in Hong Kong," said Mr Zhou.

**Note :**
The IOSCO was officially formed in 1983. Its objective is to assist members to carry out their mission, and in particular to exchange information with a view to developing securities markets and improving their operation, to co-ordinate the efforts of its members to that end and to formulate common standards. As the key international forum for securities and futures market regulators, it has 136 regulatory authority members from 80 countries. The 21st Annual Conference is being held from 16 to 20 September in Montreal. 

Mr Neoh was elected Chairman of the Technical Committee of IOSCO on Monday, 16 September 1996.

Speech by Mr Zhou Daojiong
Chairman of China Securities Regulatory Commission
at the XXIst Annual Conference of the
International Organisation of Securities Commissions

Distinguished Chairman, Ladies and Gentleman:

It is my great pleasure to attend, on behalf of the China Securities Regulatory Commission, the XXIst Annual Conference of IOSCO. I would like to extend my warm congratulations on the successful convocation of this Conference and the 10th anniversary of the founding of the IOSCO Secretariat in Montreal. To address the theme of this round-table meeting, I would like to discuss the reform of the economic system and the development of the securities market in China.

China's Economic Development in the New Era and the Mission of the Securities Regulatory Authorities

Guided by Deng Xiaoping's theory of building socialism with Chinese characteristics, tremendous achievements have been made in economic development and social progress in the past 17 years. Our national economy has maintained a fast and sustained growth momentum. During the Eighth Five-Year Plan (1991-1995), the annual GNP growth rate averaged about 12%. The reform of the economic system has undergone breakthroughs and the pattern of overall opening to the outside world has taken shape. Meanwhile, the living standards of the urban and rural people have been continuously improved and progress has been made in various aspects of social life. To further promote the economic reform and development of the economy, the Chinese Government, in its newly announced "Blueprints of the Ninth Five-Year Plan for the National Economic Development and Social Development and the Long-term Goals to 2010" clearly states that two fundamental changes will be accomplished: to transform the planned economic system to a socialist market system and to transform the economic growth mode from an extensive one to an intensive one. It is a reform of overall and far-reaching significance to realize the transition from the traditional planned economy to a new socialist market economy. It is necessary to establish and improve the basic framework of a market economy to introduce a scientific enterprise system and set up a macroeconomic system suitable for a market economy. It is a natural demand of China's economic growth in its current stage of development to realize the transformation of the growth mode from an extensive one to an intensive one.

It is an endeavor of great complexity to achieve the above-mentioned two fundamental transformations, as it involves various aspects of both macro and micro economic activities, and its success will have to be based on the reform and development of enterprises. At the present time, the reform and development of enterprises involves four major tasks: First, to establish a scientific operational mechanism for enterprises. A scientific operational mechanism for enterprises is characterized by a clear definition of enterprises property rights, a clear clarification between rights and responsibilities, a separation of government administration from enterprise management, scientific management and a focus on increasing economic efficiency; Second, to promote technological progress of enterprises. Enterprises, by means of technological progress, equipment innovation, reduction in resource consumption and saving in construction investment, can reduce costs and increase profits. Third, to promote the adjustment of industrial structure. Irrational industrial structure is one of the major factors hindering the development of enterprises. Rational industrial structure shall be developed through market forces; Last, to emphasize economies of scale. It is imperative for enterprises to abandon the primitive mode of operation characterized by being complete but small, and to pursue economies of scale.

During this important period in which China's economic system undergoes fundamental transformation, what role shall the securities market play? As a component of the market economy, the securities market is a product of economic development and reform, promoting the development of the economy through its unique mechanism.

China's securities market grew with the reform, opening to the outside world and economic development. In the six years since the establishment of the stock exchanges, the securities market has witnessed remarkable development. By the end of July 1996, the number of listed companies reached 408, representing 30 provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities in China, the listed share capital aggregated at more than 90 billion yuan, with a total market value of 695.2 billion yuan. 24 enterprises are listed overseas, and 78 enterprises have issued B-shares. There are 97 securities companies in China, 266 institutions engaged in securities operations, and more than 2,000 securities trading offices. In addition, market intermediaries such as accounting firms, law firms and investment consulting and advisory firms have been providing various services for the securities market. The number of investors has reached more than 15 million. There are currently about 100,000 people employed in the securities sector. At the same time, we have also made great progress in rule-making. Various laws and regulations were enacted and promulgated. A centrally-controlled supervision and control system are basically in place. Although the securities market has a short history in China, it has demonstrated its active role in helping enterprises raise funds, promoting the transformation of enterprises' operational mechanism, optimizing the distribution of resources and consequently promoting the reform in the economic system and the development of the national economy.

China's securities market is still at its early development stage. Its functions are still far from perfection, and its role has not yet been brought fully into play. I believe, with the reforms of China's economic system deepening further and the national economy growing constantly, the securities market will be further developed. As required by China's tasks for economic development and the realization of the strategic goals in economic growth during the Ninth Five-Year Plan period, our securities market will have a bigger role to play. Therefore, the role of China's securities regulatory authorities can be summarized as follows: to nurture and regulate the market.

In the course of the transition from a planned economy to a market economy, the development of a market system is not only a matter of concern to market participants; it also requires the government to nurture and support. As an element of the socialist market economy, the securities market is no exception. China currently adopts a financial system under which indirect financing is the mainstay supplemented by direct financing, and banks are the central players in capital financing for all social aspects. This pattern suits China's national conditions, but, judging from the current situation, the percentage of direct financing shall be gradually increased to a rational level. During the Eighth Five-Year Plan period, China's savings deposits grew at an annual average of 450 billion yuan, an annual increase of 33 percent. However, during the period from 1993 to 1996, the amount of capital financed annually by means of stocks was less than 15 billion yuan on average. It will be desirable to move some bank savings into the direct financing market and to build up a rational financing structure. This requires the securities regulatory authorities to actively expand the securities market, to encourage more enterprises to raise capital on the securities markets, to nurture institutional investors, to improve the quality of listed companies and to expand the size of the market. From the viewpoint of the micro economy, it is of utmost importance to nurture and develop the securities market for the following reasons: First, to raise construction fund for enterprises; Second, to promote the transformation of enterprises' operational mechanism; Third, to rectify the irrational asset-liability ratio of enterprises; and Last, to promote enterprise mergers and acquisitions as well as to optimize the industrial structure. Through the securities and capital market, merged, newly acquired or reorganized enterprises can combine their advantages to overcome their deficiencies, develop economies of scale and improve their competitive edge, thus improving the operational quality of the overall economy.

Regulating the market is a task that any securities regulatory authority has to fulfill. As far as China's situation is concerned, the establishment of a standardized operational mechanism for the securities market requires us to regulate the market forcefully. Market standardization is the precondition of development. The purpose of regulating the securities market is to nurture and develop it. At present, adhering to the principle of "legality, supervision, self-regulation and standardization," we shall build a sound set of laws and regulations, actively work toward the promulgation of the Securities Law, clarify the market supervision apparatus, improve the self-regulating capabilities of all market participants, strictly punish violators of market rules and protect the interests of investors. We shall also help listed companies, securities firms and investors to gain a better understanding of the securities market as a constituent of the socialist market economy, and to understand its unique operational environment, and the need to abide by laws and regulations to maintain good market order.

To Further Strengthen Cooperation to Promote the Co-prosperity of Securities Markets in Mainland China and Hong Kong

Hong Kong, due to its special geographic location and economic environment, has been pursuing a unique road for its economic development, and has formed a mature market economy. Especially after the opening and reform policies of the Chinese mainland, China's economy developed rapidly, offering unprecedented opportunities for all trades in Hong Kong and thus helping the economic growth of Hong Kong. At the same time, Hong Kong also plays an important role in helping the mainland tap the international market and in promoting its economic growth. Hong Kong is the largest trade center for the mainland's exports. Hong Kong has helped the mainland pool a large amount of foreign loans. Hong Kong's capital market has a set of sound regulations and rules and a high level of market liquidity, proving many investment channels for international investors. Hong Kong as an international financial center will have an important role in promoting China's financial sector to tap the international market. Therefore, after 1997 the economic link between the mainland and Hong Kong will continue to serve as an important factor in promoting their respective development.

Remarkable progress has been made in development and cooperation in the securities market, and exchanges and cooperation in securities circles between the mainland and Hong Kong will enjoy strong prospects after 1997. The system, experiences and new products of Hong Kong's securities market all serve as reference and guide to the securities market of the mainland. The Hong Kong market will continue to be the main place for Chinese enterprises seeking overseas listing. At present, 22 State-owned enterprises have been listed in Hong Kong. This not only helps State-owned enterprises open new channels in using foreign investment and taps Hong Kong's role as an international financial center in serving the mainland, but also supports the development of Hong Kong's securities market. As such, it is beneficial in enhancing Hong Kong's status in the international capital market and in bolstering Hong Kong's attraction to international capital, foreign financial and investment institutions.

In order to promote the co-prospertiy of securities markets in Hong Kong and the mainland, we will speed up the listing of State-owned enterprises in Hong Kong, and will soon select a group of large and medium-sized State-owned enterprises as the candidate enterprises for the fourth batch of overseas listing enterprises. Hong Kong will continue to be the main place for listing, and the international capital market will also be explored in an active and cautious manner. At the same time, the China Securities Regulatory Commission will actively cooperate with the Hong Kong Securities and Futures Commission and the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Ltd in supervising the companies listed in Hong Kong, in urging and supervising the enterprises to make full, timely and accurate disclosure, to use funds strictly according to relevant requirements, and in improving the operation and management of the listed companies.

We will continue to experiment in the issuing of B-share and try to attract more foreign investors by improving legislation, the operation mechanism of the market and the level of disclosure. The State Council has enacted unified national B-share regulations, and the State Council's Securities Committee also promulgated detailed implementation rules for these regulations. The experiment has now expanded to the entire country from the original two cities, Shanghai and Shenzhen. This year, some efficient and large enterprises with a bright future will be selected to issue B-shares. We will raise capital to fund the economic development of the mainland and develop the mainland's securities market by tapping the functions of Hong Kong's capital market.

We will also continue to strengthen supervision, cooperation and personnel exchanges with Hong Kong's securities and futures markets. On the one hand, we will strengthen contact between the securities and futures regulatory authorities of the mainland and Hong Kong, exchange information on a timely basis for better mutual understanding and close cooperation, and increase the level of regulatory cooperation. On the other hand, we will strengthen personnel training and exchanges with Hong Kong's securities and futures community so as to learn from each other and achieve joint progress.

Ladies and Gentlemen: China's securities market is still an emerging market. It has made some progress, but is far from perfection. In the course of improving and developing China's securities market, we anticipate strengthening cooperation and exchanges with members of IOSCO, to learn from each other in order to improve regulatory standards.

To conclude my speech, I sincerely wish the XXIst Annual Conference of the IOSCO a complete success!
