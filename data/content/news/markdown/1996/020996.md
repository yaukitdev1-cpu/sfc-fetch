The Securities and Futures Commission is pleased to announce the appointment of Mr Mark Dickens as a Member of the Commission and Executive Director from 1 January 1997. Mr Dickens will succeed Mr Gerard McMahon as head of the SFC's Enforcement Division.

Mr Dickens has extensive experience both overseas and in Hong Kong in all aspects of securities market regulation. In the 1980's, he coordinated the Australian NCSC's (National Companies and Securities Commission) law reform and policy development initiatives and was also responsible for all of its major investigations and civil litigation. In Hong Kong, as Senior Director of Corporate Finance Division, he has made a significant contribution to the SFC especially in the areas of takeovers regulation, listing policy and corporate governance.

Mr Dickens, 44, is a lawyer by profession and has had 18 years experience in commercial regulation. Prior to his current position as Senior Director, Corporate Finance with the Securities and Futures Commission, he occupied a number of positions in the National Companies Securities Commission from its foundation in 1980, including General Counsel and head of its Enforcement Division, with a brief spell in private enterprise with Australian Second Board Consultants Pty Limited. Prior to that, he was a policy adviser in trade practices law in the Department of Business and Consumer Affairs from 1978 to 1980.

Mr Dickens was chosen for the position by a Selection Panel, following a global search of potential candidates. The Panel, appointed by the Financial Secretary and chaired by Anthony Neoh, was directed to identify a suitable successor to Mr McMahon, whose contract expires on 31 December 1996.

Mr Anthony Neoh, Chairman of the Commission, has also paid tribute to Mr McMahon:

"Mr McMahon, who has contributed so much to the development of the SFC's culture, will be able to depart with the just satisfaction of a job very well done. He has played a seminal role in building the Commission's reputation locally and internationally as a credible and effective regulator."
