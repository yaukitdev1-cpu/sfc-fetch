The Securities and Futures Commission ("SFC") today issued for public consultation a document reviewing the Leveraged Foreign Exchange Trading (LFET) regulatory system in light of experience gained during its initial phase of implementation. The LFET Ordinance, which came into effect on 1 September 1994, was enacted to regulate the retail end of the leveraged foreign exchange trading industry in Hong Kong, which had hitherto been the subject of numerous complaints of fraud and
abusive trading practices.

The review concluded that the LFET regulatory framework has brought a much-needed measure of discipline to the industry as well as greater protection to investors against the previous abusive practices.

However, in the 18 months since the implementation of the Ordinance, the industry has also experienced a significant contraction, both in terms of business volumes and in the number of firms. Possible reasons for this decline include the previously poor reputation of the industry following scandals in 1992/93, the impact of regulation (particularly margin and minimum capital requirements), migration of some of the business to Macau, a ban by PRC authorities on leveraged forex, and increased competition from large retail banks.

In response to these developments, the SFC's review recommended that:

- the initial capital requirements of $30 million in paid-up capital for licensed traders not be changed in order to act as a barrier to entry against "fly by nights" and to ensure that traders are adequately capitalized;
- the minimum liquid capital requirement of $25 million for licensed traders be reduced to $15 million with a view to further reductions if necessary; and retaining the maximum aggregate gross position of 60 times liquid capital to cap the traders' risk-taking ability.
- the SFC be empowered to amend the minimum margin requirements in respect of specific institutions which have introduced a "no over-loss" rule into their standard client agreements and which can demonstrate their ability to ensure compliance with the rule. (Under a "no over-loss" rule, clients would only be exposed to the extent of their deposits with traders). In addition, the SFC would be empowered to waive the current requirement banning licensed traders from extending credit for margin deposits of high net worth clients provided that the licensed trader applying for the waiver can demonstrate that it has clear rules to define high net worth clients and adequate controls systems to assess clients' creditworthiness;
- the rules for segregated trust accounts be relaxed to enable traders to lay off client positions with institutions approved by the SFC using money on deposit with traders provided that the protections afforded clients under the current segregation of client asset provisions in the Ordinance are preserved.
- licensed traders be required to take out fidelity insurance in an amount which provides protection commensurate with their liabilities.
