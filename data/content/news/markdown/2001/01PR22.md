As announced by the Financial Secretary in his Budget Speech, the Securities and Futures Commission ("the Commission") has finalized proposals for new investor compensation arrangements for the securities and futures markets in Hong Kong. The Commission will be issuing a consultation paper on the subject today. 

The principle features of the proposed new arrangements are:

- To set a per investor compensation limit to provide certainty with regards to the level of compensation, which would initially be set at $150,000;
- To provide coverage for the following groups: Exchange participants - All exchange participants of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Ltd ("SEHK") and the Hong Kong Futures Exchange Ltd ("HKFE") trading in Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Ltd ("HKEx") products;
- Non-exchange participants who are licensed securities dealers, licensed commodity dealers, licensed margin financiers and exempt dealers (authorized institutions under the Banking Ordinance) trading in HKEx products;
- Formation of a new single compensation fund ("new Fund") to replace the existing Unified Exchange Compensation Fund, the Commodity Exchange Compensation Fund and the Dealers' Deposit Schemes for non-exchange participants;
- Establishment of an independent Investor Compensation Company ("ICC") that would be recognised and regulated by the Commission, to manage the new Fund;
- The ICC will determine the validity of any claims for compensation;
- Funding would be derived from a transfer of assets from the existing compensation funds (after retaining an amount required to pay prior claims made), plus additional funds from an increase of 0.002% in the levy on transactions executed on SEHK. The existing transaction levy on the transactions on SEHK will be increased from 0.01% to 0.012% so that the additional 0.002% of the levy would eventually go to the new Fund when it is formed. The existing $0.5 levy for each side of a contract executed on HKFE would continue unchanged.
In formulating these proposed arrangements, the Commission has worked with two external consultants, having taken into consideration public comments arising from the consultation paper released in 1998 and intervening market developments, such as the demutualization of the exchanges and clearing houses. 

The Commission believes that the new arrangements will further enhance the confidence of retail investors trading in Hong Kong's securities and futures markets.
