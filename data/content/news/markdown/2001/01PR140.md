The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today published its second Quarterly Report, covering the period 1 July to 30 September 2001. 

Andrew Sheng, SFC Chairman, reported that the SFC's half-yearly unaudited operating results recorded a deficit of $18 million, 25% lower than the approved budgeted half-yearly deficit of $24.1 million. Revenue was sharply reduced as the thin trading turnover lowered the SFC's transaction levy income. Other incomes also decreased significantly. Expenditure for the six months ended 30 September 2001 was higher than the same period last year, largely because of the increased headcount to cope with additional regulatory functions transferred from the Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx) and the increased spending on external relations. Total reserves at the end of September 2001 amounted to $660.2 million.

Mr Sheng noted that the SFC's financial outlook depended very much on market turnover and fee income. Given the low turnover in the first half-year of 2001/2002, it is expected that the deficit for the year will be significantly higher than the $52.9 million as projected in the approved budget, unless there is significant improvement during the rest of the financial year. The SFC has already implemented a wage and hiring freeze.

Despite the economic downturn, the SFC remained vigilant to ensure market integrity and efficiency. The SFC continued to work with the markets on a series of market reforms and de-regulation exercises. Major achievements during the quarter included:

- continuing the clause-by-clause review of the Securities and Futures Bill with the Administration and the Bills Committee;
- completion of three consultations on draft rules and codes;
- improving the quality of listings by working with the Stock Exchange to amend the Listing Rules of the Growth Enterprise Market;
- approving rules changes that would facilitate the launch of international stock futures and options by the HKEx;
- taking disciplinary actions against 20 SFC registrants and prosecuting 14 parties for market malpractice;
- completing a Retail Investor Survey to plan future investor education initiatives;
- assisting overseas regulators to crack down on an international "boiler room" fraud, where operators use temporary offices to sell fraudulent products.
