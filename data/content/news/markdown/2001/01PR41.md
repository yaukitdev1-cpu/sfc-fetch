The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today released for public consultation the draft Securities and Futures (Client Securities) Rules and the draft Securities and Futures (Client Money) Rules . Both are to be made under the Securities and Futures Bill (Bill) when enacted. The Bill is currently being considered by a Legislative Council Bills Committee. 

Unlike the existing legislative framework, the Bill does not contain any detailed requirements in relation to client assets received or held by intermediaries (meaning licensed corporations and exempt persons, the latter being authorized financial institutions). It merely provides the SFC with the necessary rule-making power to prescribe detailed and technical requirements in subsidiary legislation. This approach is consistent with modern securities legislation in order that regulators can respond efficiently to changing market conditions and practices.

The industry has expressed concern that the SFC may make rules without any public consultation breach of which would be punishable by substantial fines and imprisonment. However, any rules made by the SFC will be subject to negative vetting by the Legislative Council. In order to augment the transparency of this process and alleviate any remaining concern, the SFC now releases the draft Rules for public consultation.

In the formulation of these draft Rules, the SFC has already consulted selected brokerage firms and, where applicable, held discussions with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and its working group of representatives from the banking industry.

In essence, the draft Client Securities Rules prescribe how an intermediary or its associated entity (essentially a nominee company it operates or one within its group of companies) should deal with securities deposited by its client in Hong Kong, whether in safe custody or as collateral. The draft Client Money Rules, on the other hand, do not apply to exempt persons or associated entities which are authorized financial institutions. This is because it is not appropriate for such institutions to segregate client money from the other deposits which they maintain in the course of their banking business.

The SFC has used the new FinNet communication network to send copies of the consultation document to registered dealers that have lodged their Financial Resources Rules returns electronically with the SFC via FinNet. In addition, copies of the consultation document are available free of charge at the SFC's office and can also be found on the SFC's Internet website at http://www.hksfc.org.hk.
