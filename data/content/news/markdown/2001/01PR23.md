The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) welcomes the numerous initiatives announced by the Financial Secretary to boost the securities industry. It supports the reduction of stamp duty on stock transactions from 0.225% to 0.2% per round transaction announced by the Financial Secretary Mr Donald Tsang in his Budget Speech today. The SFC considers that the proposal would enhance the competitiveness of the Hong Kong securities market.

The Commission will be consulting the public on proposals to establish a new Investor Compensation Fund. As proposed by the Financial Secretary, there will be a small increase in the transaction levy on stock transactions at 0.002% to bring the Fund to a size of $1 billion. SFC Chairman Mr Andrew Sheng says: "The new compensation arrangements will increase investor confidence in Hong Kong's markets. The small increase in the levy will help build up the reserves of the Investor Compensation Fund quickly, whilst the impact on the market is minimal." Mr Sheng also notes that the reduction in stamp duty on stock transactions would more than offset the impact of the levy.

The Steering Committee on the Enhancement of the Financial Infrastructure (SCEFI) has been tasked by the Financial Secretary to draw up a detailed roadmap and timetable to implement the longer-term measures in upgrading the financial infrastructure. As Chairman of SCEFI, Mr Sheng reports that the Committee is working hard on ensuring that Hong Kong can have straight-through processing, scripless trading and single clearing arrangements within the next two years. The Securities and Derivatives Network, SDNet, has been successfully launched. Its first application, the electronic filing of Financial Resources Rules returns, was well received with about 40% of the returns now submitted electronically. The SFC plans to introduce e-submission of applications under Takeovers Codes, Companies Ordinances and Securities Disclosure of Interests Exemption in the second quarter of 2001.

Mr Sheng joins the Financial Secretary in expressing the hope that the Securities and Futures Bill would be enacted in the current legislative session. He says that the Bill is important in maintaining Hong Kong's status as an international financial centre. In addition, the SFC fully supports the Government's efforts in strengthening Hong Kong's corporate governance regime. To play its part, the SFC is conducting an overall review of the Codes on Takeovers and Mergers and Share Repurchases and hopes to release the review results next month. The SFC is also working closely with the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong on the review of the Listing Rules and to strengthen the regulatory regime and legislation for disclosure of market information.

The SFC also supports the Government's proposal to enable employers to bring in Mainland professionals in the IT and financial services disciplines. Mr Sheng says: "As an international financial centre and main fund raising centre for the Mainland, we need expertise from all over the world. The Mainland has a large pool of human talent that is now increasingly plugged into global markets and understand Mainland financial markets. Bringing in such talent would create new business opportunities as well as more jobs. "
