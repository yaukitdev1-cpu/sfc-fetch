Offline Trading Through Banks and Brokerages Remains Hong Kong's Favourite Trading Mode


 

Though online trading has seen encouraging growth in other key financial markets, dealing offline through banks and brokerages has remained the most popular trading mode among retail investors in Hong Kong, according to the Retail Investor Survey released by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC).

The ***Retail Investor Survey***, commissioned by the SFC, studies the profile and investment behaviour of retail investors, as well as their education needs and views on the regulatory functions performed by the SFC. A total of 1,004 retail investors were interviewed over the telephone. A similar survey was last conducted and released by the SFC in 1999 albeit sample sizes for the two surveys differed.

The Survey indicates that online trading has not picked up despite widespread use of the Internet in the territory. Only 10% of the respondents, usually younger investors with higher education level, have ever traded online. Online trading was first introduced in the territory in 1997.

The Survey also suggests that investment pattern of retail investors has remained consistent over the years. Equities continue to dominate the portfolios of Hong Kong's retail investors while funds have slowly grown in popularity. Hong Kong stocks (22.5%) were ranked the most popular investment products, followed by funds (9%) and derivatives traded on exchanges in Hong Kong (3.4%).

Retail investors have clearly moved on and want more than just stock tips. 78% wanted more information about investors' rights and responsibilities. 76% and 69% would like to know more about market malpractice and investment risks respectively.

Many respondents have indicated that certain regulatory functions should be strengthened. 45% of them said that the standards of information disclosure by listed companies should be improved. 14% and 12% supported strengthening of prohibition against insider dealings and supervision of brokerage firms respectively.

Mr Andrew Sheng, Chairman of the SFC said: "The SFC recognizes the importance of listening to the investors and understanding their areas of concerns. The Survey helps us gauge the effectiveness of our investor protection initiatives and provides a benchmark against which the performance of the SFC is measured.

The SFC will continue to work with the Stock Exchange to ensure prompt and proper information disclosure to the public and to bring the Listing Rules in line with international standards. We will also continue to watch for unusual market movements and closely monitor intermediaries.

We firmly believe that investor education is the first important step to investor protection. Information collected in this survey will facilitate us in drawing up a strategic plan for forthcoming investor education programmes. We will also conduct similar surveys in future in order to keep ourselves up-to-date of investors' education needs."

- 46% of retail investors traded only through banks, 41% only through brokerage firms, and 7% used both. Investors with more trading experience, higher income or education levels, and those who traded more frequently tended to use brokerage firms.
- Only 10% of retail investors had ever traded online. Younger investors, those with higher levels of education or income, and those who traded more frequently tended to use on-line investing services more.
- 64% of retail investors considered their understanding of the markets, investment risks, and investors' rights and responsibilities insufficient. Over two-thirds of retail investors wanted more detailed information on a broad range of subjects.


 

- The majority of retail investors were aware of SFC's education programmes and materials delivered through mass media (56% to 71%) and considered them helpful.
 



- 36% of retail investors considered SFC's overall regulatory work effective, 35% rated it average, while 22% considered it ineffective. 62% of retail investors said certain regulatory functions should be strengthened.
