The SFC announces the following changes to the mandatory offer provisions of the Takeovers Code effective today, Friday, 19 October 2001:

 

- Reduction of the trigger threshold from 35% to 30%.
- Reduction of the creeper from 5% to 2% in any 12 month period.
- The 2% creeper will apply to shareholders with holdings of between 30% and 50%, subject to transitional provisions.
- Transitional provisions for shareholders with existing holdings of 30% or more but less than 35% to be in place for 10 years.
 

Background

1. On 10 April 2001, the SFC issued a Consultation Paper on a review of the Codes on Takeovers and Mergers and Share Repurchases ("Codes"). The Consultation Paper invited the public to comment on a number of proposed changes to the Codes. The consultation period ended on 15 June 2001. A copy of the Consultation Paper is available on the SFC's website at [www.hksfc.org.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/consult/takeovers%20consultation.pdf).
2. The proposed changes included reductions of the trigger and creeper.


**Trigger** 

3. The "trigger" is the level at which a mandatory general offer is required under Rule 26 of the Takeovers Code. It is intended to represent the level at which effective control of a company would normally pass. Any person or group acting in concert that acquires more than the trigger percentage of a company must make a mandatory offer to all other shareholders in cash at the highest price paid by him or them for shares in that company in the previous 6 months.
4. The trigger has been at 35% since the Takeovers Code was introduced in 1981. The level has been reviewed from time to time and after a consultation paper was issued in September 1999 a number of submissions suggested that the trigger should be reduced.
5. The trigger level is 30% in the PRC and in London. In Singapore it is currently proposed that the level should be raised from 25% to 30%.
6. In line with Hong Kong's position as a leading global financial centre and as a bridge between the PRC and the international marketplace, the SFC and the Takeovers Panel believe there is good justification for changing the trigger to 30%.


**Creeper** 

7. The "creeper" is the percentage by which holders of between the trigger percentage and 50% can increase their holding without being required to make a mandatory offer under Rule 26.
8. The creeper has been at 5% in any 12 month period since 1981. It has been reviewed from time to time. In 1991 it was proposed that this should be changed to 5% in any 24 month period but this was not supported by the market.
9. The creeper was abolished in London in 1998, having been 2% in 1976 and then 1% since 1993. In Singapore the creeper is 3% in 12 months and it is proposed that this should be reduced to 1% in 6 months.
10. For the reasons set out in paragraph 6 above the SFC and the Panel think there is good justification for changing the creeper to 2% in any 12 month period.
11. These changes to the trigger and creeper were supported by a majority of submissions from the public in response to the April 2001 Consultation Paper.


**Transitional Provisions** 

12. In order to ensure as far as possible that all shareholders are neither worse off nor better off as a result of these changes it is necessary to include transitional provisions. In relation to the trigger these apply to those shareholders who alone or with concert parties own between 30% and 34.9% of a company at the time these new Rules are implemented.
13. For these shareholders, for as long as they continue to own between 30% and 34.9%, the old trigger of 35% will apply. If the holding falls below 30%, the new trigger of 30% will then apply.
14. For these shareholders, for as long as they continue to own between 30% and 34.9%, the new 2% creeper will not apply. For example, a shareholder owning 31% today will be able to buy and sell shares within the range of 30% and 34.9% without having to make a mandatory offer. If the holding reaches 35%, he will have to make a mandatory offer.
15. These provisions will last for 10 years from today. At the end of that time the 2% creeper will apply and it will not be necessary for the shareholder to sell any shares to take his holding below 30% to avoid a mandatory offer obligation. In the example above the 31% holder after 10 years will be able to buy up to 2% in any 12 month period and will not be forced to sell any shares.
16. Similar provisions will apply to any person or concert parties who owns shares, convertibles, warrants, options or subscription rights that together could result in their owning between 30% and 34.9%.
17. In relation to the creeper, for 12 months from today anyone who holds between 35% and 50% of a company and is about to acquire shares must look at 2 tests. Firstly, will the shares he is about to acquire, when added to any shares he has acquired since today, exceed 2%? Secondly, will the shares he is about to acquire, when added to shares he has acquired in the last 12 months, exceed 5%? If the answer is yes in either case, then he would exceed the creeper if he acquired the shares and he would have to make a mandatory offer. After 18 October 2002 the only test will be whether the total of shares acquired in the previous 12 months is more than 2%. 

For example, assuming there is no other acquisition or disposal of shares, if a person has acquired 1% of the shares in a company 3 months before today, he will be restricted from acquiring more than 2% in the 12 month period after today. Alternatively if a person has acquired 4% of the shares in a company 3 months before today, he will be restricted from acquiring more than 1% of the company's shares within 9 months following today. After that he will be subject to the 2% limit.

**Immediate effect** 

18. These changes come into effect today, Friday, 19 October 2001. This means that shareholders who currently own less than 30% will not be able to gain an unfair advantage by acquiring shares to take their holding above 30% after the announcement but before a subsequent implementation date; such shareholders would otherwise be able to make use of the flexibility afforded by the transitional provisions for shareholders with existing holdings of between 30% and 50% without incurring a mandatory offer obligation when they pass through 30%. This is consistent with the objective of ensuring as far as possible that all shareholders are neither worse off nor better off as a result of the changes.


**Register** 

19. Shareholders who already own, themselves or with concert parties, between 30% and 34.9% of a company today are asked to register their holdings with the Executive. Similarly holders of shares, convertibles, warrants, options and subscription rights that together could result in their holding between 30% and 34.9% are asked to register. When these holdings fall below 30% they are asked to notify the Executive. The register will be posted on the SFC website.


**Other changes to the Codes** 

20. Other changes to the Codes proposed in the Consultation Paper and amended in response to public submissions are still under review and will be announced as soon as possible.
