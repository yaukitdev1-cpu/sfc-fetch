The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has today launched a new publicity campaign, which includes TV and radio Announcements in the Public Interest (APIs) and a bilingual poster. The slogan of the campaign is "Get the Facts Before You Invest".

This campaign is part of the SFC's ongoing efforts to convey to the public that it is very important for investors to learn about the markets and the risks of investing, as well as their rights and responsibilities as investors.

The TV API, which will be screened in English, Cantonese and Putonghua, uses the analogy that learning is a life long process. It features scenes of a baby learning to walk and talk, a child learning to ask questions and a young person learning to shop and to choose a partner. The TV API conveys the message that learning is a lifelong process and investors need to learn about investing by equipping themselves with the facts. Investors must understand the risks and learn to make informed investment decisions by ascertaining the key facts regarding proposed investments, their intermediaries and the markets.

To help investors to learn more about investing, the SFC has published free booklets to explain the basics of investing and how investors should protect themselves and also operates the Electronic Investor Resources Centre (eIRC), located at http://www.hkeirc.org.

Screening of the API commences today (21 May 2001) on TVB, ATV, Cable TV and Phoenix Satellite TV.

Along the same creative lines, the radio API uses witty examples involving cooking, choosing diamonds and playing Chinese chess to remind the public that no one can become an expert in any field, including investing, unless they have put effort into learning about that field. The radio API also starts airing today, in Cantonese and Putonghua, on local radio stations.

Supporting the radio and TV APIs is a bilingual poster, which will be displayed in Government's poster boxes, on public notice boards of universities, public and private property developments and public transportation systems.
