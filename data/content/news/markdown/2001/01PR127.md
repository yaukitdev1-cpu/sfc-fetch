The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) is pleased to announce the removal of the minimum investment requirements for Hong Kong dollar money market funds. 

The move is part of the SFC's ongoing efforts to facilitate market development and increase choices for investors. The relaxation gives greater flexibility to the market when selling money market products to retail investors.

The minimum investment requirements were introduced in the 1980s for authorised funds investing in Hong Kong dollar denominated instruments. Chapter 8.2(i) of the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds states that the minimum level of subscription by each investor in those funds must be HK$50,000. Furthermore, if an investor's holding falls below HK$50,000 due to partial redemption, the management company must require the investor to redeem his units or shares in full. Chapter 8.2(i) is now deleted.

Mr Andrew Procter, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said the minimum subscription requirements had been put in place originally at the suggestion of the Government due to concern about the impact of money market funds on retail banking deposits in Hong Kong. With the changes in the banking sector in recent years, the requirements are no longer considered necessary. Investor protection would not be affected by the change.

"However, investors are reminded that investing in a money market fund is not the same as placing monies on deposits with a bank. The management company has no obligation to redeem units or shares at the offer value,'' he said.

It should be noted that fund management companies might still impose minimum subscription levels for their funds for business reasons. Investors should read the offering documents for information and make sure the products are suitable for them before making an investment decision.
