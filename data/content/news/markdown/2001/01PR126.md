The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today released for public consultation the[ draft Securities and Futures (Unsolicited Calls - Exclusion) Rules ("draft Rules")](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/consultation/openFile?refNo=01CP11). These are to be made under the Securities and Futures Bill ("Bill") when enacted. The Bill is currently being considered by a Legislative Council Bills Committee.

Unlike the existing legislative framework which essentially limits "call" to a visit in person and a communication by telephone, clause 169 of the Bill defines "call" to cover communication by any means, in anticipation of any future technological development that would facilitate pressuring a person into investing. At the same time, the Bill also gives the SFC the rule-making power to carve out calls not expected to exert unacceptable pressure.

This approach is consistent with modern securities legislation in order that regulators can respond efficiently to changing market conditions and practices.

While there are controls already built into the legislative system whereby any rules made by the Commission must be subject to negative vetting by the Legislative Council, the Commission now releases the draft Rules for public consultation.

In the formulation of these draft Rules, the SFC has already consulted selected brokerage and lawyer firms.

In essence, the draft Rules exclude permissible communications from the application of clause 169 of the Bill and define "a permissible communication" as a communication not made in the course of any visit in person, a telephone conversation or any other interactive dialogue in the course of which statements and responses are exchanged immediately. The Rules also give various examples to indicate when a communication is regarded as a permissible communication.

The SFC has used the FinNet communication network to send copies of the consultation document to registered dealers that have lodged their Financial Resources Rules returns electronically with the SFC via FinNet. In addition, copies of the consultation document are available free of charge at the SFC's office and can also be found on the SFC's Internet website at http: // www.hksfc.org.hk.
