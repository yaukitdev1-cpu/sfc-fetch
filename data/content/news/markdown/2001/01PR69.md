On 23 April 2001, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) took part in the second International Internet Surf Day arranged by IOSCO, of which it is a member. Participating members throughout the world simultaneously surfed the Internet to identify possible fraudulent or abusive activities relating to the securities and futures markets.

This second successful surf day again demonstrates the resolve of regulators to cooperate in the monitoring of the Internet to identify and eradicate illegal activities.

Although the Internet has been a valuable technological development for the financial markets, it has also provided a relatively inexpensive and efficient means to carry out potentially illegal activities across borders. This is recognized by IOSCO and the surf day helps not only to increase public awareness of potential investment fraud on the Internet but also sends a clear message to wrongdoers that they cannot escape detection by the international regulatory community.

The SFC has an Internet surveillance program which involves the daily monitoring of web sites, chat-rooms and bulletin boards. The purpose of this program is to detect any activities which target Hong Kong and which may infringe the legislation that the SFC enforces. In deciding whether or not Hong Kong is a target, the SFC will consider the nature of the business activity, as well as other factors, including whether a local distribution agent is used, reference is made to Hong Kong dollars and the use of the Chinese language. If during the SFC's surveillance of the Internet, potentially illegal activities aimed at another jurisdiction are detected, information concerning these activities will normally be passed to the regulator in that jurisdiction. To date, the program has resulted in the SFC prosecuting three individuals for issuing unauthorised advertisements on the Internet and taking disciplinary action against a registered intermediary for attempting to recruit introducing agent.

Investors should always take care in investing in any scheme by understanding what the scheme entails, who is behind it and, if an intermediary is involved, that it is properly licensed or authorised in the jurisdiction where it operates. Many regulators offer investor access to their list of licensed intermediaries. In Hong Kong, the "Registry Section" on the SFC's web site ([http://www.hksfc.org.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/)) provides a full list of registrants and authorised investment products.

Furthermore, in order to help investors make more informed choices about who they deal with when trading shares, the SFC has set up a Investor Alert on its Internet web site to list names of firms which are not registered with the SFC but which, based on information available to the SFC, claim to be carrying on the business of dealing in securities or giving investment advice in Hong Kong, or holding out that they do by giving a Hong Kong address on their web sites.

If investors are considering an investment opportunity found on the Internet, they should always take care. Investors are advised to:

- do some research, get hold of an annual report, check out that the company really exists;
- be aware that what looks genuine and a good investment on the Internet may just be clever marketing;
- be on your guard if there is a promise of "privileged" information, any encouragement to "hurry" or where there is a promise of unusually high returns and low risk; and
- if in doubt, seek professional advice.
Moreover, investors should take great care when considering investing based on unconfirmed information that may be available on the Internet, in particular, through chat rooms and on bulletin boards. Any investment decision should be based on information that can be verified. 

The public are invited to report any suspicious activities they come across, whether it be identified in advertisements, web sites, chat rooms or bulletin boards, pertaining to the securities and futures market in Hong Kong. This can be done by contacting the SFC's hotline on 2840-9333, completing the complaint form on our web site or alternatively e-mailing the SFC on "investor.info@hksfc.org.hk". All such reports will be treated with the utmost confidence.
