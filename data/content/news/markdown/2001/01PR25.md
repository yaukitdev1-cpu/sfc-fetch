The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today launches a new leaflet "Take Care of Your Money", in English and Chinese, for retail investors.

This leaflet forms part of the SFC's series of investor publications, one of the initiatives in its investor education programme.

The purpose of this leaflet is to remind investors to closely monitor their own securities trading account to avoid stocks or funds in it being traded or used without their authority. Tips for investors to protect themselves against misappropriation include:

- Never allow other persons, including the account executive, to use the account for transactions other than the investors' own transactions.
- Ensure transaction documents are issued in a timely manner. Keep these documents and verify the information in them.
- Do not allow contract notes and statements of accounts to be personally delivered by the account executive. This could create an opportunity for interception and subsequent changes.
- Check directly with the management of the broker firm if transaction documents contain hand-written amendments that cannot be satisfactorily explained.
- Never make out cheques payable to the account executive, or deposit money directly into the bank account of an account executive. Always make payment directly to the broker firm. It is also unwise to give blank signed cheques to anybody.
All SFC's investor publications are available free at the SFC Office and the 12 Consumer Advice Centres of the Consumer Council. Alternatively, the public can access these publications in the Investor Education section of the SFC website at http://www.hksfc.org.hk. 

Investors are also reminded that the SFC has set up an Investor Alert sub-section within the Investor Education section on its website to warn investors about unregistered stockbrokers / financial advisers that use the facilities of Hong Kong business centres or company secretarial firms to make them appear to be operating from Hong Kong. In fact, these firms operate from foreign countries.

It is generally illegal for a firm to deal in securities in Hong Kong or to claim that it does when it is not registered with the SFC as a securities dealer. If an investor deals with an unregistered stockbroker, he will not have the full protection of the Securities Ordinance.

The Investor Alert section was launched on 2 November 2000. The list now contains the names of 10 firms who are not registered but who according to information available to the SFC, claim that they are dealing in securities in Hong Kong.
