The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has today released its 12th Annual Report.

Transparency, Accountability and Corporate Governance

As the theme of its 2000-2001 Annual Report, the SFC has chosen "Transparency, Accountability and Corporate Governance" as these are issues on which we must all focus. Raising the quality of our markets is critical to Hong Kong's continued success as an international financial centre and everyone (including listed companies, exchanges, intermediaries, investors and regulators) has a role to play in this regard.

The SFC intends to raise standards of transparency and corporate governance in the Hong Kong markets. We are working closely with HKEx to expedite disclosure and access to corporate information and are taking steps to require listed companies to release quarterly reports.

As a regulator, we believe in practising what we preach. As a first step, we will release our own unaudited quarterly results beginning this financial year. The Annual Report contains a new section on corporate governance, explaining the external and internal controls which ensure that we use our powers fairly and firmly.

The Annual Report shows that our corporate governance measures meet OECD standards where they apply to statutory bodies. In addition, we have written our financial statements in plain language and according to the latest International Accounting Standards.

Co-operation and Partnership

Over the past few years, the SFC has emphasised its commitment to co-operation and partnership with the exchanges and the intermediaries. We have continued to build on our existing relationships and we are working more closely with the market than ever before. We have full confidence that, with the co-operation of all parties, we can raise the quality of our markets and ensure Hong Kong's position as an international financial centre.

As the Financial Secretary noted during his visit to the SFC yesterday, we are now working closely with the China Securities Regulatory Commission and the Mainland to develop our markets.

Our co-operation with the market and our closer working relationships are now beginning to bear fruit. These joint efforts have contributed enormously to our work during the past year which are set out below.

In the year ahead, the SFC will continue to work closely with the market and LegCo to ensure that the Securities and Futures Bill is enacted as soon as possible. We will also work very closely with the market to implement the Steering Committee on the Enhancement of Financial Infrastructure's plans to ensure that Hong Kong can achieve straight-through processing, scripless trading and single clearing arrangements within two years.

Achievements

The Annual Report reveals that 2000-2001 was one of the SFC's busiest years in which many undertakings were completed. Major achievements included:

- introducing the Securities and Futures Bill before the Legislative Council;
- overseeing the successful merger of the exchanges and clearing houses and the subsequent listing of the new company, Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEx);
- assuming oversight of HKEx and supervision of exchange participants;
- revising and releasing the Takeovers and Share Repurchases Codes for public consultation;
- revamping our licensing policies and procedures;
- supervising the introduction of the new electronic trading systems of HKEx and the first electronic IPO; and
- linking the Securities and Derivatives Network of the securities and futures community with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's electronic network.
In particular, the SFC experienced its most successful year in terms of disciplinary actions and prosecutions. We: 

- concluded 352 investigations, 237 within 12 months (198 out of 283 investigations in 1999-2000);
- successfully prosecuted four cases of market manipulation (three in 1999-2000);
- referred four cases of suspected insider dealing to the Financial Secretary for hearing by the Insider Dealing Tribunal (one in 1999-2000);
- successfully prosecuted 38 entities for unregistered activities and breaches of investment and trading ordinances (37 in 1999-2000); and
- concluded 82 disciplinary inquiries and censured 117 registrants or concerned persons (54 inquiries with 83 registrants disciplined in 1999-2000).
Mr Andrew Sheng, the Chairman of the SFC, noted that the increasing workload has put tremendous strain on the staff of the SFC and he praised their efforts. "Our staff have worked very hard in performing their duties. They have achieved all objectives with an economical use of resources. We were able to keep our expenditure at $421 million, which was 11% below the original budget and we achieved a surplus of $73 million, compared with $42 million in 1999-2000," Mr Sheng said. 

The Road Ahead

While the three-pronged financial reforms are now largely in place, the coming year 2001-2002 will focus on implementation and reviews of strategic issues. The SFC's divisions have each set out their goals for the upcoming year in the Achievements and Outlook section of the Annual Report.
