The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today released for public consultation draft guidelines on how it will decide to impose disciplinary fines on licensed intermediaries and their management. Under the proposed Securities and Futures Bill (Bill), the SFC will have the power to fine up to $10 million or three times the profit made or loss avoided through improper conduct. However, the Bill requires the SFC to issue guidance on how it will fine. 

The draft guidelines give a list of flexible considerations that the SFC will take into account when deciding whether to fine and how to fine. The more serious the improper conduct, the greater the likelihood that the SFC will impose a fine and that the size of the fine will be larger.

According to the proposed guidelines, the SFC generally regards the improper conduct as more serious if it is intentional or reckless, damages the integrity of the market, causes loss to or imposes costs on others, or provides a benefit to the firm or individual engaged in that conduct and their related parties. More importantly, the SFC will consider all the circumstances of each individual case including the nature and seriousness of the conduct, the amount of profits accrued or loss avoided, the size, financial resources and other circumstances of the firm or the individual, and other relevant factors.

The proposed guidelines are consistent with the approach adopted in the UK and US.

The SFC aims to increase the transparency of its fining decisions by publishing its fining guidelines.

For the first time, the SFC has used the new FinNet communications network to send copies of the consultation document to all those registered firms who have been electronically lodging their Financial Resource Rules returns to the SFC via FinNet. FinNet is a secure communications network built to service the financial communities in Hong Kong. This is an excellent example of how useful the network will be to registered intermediaries not only to lodge documents but also to keep in touch with regulatory developments. The SFC encourages all registered intermediaries to register with FinNet. Copies of the consultation document will also be sent by mail to all registered firms, but the SFC hopes in time to adopt FinNet as the usual means of informing registered firms of regulatory updates.
