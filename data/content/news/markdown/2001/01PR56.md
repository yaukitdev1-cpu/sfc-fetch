The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has today released new booklets, in English and Chinese, for retail investors. The titles of the new publications are: 

- ***The A-Z of Stock Investing***
- ***Getting Started in Funds and MPF***
These booklets form part of the SFC's investor publications, one of the initiatives in its ongoing investor education programme that aims to promote informed investors who are better able to protect themselves. The SFC has time and again impressed on the public the need for investors to understand the risks of investing and the need to ascertain the key facts regarding proposed investments, their intermediaries and the markets. The SFC provides a number of free investor education publications as well as Internet resources to help investors in this regard. 

***The A-Z of Stock Investing*** explains the key facts investors should be aware of when investing in the stock market. Investors must understand that risks extend beyond the fluctuation of share prices. They need to know the correct procedure for carrying out stock transactions and have to ensure that the brokerages and account executives with whom they deal have proper licences. Equally important, investors have to be familiar with the steps that must be taken to protect themselves against malpractice.

The full 26-part series of articles covering the A-Z of Stock Investing was previously published in two local newspapers. Following positive feedback, we are publishing the new booklet, which contains 15 excerpts taken from the series. The full series is available in the "Special Topics" section at the Electronic Investor Resources Centre (located at [http://www.hkeirc.org](http://www.eirc.hk/)) which is operated by the SFC.

***Getting Started in Funds and MPF*** is a publication that we have produced jointly with the Mandatory Provident Fund Schemes Authority. Comprising two parts, the brochure clarifies the facts and fallacies of funds and fund management, and explains the parameters and procedures for MPF investments.

While the publications give useful general guidance on specific issues, it is not a substitute for seeking professional advice and investors should seek such advice, where appropriate, before making their investment decisions.

All publications are available to the public for free at the SFC's office and the 12 Consumer Advice Centres of the Consumer Council. Alternatively, the public can access these publications in the Investor Education section of the SFC website at [http://www.hksfc.org.hk](http://www.sfc.hk/).
