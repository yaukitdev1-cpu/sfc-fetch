The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has noted that a wide range of equity-linked notes (ELN) are now being sold in the retail market. To help retail investors understand the nature of ELN and the inherent high risks, the SFC today publishes a new leaflet "Invest Wisely", in English and Chinese, to explain the principles underlying the ELN and the risks of investing in these products. 

Unlike traditional investment products, an ELN is a structured product that combines a debt instrument with an option. In a bull ELN, the investor buys a note and at the same time writes a put option, believing that the underlying share price will remain stable or go up at the time of valuation. A bear ELN is the opposite, where the investor predicts that the underlying share price will remain stable or fall, and writes a call option while buying a note.

The return of an ELN is usually determined by the value of a single stock, a basket of stocks, or an equity index at a future valuation date. However, there is no guarantee that holders of the ELN will get a return and in extreme cases, they may lose their entire investment.

Aside from movements in underlying share prices, investors should also be aware of other risks and considerations associated with ELN, such as:

¡P The return payable for the ELN is determined at a specific time on the valuation date, irrespective of the fluctuations in the underlying stock price before or after that specific time.

¡P The return on investment is predetermined by the terms specified in the ELN. Even if the performance of the underlying stock exceeds the investor's expectation, the investor will not receive more than the amount specified.

¡P Unlike traditional time deposits, there is no guarantee that investors will get a return on their investment or any yield.

Investors are urged to read and understand the prospectus before investing in ELN.
