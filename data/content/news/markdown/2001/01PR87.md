Continuing regulatory reform while managing expenditure within budget




The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) today published its first-ever Quarterly Report for the period 1 April to 30 June 2001 in a bid to further improve its transparency and accountability. Andrew Sheng, SFC Chairman, said: "By reporting the details of the Commission's financial position and operations on a quarterly basis, we aim to set an example of good corporate governance in Hong Kong."

Reporting on the unaudited financial results, Mr Sheng said that the SFC had achieved a surplus of $2.2 million for the quarter, as against a budget deficit for the quarter of $15 million. The SFC's reserves as of 30 June 2001 reached $680 million. The better than expected results were largely due to underspending against budget by 13%. Comparing to the same quarter last year, revenue was lower by 4.3% while expenditure was higher by 20.8%, mainly due to the increase in headcount to take over the supervision of licensed intermediaries from the Exchanges, award of merit pay, and committed expenditure on market infrastructure, technology and a strategic review.

"While managing our expenditure in a prudent manner, we continued to strengthen the regulatory framework and facilitate market development," Mr Sheng said. Major achievements during the quarter included:

- completion of seven Consultations on Codes or regulatory changes, compared with three in the same quarter last year;
- implementing four new codes and guidelines to affirm and enhance the professional standard of intermediaries;
- working with the Bills Committee on a clause-by-clause review of the Securities and Futures Bill;
- establishing a Shareholders Group to provide advice to the Commission on issues relating to shareholders' rights;
- working with the Stock Exchange on the launch of Exchange Traded Funds;
- conducting a general review of the Code on Unit Trust and Mutual Funds to bring it in line with market development;
- taking disciplinary actions against 28 registrants for misconduct and various failures and prosecuting seven parties for market malpractices;
- referring one alleged insider dealing case to the Financial Secretary for his consideration of the appointment of an Insider Dealing Tribunal.
- launching various investor education initiatives;
- working with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Hong Kong Securities Institute to organise the "Asia and Global Finance: New Opportunities" conference in May.
Mr Sheng said that the SFC's work would increase with the challenges posed by global structural changes, as intermediaries, exchanges and clearing houses merged or formed alliances. However, he promised that the SFC would continue to control expenditure tightly. "Barring major changes, we expect the year's financial position to be within budget," Mr Sheng concluded.
