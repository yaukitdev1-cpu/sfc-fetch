The SFC [Shareholders Group](/edistributionWeb/gateway/EN/news-and-announcements/news/doc?refNo=01PR57) held its second meeting early this month. Discussion focused on the possibility of companies incorporated in overseas jurisdictions listing in the Hong Kong market and how to ensure appropriate investor protection in those cases. Members of the Group generally felt that broadening the spectrum of issuers would be positive, but also emphasized that this must be premised upon quality disclosure and stern enforcement of the relevant requirements. 

At present, Stock Exchange Listing Rules provide for listing of issuers from Hong Kong, Bermuda, Cayman Islands, and the PRC. From time to time, however, the Exchange has also received enquiries about potential listing candidates incorporated in other places. Members of the Shareholders Group believed that, in principle, our market should welcome issuers from different jurisdictions. This would add further diversity to our product/service offerings.

However, since company laws differ among jurisdictions, some of the corporate governance protection measures investors enjoy could vary significantly depending on the issuer's place of incorporation. The Group discussed this issue at length. Some of the differences in applicable laws could be addressed through requiring an issuer to make additional specific undertakings. Full disclosure of the material differences between the applicable laws of Hong Kong and the overseas jursidiction should be made readily available to investors. The Group therefore emphasized that listing of more overseas issuers (and indeed, market development in general) must be premised upon effective enforcement of the relevant requirements.

Members of the Group also made a number of suggestions on issues for further study and discussion in future meetings.
