The Securities and Futures Commission is pleased to announce that it has issued a guidance note on suspension of dealings of authorised funds to fund management companies that are approved to manage public funds in Hong Kong.

In the wake of the closure of the US and some Asian stock markets in September 2001, which resulted in the suspension of a considerable number of funds authorised by the SFC, the SFC considers it useful to provide further guidance to the industry.

The guidance note sets out the SFC's view that suspension of dealings in funds may take place only in exceptional circumstances, having regard to the interests of holders. The decision to suspend dealings rests with management of a scheme. The SFC expects that the Board of Directors or the Management Company of a scheme consider all relevant factors before making a decision to suspend dealings.

The SFC generally expects that suspension should only be considered where there are difficulties in the valuation of the assets of a scheme and where such assets are affected to a material extent. Broadly in line with the practice in a number of overseas jurisdictions, the SFC considers that an exposure of 10% of a scheme's net asset value to the affected assets may generally be regarded as material.

The SFC has also introduced simplified reporting procedures to the Commission. Currently, the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds ("the Code") states that a decision to suspend dealings must be reported to the Commission immediately and a suspension notice to holders must be submitted for prior approval before publication. To ensure timely notification to holders, the SFC will no longer require suspension notices to obtain prior approval but will accept subsequent filing of such notices.

At present, suspension notices to holders are required to be published in daily newspapers. The SFC is prepared to consider alternative means of notification, such as by electronic dissemination including posting of the notice on a website. Similar flexibility is also provided for notices relating to the resumption of trading.

To ensure that investors are properly informed of the circumstances of the suspension, the guidance note sets out the minimum content of notices.

Fund managers are also reminded to continue to accept redemption requests during the period of suspension, and that those requests should be processed immediately upon resumption of dealings.

Mr Andrew Procter, SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said, "A key underlying principle of open-ended funds is that holders may freely redeem their shares or units on any dealing day. The operators of a scheme must firmly embrace this principle when considering whether to suspend dealings."

The Commission would take appropriate actions if the interests of holders were prejudiced, he said.
