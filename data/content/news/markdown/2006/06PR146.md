The SFC has produced a new educational video entitled [“Know the Pooling Risk”](http://www.invested.hk/invested/html/EN/studio/studio_audio10.htm) to explain to investors the pooling risk associated with securities margin accounts.

In the video, Alan (played by Mr Lam Tze Chung) ponders whether to open a margin account with which he hopes to make a fortune through trading stocks on margin, rendering his dream wedding come true. He is then awakened by his fiancé (played by Ms Viann Leung) about the pooling risk of opening a margin account.

The key messages for investors are:


- When an investor opens a margin account, the broker would usually ask for a written authorisation to re-pledge the investor’s securities in the margin account. That authorisation enables the broker to “pool” and re-pledge margin clients’ securities as collateral to secure bank borrowings for the broker’s use.
- A margin client’s securities collateral may be re-pledged even if the client has not borrowed from the broker.
- If the broker cannot meet a bank’s repayment demand, the bank may liquidate the securities collateral re-pledged to it by the broker in order to discharge the broker’s indebtedness. As a result, a margin client may not be able to recover all the securities collateral deposited with his broker. This is known as “pooling risk”.
- Don’t open a margin account or sign the re-pledging authorisation if you have no intention of using such a credit facility. In such a case, it is better for you to open a cash account since cash clients’ securities are required to be segregated.
- If you need to open a margin account, remember to ask your broker about its financial condition and whether your securities will be re-pledged as collateral to secure bank borrowings.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “The SFC has taken active steps to reduce the pooling risk and the relevant law reform is expected to come into effect on 1 October 2006. However, this will not completely eliminate the pooling risk. Investors also have a key role to play in looking after their own interests. For example, investors should not sign margin agreements unless they are planning to use financing from their brokers. Also investors should withdraw surplus securities collateral from their margin accounts.”
