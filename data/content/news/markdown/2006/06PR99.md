The SFC is taking resolute actions to strengthen regulation as the Hong Kong market continues to attract global investors, SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said today.

Enhancing investors’ confidence in Mainland companies is crucial as they constitute an increasingly important part of the market. The largest 10 IPOs in Hong Kong last year were all Mainland enterprises, while H-shares accounted for 80% of equity funds raised through IPOs, Mr Wheatley said in a keynote address to the ICAEW/HKICPA Conference on China & Global Markets: Challenges for business, investors and the accountancy profession (Note 1).

He said Mainland companies sought to list in Hong Kong not only for capital, but also because of their conscious and deliberate policy to import Hong Kong standards and transform their governance and performance in the process.

“Hong Kong’s standards are internationally recognised and respected,” Mr Wheatley said. “The vast majority of companies coming to the market understand the requirements and are keen to comply.”

He added: “The Mainland economy continues to provide GDP growth of above 9%. Investors want to find a way to access that growth. This is a market that has a natural attraction for global investors but those investors want confidence as to the standards that apply; that is the role for Hong Kong and the challenge for Hong Kong’s regulators.”

The SFC, the Government and the stock exchange have over the last few years made substantial efforts to improve corporate governance of issuers, to enhance the regulatory standards of the market and to achieve effective enforcement.

In particular, Mr Wheatley highlighted several key measures:


- Gate-keeping for new listings – Quality of listings has improved following the implementation of the Dual Filing arrangement in April 2003.
- Regulation of sponsors – The stock exchange in April 2005 issued new requirements on sponsors and a guidance note on due diligence work. Last month, the SFC published details of the new regulatory regime for sponsors, and has stepped up inspection.
- Statutory backing for listing requirements – The SFC and the Government, with inputs from the stock exchange and market practitioners, are in discussion to further refine the proposals to give statutory backing to major listing rules.
- Co-operation with the Mainland authorities – The SFC will continue to work with the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) and other Mainland regulators to strengthen investigation and enforcement relating to Mainland enterprises. The SFC will liaise closely with the CSRC to understand how new powers under the PRC Securities Law may further enhance the excellent regulatory relationship between the bodies.

“We have seen and will continue to see Hong Kong acting as a channel for significant flows of international capital to Mainland companies. Those investors want and expect high levels of transparency and conduct in making those investments. We have created that structure in Hong Kong. It is, however, never a finished product – markets evolve and the regulatory structure must evolve with them. The regulatory changes being made both here in Hong Kong and in Mainland China are necessary to that evolution,” Mr Wheatley said.
