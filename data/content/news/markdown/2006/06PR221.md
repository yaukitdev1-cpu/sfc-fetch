The hedge fund management market in Hong Kong has recorded remarkable growth over the past two years, according to an SFC survey.

The number of hedge fund managers doubled from 58 to 118 in the past two years. The total hedge fund assets under management (AUM) in Hong Kong of these respondents increased by a staggering 268% to US$33.5 billion over the same two-year period.

Mr Martin Wheatley, SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, said: “The findings show that Hong Kong’s hedge fund market has grown substantially and confirm Hong Kong’s position as a leading hedge fund management centre in Asia.”

Background

In light of the increased hedge fund activities in Hong Kong, the SFC conducted its first survey of hedge funds managed by SFC licensed managers in June 2006. The managers were asked to provide information on hedge funds they currently managed together with certain comparative data for the previous two years.

The purpose of the survey is to keep the SFC abreast of the hedge fund activities carried out by SFC licensed fund managers in Hong Kong. The findings will also be considered for future policy formulation as well as monitoring the development of the hedge fund market in Hong Kong.

The survey results only represented a snapshot position of the hedge fund market and this could change rapidly as hedge funds are flexible in shifting investment strategies. It should also be noted that the survey did not cover hedge funds managed overseas but investing in Hong Kong.

The Report of the Survey on Hedge Funds Managed by SFC Licensed Managers was published today.

Key findings of the survey

*1. The hedge fund industry has been growing significantly in Hong Kong over the past few years.*


- The number of respondents dealing with hedge funds doubled from 58 as at 31 March 2004 to 118 as at 31 March 2006. The number of hedge funds managed by the respondents surged by 164% from 112 to 296 during the same period, with total AUM increasing by a remarkable 268% to US$33.5 billion.
- Hong Kong has embraced both international and local hedge fund managers. Among the top 20 hedge fund managers as at 31 March 2006 (determined by AUM), 13 of them, accounting for 72% of the AUM of the top 20, were affiliates of hedge fund managers from the US, the UK and Japan, while the remainder were local operations.

*2. A generally simple equities long/short investment strategy and relatively low leverage was adopted by hedge funds. (Note 2)*


- About one-third (34%) of the hedge funds reported by the respondents adopted a simple equities long/short strategy, followed by multi-strategies (25%) and fund of hedge funds (20%). For funds that were using multi-strategies, equities long/short predominated.
- Equities were the major asset component in the hedge funds reported, in aggregate accounting for 53% of total net asset value (NAV) of the hedge funds. Other components included bonds, other hedge funds, derivatives/structured products, etc.
- 40% of the hedge funds reported by respondents did not use any leverage as at 31 March 2006. Among those that used leverage, a majority of them had an external leverage of less than two times of the NAV.

*3. Institutional investors formed the bulk of the client base, and performance varied among hedge funds. (Note 2)*


- Most of the hedge funds reported by respondents served and/or targeted institutional investors. That hedge funds were not common among retail investors is further evidenced by the relatively smaller size of the retail hedge funds in Hong Kong (the total net asset size of the 13 retail hedge funds authorised by the SFC was US$1.15 billion as at 31 March 2006).
- 20% of the hedge funds reported by respondents had an annualised return of more than 20% over the two-year period to 31 March 2006, whereas 16% reported negative returns.

Mr Wheatley said: “The SFC recognises the important role played by hedge funds. They aid market efficiency and add liquidity. The SFC maintains a close dialogue with both local practitioners and regulators of other major financial markets and has been participating actively in the setting of international hedge fund regulation standards. Such co-operation and partnership with both international regulators and the industry is key to the healthy development of the hedge fund market in Hong Kong.”

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “We encourage the hedge funds industry to be more transparent and provide investors with timely and useful information. It is also very important for the hedge funds’ senior management and counterparties to put in place rigorous risk management measures.”

Notes to Editor:

1. Our regulatory framework

Direct regulation over hedge funds is not practical, if not impossible. This is because many of the hedge funds are domiciled overseas. However, these funds are not unregulated. Hedge funds that are using the Hong Kong platform are still subject to the law against fraud, insider dealing and market misconduct.

All hedge fund managers who operate in Hong Kong are required to be licensed regardless of whether the related hedge funds are privately or publicly offered. Once the hedge fund managers are licensed, they are subject to our ongoing supervision and surveillance.

Definition of hedge funds

There is no formal definition of the term “hedge funds”. For the purpose of this survey, funds that use alternative investment strategies, pursue absolute returns, charge performance-based fees in addition to a management fee, and have investment mandates that give managers more flexibility to shift strategies are generally regarded as hedge funds. The survey covered hedge funds that are authorised by the SFC as well as those that are not. It did not cover hedge funds that were managed overseas even they invested in Hong Kong.

Period covered

The survey primarily collected data at the snapshot of 31 March 2006. It also collected some data at the snapshots of 31 March 2004 and 2005 for comparison and trend analysis.
