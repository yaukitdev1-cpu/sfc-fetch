SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley, has called on listed companies to disclose their trading performance more frequently.

Speaking at a conference on investor relations today, Mr Wheatley said UK listed companies released such information throughout the year, while Hong Kong companies seldom released similar information other than when they released the results.

He said the stock exchange had in September 2006 clarified that the requirement for a profit forecast to be reviewed by accountants, thought to be the main disincentive for companies to disclose trading performance, was not triggered automatically. A review and public report by accountants is only necessary when a profit forecast is included in a listing document, announcement or a circular that is issued in connection with notifiable or connected transactions.

“With December year ends or half year ends approaching I would hope to see companies looking to inform investors as to their trading performance when this has deviated from the market’s expectation,” Mr Wheatley said.

Mr Wheatley also called on companies to consider if XBRL (eXtensible business reporting language) could help promptly and effectively disseminate financial information to investors (Note 1).

He said ready availability of listed company financial information was an important resource for investors. He said companies and regulators had over the last few years sought ways to make financial information readily searchable electronically, and some jurisdictions had chosen the machine-readable XBRL although support across the world was mixed.
