The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

In the 12 months from April 2005 to March 2006, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 86 listing applications. The SFC received 122 listing applications from April 2004 to March 2005. Whilst the number of listing applications has decreased, there was a noticeable increase in the number of applications with planned offer sizes exceeding $1 billion (28 such cases as compared to 21 cases for the previous 12 months).

Of the 86 listing applications, we raised comments on 45 cases, of which 11 remain active, 14 have lapsed or been withdrawn or rejected, and 20 have been listed or approved for listing.

In the three months from January to March 2006, the SFC received 13 listing applications. This compares to 14 listing applications in the three months from October to December 2005 (Note 1). Of the 13 applications, we commented on 8 cases. Our average response time was seven working days.

Disclosure deficiencies

The SFC continues to note with concern a tendency of serious deficiencies in the factual disclosures in the first draft prospectuses submitted with the initial listing applications. The quality of disclosure in the first draft prospectus reflects the sufficiency of preparation of the listing application, and the underlying due diligence by the sponsor. The nature and degree of deficiencies in some cases suggest that the sponsor might not have had sufficient understanding of the listing applicant or the sponsor might not have made appropriate enquiries into certain special events that warrant particular attention.

In one case reviewed, despite repeated queries by regulators the sponsor was unable to provide consistent and corroborated explanation on the reasons underlying the frequent changes of auditors during the track record period, or how material outstanding issues identified by the outgoing auditors have been resolved. It was also revealed during the course of vetting that certain key features in the listing applicant’s business model were omitted from the disclosures in the initial draft prospectus, suggesting a lack of understanding of the applicant’s business, and insufficient verification of facts by the sponsor before submission of the listing application.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, the SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: “Sponsors are vested with the important responsibility of ensuring complete and accurate disclosure of material information in the prospectus to enable investors to make an informed investment decision. Sponsors are expected to exercise reasonable care and professional scepticism in verifying the information in listing applications before submission. The SFC will take tough action against sponsors who failed to comply with relevant regulations applicable to them, in particular, the Code of Conduct, the Corporate Finance Adviser Code of Conduct, Practice Note 21 to the Main Board Listing Rules and Practice Note 2 to the GEM Listing Rules in relation to due diligence by sponsors.”

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
