The majority of SFC’s operations will move to a five-day week from 1 July 2006, in line with a similar move by the Government.

Starting from 1 July, Government offices will be closed on Saturdays in phases. The SFC has carefully considered the feasibility of changing to a five-day week, and has concluded that there should be no significant impact on the market if essential operations are maintained on Saturdays.

Despite the closure of the general offices, the SFC will continue to open the Investor Hotline for telephone enquiries on Saturdays, while documents delivered to the SFC on Saturdays will also be received, as is the case now.

The working hours of SFC staff during weekdays will be lengthened so that total working hours will remain unchanged (Note 1). Market practitioners may continue to contact SFC staff in emergency situations and the SFC have measures in place to ensure that there would be adequate staff to deal with such situations.

SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “As the total working hours would remain unchanged, we expect productivity to remain the same or perhaps even to improve as a result of enhanced morale. We do not expect any material impact on the investing public or market practitioners.”

“In addition, a five-day week is considered an attractive working condition as it facilitates work-life balance. It may assist in the SFC’s staff retention and recruitment,” he said.

The SFC notes that Hong Kong’s stock and futures markets are not open on Saturdays. Furthermore, a five-day week system has long been in practice among securities regulators in the US, Canada, Australia, and many parts of Europe including the UK. The Mainland also operates a five-day week.

- The 24-hour SFC Infoline (2840 9393) telephone enquiry system will continue to operate, providing: general SFC information; information on all licensing related issues; and faxed copies of licensing application and notification forms, and annual returns. Callers may also record their enquiries on licensing matters. For frequently asked questions, a list of FAQs relating to licensing issues is also available on the SFC website (www.sfc.hk).

The SFC will issue a circular to intermediaries informing them of the changes. FAQs will be posted on the SFC website.

The new arrangement will be kept under review.
