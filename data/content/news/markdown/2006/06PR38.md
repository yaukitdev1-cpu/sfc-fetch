The SFC is concerned about recently reported cases involving the misappropriation of clients’ assets by staff of broker firms. The SFC wishes to remind investors to stay vigilant to protect their own interests.

Investors should:


- review carefully the trading documents and verify any errors or irregularities noted directly with the back-office department of their broker firm;
- settle transactions by cheques crossed to the broker firm or by depositing money directly into the bank accounts specified by the firm. Investors should immediately alert their broker firm if:
- the money they have paid to the firm or the shares that should have been delivered to them are not booked into their accounts;
- the sale proceeds or dividends due are not received; or
- the cheques paid to them are not issued by the firm.

Investors should not:


- allow any third parties like broker firms’ staff to handle their trading documents such as contract notes and monthly statement of accounts. They should receive all trading documents directly from the firm;
- let anyone else to use their accounts for trading. As the account holders, investors are responsible for the transactions conducted through their accounts.

Investors are invited to visit the SFC’s InvestEd website at www.InvestEd.hk for more advice on how they can better protect themselves against losses due to misappropriation or unauthorised trading. There are both flash videos and text educational information on this topic at the “Rules and Regulations – Misconduct” subsection within the “Features & Dr Wise” section.

Furthermore, the SFC has issued a circular to all stockbrokers requiring senior management to take immediate steps to strengthen anti-fraud controls and tighten management supervision in order to protect the firms and their clients. The circular also sets out a checklist of important controls that firms should refer to when assessing their anti-fraud framework (Note 1).

The SFC also wishes to remind broker firms which offer margin financing that at a time of high market volatility, they will face higher risks relating to the ability of their margin clients to meet margin obligations.

SFC's Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, Mrs Alexa Lam, said: "The SFC is close to publishing its conclusions on regulatory measures to mitigate risks arising from brokers' margin financing business and re-pledging of client shares. In the meantime, firms must therefore be particularly vigilant about their internal controls as well as their risk management especially relating to margin financing."

In this regard, the SFC also reminds investors that they should maintain a cash account, instead of a margin account, if they do not need margin financing.
