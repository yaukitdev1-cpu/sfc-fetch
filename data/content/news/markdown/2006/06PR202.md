Nine out of 10 investors were satisfied with their Investment Advisers’ (IAs) services notably on customer services front, but wanted more suitable product recommendations from them, according to the SFC’s first Survey on Engagement of Investment Advisers (Note 1).

64% of investors sampled in this survey used banks’ investment advisory services. 25% used services of SFC-licensed investment advisers or fund houses, and 11% used investment advisory services provided by insurance agents/brokers.

The SFC has conducted the Survey to facilitate it to tackle mis-selling of complex products, which has been identified as one of the SFC’s major regulatory challenges. It aims to understand how investors choose their IAs, their experience in dealing with IAs, and level of satisfaction with the IAs’ services and recommendations.

Choosing IAs

In choosing an IA firm, investors primarily looked at reputation (79%) and service quality (66%). At the IA representative level, most investors considered the individual’s product/market knowledge (74%), willingness to understand client’s circumstances and address client’s concerns (68%), as well as their ability to explain matters clearly (62%).

Dealing with IAs

48% of investors stated that they had set their investment goals before they met their IAs. 32% set their goals based on the results of financial health analysis conducted by their IAs.

Most investors wished to learn from their IAs the expected return of products (87%), investment amount (82%) and key product features (79%).

Before buying an investment product, 80% said they had found out about the expected return and calculation method, and 79% about fees and charges and historical performance. When making investment decisions, most investors relied on their IAs’ verbal explanations/financial plans (72%) or the product’s offering documents/fact sheets (63%).

Level of Satisfaction

Overall, 88% of investors were satisfied with their IAs’ services and 63% agreed that “IA services are a good value for money”. The best-rated areas are customer service standards, such as friendliness and helpfulness (88.7% rated excellent or good); easy access to account information (81.3%); and prompt return of phone calls (80.9%).

However of concern was the fact that 44% of investors said they did not receive a written financial plan from their IAs and only 7% said that their IA disclosed commissions or rebates arising from product recommendations.

When asked what IAs should do to improve their services, more than half of investors (57%) expected their IAs to provide more suitable product recommendations.

SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “It is good to see from the Survey that investors are aware of the importance of IAs providing suitable investment advice or recommendations. While the SFC expects its licensed IAs to always put their clients’ interests first, investors should also protect their interests by finding out all they can about a product before they commit to it. This echoes our investor education theme of the year – *before you invest, ask the right questions*.”
