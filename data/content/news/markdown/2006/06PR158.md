The total asset size of the combined fund management business in Hong Kong grew 25% last year to HK$4,526 billion at the end of 2005, the latest [SFC Fund Management Activities Survey](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/speeches/public/surveys/06/fmas2005_eng.pdf) (FMAS) has found.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said: “The aggregate asset under management of our fund industry has seen another year of stellar growth. This is a testimony of the professionalism, enterprise and hard work of our fund managers and advisers. The SFC will continue to work in partnership with the industry to anchor Hong Kong’s position as an asset management hub of Asia.”

Background

The FMAS is an annual survey conducted by the SFC to collect information and data on the general state of affairs of the fund management industry in Hong Kong. It helps the SFC plan its policies and operations. The survey covers the fund management activities of two types of firms in Hong Kong, namely:

(1) corporations which are licensed by the SFC and engage in asset management and fund advisory businesses (collectively “licensed corporations”); and

(2) banks which engage in asset management and other private banking activities (collectively referred to as “registered institutions”), and are subject to the same regulatory regime (i.e. the Securities and Futures Ordinance) as the licensed corporations in respect of their fund management activities.

Main findings of FMAS 2005

(1) **A sizable growth of 25% in value**

Combined fund management business (Note 1) amounted to $4,526 billion at the end of 2005, 25% higher than the $3,618 billion in 2004 (2004: 23%). Compared to 2003, the year in which registered institutions began to participate in the survey, the combined fund management business registered a two-year growth of 54%.


Breakdown of Components in Combined Fund Management Business



(2) **Licensed corporations still the major player**
SFC licensed corporations reported $3,457 billion of asset under management and advisory business. An additional $38 billion worth of assets were managed in the form of real estate investment trusts (REITs). In aggregate, they represented 77% of the combined fund management business. Registered institutions accounted for the remaining 23%, or $1,031 billion.

(3) **63% or $2,830 billion of the assets of the non REITs fund management business ($4,488 billion) sourced from overseas**
Hong Kong continued to be a leading fund management centre in Asia, with 63% of the assets of the fund management business sourced from overseas. In terms of value, assets sourced from overseas registered a year-on-year growth of 25% (2004: 22%).

(4) **53% or $1,725 billion of the total non REITs assets under management ($3,242 billion) were managed onshore.**

The continuous growth in the fund management business demonstrated Hong Kong’s success in strengthening itself as a renowned fund management hub in Asia for investors worldwide and a unique platform for exposure to Mainland investments. Reflecting the importance of this industry, the size of Hong Kong’s combined fund management business as at end 2005 was equivalent to 55% (2004: 54%) of Hong Kong’s stock market capitalisation as at the same date.

Development of Hong Kong’s retail fund industry

During 2005, the SFC authorised 238 funds and three REITs. At the end of December 2005, there were 1,964 authorised unit trusts and mutual funds (excluding REITs) in Hong Kong, with total net asset value of $5,210 billion, up 21% from 2004.

The last quarter of 2005 saw the takeoff of the Hong Kong REIT market. To date, four REITs managed by licensed corporations in Hong Kong have been successfully launched and listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Such development could be attributed to the amendments made to the Code on REITs in June 2005 by the SFC to allow REITs with investment in overseas properties and a higher gearing ratio.

The SFC also authorised the first bond index-tracking Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) in Asia under the Asian Bond Fund (ABF) 2 project. The successful launch of the two bond ETFs in 2005 has raised the awareness level and interest of the public in bonds and increased their investment choices. It also marks an important milestone in Hong Kong’s drive for the development of a deeper bond market.

In respect of SFC-authorised retail hedge funds, the SFC revised the Hedge Fund Guidelines last year to enhance measures for safeguarding investors’ interest and the level of transparency.

Mrs Lam said: “Over the years, we have worked alongside the asset management industry to introduce new and innovative products. These have included guaranteed funds, listed exchange traded funds and index tracking bond funds, hedge funds, REITs, funds investing directly in A-shares, leveraged funds, futures funds, etc. As a securities regulator, our job is to maintain high standards which will attract investors and quality issuers which, in turn, will facilitate growth of the market.”

The report of FMAS 2005 is available at the SFC office and on its website under [“Speeches, Publications & Consultations” – “Publications” - “Surveys & Reports”](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/html/EN/speeches/public/surveys/surveys.html).

Notes to Editor:

1. “Combined fund management business” comprises fund management business and SFC-authorized real estate investment trusts management business.

“Fund management business” comprises asset management, advisory business and other private banking activities.

“Asset management” refers to the provision of services which constitutes type 9 regulated activity as defined in Schedule 5 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance, but excludes REIT management, and “assets under management” shall be construed in the same manner.

“Advisory business” refers to the provision of pure investment advisory services on funds/portfolios, which constitutes type 4 and/or type 5 regulated activity as defined in Schedule 5 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

“Other private banking activities” are defined as the provision of financial services to private banking clients other than by means of type 9 regulated activity. They include providing the service of managing clients’ portfolio of securities and/or futures contracts wholly incidental to the carrying on of type 1 and/or type 2 regulated activity.
