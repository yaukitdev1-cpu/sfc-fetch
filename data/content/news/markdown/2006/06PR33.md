Six investors have won the “My Investment Story” competition with their vivid narration and memorable lessons learnt.

The competition is jointly organised by the SFC, Radio 5 of the Radio Television Hong Kong and The Salvation Army to help investors establish a proper attitude towards investing and draw their attention to the risks associated with investing and other noteworthy issues (Note 1).

A total of 100 contestants entered the competition and shared their investment experiences. Three winners have been selected for each of the two categories: Category 1 -- investing in Hong Kong stocks; and Category 2 -- investing in warrants, SFC-regulated investment products or a portfolio of such products (Note 2).

The champion story in Category 1 is about an unsuccessful investment experience. Based merely on others’ recommendations, an investor excessively leveraged up and put all his stakes in one stock. Adverse market development resulted in a margin call which put him in a dilemma. He did not wish to realise the loss but had insufficient money to meet the margin call. His girlfriend scolded him for following the herd, lacking proper risk management and putting all the eggs in one basket. Eventually, he took her advice to cut loss and sold the stock.

The investor who won the first prize in Category 2 made a profit the first time he had traded warrants, without actually knowing how a warrant worked. Mistaking that trading warrants was simply like buying a stock with a smaller outlay, he had to pay his price as beginner’s luck ran out. He has since learnt more about warrants and invests more wisely.

At the award presentation ceremony, Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Chief Operating Officer, said: “For the first time, the SFC has used a story competition to promote investor education. We are pleased with the encouraging response.”

“All contestants recognise the importance of doing homework and understanding the characteristics of an investment product before investing in it. Their experiences echo the SFC’s investor education theme for 2006 - *Before you invest, ask the right questions*,” he added.

The 10 best stories have been adapted into Cantonese radio dramas. They will be broadcast at 11:30 am every Thursday starting from 2 March 2006 in “Programme for the Elderly” on RTHK’s Radio 5 (AM783 / FM99.4, Tseung Kwan O / FM106.8, Tuen Mun and Yuen Long / FM92.3, Tin Shui Wai), and on the RTHK website at [http://www.rthk.org.hk](http://www.rthk.org.hk). The stories will also be published in the Sing Tao Daily in each following Monday beginning 6 March.

The “Studio” section of the SFC’s InvestEd website at [www.InvestEd.hk](http://www.InvestEd.hk) will later carry the whole series of the dramas.
