Independent directors of a listed company are expected to stay with the company at a time of crisis, Mr Martin Wheatley, SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, has said.

Market expectation of directors and particularly independent non-executive directors (INEDs) had risen, Mr Wheatley today said at the Hong Kong Institute of Company Secretaries Corporate Governance Conference 2006.

He said it was generally accepted that the board of directors was the first and best line of defence against corporate fraud.

Corporate governance codes around the world emphasised the role and responsibilities of the non-executive directors, including the need for an independent board and INEDs, Mr Wheatley said.

He said the most common reason cited for a director’s resignation in Hong Kong was “personal reasons”. In two recent cases, two directors resigned citing “personal reasons” but without mentioning that one was jailed for a criminal offence and the Insider Dealing Tribunal banned the other from holding any directorship.

There also appeared to be a tendency for non-executive directors to resign just before the company announced that it might be facing financial difficulties, Mr Wheatley said.

“In times of crises independent directors should play a crucial role,” he said. “We would expect independent directors to continue to act as directors at the time of crisis.”

In circumstances where directors were prevented from fulfilling their duties and had to resign, the reasons must be fully explained to shareholders and the market, he said.

Mr Wheatley said while corporate governance reforms in Asia remained work in progress, there seemed to be improved disclosure.

In addition to directors, intermediaries such as sponsors and auditors also played a pivotal role in promoting good practices and ensuring the integrity of information of companies, he said. Regulators must guard against complacency and strengthen enforcement efforts to improve standards, with the co-operation with regulators in other jurisdictions where necessary.

“Investors must pro-actively demand for better corporate governance practices by exercising their rights at general meetings and not just vote with their feet or food,” Mr Wheatley said.
