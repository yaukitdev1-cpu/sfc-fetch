SFC Chairman, Mr Eddy Fong, has made his first visit to the China Securities Regulatory Commission in Beijing. During his two-day trip that ended yesterday (29 November), Mr Fong met CSRC Chairman, Mr Shang Fulin, as well as other senior CSRC officials.

Mr Fong’s visit was a courtesy call, following his recent appointment as Chairman of the SFC (note 1), and the two Chairmen took the opportunity to discuss matters of mutual interest. They discussed a wide range of topics including the Mainland’s state-owned shares reform and the experience and benefits of Mainland companies’ listing in Hong Kong. Mr Shang expressed that the CSRC supported Hong Kong’s status as an international financial centre, supported the listing of Mainland enterprises in Hong Kong and their making full use of both the domestic and overseas markets for development and promotion of reforms. In addition, Mr Fong gave an account of the current developments of the Hong Kong capital market and the corresponding regulatory efforts of the SFC.

During the discussion, Mr Shang and Mr Fong agreed that the existing co-operation between the CSRC and the SFC had yielded significant positive results. There is an effective communication mechanism between both Commissions and there is also secondment of personnel for exchange of experience. Both parties believed that the two regulators should continue to develop this excellent relationship.

While he was in Beijing, Mr Fong also visited Mr Zhou Xiaochuan, Governor of The People’s Bank of China and Mr Cai Esheng, Vice President of the China Banking Regulatory Commission.

Mr Fong said: “It was a great pleasure to meet Chairman Shang and our other Mainland officials in my new position as SFC Chairman. Support from the Mainland is indispensable to the strengthening of Hong Kong’s status as a financial centre. While the Mainland has abundant capital and listing resources, Hong Kong’s market development has started earlier and its systems are relatively more mature. Both markets each has its own development strengths. Therefore, it is important that the regulators maintain an active dialogue and close co-operation.”

Mr Fong was accompanied by several senior SFC executives, including Executive Directors Mrs Alexa Lam, Mr Brian Ho and Mr Keith Lui.
