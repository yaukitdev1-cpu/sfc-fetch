The SFC issues the following update on the operation of the Dual Filing arrangements under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

In the three months from October to December 2005, the SFC received, via the Stock Exchange, 14 listing applications. This compares to 36 listing applications received in the previous quarter (Note 1). Of the 14 applications, we issued comments on 8 cases. Our average response time was seven working days. In the previous quarter we commented on 17 cases of the 36 applications received.

As reported previously (Note 2), in the three months from April to June 2005 we commented on 12 out of 23 applications received. Of those 12 cases, five cases lapsed due to the expiry of the six-month application period and seven have been listed or have been approved by the Stock Exchange to be listed.

Quality of disclosure

In several cases, the SFC noted serious deficiencies in the disclosure of key factual information in the draft prospectuses submitted with the initial listing applications, which suggested that the preparation or due diligence performed was not sufficiently advanced to render the draft prospectuses ready for vetting. In one case, the section describing the industry sector in which the applicant operates barely covered one page, and descriptions of the applicant’s business were largely based on directors’ belief without much elaboration of the bases of such belief. In another case, the disclosures in the draft prospectus did not properly identify the business and operating assets of the applicant and segregate them from those of its parent, indicating that the pre-listing reorganization had not reached an advanced stage. In these cases, the information deficiencies involve key factual or historical information that should have been clarified and verified prior to the submission of the listing application. Omission of such basic information begs the question of whether the draft prospectus had been properly prepared before submission.

Mr Peter Au-Yang, SFC’s Executive Director of Corporate Finance, said: “Whilst the initial draft prospectus may be subject to revisions in the course of the listing application process, it should contain all the essential factual information, already sufficiently verified to give readers a proper understanding of the applicant’s business. Before filing a listing application, the sponsors should ensure that sufficient due diligence work has been performed on its contents to meet this test”.

The SFC issues this quarterly update as part of its efforts to ensure the transparency of the Dual Filing regime.
