Investors must be particularly alert to the possible increase in trading and volatilities of stocks around the time of the rebalancing of the Hang Seng Index (HSI), the SFC has advised in a new research published today.

HSI Services Limited today announced changes to the HSI. The August 2006 review marked the first time inclusion of one H-share company in the HSI. Earlier, HSI Services Limited also unveiled that the methodology for compiling the HSI would be changed from a full market capitalisation weighted formula to a freefloat-adjusted market capitalisation weighted formula, with a 15% cap on individual stock weightings.

The SFC research explores the possible impact of the rebalancing exercises, and highlights matters that investors need to be aware of when making investment decisions around the time of such adjustments.

According to the research, since the weightings of large cap stocks will be capped under the new methodology, the portfolios of funds tracking or otherwise associated with the HSI may need to be rebalanced by selling some large cap stocks and buying other constituent stocks. The attached charts show the weightings of some major constituent stocks before and after the Phase One adjustments. The changes mean that some stocks could experience heavy trading and price volatilities immediately before and after the rebalancing exercises. However, it is not possible to predict precisely the impact of the adjustments because many variables remain unknown, including the prevailing market situation and investor sentiment. Therefore, it is necessary for investors to be aware of possible rebalancing activities and their effects, and to take such information into account when making their investment decisions.
