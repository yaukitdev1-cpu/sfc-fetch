The SFC today presented to the Legislative Council Panel on Financial Affairs a report outlining plans to enhance the protection of client assets held by brokers.

Mr Martin Wheatley, SFC’s Chief Executive Officer, said the root problem in the three recent broker failure cases was integrity issue, where the senior management of the firms abused the trust placed in them by their clients and failed to discharge their duty of care in safekeeping their clients’ assets (Note 1).

In order to enhance investor protection, the SFC has devised a 3-pronged immediate action plan, under which the SFC will:


- continue its rigorous supervision of brokers;
- continue to encourage investors to make greater use of Investor Participant accounts (IP accounts) and take proactive measures to detect irregularities in their accounts; and
- discuss with the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants on circularisation of client accounts.

“It is essential that all relevant parties including regulators, auditors, intermediaries and the investors themselves work together to minimise fraud and misconduct. We believe our 3-pronged action plan will help facilitate this objective,” Mr Wheatley said.

In the longer term, the SFC intends to review the existing asset custody arrangements. The SFC will also work with HKEx to explore further enhancements of IP accounts, and consider tougher disciplinary sanctions imposed on firms for misconduct and deficiencies.

The SFC understands that clients of the three failed brokers are anxious to get back their securities. The SFC has urged the respective administrators to return the assets to clients as quickly as possible and keep them timely informed

“I share the investors’ concern. Our objective is to ensure there is minimum delay and cost. We are in daily close contact with the administrators,” Mr Wheatley said.

Regarding the progress of returning client assets in the case of Whole Win Securities Limited, the SFC understands that the administrator has been distributing to clients those available client assets that he is empowered by a Court Order to distribute. The administrator has indicated his intention to provide a further update to clients in the near future.

For Tiffit Securities (Hong Kong) Limited, the SFC today obtained a final order from the Court confirming the appointment of Mr Jacky Muk and Mr Eddie Middleton as its administrators. The administrators have completed a circularisation of Tiffit’s clients. The distribution process is time-consuming as all legal and technical considerations need to be properly addressed. An application to court setting out the proposed method of returning securities to clients is imminent.

Regarding Wing Yip Company Limited, the latest of the three broker failure cases, the deadline for clients to submit claims to the administrators expired on 8 September 2006. The administrators are preparing the documentation required for seeking court approval for distribution of client assets, which also may take some time.
