SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, gave a speech today at a luncheon of the Hong Kong Institute of Directors.

Mr Wheatley said recent market turnover surges showed that there was abundant liquidity in the global market. This, however, was pursuing a finite number of investible choices including stocks of good value.

He said that some recent global studies on the competitiveness of markets and corporations had found that investors attached huge importance to corporate governance when assessing the value of a stock.

The studies commended Hong Kong on its enforcement regulation, he said, but indicated that there was room for improvement on corporate governance culture, and the political and regulatory environment.

“Good corporate governance is both a concept and a practice that must be worked on by people in a company at all levels, it is the people inside a company that makes a vital difference between good and bad corporate governance. It is not about the company being able to tick the maximum number of boxes about attendance at Board meetings or disclosure of directors’ pay,” Mr Wheatley said.

In order to deliver good corporate governance standards to their investors, Mr Wheatley said directors were expected by the market and investors to be committed to ensuring the following:


- Accountability to stakeholders
- Proper checks and balances
- Communication with stakeholders

“In addition to putting in place procedures and systems for corporate governance, directors have a more challenging task, that is to ensure that the corporate governance is effectively put in place and that staff at all levels, from the top to the bottom of the company, understand the need for good corporate governance and are involved in the process,” he said.

The SFC would ensure the regulatory environment was conducive to good corporate governance. Mr Wheatley said corporate governance was also a central theme of the SFC’s regulatory work on the enhancement of standards of intermediaries.
