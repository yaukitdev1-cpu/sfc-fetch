The SFC is pleased to note that the Financial Secretary, Mr Henry Tang, has made upgrading market quality one of the four most important initiatives to enhance Hong Kong’s financial services.

SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “The SFC welcomes the Government’s confirmation of its plan to introduce legislative changes later in the year to give statutory backing to the more important listing requirements.”

The Financial Secretary said in his Budget Speech today that a Securities and Futures (Amendment) Bill would be introduced to strengthen the regulation of listed corporations and further increase local and overseas investors' confidence in Hong Kong’s securities market.

The SFC also supports the formation of a Financial Reporting Council as part of the overall effort to upgrade market quality.

The SFC also notes the Financial Secretary’s proposed reduction of the transaction levies for securities and futures trades on the exchanges.

The SFC believes that the lower transaction cost as a result would provide additional incentive for investors to participate in the market. Investors have since last December been exempt from paying investor compensation levies on stock and futures transactions. (Note 1)

Mr Wheatley said: “The SFC uses its resources only where it is necessary for the discharge of its statutory duties. The tight control of expenditure in the past years has contributed to our healthy reserve.”

“While transaction levy has been a major income source for the SFC, we are confident that the reduction will not affect the effectiveness and efficiency of our statutory regulatory functions,” he said.

Notes to Editor:

1. Under the proposal, the levy rate for securities trading will be reduced from 0.005% to 0.004%. The levy for futures/options contracts will also be reduced by 20% to $0.8 or $0.16 per leviable transaction, depending on the type of contracts. The reduction is subject to the approval from the Chief Executive in Council and the making of an amendment order.

The investor compensation levy is 0.002% on stock transactions and $0.5 ($0.1 for smaller size contracts) for each futures contract. The exemption has been effective from 19 December 2005 under a new levy collection mechanism.
