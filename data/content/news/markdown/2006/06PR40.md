About 470,000 more Hong Kong adults had invested in financial products during the previous two years but investment knowledge of retail investors needs boost, according to a new SFC survey.

Conducted in September to November 2005, the Retail Investor Survey 2005 sought to estimate retail participation in different investment products (see Table 1), measure the level of financial understanding of retail investors and solicit the public’s views on the SFC’s investor education work.

Retail participation rates

Of the 5,210 adults interviewed by telephone, 1,915 had invested in one or more investment products in the previous two years and were identified as retail investors. This represented a participation rate of 36.8%, or about 2.10 million adults, up from 29.8%, or about 1.63 million adults, in a similar survey in 2003.

Hong Kong stocks were still the most popular investments among retail investors with a participation rate of 28.3%, up from 23.8%. Managed funds (excluding MPF investments) ranked second. The participation rate nearly doubled to 18.1%.

Financial understanding of retail investors

For the first time, the Survey used seven multiple-choice and yes/no questions to test retail investors’ level of financial understanding in some issues about basic investor rights and the most common investment tools. The average score was 3.39 out of 7. The correct rate per question ranged from 40.4% in the question about equity-linked deposits to 53.2% in the question about voting rights (see Table 2).

Investor education

The Survey also reaffirmed the importance and need of investor education. Over 90% of retail and potential investors (Note 1) strongly agreed or agreed that the securities industry as a whole should do more to educate the public about how to make informed investment decisions.

Those retail and potential investors who were aware of the SFC’s education work rated the Alert List and press releases on scams, the InvestEd website (Note 2) and radio segments most useful in disseminating educational information. 73.6% of the retail and potential investors surveyed favoured television programmes as a means to receive investor education messages, followed by investor stories/articles/Q&As in newspapers and radio segments.

SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “The Survey provides valuable input to the planning of our investor education work. We are currently running educational columns in several widely distributed newspapers. Radio dramas adapted from the best 10 winning stories in the ‘My Investment Story’ Competition are being broadcast now. We are also making a new television series on warrants and alternative investments. We will further promote our InvestEd website to the public to encourage their access to this investor education portal.”

“This was our first attempt to measure retail investors’ financial understanding. The results are thought-provoking and we may broaden the scope of questions when we do a similar exercise in the future. As indicated in the survey, it is important for all parties – regulators, market operators, investment advisers, brokers and educators to each play an appropriate role in increasing the financial knowledge of our retail investors.”

The detailed report of the Retail Investor Survey 2005 is attached to the electronic version of this press release.
