Around half of all stock investors refer to financial reports when making trading decisions, according to an SFC Stock Investor Survey. By doing so, these investors might reduce their losses.

Conducted from September 2005 to February 2006, the Stock Investor Survey studies the behaviour of retail investors in stock investments such as IPOs, and the factors they considered in picking stocks and making trading decisions. The survey successfully collected the views of 500 stock investors (Note 1).

Around half of the stock investors stated that they referred to financial reports (51%) and company announcements (48.4%) when making stock trading decisions. On the other hand, nearly the same proportions of stock investors counted on stock commentators’ recommendations on TV, radio or newspapers (48.2%) and media reports (47.6%) for investment decisions.

When asked about their understanding of a listed company before buying its shares, 76% of stock investors responded that they knew about the company’s business nature, 67.2% had monitored the stock’s price trend and 65% were interested in the company’s prospects. They were less knowledgeable of the company’s management (27%) and treatment of minority shareholders (16.4%).

Trading Results

When reviewing the performance of their stock investments in a defined six-month period, 53.4% of stock investors responded that they made profits, 21.4% suffered losses, 0.2% broke even and 25% said they did not know or were not sure. While there was no statistically significant difference between the amount of profit made by stock investors who read financial reports and that made by stock investors who did not, stock investors who did not read financial reports suffered a bigger trading loss (25.2%) than those who read financial reports (16%).

SFC Chairman, Mr Martin Wheatley, said: “It is important for investors to do their homework to analyse the company’s fundamentals before committing their money. The Stock Investor Survey reveals that many stock investors rely on media reports and stock commentators’ recommendations in making trading decisions. Investors must be aware that given the constraints in airtime or column space, the assumptions behind investment research reports and recommendations are often not included. Nor have those recommendations taken into account the personal circumstances of individual investors. Investors should consider carefully whether those recommendations are suitable for them.”

Other Findings

The survey finds that 82% of stock investors had subscribed for shares in an IPO. The top three factors that they had considered were the company’s prospects (81.7% of them), current financial position (54.4%) and comments in the media (46.1%).

However, only 44.9% of IPO share subscribers had read the prospectus before handing in their applications.

Among those who did not read the prospectus, over half of them found the document too long (82.3%), too legalistic (57.1%) or considered newspapers or radio/TV programmes better sources of information about the company (52.2%).

When submitting their applications, 52.9% of IPO share subscribers usually used “white forms”, and this was mainly because they preferred holding physical scrips in their own names. 35.6% preferred “yellow forms” as the allotted shares can be directly credited to their accounts at the intermediaries. 11.5% subscribed through the Internet, primarily for convenience.

16.3% of IPO share subscribers usually sold the allotted shares on the first trading day. The main factors affecting whether to sell the allotted shares quickly were comparison between trading price and issue price (58.3%) and prevailing market sentiment (56.6%).
