The SFC today unveiled measures to increase protection of investors against misappropriation by stockbrokers.

Mrs Alexa Lam, SFC’s Executive Director of Intermediaries and Investment Products, said there were three important aspects to addressing the issue:


- Continuing rigorous supervision of brokers
- Maintaining dialogue with the accounting profession
- Educating investors on how to protect their own interests

Mrs Lam said the SFC had devoted additional resources to the supervision of brokers, and would continue to take decisive and tough actions against dishonest practitioners.

Noting the auditors’ obligation under the Securities and Futures Ordinance to review how a broker handles its client assets and report any regulatory breaches to the SFC, Mrs Lam said the SFC would also discuss with the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants measures to strengthen co-operation.

The SFC would refer suspected cases of professional negligence of auditors to the HKICPA and require auditors to conduct client circularisation in annual audits, she said.

“The SFC will remain vigilant in supervising brokers, and will continue to help investors understand how to protect their own interests,” she said.

At a media briefing, Mrs Lam explained several common tricks of brokers seeking to misappropriate client assets, as well as the warning signals that investors should look out for. [(Please see attached presentation.)](http://www.sfc.hk/sfc/doc/EN/general/general/press_release/06/06pr179_ppt.pdf)

Investors are advised to take three measures to protect their own interests:

1. Open an Investor Participant (IP) account;
2. Ensure timely receipts of the originals of monthly statements, verify the information immediately, and never allow your broker to hold them for you; and
3. Upon request from the auditors for verification of your account positions, perform checking immediately and revert to the auditors direct.

Investors are also urged to contact the SFC if they suspect any irregularities when dealing with their brokers. To learn more about how to protect their own interests, investors may visit the [SFC InvestEd website at www.InvestEd.hk](http://www.InvestEd.hk).
