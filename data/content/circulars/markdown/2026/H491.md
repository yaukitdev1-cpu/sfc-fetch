Circular to All Licensed Corporations
Implementation of prudent risk management policies and procedures

Recent inspections conducted by the Commission have revealed that some securities dealers
may be failing in their obligation to manage their risks prudently. In view of these poor
controls and recent market volatility, the Commission is concerned as to the actual or
potential financial well-being of these licensed corporations, particularly in the event of client
overtrading, a major client default, or falling prices for securities held by them against their
client receivables. If the liquidity of these licensed corporations were to be squeezed, their
ability to meet settlement obligations to the clearing house would be threatened, as also
would be the interests of their clients.
The Commission’s inspections reveal that there are some securities dealers whose risk
management policies may either be inadequate or not adequately complied with, and whose
observance of the Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities
and Futures Commission (“Code of Conduct”) and/or the Management, Supervision and
Internal Control Guidelines for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and
Futures Commission (“Internal Control Guidelines”) may be unsatisfactory.
The Commission wishes to inform licensed corporations that it will not tolerate these
types of deficiencies and that it will not hesitate to take firm regulatory action to
address them. If necessary, the Commission will impose restrictions on the manner in
which licensed corporations are permitted to conduct their business. These restrictions
might include the imposition of conditions on a licensed corporation’s licence pursuant
to Part V of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (“SFO”), or the imposition of
restrictions pursuant to Part X of the SFO, including in relation to the manner in which
a licensed corporation conducts its business or deals with property. These types of
actions by the Commission will involve public notification of the restrictions imposed by
it.
Unacceptable risk management practices are potentially many and varied, but from the
Commission’s recent observations they could include the following:
(a)

The absence of formal risk management policies and procedures.

(b)

Where such policies and procedures exist, failure to ensure that they are observed and
strictly enforced, for example, where management overrides them actively or tolerates
non-observance without proper justification.

(c)

Failure to conduct comprehensive reviews of these practices at suitable intervals or
wherever there is any significant change in its business or operations.

(d)

Imprudent credit policies, including the following:
(i)

Failure to apply a sufficient haircut to securities against which it is prepared to
extend financial accommodation, bearing in mind their liquidity and volatility in
prevailing market conditions.

(ii)

Excessive exposure to individual clients.

(iii) Excessive exposure to particular securities held against client receivables,
especially those securities with low market turnover and high volatility.
(iv) Failure to take sufficient risk mitigation measures before allowing clients to
acquire exceptionally large positions in volatile and illiquid securities.
(e)

Failure to institute effective measures to prevent clients from exceeding pre-set limits,
including intra-day exposure limits on day-traders.

(f)

Lax margin call procedures, for example, where there are no escalation procedures in
the event of a client failing to meet successive margin calls or, where there have been
successive outstanding margin calls, allowing a client to continue buying stocks on
credit.

(g)

Allowing cash clients long credit periods to settle their trades or allowing cash clients to
trade in circumstances when their accounts are significantly under-secured.

(h)

Taking on settlement obligations that significantly exceed the aggregate of a licensed
corporation’s available cash and banking facilities.

Licensed corporations are required to periodically refer to the relevant requirements in the
Code of Conduct and Internal Control Guidelines and to critically assess the appropriateness
of their risk management policies and procedures in the light of those requirements and their
own business activities from time to time. Licensed corporations must take steps to rectify
any deficiencies in their policies and procedures as soon as they are identified and must, at all
times, strictly observe them. Any deviation from credit control and margin call policies
should be subject to approval by senior management, which approval should at all times be
exercised with caution.
In particular, firms should refrain from accepting any buy order or extending any further
credit in the case of a client who has failed to meet successive margin calls. Moreover, firms
should take prompt action to collect these outstanding debts.
