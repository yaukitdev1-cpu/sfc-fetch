Actions taken by the SFC to assist brokers to
prepare for listed RMB securities dealing/clearing
The SFC has taken a series of actions to assist brokers to prepare
themselves for participating in dealing of listed RMB-denominated
securities and clearing of transactions in such securities
(collectively referred to as “listed RMB securities business”)

– SFC, in close liaison with the HKEx and HKMA, coordinated
the preparation of the HKEx and brokerage industry for listed
RMB securities business and discussed the related policies.

– SFC Organized a meeting with brokerage industry associations,
HKEx and HKMA in Aug 2010 to assist them to understand the
policies and regulatory requirements that are relevant to listed
RMB securities business, and discuss the related preparation
and operational issues.

4

Actions taken by the SFC to assist brokers to
prepare for listed RMB securities dealing/clearing
– SFC issued two circulars in Aug 2010 and Sep 2010 to remind
brokers to prepare for future launch of listed RMB-denominated
products and matters that they should pay particular attention to in
the preparation, including:

 Conduct testing of their trading and clearing systems with HKEx;
 Review the readiness of front office, back office and internet
trading systems, accounting records, trading documentation;

 open RMB bank accounts and set up RMB credit facilities as soon
as possible;

 Establish effective operating and risk management controls etc.

Actions taken by the SFC to assist brokers to
prepare for listed RMB securities dealing/clearing
– SFC issued a circular on 11 February this year to all SEHK
Participants and CCASS Participants (collectively referred to as
“Participants”) urging Participants prepared to trade or clear listed
RMB securities to participate in the end-to-end Simulation Test for
Trading/Clearing of RMB Products and Payment Pilot Run
(collectively referred to as “RMB Readiness Test”) to be held by the
HKEx in March. In the circular, SFC also reminded participants

 Should prepare themselves before conducting the RMB Readiness
Tests and thoroughly examine the readiness of their financial and
operational infrastructures and procedures as soon as possible

 Participants who fail to ensure their readiness to conduct listed
RMB securities business should refrain from dealing in listed RMB
securities or clearing transactions in such securities.

Participants should prepare themselves
Participants prepared to conduct listed RMB securities business
should

– Thoroughly review and test their readiness for conducting
listed RMB securities business as soon as possible, and
make all necessary changes and arrangements immediately;

– Participate in the RMB Readiness Test to be held by HKEx in
March and prepare themselves beforehand;

– After completing that Test, confirm whether every aspect of
themselves is ready for conducting listed RMB securities
business, and submit to the HKEx the Confirmation (Remark).
Deadline for submission of the Confirmation is 6pm, 21 March
2011 (Monday).
Remark：Please refer to Attachment IV of the circular issued by SEHK on
17/2/2011(CMD/TMO/008/11) and Attachment II of the circular issued by
HKSCC on 22/2/2011(CD/CCO/CCASS/040/2011)

Thorough review and testing help identify
hidden issues
Participants should not presume their financial and operational
infrastructures and procedures are ready for conducting listed RMB
securities business.

 Thorough review and testing, including participating in the RMB Readiness
Test to be held by HKEx in March, may enable you to identify hidden
system and operational issues for early resolution, thus reducing
operational risks. Participants should try their best to anticipate and identify
all probable scenarios, obstacles and problems that they may encounter
when conducting RMB securities business, and design, in consultation with
their IT department/system vendors and relevant internal departments,
comprehensive plans for system testing and simulation of operations. The
testing and simulation should be diligently conducted under close
supervision by management. Participants should diligently resolve issues
identified during the process, in order to ensure their operations can run
smoothly when they are applied to production in future.

 Participants should maintain sufficient audit trails of the procedures and
results of their review and testing for future checking.

10

Back-ups
Participants should, as far as possible, conduct testing in the
testing environment of their systems so as to avoid affecting the
production environment. Participants using their production system
for conducting testing must

 Ensure that all data in the production database have been
properly backed up prior to the testing and proper
preparation have been made for restoring the production
data to the production system;

 Upon completing the testing, clean up all test files and test
data in the production system and restore the production
data to the production system to ensure the system can
function smoothly when the firm resumes business.

Key considerations for self-examination
When Participants review and test their readiness, they should
refer to the relevant information posted on the HKEx Website
(Preparation for Trading and Clearing of Renminbi (RMB)
Products) and follow the relevant guidance issued by the SFC,
SEHK and HKSCC thereon (including circulars, FAQs and
checklist etc).

ATTENTION：Those guidances are general
guidelines only. Participants must consider their
own circumstances (their own system design,
infrastructures，work flows etc) and design
appropriate and comprehensive procedures for
the review and testing.

12

Key considerations for self-examination
 Open RMB bank account and secure working capital / banking
facilities (such as overdraft facility and foreign exchange line)

 Open RMB trust account with banks for segregating client money
received in RMB in accordance with the Client Money Rules

 Set up RMB Designated Bank Account at CCASS for clearing
purposes
ATTENTION: Participants wanting to participate in the RMB
Readiness Test held by HKEx in March must apply
to the HKSCC for setting up a RMB Designated
Bank Account at CCASS by 7 March 2011.

Key considerations for self-examination
 Systems and operational procedures
– Review (including thorough testing) the front office and back
office systems, including systems for securities dealing,
settlement, accounting, record keeping etc., in order to
ascertain whether they can smoothly process and record
transactions in listed RMB securities and settlement and
clearing of such transactions and properly interface with the
relevant HKEx systems

– Formulate company policies and related internal processes
and control procedures etc. for management of listed RMB
securities business

14

Key considerations for self-examination
 Risk Management
– Design appropriate risk management control measures and procedures
 FX risk
 Liquidity risk
– Prepare RMB funding / bank credit facilities for working capital or
contingency use

– Monitor and manage currency mismatch risk and establish
corresponding policy, for example, require client to make upfront
deposit if you are uncertain about his ability to settle in RMB

 Margin control / client default risks – issue margin calls and collect
adequate margin from clients on a timely basis

15

Key considerations for self-examination
 Design disclosure of material information about
transactions in listed RMB securities to clients, such as

 How to disclose information about the fixing of exchange rate
for conversion and settlement currency for clearing
transactions with clients

 How to disclose transaction details and related charges, levy
and stamp duty on trade confirmations, contract notes and
account statements*
* Details please refer to the Securities and Futures (Contract Notes, Statements
of Account and Receipts) Rules

16

Key considerations for self-examination
 Staff Training
– Provide appropriate training to front office and back office
staff to ensure that they are familiar with the policies, system
operations and workflows etc. for dealing, clearing, FX
translation, settlement, accounting etc. for RMB securities
transactions

– attention should be drawn to the differences between RMB
securities transactions and HKD-denominated securities
transactions, for example, exchange rate risk and the
possibility of clients using a different currency for settling
transactions.

17

Key considerations for self-examination
 Clients
– Design proper KYC procedures to ascertain the source of
clients’ RMB funds and ability to settle with RMB.

– Clearly explain to clients the risks associated with listed
RMB securities dealing, for instance exchange rate risk, and
other characteristics of dealing and settlement of listed RMB
securities, such as the company's policy on client settlement
default, before providing such services to the client.

– Remind clients to open RMB bank accounts for money
settlement purpose if they wish to receive payments in RMB
(such as sale proceeds and dividends distributed in RMB)
via banks.

18

Key considerations for self-examination
 Clients
– Confirm with those clients who settle through their own
custodians or Investor Participant Accounts whether their
custodians / themselves have set up RMB Designated Bank
Account at CCASS for settlement purpose. Otherwise,
alternative channel of settlement should be agreed with the
client.

19

Key considerations for self-examination
 Brokers who are not ready for conducting listed RMB
securities business should put in place measures to
prevent inadvertent trading of, or acceptance of client
trading instructions on, listed RMB securities. For example:

– Notify all staff in writing that no client trading instructions on
listed RMB securities should be accepted

– Explain to clients clearly that no services in listed RMB
securities are offered by your firm currently upon clients’
enquiries

– Closely monitor trading activities. If inadvertent transaction
in listed RMB securities is identified, contact HKEx
immediately to discuss any need for taking remedial action.

Frequently Asked Questions
 Can a personal customer exchange more than RMB 20,000
per day with brokers?

– The RMB 20,000 daily cap is a requirement set out in the
Clearing Agreement entered into between banks and the
Clearing Bank for RMB Business in Hong Kong.

– The requirement will not be relevant to brokers as they are
not a party to the Clearing Agreement.

– Brokers who intend to exchange RMB with their clients are
reminded to ensure compliance with the applicable law and
regulations concerning currency exchange and prudently
manage the risks of exchange rate fluctuation and currency
mismatch.

Remarks: The above is only an extract of the FAQs relevant to brokers. Please refer to HKEx’s website for full
version of the FAQs.

Frequently Asked Questions (Cont’d)
 Can brokers arrange credit lines with banks to support
their settlement activities?

– There is no specific restriction. It will be a commercial
decision of banks, having regard to credit and other relevant
considerations, to provide credit to non-personal customers,
including brokers.

Remarks: The above is only an extract of the FAQs relevant to brokers. Please refer to HKEx’s website for full
version of the FAQs.

Frequently Asked Questions posted on HKEx
website (Cont’d)
 Can brokers provide RMB margin finance to personal
customers?

– Brokers who provide RMB margin financing to customers should
comply with the usual regulatory requirements and base on
prudent risk management considerations.

– Brokers who intend to provide RMB margin financing to clients for
dealing in listed RMB securities should pay special attention to the
risks of currency mismatch and exchange rate fluctuations in
designing margin call systems.

– If brokers intend to borrow RMB from banks for funding their
margin financing business, they should take note of the restriction
on RMB lending by banks to personal customers under the
Clearing Agreement and HKMA’s circular of 3 September 2010 to
authorised institutions in relation to such restriction.

Remarks: The above is only an extract of the FAQs relevant to brokers. Please refer to HKEx’s website for full
version of the FAQs.

Frequently Asked Questions (Cont’d)
 Can brokers accept RMB as collateral for offering trading line?
– Brokers may accept RMB cash collateral from clients provided that
 such collateral is received and held in Hong Kong;

 proper agreement is entered into with the relevant client; and
 proper safeguard is in place to ensure timely return of the
collateral to the client in accordance with the terms of the
collateral agreement.

– If the cash collateral falls within the definition of "client money" in Part 1
Schedule 1 of the SFO and the scope of application of the Client Money
Rules as set out in section 3(1) of the Rules, the broker should comply with
the Rules in handling such client money

– Brokers accepting RMB cash collateral from clients should properly
manage the risks of currency mismatch and exchange rate fluctuations in
designing margin call system.

Remarks: The above is only an extract of the FAQs relevant to brokers. Please refer to HKEx’s website for full
version of the FAQs.

Frequently Asked Questions posted on HKEx
website (Cont’d)
 Can RMB and RMB-denominated assets listed or traded in Hong
Kong be counted as "liquid asset" under the FRR?

– The relevant FRR provision is section 18(2) of the FRR
"a licensed corporation shall not include in its liquid assets -

– (a) any asset that is an amount of a currency that is subject
to exchange control; or

– (b) any assets the proceeds of which upon realization are not
freely remittable to Hong Kong,
unless the licensed corporation reasonably believes that approval
for the remittance of such currency or proceeds to Hong Kong
can be obtained from the relevant authority within one week of
application by it for such approval.”

Remarks: The above is only an extract of the FAQs relevant to brokers. Please refer to HKEx’s website for full
version of the FAQs.
28

Frequently Asked Questions (Cont’d)
– Under the current policies and arrangements in relation to RMB
business in Hong Kong:

 RMB cash notes held by LCs in Hong Kong or RMB deposits
held by LCs in bank accounts in Hong Kong with Participating AIs
would not be considered as assets falling within section 18(2)(a)
of the FRR provided that they are not subject to any restriction on
conversion with Participating AIs in Hong Kong.

 RMB-denominated assets listed or traded in Hong Kong the
proceeds of which upon realization are payable in Hong Kong
would not be considered as assets falling within section 18(2)(b)
of the FRR provided that such proceeds are not subject to any
restriction on conversion with Participating AIs in Hong Kong.

Remarks: The above is only an extract of the FAQs relevant to brokers. Please refer to HKEx’s website for full
version of the FAQs.
