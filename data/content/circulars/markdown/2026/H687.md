27 November 2008
Letter to Management Companies of SFC-authorised Funds
Dear Sirs,
Ongoing monitoring of counterparty risks of SFC-authorised funds under your
management and risk mitigation
This letter is issued to all management companies of SFC-authorised funds, so please
forward this letter to all such management companies within your fund group for their prompt
attention.
As you are aware, the recent developments of the financial market have caused increasing
concerns and called for rigorous scrutiny of the creditworthiness of counterparties in the
industry. We trust that you have stepped up your ongoing monitoring of the SFC-authorised
funds under your management (the “Funds”, each a “Fund”) in this regard.
As part of your risk management process, on top of the counterparty risks relating to the
Funds’ investment exposure, you should also on an ongoing basis satisfy yourself as to the
continued suitability and financial standing of any appointed trustee/custodians and their
respective delegates of the Funds (collectively, the “trustees and custodians”). In this regard,
we would like to remind you of the following critical areas in particular:
Proper segregation of the funds’ assets
(a)

First and foremost, you must ensure that assets of individual funds held by the
trustees and custodians for different beneficiaries are properly segregated from each
other, i.e. the trustees and custodians have not and will not commingle assets of the
Funds with the properties of the trustees and custodians or such properties held by
the trustees and custodians for other funds or customers, so that the assets of the
Funds are fully protected and readily retrievable in the event of any fallout of any of
the trustees and custodians.

Adequate control procedures for stock lending, repo and other similar over-the-counter
transactions
(b)

Where a Fund is involved in any stock lending, repo or other similar over-the-counter
transactions, you must exercise due care and undergo due diligence in selecting the
counterparties for such transactions. You should also ensure that sufficient collateral
are received and held by the Fund in respect of these transactions, such collateral to
be quality, liquid collateral and must be maintained at adequate levels, after prudent
haircut and daily marked-to-market, to ensure that the Fund’s exposure is fully
covered at all times including any possible event of counterparty default.

Adequate and up-to-date disclosures to investors on stock lending, repo and similar over-thecounter transactions
(c)

Further to item (b) above, if a Fund may enter into any stock lending, repo or similar
over-the-counter transactions, details of such arrangements should be disclosed in
the Fund’s offering documents. At a minimum, the following information should be
provided to investors:
(i)

a statement spelling out whether all incremental incomes generated from
such transactions will be accrued to the Fund (if the incomes are to be
shared between the Fund and any operating party, details of the sharing
basis should be disclosed);

(ii)

Criteria for selecting the counterparties for such transactions in terms of
financial standing, etc;

(iii)

Form and nature of the collateral received by the Fund in respect of such
transactions (e.g. cash or liquid securities with value greater than or equal to
the value of the securities lent);

(iv)

maximum level of the Fund’s assets available for such transactions (e.g. as
a percentage of the Fund’s net asset value);

(v)

where the securities lending agent is an affiliate of your group of companies,
details of such connected party transactions (please note that the securities
lending fee should also be disclosed in the connected party transaction
section of the Fund’s annual reports).

Heightened monitoring of trustees and custodians domiciled overseas
(d)

Where the trustees and custodians are domiciled in a jurisdiction outside Hong Kong,
you should heighten your monitoring as to the continued suitability and financial
standing of such trustees and custodians, taking into account the level of regulatory
safeguards as well as the bankruptcy laws and procedures in such jurisdictions which
may or may not be comparable to that in Hong Kong or other developed jurisdictions.

(e)

You should ensure that even if in extreme market condition, the Funds’ assets held
by the trustees and custodians are readily accessible to meet demands of the
investors in redemption and will not be subject to restrictions on repatriation. If there
is in fact any restriction on repatriation, this would call into question whether the
choice of the trustees and custodians is or remains a proper and appropriate choice,
having regard to current market circumstances and heightened redemption pressures.

Effective business continuity plan
(f)

You must establish and put in place an effective business continuity plan to deal with
potential contingency situations such as the fallout of the trustees and custodians,
with a view to protecting the Funds from adverse impact resulting from any market
events.

Please note that the items set out above are by no means exhaustive. It is incumbent upon
you to properly monitor and manage the risks of the Funds and to take prompt and effective
measures to reduce and mitigate any potential counterparty, operational and other risks that
the Funds may face, including the risks associated with the trustees and custodians.
Furthermore, the same also apply in your ongoing monitoring of other service providers of
the Funds, e.g. execution brokers, whereby transactions with them may expose the Funds to
any significant counterparty risks.
You are expected to keep us informed as soon as possible if there is any untoward
development or issue concerning the trustees and custodians or other service providers that
may have a significant impact on the Funds.
Should you have any questions, please do not hesitate to contact the case officer
responsible for your fund group.
