   Circular to Licensed Corporations and Associated Entities

Anti-Money Laundering / Combating Terrorist Financing

   (1) US President’s Executive Order 13224
(2) Non-cooperative Countries and Territories
(3) United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance



     1) US President’s Executive Order 13224 (“Executive Order”)


    Further to our circular dated 30 September 2004, this is to inform you that the US Government has recently added one entity under the Executive Order.  The name of this additional entity can be found in the website of the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the US Treasury www.ustreas.gov/offices/eotffc/ofac/sanctions/terrorism.html under “Name of Entity Designated on 10-15-2004”.


    Licensed corporations and associated entities should check whether they have had transactions or dealings with the individuals and the entities named in the list.  In order to fulfil the obligations under section 12 of the United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance (the “Ordinance”), they should promptly notify the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (“JFIU”) if such reviews reveal any suspicious accounts, transactions or dealings.


    As the designated individuals and entities under the Executive Order may be updated by the US Government from time to time, licensed corporations and associated entities are reminded to browse the website of the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the US Treasury regularly for the latest information.


     2) Non-cooperative Countries and Territories

    In our circulars dated 14 December 2001 and 17 November 2003, the decision of the Financial Action Task Force on Money Laundering (“FATF”) to apply counter-measures against Nauru and Myanmar respectively was brought to your attention.  Please note that the FAFT has recently decided to withdraw counter-measures against these two countries as they have now introduced new measures to remedy anti-money laundering deficiencies.  Both countries will, however, continue to remain on the list of non-cooperative countries and territories (“NCCTs”).  The updated list of NCCTs released by FATF is as follows:


|Cook Islands                            |Indonesia                            |Myanmar                                    |
|Nauru                                   |Nigeria                              |Philippines                                |


    Licensed corporations and associated entities are advised to give special attention to business relationships and transactions with persons, including companies and financial institutions, from the above NCCTs, in accordance with Recommendation 21 Note [1]of the FATF Forty Recommendations.


    As the NCCTs list designated by the FATF and those subject to counter-measures may be updated from time to time, intermediaries and, where applicable, their associated entities are reminded to browse the FATF website www.fatf-gafi.org regularly for the latest information.


    The SFC will alert intermediaries of changes to the list and FATF’s recommended counter-measures, if any, through circulars in due course.


(3)	An updated list specifying terrorists and terrorist associates designated by the United Nations Security Council Committee was separately published under the Ordinance and in the Gazette on 21 October 2004 (G.N. 6735).  Compared with the previous list referred to in our circular dated 20 August 2004, the following names of individuals and entities have been added to the new list:


    Part C (Individuals belonging to or associated with the Al-Qaida organization)

        • Suliman AI-Buthe (Item 21)

     Part D (Entities belonging to or associated with the Al-Qaida organization)

        • AI-Haramain Foundation: Union of the Comoros (Item 26)
        • AI-Haramain Foundation : United States of America (Item 27)

      The new list can be found in the Government website www.gld.gov.hk/eng/welcome.htm under eGazette Note [2].  A copy of the list in MS Word format is attached to facilitate the update of your electronic database (Attachment).


      The SFC reminds licensed corporations and associated entities to take all necessary steps to ensure compliance with the Ordinance.  Besides reporting to the JFIU of any transactions or relationships you have or have had with the named individuals and entities, you should refrain from, amongst other things, providing or collecting, by any means, directly or indirectly, funds to the named individuals and entities pursuant to the Ordinance.  Previous circulars on this subject are available in SFC’s website www.hksfc.org.hk.

Identifiers and acronyms in the list

Identifiers without any information, or followed by the letters "na" (not available), indicate that information pertaining to that identifier is currently not available, notwithstanding the United Nations Security Council 1267 Committee's efforts to obtain it. Explanations of the identifiers used in the list are provided below.

Individuals
Name 1, Name 2, Name 3, Name 4: The names of individuals on the list are specified in the order that they would appear in a national passport. Four separate name fields have been provided for each individual on the list in order to allow for the full range of naming conventions used in those parts of the world where Arabic-based names are common. When four names are provided the naming convention is as follows: Name 1 is the individual's first name, Name 2 is the name of the individual's father, Name 3 is the name of the individual's grandfather and Name 4 is the individual's family name. In many instances, individuals included in the list are represented by less than four names. Where appropriate, the family name has been bolded, regardless of the field it occupies. There are several possible reasons why less than four names are provided for some individuals on the list, including: a) information regarding some parts of the complete name is unavailable; or b) the naming convention used in a given individual's country of origin may not employ all four name components used in other countries where Arabic-based names are common. Users should bear in mind that transliterating Arabic and other names into Latin characters can result in a number of variations. Cross referencing names with other elements of information contained in the list is therefore highly recommended.
