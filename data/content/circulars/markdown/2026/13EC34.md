Circular to Intermediaries and Other Persons Engaging in Activities concerning
Over-the-counter (“OTC”) Derivative Products or Transactions
Survey of Participants in the Hong Kong OTC Derivatives Market 2013
1.

The Securities and Futures Commission (“SFC”) and the Hong Kong Monetary
Authority (“HKMA”) jointly issued a consultation paper and a supplemental
consultation paper concerning the establishment of a regulatory regime governing
Hong Kong’s OTC derivatives market in October 2011 and July 2012 respectively.
The conclusions resulting from these consultation exercises were published in July
2012 and September 2013 respectively. Both the consultation papers and the
conclusions can be downloaded from the SFC’s website at www.sfc.hk.

2.

In accordance with the consultation conclusions, it is now intended that a regime be
established for the regulation of the OTC derivatives market in Hong Kong. To this
end, the Securities and Futures (Amendment) Bill 2013, (“Amendment Bill”) was
gazetted on 28 June 2013 and introduced into the Legislative Council on 10 July
2013. The Amendment Bill provides for the regulation of participants in the Hong
Kong OTC derivatives market by the establishment of two new types of regulated
activity under Schedule 5 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (“SFO”) – Type 11
regulated activity (dealing in OTC derivative products or advising on OTC derivative
products) (“RA 11”) and Type 12 regulated activity (providing clearing agency
services for OTC derivative transactions) (“RA 12”). In addition, the existing scope of
Type 9 regulated activity (asset management) will be expanded to cover the
management of a portfolio of OTC derivative products (“expanded RA 9”) and the
existing scope of Type 7 regulated activity (providing automated trading services) will
be expanded to cover the provision of automated trading services (“ATS”) for OTC
derivative products (“expanded RA 7”).

3.

The Amendment Bill also provides for the introduction of mandatory OTC reporting
and clearing obligations.

4.

Please refer to the Annex to this Circular for the proposed definitions of “OTC
derivative product”, the expanded RA 7, the expanded RA 9, RA 11 and RA 12, as
provided for under the Amendment Bill. The proposed transitional arrangements
applicable to those already conducting the expanded and new RAs, are also provided
for in the Amendment Bill.

5.

To gauge the number of corporate participants in the OTC derivatives market who
may be affected by the introduction of the proposed regime, the SFC has decided to
conduct a survey of the Hong Kong market. This survey is intended to assist market
participants in identifying and anticipating any changes to the SFO that might affect
them. It is also intended to assist the SFC in anticipating the implications of these
changes insofar as its resources are concerned, with the objective of better facilitating
a smooth transition from an unregulated OTC market in Hong Kong to one which is
regulated. We, therefore, request all corporations in Hong Kong that are likely to be
affected by the proposed regime (i.e. those providing ATS for, or managing portfolios
of, OTC derivative products, dealing in or advising on OTC derivative products,

The proposed new and expanded RAs
This Annex comprises three parts – Part A is a brief summary of the scope of the proposed
new and expanded regulated activities; Part B includes excerpts from the Amendment Bill,
including the proposed definitions of the new RAs and definitions which are related to the
new and expanded RAs; and Part C includes the consequential amendments to the current
definitions of existing RAs (in alphabetical order) which are provided for in the Amendment
Bill, with the amendments in question being marked up in red for ease of reference.
For a better understanding of the proposed scope of each new and expanded RA, please
refer to the relevant draft definitions as set out in Part B and Part C. In addition to this Annex,
you may also refer to the related consultation papers and conclusions, and the Amendment
Bill, for more detailed background information. It is possible that the provisions of the
Amendment Bill may undergo change prior to its enactment by the Legislative Council.

Part A – Summary of the scope of the proposed new and expanded regulated
activities
1. RA 11
The proposed RA 11 will closely resemble the existing dealing and advising definitions
contained in Schedule 5 of the SFO. It will encompass dealing in and advising on OTC
derivative products, and will be defined to mean –
(a) entering into or offering to enter into OTC derivative transactions;
(b) inducing or attempting to induce another person to enter into, or to offer to enter into,
OTC derivative transactions; or
(c) giving advice on, or issuing analyses or reports on whether, which, the time at which,
or the terms or conditions on which, OTC derivative transactions will be entered into.
The proposed RA 11 will be subject to carve-outs –
(i) to deal with overlaps between the scope of RA 11 and the scope of the existing RAs;
(ii) to preserve, as appropriate, some of the carve-outs under the existing RA definitions,
so that the introduction of RA 11 does not inadvertently remove the benefit of carveouts that currently exist and which should be retained for OTC derivative products;
and
(iii) to exclude dealing and advising activities undertaken by certain types of persons, or
in certain types of situations (e.g. activities undertaken by authorized institutions
(“AIs”) and approved money brokers (“AMBs”), within the meaning of the Banking

2. RA 12
The proposed RA 12 will include the provision of clearing and settlement services where
these are provided: (i) in respect of OTC derivative transactions, (ii) through a central
counterparty (“CCP”) (whether located in Hong Kong or elsewhere), (iii) directly as a
member of the CCP or indirectly through another person that is such a member, and (iv)
on behalf of another person. RA 12 will therefore encompass the activities of both –
(a) members of a CCP; and
(b) persons that intermediate between any such member and a counterparty to an OTC
derivative transaction, in respect of which clearing agency services are provided,
except where such members or persons are clearing their proprietary trades only.
Additionally, carve-outs will be provided to exclude - (i) the CCP itself, (ii) AIs and AMBs,
(iii) an overseas member of a local CCP (i.e. an “acceptable participant” under the
Amendment Bill), provided certain conditions are met, and (iv) any agent of a clearing
member that provides only marketing support and does not handle client monies or
assets.
3. Expanded RA 9
Currently, RA 9 only encompasses the management of portfolios of securities or futures
contracts, or of collective investment schemes authorized under section 104 of the SFO,
the underlying property of which is usually real estate. Under the Amendment Bill, the
existing RA 9 is expanded to also encompass the new component of the management of
portfolios of OTC derivative products. Unlike RAs 11 and 12 and the expanded RA7, AIs
and AMBs will not be carved out from the expanded RA 9. The existing carve-outs that
allow persons licensed or registered for RA 1 to manage portfolios of securities without
being licensed or registered for RA 9, will similarly be expanded so that persons licensed
for RA 11, AIs and AMBs may manage portfolios of OTC derivative products without
being required to be licensed or registered for the expanded RA 9 if such management is
incidental to their dealing in OTC derivative products.
4. Expanded RA 7
Currently, the definition of ATS only encompasses facilities for the trading or clearing of
securities or futures contracts. Under the Amendment Bill, the expanded RA 7 also
includes facilities for the trading or clearing of OTC derivative products. Carve-outs will
be provided to AIs and AMBs which provide ATS to facilitate the trading of OTC
derivative products where this is wholly incidental to their activities of dealing in OTC
derivative products in the ordinary course of business.
