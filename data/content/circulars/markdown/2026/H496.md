Circular to Licensed Corporations and Associated Entities
Anti-Money Laundering / Combating Terrorist Financing
Questions and Answers on Identification and Handling of
High Risk Customers and Politically Exposed Persons (“FAQs”)

The Guidance Note on Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (“Guidance
Note”) requires licensed corporations and associated entities to apply customer due diligence
(“CDD”) measures on a risk sensitive basis, in particular enhanced CDD should be adopted
for higher risk categories of customers. Licensed corporations and associated entities are
also required to have in place appropriate risk management system to determine whether a
customer is a politically exposed person (“PEP”) and perform enhanced CDD on this type of
customer on a risk-sensitive basis.
These FAQs provide guidance to licensed corporations and associated entities of the
importance on identifying high risk customers and PEPs and the ways to handle these
customers.
Licensed corporations and associated entities should take these FAQs into account when
implementing a system for ensuring compliance with the relevant requirements under the
Guidance Note and apply these FAQs having regard to their specific nature of business,
organizational structure, type of customer and transaction etc.
A copy of the FAQs is enclosed in the Appendix for your information. The e-version of the
FAQs is available for download on the SFC website at http://www.sfc.hk under the section
“Intermediaries, Licensing & Investment Products – Intermediaries Supervision Related
Matters - Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing – Frequently Asked
Questions on Guidance Note”.

6.9

Why is it important to identify politically
exposed persons (“PEPs”)?

PEPs are individuals who are or have in the past been
entrusted with prominent public functions, such as
senior politicians, senior government, judicial or
military officials, senior executives of state owned
corporations, and important political party officials.
PEPs are considered to be an increased risk for
money laundering as some PEPs have in the past
abused their public powers for illicit enrichment at
the expense of the country or state owned corporation
(e.g. through misappropriation of public funds or
receipt of bribes for their own use or benefit). The
risk is higher in relation to PEPs from countries
where corruption is widespread. Business
relationships with family members or close associates
of PEPs involve reputational risks similar to those
with PEPs themselves as they have also been known
to assist PEPs by holding funds derived from such
sources.

6.9

What enhanced CDD measures should be
applied on PEP customers?

When dealing with a PEP customer, LCs are required
to:
(a) obtain senior management approval for
establishing a business relationship with a PEP
customer;
(b) take appropriate measures to ascertain the source
of wealth and source of funds of the customer;
(c) conduct enhanced on-going monitoring of the
transactions of the PEP customer with attention
paid to any unusual transactions and transaction
patterns or inconsistent behaviour that may

6.2

Why is it important for LCs to adopt a riskbased approach in combating money
laundering (“ML”) and terrorist financing
(“TF”)?

The adoption of a risk-based approach allows LCs to
identify the degree of ML/TF risk associated with
their business activities and to focus their AML
compliance program and resources on those business
activities, customers and transactions that pose the
greatest risk of ML/TF.

What are the risk criteria that should be
taken into account by LCs in determining
ML/TF risk and in identifying high risk
customers?

LCs have to use their judgment, knowledge and
expertise to develop an appropriate risk-based
approach. There is no agreed upon set of risk
categories. However, some commonly used risk
criteria are:

(1) Country/ geographic risk (For example, where
does your customer reside? Where is your corporate
customer headquartered? Some might be located in
countries with higher risk of ML or in jurisdictions
insufficiently applying FATF standards.)
