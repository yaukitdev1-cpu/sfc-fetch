Guidance to Licensed Corporations and Registered Institutions
In relation to Derivative Products under the Code of Conduct
The SFC noted that some industry participants have implemented or intend to implement
measures in determining whether an SFC-authorized fund (“fund”) is a derivative product for the
purpose of the Code of Conduct1. Questions have been raised about the criteria to be used in
this circumstance.
After considering submissions from industry participants, the SFC provides the following
guidance to facilitate an intermediary in performing its product due diligence.
It is the obligation of an intermediary to conduct product due diligence for the purpose of the
Code of Conduct. In determining whether a fund is a derivative product, it is open to the
intermediary to take account of the classification2 assigned to it by the issuer and should also
take the action set out in this guidance.
1.

In determining whether a fund is a derivative product, an intermediary should, where
appropriate, seek written explanations from the product issuer of the rationale for a
fund’s classification provided to it.

2.

The written explanations should list the operative factors applied by the product issuer in
determining whether a fund is a derivative product. These may include the function
derivatives play in the structure of the fund3, the duration and the extent to which
derivatives are being used by the fund.

3.

The intermediary should then be in a position to, having regard to all the information
available, including explanations from the product issuer, determine whether the fund is
a derivative product.

4.

The intermediary should also request the product issuer to inform it as soon as
reasonably practicable should there be any changes in the classification made by the
product issuer of an existing fund.
