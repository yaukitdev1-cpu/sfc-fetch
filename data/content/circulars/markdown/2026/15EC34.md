Circular to all Licensed Corporations on Internet Trading
The SFC has today provided further assistance to licensed corporations (“LCs”) by launching
an internet trading self-assessment checklist (the “Checklist”) on our website
(http://www.sfc.hk/web/files/IS/forms/SFC%20Self-Assessment%20Checklist%20Eng%202015.xlsm).
This is the third circular to the LCs following our recent review of internet trading systems of
selected LCs.
Two related circulars were issued on 27 January and 26 November 2014 which (i) summarised
major design and control deficiencies that might expose the LCs and their clients to security
and integrity risks identified during the course of the review; and (ii) suggested certain controls
and procedures.
The Checklist provides guidance for LCs to conduct regular self-assessment of their internet
trading systems, network infrastructure, related policies, procedures and practices in order to
identify areas that require improvement and, where needed, enhance the same so to ensure
compliance with the relevant electronic trading requirements1 .
Given the potential impact to investors and to market integrity, LCs providing internet trading
services to clients are expected to closely monitor the integrity, reliability, security and capacity
of the internet trading systems and maintain sufficient resources to cope with any increase in
business volume transacted through their internet trading systems. LCs are also expected to
complete the Checklist as part of their regular review of their internet trading systems and
rectify deficiencies (if any) as soon as practicable. For this purpose, SFC staff may review the
internet trading systems of LCs from time to time, including the self-assessment results.

Internet Trading Self-Assessment Checklist
All licensed corporations (“LCs”) that provide internet trading services to clients are expected to
perform regular self-assessment of their internet trading systems, network infrastructure,
related policies, procedures and practices so to ensure compliance with the relevant electronic
trading requirements (please see Paragraph 18, Schedule 7 and amendments to schedule 6 of
the Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures
Commission).
The following internet trading self-assessment checklist (the “Checklist”) has been developed to
provide guidance for LCs to conduct such self-assessment and LCs providing internet trading
services to clients are expected to complete the Checklist from time to time and to rectify
deficiencies (once identified) as soon as practicable.
In particular, senior management of a LC should
(a) Appoint an appropriate responsible officer for performing this self-assessment and reporting
his/her overall conclusions with recommended actions to [the Board] [senior management]
if any to rectify deficiencies noted with an indication of urgency;
(b) Develop and agree on a concrete action plan for rectifying any deficiencies identified (with
envisaged timeframe); and
(c) Be able to demonstrate the self-assessment performed, conclusions drawn as well as
follow-up actions taken upon request by SFC staff.
Given the technical nature of parts of the subject matter, not all responsible officers so
appointed may have the necessary information technology (“IT”) expertise. In these
circumstances, the responsible officer may rely on another person(s) (such as the head of the
IT department of the LC or its group, or external contracted vendors that have developed the
internet trading systems and/or are responsible for maintaining the systems) to the extent that
the responsible officer is reasonably satisfied with the competence of such person(s).
It should be emphasized that the contents of the Checklist are not intended to prescribe some
minimum standards that are universally applicable that will guarantee the adequacy of a LC’s
internet trading systems, etc. Depending on the nature and scope of internet trading services
provided, senior management of a LC can develop and implement systems, controls and
procedures that are commensurate with the business needs of the LC. As such, different
standards may justifiably be set for different controls and procedures.
