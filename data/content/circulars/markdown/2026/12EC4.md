Circular to All Intermediaries
Beware of “Email Scam”
All intermediaries are advised to be on alert of fraudulent email instructions.
Email Scams
Recently, the SFC and the Commercial Crime Bureau (“CCB”) have received reports of email
scams from some brokerage firms in Hong Kong. It was suspected that fraudsters had
compromised and hijacked the email accounts of individual clients of the firms and sent
fraudulent orders and fund transfer instructions to the firms. The scams were discovered when
the genuine clients enquired with the firms about the transactions in question.
Guarding against email scams
The above incidents highlight the importance of safeguarding against the risks associated
with accepting email instructions, particularly, that client’s email account might have been
compromised and used by fraudsters to send fraudulent instructions.
Based on our experience, fraudsters gain unauthorized access to the victim’s email account to
find out crucial information concerning the financial situation and business relationships the
victim currently has. The fraudsters will forge the email address of the victim or send emails
to both the victim and his/her trading partner(s), informing the other of a change in the email
address for future correspondence and transacting bank account details to be used for
business transactions. The fraudsters may then send a fraudulent email instructing the
victim’s trading partner to execute transactions or transfer funds to a third-party account.
These instructions may be provided with fraudulent letters of authorization, also emailed from
the fraudulent email account.
Intermediaries are reminded to ensure that their policies and procedures could adequately
address the risks associated with each method that the intermediary allows for transmission of
client instructions. In addition, they should have procedures in place to verify the authenticity
of suspicious email instructions, e.g. by confirming the instruction with the client via reliable
alternative channel. They should handle with extra care email instructions to make third
party payments. Such requests should be supported by original written authorizations duly
signed by the client and subject to written approval by management of the intermediary.
Intermediaries may consider asking clients to pre-register third-party accounts to which
payments may be made and take additional procedures to verify the instruction that request
for payments to unregistered third-party accounts.
It is recommended that intermediaries should establish mechanism to identify and respond to
“red flags”, such as orders or payment requests that are inconsistent with the client’s normal
practice or requests for immediate payments. They should also raise the awareness of their
staff to suspicious requests and provide training on applicable regulatory requirements,
policies and procedures and perform review to ensure compliance by the staff. Furthermore,

Intermediaries are also reminded to vigilantly monitor the risk of hacking and implement
appropriate and effective IT security controls. Both SFC and CCB strongly advise
intermediaries to implement appropriate control techniques against the risks of hacking. Some
suggested techniques are provided below:

It should be noted that the above control measures and techniques are by no means
exhaustive. SFC and CCB suggest that each intermediary reviews its own circumstances
and ensures that appropriate and effective control procedures are put in place and effectively
enforced1.
