Circular

21 October 2024

Circular to applicants seeking SFC authorisation of
unit trusts and mutual funds
Launch of a new Fund Authorisation Simple Track (FASTrack)
1.

The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) currently processes new fund
applications under a two-stream approach where applications are classified as standard
or non-standard.

2.

The processing time for all new fund applications has consistently met our target in the
past years. The average processing time for standard and non-standard applications in
the past five years was less than 1.5 months and 2.5 months respectively. This falls
within the SFC’s targeted processing time which is on average one to two months for
standard applications and within two to three months for non-standard applications.

3.

The SFC has entered into mutual recognition of funds arrangements with jurisdictions
outside Hong Kong (MRF Jurisdictions)1. As the regulatory regimes of these MRF
Jurisdictions provide comparable investor protection for retail investment funds to that in
Hong Kong and cooperation arrangements between the SFC and home regulators are in
place including the exchange of information, the SFC sees room to further expedite the
authorisation process for funds from MRF Jurisdictions.

4.

The SFC now launches a new “Fund Authorisation Simple Track” (“FASTrack” in short)
for simple funds domiciled and regulated in MRF Jurisdictions that seek authorisation for
public offering in Hong Kong.

5.

FASTrack aims to grant fund authorisations within 15 business days from applications if
successful to promote overall fund authorisation efficiency and maintain the
competitiveness of the asset management industry in Hong Kong.

Scope of funds and requirements on management company and investment delegates
under FASTrack
6.

A simple fund from an MRF Jurisdiction will be processed under FASTrack (FASTrack
Fund) if the following criteria are met:
Type of funds
a) The fund is (i) an equity, bond or mixed fund; (ii) an exchange-traded fund or unlisted
fund tracking an index adopted by other existing SFC-authorised fund(s) or a plain
vanilla index2; or (iii) a feeder fund (where the underlying master fund is eligible for
FASTrack); and

7.

FASTrack Funds are not expected to contain novel features, have material issues or
bear wider policy implications.

Processing time and performance pledges
8.

FASTrack Funds are intended to cover simple funds which are already subject to home
regulators’ supervision. As such, the SFC will adopt a more risk-based approach in
vetting the applications and aim to grant authorisation within 15 business days upon
receipt of complete and quality submissions from the applicants.

9.

Under the expected timeframe for FASTrack, the SFC will:

10.

•

take up or refuse to take up an application within five business days upon receipt of
the application; and

•

grant authorisation within 10 business days from the take-up date, if the application is
successful.

The SFC will conduct post-vetting to ensure applicable authorisation conditions are
complied with.

Implementation
11.

FASTrack will take effect on 4 November 2024 (Effective Date) with a six-month pilot
period ending on 4 May 2025.

12.

Applications meeting the above criteria received on or after the Effective Date will be
processed under FASTrack. Otherwise, the fund will be processed under the existing
processes for standard or non-standard applications.

13.

Obligations of applicants
15.

Applicants must discharge their responsibility and ensure that their SFC-authorised
funds comply with the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds, the applicable principles
and requirements under the SFC Handbook for Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds,
Investment-Linked Assurance Schemes and Unlisted Structured Investment Products, as
well as other applicable guidelines published by the SFC from time to time. The SFC will
take appropriate regulatory actions in cases of non-compliance to ensure that investor
protection is upheld.

16.

Should you wish to clarify any aspects of this circular, please contact the relevant case
officers in charge.
