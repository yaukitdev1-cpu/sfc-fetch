Circular to Management Companies of SFC-authorized Funds and
Issuers of SFC-authorized Investment-Linked Assurance Schemes
Surveillance exercise on product key fact statements and offering
documents
The Commission has conducted a surveillance exercise on a number of product key fact
statements (“KFS”) of SFC-authorized unit trusts and mutual funds (“SFC-authorized
Funds”) and revised offering documents of SFC-authorized investment-linked assurance
schemes (“SFC-authorized ILAS”).
The purpose of this circular is to provide guidance to the industry on the production and
refinement of disclosure and to inform the industry of the action the Commission has taken
on the basis of our surveillance findings, as part of the Commission’s ongoing efforts to
maintain an open dialogue with the industry and to assist them in meeting our regulatory
requirements.

Background
The SFC’s Handbook for Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds, Investment-linked Assurance
Schemes and Unlisted Structured Investment Products (“Handbook”) came into effect on 25
June 2010. Under the consolidated regime, all product issuers were required to produce KFS
and revised offering documents to satisfy the disclosure requirements as set-out in the
Handbook.
A transitional period was given to Existing SFC-authorized Funds and Existing SFCauthorised ILAS1 that would continue to be marketed to the public of Hong Kong on or after
25 June 2011 to comply with these requirements. Issuers were reminded of these
requirements by way of a circular dated May 2011.2
Prior to 25 June 2011, the Commission gave directional comments on more than 140 draft
KFS submitted by more than 60 fund houses and vetted every KFS of all then Existing SFC1

Surveillance Methodology
Transitioned Funds
KFS of Transitioned Funds were selected for review under the surveillance exercise on the
basis of: assets under management (“AUM”) based/market impact priority ranking;3 the
complexity of the funds; and observations regarding the quality of draft KFS submitted during
the directional comments process. At least 1 KFS from each issuer of Transitioned Funds
was selected for surveillance.
Based on the surveillance criteria outlined above, 209 KFS were selected so far representing
13% of the total population. The KFS were reviewed against the offering documents of the
SFC-authorized Funds, the requirements under the Handbook and other guidelines as
published by the Commission from time to time.
Transitioned ILAS
As the Commission had previously vetted all KFS of the Transitioned ILAS prior to 25 June
2011, the focus of the surveillance program was on the revised offering documents which
were authorised under the streamlined measures.4 Offering documents were selected on the
basis of AUM-based/market impact priority ranking and datedness of the relevant ILAS.
Based on the surveillance criteria outlined above, 25 sets of offering documents
(representing a sample size of 24.5% and covering 23 different issuers) were selected. The
offering documents were reviewed against the Handbook and other guidelines as published
by the Commission from time to time.

Observations and further guidance
At the outset, the Commission notes that the majority of the sample KFS of the Transitioned
Funds reviewed were generally in compliance with disclosure standards set out in the
Handbook including the relevant code, and did not contain serious disclosure issues. For a
small minority in which there were issues, the Commission appreciates that the requirement
to produce KFS is a new measure and is sympathetic to those issuers who have difficulties in
fully understanding and applying the new requirements. The Commission recognises that
issuers have, to a varying extent, put in an effort to attain the requisite disclosure standard.
As part of our ongoing efforts to assist the industry in meeting our regulatory requirements,
the Commission provides the following points of guidance to assist in the production and
refinement of the disclosure in the KFS going forward.
Disclosure of financial derivative instruments of the Transitioned Funds
•

The KFS and the offering documents of the fund should consistently set out the usage of
financial derivative instruments (“FDI”). For example, if the offering document sets out
that the fund will invest in FDI for investment purposes the KFS must make a similar
disclosure (i.e. the KFS should disclose that the fund will invest in FDI for investment
purposes and not merely for efficient portfolio management (“EPM”) purposes. In addition,
where the fund uses FDI for investment purposes, the KFS should outline whether such
investment is extensive or not, in a way which is consistent with the offering document).

Disclosure of objectives and investment strategies
•

The KFS should outline the key features of the fund, which include the objectives of the
fund and the investment strategies used to achieve such objectives. The disclosure on
these points will, as necessitated by the medium, be reduced as compared to the offering
documents but should not be inconsistent with the offering documents. For example, the
KFS and the offering documents must make consistent disclosure regarding the
investment allocation percentages or weightings given to specific countries/sectors. The
KFS should also disclose the types of instruments the fund will invest in to achieve the
objectives of the fund.

Risk disclosure in the KFS of the Transitioned Funds
•

Further to the above point, where a fund employs FDI extensively for investment
purposes, the KFS must contain appropriate disclosure in the KFS of the risk of
substantial or total loss arising from the extensive use of FDI as well as other key risks
associated with investing in FDI (for example, counterparty, liquidity, leverage, volatility
and valuation risk).

•

KFS must disclose the risks that are specific to the fund and the investments it makes.
For example: (a) funds that invest in futures and options must disclose in the KFS the risk

•

Fund KFS are fundamentally documents that are meant to present key information in
simple terms in a compact format. The KFS disclosure should use plain language to
explain the investment strategy of the fund and/or features of the fund rather than using
technical and non-laypersons’ terms.

Disclosure in the offering documents of the Transitioned ILAS
The Commission notes that in the majority of matters, the disclosure in the revised offering
documents of the Transitioned ILAS was satisfactory.
•

ILAS issuers should bear in mind that all the offering documents of ILAS, including the
KFS and the principal brochure, must comply fully with the Code on Investment-Linked
Assurance Schemes including making all statements required under the code.

•

Further, ILAS KFS must make consistent disclosure with the principal brochure. For
example, the fee and charges and calculation of market value adjustment must be
consistent across the offering documents.

Our action
Depending on the level of the deficiency and issues involved, the Commission has taken
follow-up action as briefly summarised below:
•

In the few isolated cases where the Commission identified material inconsistency or
deficiency between the KFS and the offering documents, we have required relevant
issuers to take immediate remedial action. Such action includes ceasing marketing and
acceptance of subscriptions, rectifying the issues on an urgent basis and preparing
remedial plans. We are pleased to note that issuers have been very cooperative. They
have proactively dealt with our requests and engaged the Commission in the remedial
process.

•

Where the Commission identified deficiency in the disclosure in the KFS or offering
documents which were of lesser concern, we required the relevant issuers to make
rectification on an expedited basis. For instance, for funds that merely state that FDI are
used for efficient portfolio management and/or investment purposes, it is expected that

Summary
While we appreciate the industry may need a reasonable time to adjust and grapple with the
enhanced disclosure requirements, we expect that all SFC-authorized Funds and ILAS
issuers will take note of the deficiencies observed during the surveillance exercise, and the
further guidance provided in this circular in preparing and updating their offering documents
(including KFS). We also expect issuers to take note that where the Commission identifies
disclosure which does not meet the requisite standards, it will be acted upon promptly and as
such this circular should serve as a timely reminder to all issuers to review the adequacy of
their current disclosure documents. We stress that it is the issuer’s responsibility to ensure
that all the offering documents they prepare are compliant with the relevant product codes,
the Handbook and other guidelines that are published by the Commission from time to time.
The SFC may conduct further surveillance of KFS as it thinks fit. Further guidance or
workshops may be given in due course.
