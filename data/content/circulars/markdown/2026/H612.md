(1) FATF Statement on Iran and Democratic People’s Republic of Korea
Further to our circular issued on 30 July 2010, this is to inform you that the Financial
Action Task Force on Money Laundering (“FATF”) issued an updated statement on 22
October 2010 concerning the anti-money laundering/combating the financing of terrorism
(“AML/CFT”) regimes in Iran and Democratic People’s Republic of Korea, which are
subject to periodic review by the FATF. The statement can be found on the website of
FATF (http://www.fatfgafi.org/documentprint/0,3455,en_32250379_32236992_46236900_1_1_1_1,00.html).
In this statement, the FATF reported on the progress of the abovementioned jurisdictions
in addressing the strategic deficiencies in their respective AML/CFT regimes and called
on its members and other jurisdictions to take specific actions as follows:
Iran
The FATF welcomed the recent steps taken by Iran but remained concerned by Iran's
failure to meaningfully address the on-going and substantial deficiencies in its AML/CFT
regimes. The FATF reaffirms its call on its members and other jurisdictions to continue to
apply counter-measures to protect the international financial system from the on-going
and substantial money laundering and terrorist financing risk emanating from the
jurisdiction.
Licensed corporations (“LCs”) and associated entities (“AEs”) should continue to apply
the measures specified in our circular of 31 March 2009 when handling transactions
related to Iran.
Democratic People’s Republic of Korea
Democratic People’s Republic of Korea was listed by the FATF as having strategic
AML/CFT deficiencies but not having committed to an action plan developed with the
FATF to address key deficiencies as of October 2010. The FATF calls on its members to
consider the risks arising from the deficiencies associated with this jurisdiction.
LCs and AEs should treat transactions associated with this jurisdiction as posing a higher
AML/CFT risk and subject them to increased scrutiny and enhanced due diligence.

(3) US President’s Executive Order 13224 (“the Executive Order”)
Further to our circular issued on 6 October 2010, this is to inform you that the US
Government has updated the list of designated individuals and entities issued under the
Executive Order. The updated information can be found on the website of the Office of
Foreign Assets Control of the US Treasury (“OFAC”)
http://www.ustreas.gov/offices/enforcement/ofac/programs/terror/terror.pdf under the
headings of “Name of entity updated on 10-19-10”, “Names of individuals removed on 1019-10”, “Names of individuals added on 10-26-10”, “Name of entity added on 11-3-10”,
“Name of entity added on 11-4-10”, “Names of individuals added on 11-4-10”, “Names of
individuals added on 11-24-10”, “Name of entity updated on 11-24-10”.
LCs and AEs should update their list of designated terrorists and terrorist associates
accordingly, check all these new or changed names against their records, and report any
transactions or relationships they have or have had with the named individuals or entities
to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit.
Furthermore, as the designated individuals and entities under the Executive Order may
be updated by the US Government from time to time, LCs and AEs are reminded to
browse the website of the OFAC regularly for the latest information.
