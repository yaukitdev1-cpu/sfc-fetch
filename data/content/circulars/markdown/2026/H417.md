Frequently Asked Questions on
Presentation of benchmark performance in advertising materials
With reference to practices and requirements on performance presentation for collective
investment schemes (CIS) in major international markets, the SFC has reviewed the use of
performance comparisons in advertising materials of authorized schemes. The information
set out in this letter and the attached Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) is designed to assist
you to understand the policy of the SFC in implementing certain provisions of the
Advertising Guidelines in the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds (Code) and seeks to
clarify how the Code applies in certain circumstances.
General Principles
Pursuant to Appendix F of the Code, the general principle is that no advertisement can be
made that is false, biased, misleading or deceptive. While there are no specifically prescribed
rules in choosing the benchmark to be presented, a comparison of performance figures should
be fair, accurate and relevant, comparing “like with like”. For example, the comparison of a
Hong Kong equity fund against a global index would be misleading. When a benchmark or
index chosen has both a total return calculation basis (which includes dividends and
distributions) and a price return basis, the fund manager should choose the most appropriate
index in accordance with the CIS’ distribution policy when showing performance of the CIS
against an index. In the case where the CIS’s performance is shown for a cumulative class of
shares/units or when the CIS does not make distributions, it should be compared against a
total return index, if available. Where the benchmark or index used does not have a total
return index, clear disclosures should be made to disclose the basis of calculation for both the
performance of the CIS and the benchmark.
The SFC does not dictate the use of any particular benchmark but fund managers should be
prepared to justify the relevance of their chosen benchmark and its comparability with the
Fund’s investment policy/performance.

Implementation
Any new advertisements must comply with the principles expressed in this letter and the
attached FAQ with immediate effect. In the case of factsheets, the format of which has
already been approved, if the basis of calculation of the benchmark performance needs to be
changed, we would generally expect that such past performance should be recalculated using
the appropriate basis within a transition period of 3 months. The revised format of the
factsheets should be submitted to the SFC for authorization as soon as possible such that any
factsheets issued 3 months after the date of this letter must comply in full with the principles
expressed herein and in the FAQ.
The SFC reserves the right to require the issuer of any advertising materials (whether
previously authorized or not) to cease its distribution or publication and take other remedial
actions where there are serious concerns about the performance presentation until the
presentation is rectified.
If you wish to clarify any aspects of this letter, please contact the team supervisor or case

Frequently asked Questions and Answers
Question
We use the Hang Seng Index as the benchmark
for our fund. As you know, the Hang Seng Index
is a price return index whereas our fund only
offers an accumulation class of shares. Can we
continue to use the Hang Seng Index?

Answer
We understand the Hang Seng Index provider presents the same index on a price return
basis as well as a total return basis. For presenting performance of an accumulation
share class, it would not be comparing “like with like” to use the Hang Seng price
return index. Thus, the manager is required to change the comparison to the total
return index where the total return index is available.

2.

The benchmark which we consider as most
appropriate for the fund is a price return index
which does not provide performance figures on a
total return basis, can we use this index?

The SFC expects that any comparison should be on a “like with like” basis. However,
where the benchmark considered by the manager to be the most appropriate is not
calculated on the same basis as the fund, the following would be acceptable:
• If the fund has a distribution class of shares where all income/dividends received
from the underlying holdings of the fund are distributed, the fund manager may
choose to show performance of the distribution share class so that the basis of
calculation of the fund and the benchmark is the same.
• If the fund only has accumulation class of shares or the fund does not make
distribution, then, the notes should clearly disclose the basis of the calculation (i.e.
the fund performance includes dividend/income reinvestment and the benchmark is
a price return index that does not include reinvestment of dividends/income.)

3.

We use interest rates (e.g. 3-month LIBOR) as
our benchmark, is this permissible?

Benchmarks with reference to interest rate trends may not take into account compound
interest/reinvestment of income. If the benchmark or index chosen has a calculation
basis which takes into account compound interest/reinvestment of income, such basis
should be used in comparing the performance of an accumulation share class. If the
fund’s performance is shown assuming accumulation or reinvestment of income, but
the benchmark or index chosen does not take into account compound
interest/reinvestment of income then, the notes should disclose the basis of the
calculation (i.e. the fund performance includes income reinvestment and that the
benchmark (LIBOR) shows interest rate trends which do not take into account
reinvestment of income.)

Question
We have created a customised index for our fund
(e.g. 40% MSCI Global Capital Markets + 40%
Lehman Brothers Fixed Income US Aggregate +
20% Hang Seng Index), not all of the indices use
the same basis. What should we do?

Answer
The question is whether you are comparing “like with like”. As far as possible, the
performance of each of the indices should use the same basis of calculation as the
fund’s performance. Thus, if the fund performance relates to an accumulation class of
shares or the fund does not make distribution, fund managers should choose to use total
return indices. If there is no total return index for a particular component of the
benchmark, this should be explained by way of clear disclosures.

We are currently using a price return index but
the benchmark we are using has a total return
version, do we need to change it? And if so, do
we need to back-date the calculation?

Yes. As far as possible, the performance of the benchmark index should use the same
basis of calculation as the fund’s performance and the same basis of calculation should
be used for the whole period for which the performance is shown. Any new
advertisements must comply with the principles expressed in these FAQs immediately.
In the case of factsheets, where the standard format has already been approved, we
would generally expect the benchmark’s past performance be recalculated using the
appropriate basis within the transition period of 3 months. The revised format of the
factsheets should be submitted to the SFC for authorization as soon as possible such
that any factsheets issued 3 months after the date of these FAQs comply in full with the
principles expressed herein.

6.

Our fund is an index-tracking fund.
The
objective is to track a price return index which
does not take into account dividend reinvestment
(e.g. Hang Seng Index). Is this permissible?

The SFC does not dictate the investment objective or investment strategy of a fund. If
the fund shows performance relating to an accumulation share class or the fund does
not make distributions, and the fund manager wishes to compare this against the price
return index, the fund manager must show the performance of the total return index in
addition. The notes should disclose the basis of the calculation (i.e. the fund
performance and total return index include dividend reinvestment and the price return
index does not take into account reinvestment of dividends.)
