30 March 2009

Circular to All Licensed Corporations, Responsible Officers,
Licensed Representatives and Registered Institutions
Regarding the Waiver of Annual Licensing Fees
This circular is being issued to remind all licensed corporations, responsible officers, licensed
representatives and registered institutions that the Commission will waive payment of all
annual licensing fees that would otherwise be payable by them during the period of one year
from 1 April 2009 to 31 March 2010 (both dates inclusive) (“waiver period”). The waiver was
first announced by way of a press release issued by the Commission on 20 February 2009.
The waiver will apply to annual licensing fees which would ordinarily become payable during
the course of the waiver period. The objective of the waiver is to relieve licensed
corporations, responsible officers, licensed representatives and registered institutions of the
obligation to pay the full amount of the annual licensing fee that is applicable to each of them.
The waiver will apply equally to each licensed corporation, responsible officer, licensed
representative and registered institution (including licensed individuals whose accreditation is
transferred from one licensed corporation to another during the waiver period) so that each
will benefit to the full extent of the annual licensing fee which is applicable to them, but not by
more than that amount.
The waiver applies to annual licensing fees that become payable on or after 1 April 2009.
Accordingly, if an annual fee becomes payable before 1 April 2009 (even as late as 31 March
2009), it must be paid in the normal manner even though actual payment might occur after 1
April 2009. In such a case, payment of the next annual licensing fee, which will become
payable during the latter part of the waiver period, will be waived.
Where a new licence or registration is granted on or after 1 April 2009, the licensee or
registered institution will not incur an annual licensing fee until after 31 March 2010.
Because such annual licensing fees will only become payable after the waiver period expires,
payment of these fees will not be waived.
Where annual licensing fees would ordinarily become payable during the waiver period, the
Commission will either not issue the usual demand for payment, or will advise a “nil” balance
payable. Whichever of these two options is utilised in any case will be determined by the
particular circumstances of the case. However, the effect will be identical in either situation.
The waiver applies only to annual licensing fees that become payable during the waiver
period. Accordingly, the payment of all other fees due and payable to the Commission,
including licence application and transfer fees, will not be affected.
The waiver will only remain in force for the duration of the one year waiver period and is not
intended to be extended beyond 31 March 2010.
