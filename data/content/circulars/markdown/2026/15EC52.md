Circular to Licensed Corporations in relation to
Consultation Response on Establishment of Effective Resolution Regime
for Financial Institutions in Hong Kong

The Government and the financial regulators, namely the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, the
Securities and Futures Commission and the Insurance Authority, on 9 October 2015,
released a consultation response to the second stage of public consultation1 on proposals to
establish a cross-sector resolution regime for financial institutions (FIs), including financial
market infrastructure, in Hong Kong.
The majority of respondents to the second stage of public consultation continued to be
supportive of the proposals to establish an effective local resolution regime in line with the
latest international standards set by the Financial Stability Board (FSB).
Taking into consideration respondents’ comments and observations as well as ongoing
developments in international practice, the consultation response contains further information
on the latest policy position in relation to certain aspects of the proposed regime, including
pre-resolution powers, loss-absorbing capacity requirements to facilitate bail-in, resolution
funding arrangements, the recognition of cross-border resolution actions and safeguards for
those affected by resolution action, including appeal mechanisms.
For licensed corporations (LCs), the scope of the regime remains as proposed in the second
stage consultation paper2. The following LCs should fall within the scope of the regime:
(a) LCs which are themselves designated as non-bank non-insurer global systemically
important financial institutions (NBNI G-SIFIs3);
(b) LCs which are subsidiaries or branches of groups which are identified as being (or
containing) G-SIFIs4.
For those LCs that are subject to the regime, they will still have to meet certain conditions
similar to those proposed in the second stage consultation paper5, before resolution can be
initiated in Hong Kong.
In addition, as Hong Kong plays host to a significant number of FIs operating as subsidiaries
or branches of foreign firms, it is proposed to have a statutory recognition framework
enabling a resolution authority, after consultation with the Financial Secretary, to recognize

all or part of a foreign resolution action, to the extent that it would produce substantially the
same legal effect in Hong Kong. A resolution authority may recognize a foreign resolution
action irrespective of whether the conditions for initiation of resolution locally are met, and
such recognition may have effect with respect not only to within-scope FIs but all FIs,
including LCs.
However, other conditions must be met before a resolution authority can recognize a foreign
resolution action6; specifically recognition must not be granted if the resolution authority is of
the opinion that recognition would: (i) have an adverse effect on financial stability in Hong
Kong; (ii) not deliver outcomes that are consistent with the resolution objectives; or (iii)
disadvantage Hong Kong creditors or shareholders (or both) relative to their counterparts
overseas. Moreover, a resolution authority could only recognize a foreign resolution action if
it is of the opinion that an arrangement is in place such that any Hong Kong shareholders or
creditors would be eligible to claim compensation broadly consistent with what they would be
eligible to claim if the resolution action had been originated locally. A resolution authority
may also take into account any fiscal implications for Hong Kong in deciding whether to
recognize a foreign resolution action.
It is expected that a bill to establish the local resolution regime will be introduced into the
Legislative Council by the end of this year.
