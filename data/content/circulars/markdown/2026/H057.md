COMMODITY EXCHANGE COMPENSATION FUND

The Commodity Exchange Compensation Fund has been
established in relation to default by exchange participants of
the Hong Kong Futures Exchange Limited.

What is the Commodity Exchange Compensation Fund (CECF)?

It is a pool of monies established under the Commodities Trading Ordinance to pay
as specified in the Ordinance allowed claims of investors who suffer pecuniary losses
as a result of a default by a broker who is an exchange participant of the Futures
Exchange.

Where does the money come from?

The Futures Exchange is required under the Commodities Trading Ordinance to
deposit $100,000 per exchange participant with the CECF. It also directs contract levy
of each contract to it.

Who administers or manages the CECF?

All claims are assessed and processed by the Futures Exchange. The CECF is
administered by a statutory committee, Futures Compensation Fund Committee,
which comprises representatives of the SFC and the Futures Exchange. Monies in the
CECF is in the custody of the SFC.

Under what circumstances will I be able to claim from the CECF?

The CECF covers pecuniary losses of clients resulting from the default in connection
with trading on the Futures Exchange of a Futures Exchange participant, or its
director, partner or employee in respect of any money, futures contract or other
property of the client. Default means a default arising from the bankruptcy or winding
up of the exchange participant, or any breach of trust, defalcation, fraud or
misfeasance committed by the exchange participant or by any of its director, partner
or employee, in respect of any money, futures contract or other property of the client.

How do I claim compensation and is there a time limit for filing a
claim?

Should this unfortunate experience happen to you, you should lodge a claim with the
Futures Exchange within three months of your becoming aware of the default. Also,
the Futures Exchange will issue a claims notice in at least one English and one
Chinese newspaper if it considers a broker to be in default. If you have not realised
the default until the issue of such a claims notice, you should file a claim with the
Futures Exchange within the period specified in the claims notice.

What documentation is required to accompany a claim?

To substantiate your claim, you might have to submit the originals of documents that
the broker has given or sent you about your investments and account position. These
might include the client's agreement, contract notes, statements of accounts, cheque
drawing records and bank statements etc. The more evidence you can provide, the
easier it will be to deal with your claim. If you cannot produce the required
documents, or if further information is required from you, the Futures Exchange may
contact you for an explanation of events or submission of further materials.

How will I be informed of the outcome of the claim?

After considering the claim, the Futures Exchange may determine that the claim
should be allowed, disallowed or allowed in part, and will inform all claimants of its
decisions and the reasons for any disallowance It is therefore necessary that you keep

the Futures Exchange updated of any subsequent changes in the information
submitted, i.e. change of address, contact phone number or amendments to the amount
of your claims etc.
If you are dissatisfied with the Futures Exchange's determination, you can lodge an
appeal with the Court within one month after the service of the determination notice.

Is there a limit on the compensation payout from this CECF?

Yes, a maximum amount of $2 million applies to any default of a Futures Exchange
participant, and if the total amount of claims is more than $2 million, then successful
claimants will be paid on a pro-rata basis of their allowed claims.

How long would the assessment take and when can I expect to be
paid?

The legislation requires the Futures Exchange to make determination within six
months and if the Futures Exchange cannot make the determination within the sixmonth period, it has to apply to the SFC for an extension. You will, however,
appreciate that assessments will necessarily involve complex and detailed
investigation work which may be both intensive and time consuming.

What if I have an account opened with a Futures Exchange
participant's subsidiary or its associates?

No claim may be made in respect of default by any entity other than an exchange
participant of the Futures Exchange.
For details of the CECF, you may contact the Supervision of Markets Division of the
SFC or the Futures Exchange for further information. Despite the availability of this
protection, you are advised to exercise discretion and judgement in selecting your
broker, and stay vigilant.
