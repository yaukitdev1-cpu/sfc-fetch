Circular to Licensed Corporations
Engaged in Introducing Broker Business and Their Connected Execution Brokers

The SFC has identified during its onsite inspections various breaches and non-compliance with
the licensing, Code of Conduct1 and AML Guideline2 requirements by some licensed
corporations engaged in introducing broker business (“introducing broker”). Introducing broker
business refers to the introduction of clients to other brokers (“execution broker”) with which the
clients open accounts for trading in securities, futures and/or leveraged foreign exchange
contracts, with or without providing the service of relaying trade orders of the client to the
execution broker.
Some execution brokers have also been found to have failed to observe the Code of Conduct
and AML Guideline requirements for account opening and client agreements when on-boarding
new clients introduced by their introducing brokers.
The business model of introducing brokers varies according to their business activities 3.
Irrespective of the variations in business model, licensed firms operating introducing broker
business and licensed execution brokers are reminded to pay special attention to the following
compliance and operational matters, and implement and maintain appropriate measures to
ensure their compliance with all applicable rules and regulations.
Introducing broker
Licensing requirements
Some introducing brokers are subject to licensing conditions which, among other things:
(1) Limit their business activities in the relevant regulated activity to introducing clients and/or
communicating client orders to the execution brokers.
It was noted that some of the introducing brokers inspected had conducted business
beyond their permitted scope of activities, thereby breaching this licensing condition. For
example, introducing brokers, which were subject to licensing conditions that limit their
business activities in Type 1 regulated activity, introduced clients to a bond issuer but not an
execution broker for the subscription of unlisted bonds, or provided discretionary account
management services to clients.

(2) Prohibit them from “holding” client assets.
Some of these introducing brokers were found to have taken part in the delivery of bond or
share certificates belonging to clients to relevant parties in the settlement and custody
process. Given that the definition of “hold” is very wide under the Securities and Futures
Ordinance (and includes, among other things, being in possession of the client assets or
being in a position to transfer the client assets to himself or otherwise receive the benefit of
these assets), the above amounts to a breach of this licensing condition.
Introducing brokers should exercise ongoing vigilance to ensure that they do not conduct
activities or handle client assets in a manner which constitutes a breach of any of their licensing
conditions. Particularly when embarking on new business activities or introducing new services,
introducing brokers should carefully assess whether the new business activities or services fall
within the restrictions included in their licensing conditions. Introducing brokers should also
report (i) any significant changes in the nature of the business carried on or to be carried on and
types of services provided or to be provided; and (ii) any significant changes in their business
plan to the SFC pursuant to the Information Rules4.
Third party trading authorisation
Some introducing brokers have, without authorisation from clients, taken trade orders from third
parties and relayed them to the execution brokers for execution for the account of clients,
seeking to rely on written authorisations which only authorised the execution brokers to take
trade orders from the third parties for the clients’ accounts. This may lead to possible legal
disputes and non-compliance with the relevant regulatory requirements5.
Before taking trade orders from a third party who purports to give the trade instructions on behalf
of a client, introducing brokers should (i) ensure and verify that the client has given written
trading authorisation for the introducing broker to accept trade orders from the third party6 (for
relaying to the execution broker for the account of the client); and (ii) identify the third party and
take reasonable measures to verify their identity7.
Execution broker
Account opening procedures
Some execution brokers adopt a non-face-to-face account opening approach and rely on their
introducing broker(s) to witness the signing of the client agreement and sighting of the related
identity documents (“Certification Process”). However, it was noted that some of these
execution brokers did not obtain a confirmation from the introducing broker certifying that the
client’s identity document copy provided to the execution broker is a true copy of the original
identity document which the introducing broker has seen8.
Execution brokers should implement effective policies and procedures to ensure the Certification
Process is properly carried out by any certifier (including an introducing broker) being used
during the non-face-to-face account opening process. Execution brokers are also reminded to

Client agreement with the introducing broker and the execution broker
It was noted that the client agreement which some introducing brokers and execution brokers
entered into with the client did not contain some of the content required by the Code of
Conduct12. In particular, some of these client agreements lacked a clear description of the
nature of services to be provided to or available to the client13 including the role and obligation of
the introducing broker or the execution broker (as the case may be) in the dealing, settlement
and custody process (where applicable).
Introducing brokers and execution brokers are reminded to, before services are provided to the
client, enter into a written client agreement which clearly sets out the abovementioned
information and complies with the relevant rules and regulations14. This client agreement
should also include, among other things, the introducing broker/execution broker’s undertaking
to notify the client in the event of any material change to the relevant provisions of the client
agreement15; in particular, provisions regarding the nature of services to be provided to or
available to the client.
Conversely, the introducing broker and execution broker should each consider seeking to
ensure that the client will immediately notify it if the client terminates its relationship with the
other broker. In such a case, the remaining broker should review the scope and process of
services being provided, assess the need for any necessary modifications, and revise the client
agreement accordingly in a timely manner.
