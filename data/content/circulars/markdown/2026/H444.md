Circular to Licensed Corporations and Associated Entities

Anti-Money Laundering / Combating Terrorist Financing


   (1) United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance


   (2) US President’s Executive Order 13224


   (3) Combating Financing of Weapons of Mass Destruction Activities



(1)	United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance (the “UNATMO”)

      An updated list specifying terrorists and terrorist associates designated by the United Nations Security Council Committee (“UNSCC”) was published under the UNATMO in the Gazette on 1 September 2006 (G.N. 5445).


      As compared to the previous list attached to our circular dated 12 June 2006, a summary of changes is set out in Attachment 1. For details, you may refer to the  press releases issued by the UNSCC on 25 July 2006, 2 August 2006, 3 August 2006 and 4 August 2006 (Attachment 2).


      The new list can be found on the Government website http://www.gld.gov.hk/eng/welcome.htm under eGazette Note [1] in Services to Public.  A copy of the list in MS Word format is attached to facilitate the update of your electronic database (Attachment 3).


      Licensed corporations (“LCs”) and associated entities (“AEs”) are reminded to take all necessary steps to ensure compliance with the UNATMO.  Besides reporting to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (“JFIU”) of any transactions or relationships you have or have had with the individuals and entities specified in the above list, you should refrain from, amongst other things, providing or collecting, by any means, directly or indirectly, funds to these individuals and entities pursuant to the UNATMO.





(2)	US President’s Executive Order 13224 (“Executive Order”)

      Further to our circular issued on 6 January 2006, this is to inform you that the US Government has added individuals and entities under the Executive Order.  The name of the additional individuals and entities can be found on the website of the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the US Treasury (“OFAC”) http://www.ustreas.gov/offices/enforcement/ofac/programs/terror/terror.pdf under “Name of Individuals and Entities Designated on 8 February 06, 18 February 06, 23 March 06, 13 April 06, 20 July 06, 3 August 06, 29 August 06 and 7 September 06”.


      LCs and AEs should check whether they have had transactions or dealings with the individuals and the entities named in the list.  In order to fulfil the obligations under section 12 of the UNATMO, you should promptly notify the JFIU if such reviews reveal any suspicious accounts, transactions or dealings.


      As the designated individuals and entities under the Executive Order may be updated by the US Government from time to time, LCs and AEs are reminded to browse the website of the OFAC regularly for the latest information.

(3)	Combating Financing of Weapons of Mass Destruction Activities



      Further to our circular dated 19 December 2005, the SFC would like to remind LCs and AEs to be vigilant in guarding against terrorist financing, particularly on the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction (“WMD”), and to ensure that proper measures are in place to mitigate the risks that they are used for terrorist financing.


      In identifying names that may trigger suspicion, LCs and AEs could draw reference from relevant designation by overseas authorities (such as the designation list under the US Executive Order 13382) in addition to those lists that the SFC draws to the attention of LCs and AEs from time to time.


      If LCs and AEs identify any transactions or dealings that are suspected to be related to WMD proliferators, they should report the suspicious transactions or dealings to the JFIU and discuss with the JFIU on how those customers and accounts should be handled.

      SECURITY COUNCIL AL-QAIDA, TALIBAN SANCTIONS COMMITTEE

       

      APPROVES CHANGES TO CONSOLIDATED LIST

       


           Significant changes to the Consolidated List, approved by the Al-Qaida and Taliban Sanctions Committee, were introduced on 25 July 2006 as indicated below.


           Permanent reference numbers


           In order to address problems caused by the fact that the number appearing in front of listed individuals and entities is subject to change every time the Consolidated List is updated, the Committee is hereby replacing sequential numbering with permanent reference numbers.


           The permanent reference number consists of three letters and two numbers. The first letter, T or Q, indicates Taliban or Al-Qaida.  The second letter, I or E, indicates individual or entity.  The third letter represents the first letter of the last name.  The first number represents the order in which the individual or entity was added to the Consolidated List. The second number represents the year the individual or entity was added to the Consolidated List.


           As an example, the permanent reference number assigned to Usama Muhammed Awad bin Laden is QI.B.8.01 where Q indicates that he appears on the Al-Qaida section of the Consolidated List, I indicates that he is an individual, B that his last name starts with a B, 8 that he was the eighth individual to be added to the Al-Qaida individuals section of the Consolidated List, and 01 that he was added in 2001.


           As the reference numbers are permanent the numbers assigned to an individual or an entity will be maintained if sanctions against that individual or entity are lifted and the individual or entity is placed in the “De-listed” section.


           Member States are encouraged to make use of the permanent reference numbers in their future communications with the Committee.






           Alphabetization of the Taliban section of the Consolidated List


           Since the inception of the Consolidated List individuals on the Taliban section of the List have been arranged according to their designation at the time when the Taliban controlled territory in Afghanistan whereas individuals associated with Al-Qaida have been arranged alphabetically.


           To address this inconsistency and to ease implementation the Committee has decided to arrange both the Taliban and the Al-Qaida sections of the Consolidated List alphabetically.


           Addition of original script


           To alleviate concerns regarding the accuracy of the transliteration of names on the Consolidated List and to ease implementation by Member States the Committee has decided to include, where possible, the names of listed individuals in original script as the names would appear in the individuals’ travel documents.


           At this time, original script has been added for 155 individuals on the Al-Qaida section of the Consolidated List (152 in Arabic script and three in Cyrillic script). With the assistance of the Monitoring Team, the Committee intends to continue its work to add original script where possible. When submitting names for addition to the Consolidated List to the Committee, Member States are encouraged to provide the names in both transliteration and original script.


           Additions and corrections to the Consolidated List
