In light of the impending commencement of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (“SFO”) on
1 April 2003, the SFC reissues the above three guidelines.
Fit and Proper Guidelines
These Guidelines outline the areas that the SFC will consider in determining a person’s
fitness and properness to be licensed or registered. They replace the Fit and Proper Criteria
published in December 2000 and are more reader-friendly with more examples under each
heading.
Guidelines on Competence
Replacing the Guidance Note on Competence published in June 2001, these Guidelines set
out the competence requirements on the academic or industry qualifications, regulatory
knowledge, industry and management experience expected of an individual applying for
licence. In respect of a corporation, these Guidelines set out the expected business structures
and personnel resources. Apart from relaxing a few requirements to facilitate individuals
enter into the industry and to aid intermediaries diversify into other product areas, there has
been no change in the basic policies. The relaxations are in the following areas:

Exempting a highly experienced representative from the recognised industry
qualification (“RIQ”) requirement when applying for approval as a responsible officer.
Exempting a highly experienced representative from the local regulatory framework
examination (“LRF”) requirement, subject to acquiring 5 additional CPT hours, when
applying for approval as a responsible officer.
Exempting a highly experienced representative/responsible officer from both the RIQ
and LRF requirements, subject to acquiring 10 additional CPT hours and business
restrictions, when applying for approval to engage in each additional regulated
activity.
Exempting a representative applicant with sufficient compliance or regulatory
experience from the LRF requirement.

Guidelines on Continuous Professional Training (“CPT”)
These Guidelines replace the Guidance Note on CPT published in June 2001. They set out
the requirements for licensed representatives to continuously update their knowledge and
skills through continuous professional training, and for licensed corporations to maintain an
appropriate continuing education programme for their representatives.
Likewise, the fundamental requirements remain. There are relaxations in the following areas:
•

•
•

Multiple counting of CPT units – A representative could multiple count his/her same
CPT activities in respect of his/her various regulated activities that have the same
competence requirements2. Thus a representative licensed to deal in securities as well
as advise on securities would only require 5 CPT hours annually.
Licensed representatives and corporations will no longer be required to annually
report on their CPT compliance. However, records of CPT programmes will still
have to be kept and subject to inspection by SFC staff.
Time spent by licensed representatives in meetings or activities as members of the
SFC’s regulatory committees or official working groups would be recognised as CPT
units.
