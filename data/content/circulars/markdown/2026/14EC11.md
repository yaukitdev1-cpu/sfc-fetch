Circular to all licensed and registered persons relating to
Paragraphs 8.3 and 8.3A of the Code of Conduct
The purpose of introducing disclosure requirements under Paragraphs 8.3 and 8.3A of the
Code of Conduct 1 is to mitigate conflicts of interests between clients and licensed or
registered persons, as well as to enhance transparency. Under Paragraphs 8.3 and 8.3A,
licensed or registered persons are required to make disclosure of transaction related
information to their clients. The purpose of this circular is to clarify the application of these
requirements.
Paragraph 8.3 Part A (a)(ii)
This paragraph covers situations where a licensed or registered person enters into “back-toback transactions” concerning an investment product.
The notes to Paragraph 8.3 Part A (a)(ii) describe a scenario of a “purchase order”, namely
where a licensed or registered person, after receiving a purchase order from an investor,
purchases an investment product from a third party and then sells the same investment
product to the investor and no market risk is taken by the licensed or registered person.
The Commission noticed that some intermediaries interpret the phrase “back-to-back
transactions” as referring only to “purchase” order transactions, while others interpret it as
referring to transactions covering both “purchase” and “sell” order transactions.
The Commission would like to clarify that when a client sells an investment product to an
intermediary under a back-to-back arrangement i.e. when the intermediary purchases the
product from the client and sells it to a third party, potential conflicts of interest, similar to a
“purchase” order scenario, may also arise. As such, “sell” order transactions are also
covered under the phrase “back-to-back transactions” for the purposes of Paragraph 8.3.
Paragraph 8.3A
The Commission is aware that some intermediaries interpret this paragraph as referring only
to transactions where an intermediary is acting as the initial distributor of an investment
product and as not referring to transactions where it sells an investment product to or buys
such product from a client. However, other intermediaries do not make such a distinction.
Accordingly, the Commission would like to clarify that the disclosure of transaction related
information under Paragraph 8.3A (including the disclosure of monetary and non-monetary
benefits under Paragraph 8.3) should not be limited to transactions where the intermediary is
acting as the initial distributor of an investment product. To do so would deny investors
carrying out other transactions information concerning the benefits received by
intermediaries and prevent them from making an informed investment decision.

The Commission understands that some intermediaries already have systems in place for
the purpose of complying with the above-mentioned requirements under Paragraphs 8.3 and
8.3A of the Code of Conduct. Others have expressed a need for time to implement changes
to computer systems and to provide training to relevant staff. The Commission expects all
intermediaries to comply with the requirements under these two Paragraphs. Where system
changes are required by an intermediary, the Commission expects full compliance with the
particular requirements no later than five months from the date of this circular by that
intermediary.
