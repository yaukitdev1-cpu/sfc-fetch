Circular to All SEHK Exchange Participants
Reminder to ensure compliance with key requirements

In the course of our broker supervisory work, we have identified a number of regulatory
breaches and control deficiencies which undermine investor protection and damage the
reputation of the broking industry of Hong Kong.
The Commission will not tolerate these types of breaches and deficiencies and has
already taken firm regulatory action against the more serious breaches. You are
required to put in place proper systems and controls to monitor and ensure full
compliance with applicable regulatory requirements.
In particular, brokers should pay particular attention to the following key areas:
•
•
•
•

Client assets should be promptly and properly accounted for and adequately
safeguarded;
Liquid capital position should be monitored closely and diligently to ensure compliance
with the FRR (being the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules) at all times;
Accurate and complete contract notes and statements of account should be despatched
to all clients on a timely basis; and
Adequate resources and systems (including human, IT or otherwise) which are
commensurate with the firm’s business activities should be implemented.

A.

Safeguarding of client money
Noted breaches of the Securities and Futures (Client Money) Rules include

B.

(1)

Client monies, instead of being safekept in the trust account, were used to meet
settlement obligations of other clients and/or the broker itself.

(2)

Bank accounts for keeping segregated client money were not designated as trust/
client account.

(3)

Paying brokers’ own money into HK$ client segregated accounts instead of
keeping client money received in a foreign currency in client segregated accounts
in that same foreign currency.

Safeguarding of client securities
Noted breaches of the Securities and Futures (Client Securities) Rules include
(1)

Client securities kept at segregated CCASS accounts were used to meet
settlement obligations of other clients or the broker.

(2)

Cash clients’ securities were co-mingled with margin clients’ securities in the
CCASS designated sub-accounts.

(3)

Clients were allowed to withdraw securities without providing written direction.

In addition, some firms were found to be deficient in the way they safeguarded their
client securities by

C.

(1)

not properly performing stock reconciliation. For example, there were no regular
reconciliations of stock positions to sub-accounts level, stock reconciliations were
not subject to independent review by senior staff members and there were no
proper or prompt follow-up actions on discrepancies and negative stock positions.

(2)

not properly using the CCASS maker-checker facilities. For example, no limit
was set or some unnecessarily high limits were set for some staff and some staff
members were using CCASS passwords of staff who had already left or sharing
their passwords with other staff members.

(3)

not timely and accurately transferring client securities to appropriate CCASS
client segregated stock accounts.

Information for clients
Noted breaches of the Securities and Futures (Contract Notes, Statements of Account
and Receipts) Rules (“Contract Notes Rules”) include
(1)

In one extreme case, the firm only issued statements of account to clients upon
request.
Provision of contract notes and statements of account is mandatory under law – a
firm must not override this by imposing its own preconditions.

(2)

In another case, the firm treated all its clients as hold-mail clients and therefore
did not send any contract notes etc. to its clients even though none of these
clients had authorised the firm to hold his/her mail.

(3)

Some of the monthly statements of account issued to clients were misleading or
incomplete for
(a)

deeming all securities transactions to have already been settled on T+2
irrespective of whether the client had actually settled the transactions;

(b)

treating physical scrip pending for a client’s collection to have already been
collected by the client on the day of the instruction despite the fact that the
scrip had yet to be collected by the client;

In addition, the following control deficiencies could result in clients assets not properly
protected or fraudulent acts not detected:
(1)

Contract notes and statements of account were passed to account executives for
onward delivery to their clients without proper safeguards against tampering by
the account executives and non delivery or late delivery to clients.

(2)

Account executives could access the settlement system to amend or print trade
documents or otherwise intercept such documents before despatch to clients.

(3)

Lax control over the change of client contact details (for example, no independent
check of amendments of client particulars before effecting the change into the
system) which could facilitate tampering by staff.

(4)

Undelivered trading documents were not promptly followed up to ensure that
client contact details would remain accurate and up-to-date.

(5)

There was no control log to monitor timely collection of trade documents by holdmail clients.
