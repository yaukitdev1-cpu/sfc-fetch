Circular to applicants of SFC-authorised investment products
Revised application lapse policy
1.

The purpose of this circular is to inform applicants seeking SFC’s authorisation of unit
trusts and mutual funds, investment-linked assurance schemes, unlisted structured
investment products and real estate investment trusts (“SFC-authorised Investment
Products”) of the revised application lapse policy to be adopted with effect from 1
January 2014.

Background
2.

We regularly review and seek to improve our authorisation process wherever
practicable without compromising investor interest and protection.

3.

An efficient and effective authorisation process is important for broad investor choice
and market development. We note in this connection that the average time taken to
process authorisation applications tends to be relatively long in recent years. Industry
participants have also approached us on this issue.

4.

To address this, we have reviewed our internal processes and data. It was noted that
on average the total processing time attributable to the SFC has been significantly less
than that attributable to applicants. For instance, out of 111 funds authorised from
January to July 2013, only 28% of the total processing time was attributable to the SFC,
while 72% was attributable to the applicants.

5.

This situation is far from optimal. It could also adversely impact other applicants who
are ready and able to launch their products but whose applications could not be
processed speedily because the SFC’s resources are taken up by applications that are
not ready. Importantly, a long processing time discourages potential applicants from
obtaining authorisation in Hong Kong, and could damage the reputation of Hong Kong
as an international asset management centre.

6.

This issue was also noted by former members of the Process Review Panel for the
SFC (“PRP”). In particular, it was noted that some applicants might have taken
advantage of the application system by submitting premature applications. There were
also concerns about the resource implications for the SFC in dealing with inactive
applications1.

7.

In response to the PRP’s concerns and suggestions, we introduced a 12-month
application lapse policy in June 2010 with a view to weeding out applications where
there was no serious intention to proceed. Under this policy, if for any reason 12
months have elapsed from the date of take-up of an authorisation application (“Take-up
Date”) and no authorisation has been granted, the application will lapse subject to the

8.

After the implementation of the current 12-month application lapse policy, the situation
improved but only slightly. We continue to see a significant number of applicants who
do not respond to requisitions fully and promptly until we have issued a letter of
mindedness, generally 9 months after the take-up of an application by the SFC, to
remind and inform applicants that the SFC is minded to refuse their application at the
expiry of the 12-month application period unless all outstanding issues are addressed
and all applicable requirements are complied with.

9.

Having regard to the experience in administrating the current 12-month application
lapse policy and upon discussions with and the recommendations made recently by the
PRP2, we have decided to revise the current 12-month application lapse policy. We
have also consulted relevant industry participants on this issue.

Applications received on or after 1 January 2014
10. To further enhance the authorisation process, we will adopt a revised application lapse
policy with effect from 1 January 2014 whereby the time period for the lapse of
applications will be reduced from 12 to 6 months.
11. As such, all applications for authorisation of SFC-authorised Investment Products
received on or after 1 January 2014 will be processed according to the following
revised timeline:
•

If, for any reason, 6 months3 have elapsed from the Take-up Date and no
authorisation has been granted, the application will lapse subject to the SFC’s right
to grant an extension at its sole discretion4. The application fee in respect of the
application will not be refunded to the applicant.

•

If, for any reason, 4 months5 have elapsed from the Take-up Date and no
authorisation has been granted, the SFC will issue a letter reminding and informing
the applicant that the application will in general lapse at the expiry of 6 months from
the Take-up Date.

•

Once an application has lapsed, if the applicant wishes to seek authorisation of the
SFC-authorised Investment Products, it shall make a new application, whereupon it
will need to pay the application fee for the new application and repeat the
application procedures.

12. As in the case when we first introduced the current 12-month application lapse policy,
the revised 6-month application lapse policy will not apply to applications for
authorisation of approved pooled investment funds which are made pursuant to both
the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds and SFC Code on MPF Products.
13. We hope this revised application lapse policy initiative would encourage applicants to
respond to requisitions in a timely fashion as well as result in a shorter turn-around time
on our part, so that an overall more expedient process can be achieved. Although we
would aim to complete our authorisation process in less than 6 months, this new
initiative would serve as a general indication on the approval timing.
14. On the other hand, in cases of non-compliance with any key requirement(s), the SFC
would be minded to issue a letter of mindedness to refuse an application earlier than 4
months from the Take-up Date where appropriate so that there is efficient use of
resources for processing proper applications. Furthermore, applicants are reminded
that we may refuse to take up any application where the materials submitted are not in
good order or otherwise not suitable for clearance.
Existing applications received before 1 January 2014
15. Existing applications for authorisation of SFC-authorised Investment Products received
before 1 January 2014 in respect of which no authorisation has been granted before 1
January 2014 will continue to be subject to the current 12-month application lapse
policy.
General
16. To provide further guidance to market practitioners on the revised application lapse
policy and the transitional arrangements, we will update the Frequently Asked
Questions in respect of the relevant types of SFC-authorised Investment Products
posted on our website (www.sfc.hk).
17. Should you have any questions, please contact the relevant case officers in charge.
