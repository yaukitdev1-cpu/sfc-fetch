Circular to Licensed Corporations and Associated Entities
Further Reminder of Business Continuity Arrangement
during the 6th Ministerial Conference of the World Trade Organization
We refer to our circular issued on 4 November 2005 in connection with the upcoming 6th
Ministerial Conference of the World Trade Organization (MC6) from 13th to 18th December
2005 at the Convention and Exhibition Centre in Wanchai.
In addition to an expected 10,000 delegates to the MC6, thousands of anti-WTO protesters
are expected to converge on Hong Kong. Experience gained at previous sessions of the
Ministerial Conference, suggests that some protesters may resort to non-peaceful means in
order to disrupt the Conference. Apart from street protests, there is potential for cyber attacks
targeting financial services over the internet. The risk of terrorist attacks being staged during
the Conference period has not been ruled out.
All licensed corporations and associated entities should take reasonable precautions to ensure
that their operation is not affected by MC6 and/or minimize potential damage to their clients.
We hope to offer some practical advice to these firms, especially securities brokers and
futures brokers. To this end, we have compiled a list of questions that these firms may
usefully ask themselves. However, we need to stress at the outset that this list should not be
taken to be exhaustive or comprehensive. The following list is primarily designed to act as a
catalyst that helps focus one’s mind on business continuity arrangement. In this regard,
senior management should be mindful of their responsibilities to properly manage the risks
associated with the business of the firm, including operational risk which in turn covers
business continuity.
List of questions
(a)

Have you assigned a senior member of staff to take care of the business continuity
arrangement, in particular, arrangement covering the following aspects?
Identifying critical systems and functions;
Planning for business continuity arrangement;
Making decision to activate the arrangement;
Overseeing the business recovery process;
Communicating with other staff members about the arrangement; and
Coordinating with relevant external parties to resume the business operations.

One of the key responsibilities of this person should be to map out the full operational
details of the business continuity plan and brief other staff members accordingly so
that each will know exactly what to do, and when.
(b)

Do you have another office from where you can operate if your staff is unable to
access your office at one particular location?

(c)

Have you considered how you can serve your clients if they cannot visit or telephone
your office?
Do you have any arrangement to inform your clients of what they should do in this
situation? For example, clients may need to contact your other offices or to call your
sales staff on their mobile telephone. Would it be advisable, having regard to relevant
considerations, for you to forewarn your clients that you might not be able to deliver
the same level of service in the circumstances?

(d)

Have you put in place a clear emergency contact arrangement to cover all key staff
members, and ensured that contact details are kept up-to-date?

(e)

Have you regularly backed up your critical business and transaction data and stored
such data off-site? Have you taken any measures to ensure the back-up data can be
available for use in your back-up computer system within a reasonable timeframe?
Have you arranged adequate processing capacity of your back-up facilities for
maintaining minimum level of services? Would you consider doing this more
frequently around the time of the MC6 and making more back-up copies for storing at
different site locations?
Assuming a worst-case scenario, will it be possible for your trading staff to work with
hard copy of client data so that your clients can continue to place orders or
withdraw/deposit securities or money?
Have you considered whether your firm should keep hard copies of the latest client
data as at day-end of 12 December 2005?

(f)

In the case of an Exchange Participant, have you considered any alternative trading
arrangement to effect clients’ trade orders such as the following examples if your
office is not accessible?
Trading via another Exchange Participant on your behalf; or
Using back-up terminals provided by the Exchanges

(g)

Do you have other means to update your data in case you are unable to run your
program and update your data in the normal way? For example, have you made an
arrangement with your systems vendor to use its facilities when needed?

(h)

Have you assessed the likelihood of any potential claims from clients in case the firm
cannot provide the same level of service to them? Have you recently assessed
adequacy of existing insurance coverage?

Firms are urged to critically assess their business continuity arrangement as a matter of
urgency to alleviate any potential effect on their clients. Finally, we would further remind
that firms should update their information, in particular the emergency contacts, with the
Commission.
