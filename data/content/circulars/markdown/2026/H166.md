(1)

United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance (“Ordinance”)
This is to inform you that an updated list specifying terrorists and terrorist associates
designated by the United Nations Security Council Committee was published in the
Gazette (G.N. 858) on 14 February 2003. The list is available on the Government
website www.info.gov.hk/pd/content/index.htm.
This updated list superceded all previous lists under the Ordinance. Two new
individuals, Abdurraham, Mohamad Iqbal and Isamuddin, Nurjaman Riduan,
which were designated by the US Government under the US President’s Executive
Order 13224 (the “Executive Order”) on 24 January 2003 (please see our circular of
29 January 2003), have been added under items 6 and 49 respectively to the
“individuals” list in part B.
Two entities, namely Stichting Benevolence
International Nederland and Lashkar i Jhangvi (LJ), which were designated under
the Executive Order on 24 and 30 January 2003 (please see our circular of 29 January
2003 and 5 February 2003), have been added under items 52 and 65 respectively to
the “entities” list.
The SFC reminds all registered intermediaries to take all necessary steps to ensure
compliance with the Ordinance. Besides reporting to the JFIU of any transactions or
relationships you have or have had with the named individuals, you should refrain
from, amongst other things, providing or collecting, by any means, directly or
indirectly, funds to the named individuals or entities pursuant to the Ordinance.
Previous circulars on this subject are available in SFC’s website www.hksfc.org.hk.

(2)

Non-cooperative Countries and Territories
Further to our circular dated 23 October 2002, we would like to draw your attention to
the removal of Grenada from the list of NCCTs by the Financial Action Task Force
(“FATF”). The FATF decided to remove this jurisdiction from the list of NCCTs at
its recent Plenary Meeting because the jurisdiction has implemented significant
reforms to its money laundering system.
Further to our circular dated 7 January 2003, FATF announced on 14 February 2003
to withdraw the application of countermeasures with respect to Ukraine as a result of
the recent enactment by Ukraine of comprehensive anti-money laundering legislation
that addresses the main deficiencies identified by the FATF in 2001 and reaffirmed in
December 2002. However, Ukraine will remain on the list of NCCTs until it has
implemented effectively its new anti-money laundering legislation.
No new NCCTs have been identified and the updated list of the remaining 10 NCCTs
is as follows:
Cook Islands
Egypt
Guatemala
Indonesia

Myanmar
Nauru
Nigeria

Philippines
St. Vincent and the Grenadines
Ukraine

Registered intermediaries are advised to continue to give special attention to
businesses and transactions with persons, including companies and financial
institutions, in the above NCCTs, in accordance with Recommendation 21Note of the
FATF Forty Recommendations.
The FATF has previously called on its members to apply counter-measures to Nauru
due to the failure to address the deficiencies of its offshore banking sector. The
application of counter-measures against Nauru as set out in our previous circular
dated 23 October 2002 should be continued.
Furthermore, in view of the inadequate progress made by the Philippines to enact
legislation to address previously identified deficiencies in their anti-money laundering
regimes, the FATF has recommended the application of counter-measures from 15
March 2003 in respect of the jurisdiction unless the Philippines Government enacts

(3)

US President’s Executive Order
Further to our circular dated 5 February 2003, this is to inform you that the US
Government has designated an additional name, Gulbuddin Hekmatyar, under
the Executive Order. The additional name can be found in the website of the Office
of Foreign Assets Control of the US Treasury www.ustreas.gov/ofac under Names of
Those Designated on 18 February 2003.
Registered intermediaries should check whether they have had transactions or
dealings with the name identified in the list. In order to fulfil the obligations under
section 12 of the Ordinance, registered intermediaries should promptly report to the
JFIU if such reviews reveal any suspicious accounts, transactions or dealings.
As the designated individuals and entities under the Executive Order may be updated
by the US Government from time to time, you are reminded to browse the website of
the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the US Treasury regularly for the latest
information.
