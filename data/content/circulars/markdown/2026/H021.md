UNIFIED EXCHANGE COMPENSATION FUND

The Unified Exchange Compensation Fund has been
established in relation to default by exchange participants of
the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited.

What is the Unified Exchange Compensation Fund (UECF)?

It is a pool of monies established under the Securities Ordinance to pay as specified
in the Ordinance allowed claims of investors who suffer pecuniary losses as a result of
a default by a broker who is an exchange participant of the Stock Exchange.

Where does the money come from?

The Stock Exchange is obliged to deposit HK$50,000 with the UECF in respect of
each trading right.

Who administers or manages the UECF?

All claims are assessed and processed by the Stock Exchange. Although the Stock
Exchange has a compensation committee that oversees the assessment work, the
UECF is administered by a statutory committee, Securities Compensation Fund
Committee, which comprises representatives of the SFC and the Stock Exchange.
Monies in the UECF is in the custody of the SFC.

What are the requirements for becoming eligible to claim?

You will not be compensated for normal risks associated with stock investments,
such as where your shares have gone down in value. You are entitled to claim
compensation from the fund if you suffer losses because the broker fails to meet its
legal obligation in relation to the money, securities or other property which you have
entrusted to it and the broker is in default. The broker must be an exchange participant
of the Stock Exchange with whom you have established a direct client relationship to
trade in securities listed on the Stock Exchange. In this respect, the term "default"
may imply the broker is in bankruptcy, winding up or insolvency and cannot give you
back your money or securities; or the broker committed any breach of trust or the
broker, partner or employee of the broker committed misappropriation, fraud or
misfeasance.

How do I claim compensation and is there a time limit for filing a
claim?

You must lodge a claim with the Stock Exchange within 6 months of your becoming
aware of the broker's default. Also, the Stock Exchange may issue a claims notice in
at least one English and one Chinese newspaper if it considers a broker to be in
default. If you have not realised the default until the issue of such a claims notice, you
should file a claim by filling out a claim form obtainable from the Stock Exchange
within the period specified in the claims notice.

What documentation is required to accompany a claim?

To substantiate your claim, you might have to submit the originals of documents that
the broker has given or sent you about your investments and account position. These
might include the client's agreement, contract notes, receipts for securities, statements
of accounts, cheque drawing records and bank statements etc. The more evidence you
can provide, the easier it will be to deal with your claim. If you cannot produce the
required documents, or if further information is required from you, the Stock
Exchange may contact you for an explanation of events or submission of further
materials.

How will I be informed of the outcome of the claim?

After considering the claim, the Stock Exchange may determine that the claim should
be allowed, disallowed or allowed in part, and will inform all claimants of its

decisions and the reasons for any disallowance. It is therefore necessary that you keep
the Stock Exchange updated of any subsequent changes in the information submitted,
i.e. change of address, contact phone number or amendments to the amount of your
claims etc.
If you are dissatisfied with the Stock Exchange's determination, you can lodge an
appeal with the Court within three months after the service of the determination
notice.

Is there a maximum limit of payout or will I receive a 100%
compensation if my claim is substantiated?

Compensation payments, which are made in cash only, take into account the value of
securities, and are limited to $8 million per broker default. If the total of the allowed
claims exceeds this limit, the amount will be shared by all the successful claimants
and the payment to you will be prorated in accordance with your allowed claim
amount.

Can I submit a claim for losses suffered long ago?

Since the UECF only covers cases where the defaulting broker is an exchange
participant of the Stock Exchange, it follows therefore that only defaults arising after
the unification of the four Exchanges (Hong Kong Stock Exchange, Far East
Exchange, Kam Ngan Exchange and Kowloon Stock Exchange) in April 1986 may be
considered.

How long would the assessment take and when can I expect to be
paid?

There is no statutory time limit on when the claims must be determined or when
compensation monies must be paid. However, considering that the UECF first allows
three months for claims submission, and a further three months period for appeal
after claims have been determined, the total processing time could be considerable
especially when a large number of claims is received.

Upon determination of all claims, if the appeal period passes without further
hindrance, the SFC will proceed to send payment cheques to successful claimants by
registered mail.

What if I have an account opened with a Stock Exchange participant's
subsidiary or its associates?

No claim may be made in respect of default by any entity other than an exchange
participant of the Stock Exchange. Therefore you should make sure that it is an
exchange participant who holds the money/securities and not anyone else if you wish
to receive the protection of the UECF.

What if I trade securities via a bank, will I be covered?

If you establish a relationship with a bank and it defaults, you will not be covered by
the UECF if the bank is not an exchange participant of the Stock Exchange. In most
cases, banks have been declared as "exempt dealers" by the SFC, and as banks are
Authorised Institutions under the Banking Ordinance, their operations including
securities dealing business and financial soundness are regulated by the Hong Kong
Monetary Authority (HKMA). You should therefore bring the matter to the attention
of the HKMA if you encounter any problems which you cannot resolve with the
management of a bank.
For details of the UECF, you may contact the Supervision of Markets Division
of the SFC or the Stock Exchange for further information. Despite the
availability of this protection, you are advised to exercise discretion and
judgement in selecting your broker, and stay vigilant.
