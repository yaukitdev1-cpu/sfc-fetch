Circular to All Licensed Corporations Licensed for Dealing in Securities
Continuous and Close Supervision of Firm’s Operations
The SFC is concerned about the recently reported cases involving the misappropriation of
clients’ assets by the staff of brokerage firms. The most recent case of misappropriation
reported by a firm just a few days ago points to a problem that highlights ineffective
internal controls and inadequate management supervision by some firms. In order to
protect the firm and their clients from losses, the senior management of all firms must
place fraud prevention as a high priority and immediately address any inadequacies in
their anti-fraud framework. Failure to do so not only leads to financial but also
reputational damage suffered by the firm. It could also expose the firm’s clients to loss.
The SFC will not hesitate to take appropriate action against those firms and their senior
management who have failed in their duties with respect to internal controls and
management supervision.
The various types of fraud that have occurred include unauthorised trading on clients’
accounts, disposal of clients’ securities without clients’ knowledge and provision of
forged statements to clients by account executives.
Senior management should identify and assess the fraud risk levels and the adequacy of
existing controls in their firms without delay. A firm should notify the SFC as soon as
practicable once a case of alleged fraud is uncovered or identified.
Senior management should ensure:
•

close supervision of the firm’s operations and monitoring of trading activities
including staff dealing;

•

key functions are appropriately segregated, especially those duties and functions
which when performed by the same individual may result in undetected errors or
may be susceptible to abuse;

•

that various forms of “hold mail” arrangements should not be allowed without
proper controls in place to prevent abuse;

Appendix 1
Checklist of Important Controls
1. Management and supervision
The senior management of all brokerage firms should:•

establish and implement effective policies and controls to prevent and detect
potential fraud as early as possible;

•

ensure their anti-fraud control structure is consistently maintained over the longer
term and that fraud prevention is considered an ongoing priority;

•

diligently supervise the operations of the firm including persons employed or
appointed to conduct business on behalf of the firm;

•

be responsible for close supervision of the firm’s operations and monitoring of
trading activities including staff dealing. If the structure of the firm’s business
does not allow complete segregation of duties, the management must supervise
each vital stage of its business processes very closely to prevent abuse and fraud;
and

•

regularly review the activities of inactive/dormant accounts as these accounts can
easily be used to conceal unauthorised transactions.

2. Delivery of trading documents to clients
•

Trading documents including statements, contract notes etc. are an effective tool
for clients to monitor their own transactions traded and the assets that they have
deposited with a firm. The management should ensure that the issuing process of
such trading documents is properly protected against tampering and abuse by
unauthorized staff to ensure the integrity and authenticity of the documents.
Therefore, a firm should have an integrated approach to fraud management by
ensuring that trading documents are centrally handled and delivered promptly and
directly to clients by a back-office department/section.

•

Various forms of “hold mail” arrangements should not be allowed without proper
controls in place to prevent abuse.

•

Restricted or controlled access to a firm’s company-headed stationery would help
minimise possible falsification of a firms’ trading documents.

3. Independent confirmation with clients
•

All client information database(s) should be maintained by non-dealing staff. All
changes to the database should be properly supervised and approved by senior
management.

•

Where practicable, it is recommended to have a firm’s back-office staff call up
their clients and directly confirm the client’s transactions, stock and cash holding
on a regular basis. This useful control would help identify any unauthorized
transaction and misappropriation of clients’ assets.

4. Prompt and independent handling of clients’ complaints
•

A firm should ensure that all clients’ complaints regarding the firm’s operations
are promptly and appropriately handled by an independent complaints or
customer service section.

5. Handling of clients assets
•

Where appropriate, client money and securities should be directly received from
or delivered to clients. Any request for third party checks or fund transfers to third
party accounts should first be reviewed and approved by senior management.
