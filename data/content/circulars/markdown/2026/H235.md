(1)

US President’s Executive Order 13224 (“Executive Order”)
Further to our circular dated 30 May 2003, this is to inform you that the US Government
has designated additional names under the Executive Order. The additional names can
be found in the website of the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the US Treasury
www.ustreas.gov/offices/enforcement/ofac/sanctions/terrorism.html under Names of
Individuals Designated on 6 June 2003.
Licensed corporations and associated entities should check whether they have had
transactions or dealings with the individuals named in the list. In order to fulfil the
obligations under section 12 of the United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance,
they should promptly notify the JFIU if such reviews reveal any suspicious accounts,
transactions or dealings.
As the designated individuals and entities under the Executive Order may be updated by
the US Government from time to time, licensed corporations and associated entities are
reminded to browse the website of the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the US
Treasury regularly for the latest information.

(3)

Non-Cooperative Countries and Territories (“NCCTs”)
The Financial Action Task Force on Money Laundering (“FATF”) regularly updates its
list of NCCTs. At its recent plenary meeting, it has decided to remove St. Vincent and
the Grenadines from the list of NCCTs. The updated list of the remaining 9 NCCTs is as
follows:
Cook Islands
Egypt
Guatemala

Indonesia
Myanmar
Nauru

Nigeria
Philippines
Ukraine

Licensed corporations and associated entities are advised to give special attention to
businesses and transactions with persons, including companies and financial institutions,
in the above NCCTs, in accordance with Recommendation 21Note 2 of the FATF Forty
Recommendations.
The FATF will regularly review the NCCT list. Changes to the list, if any, will be
conveyed to licensed corporations and associated entities through circulars in due course.

(4)

New Members of FATF
The FATF also admitted South Africa and the Russian Federation as full members of the
FATF, following a positive outcome of the first mutual evaluations that assessed their
systems for combating money laundering and terrorist financing.
