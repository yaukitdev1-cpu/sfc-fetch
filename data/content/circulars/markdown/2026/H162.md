Securities and Futures (Insurance) Rules
As you are aware, the Securities and Futures (Insurance) Rules (“the Rules”) were gazetted
on 17 January 2003. These Rules, which were made under section 116(5) of the Securities
and Futures Ordinance (“SFO”), set out the obligations of certain licensed corporations to
take out and maintain insurance in relation to the specified risks for not less than the specified
amount. Pursuant to section 118(1)(a)(i)(B) of the SFO, maintaining the required insurance
is one of the conditions of a licence granted under section 116(1) for carrying on a regulated
activity.
As far as the participants of Hong Kong Futures Exchange Limited (“HKFE”) are concerned,
section 4(3) of the Rules requires that the HKFE Participants shall take out and maintain
insurance under a master policy of insurance approved by the SFC if the SFC has approved
such a master policy under section 5(3) of the Rules. Similar requirement is also provided in
respect of the participants of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited (“SEHK”) under
section 4(2) of the Rules.
As for securities margin financiers and securities and commodity dealers which are not
exchange participants, subject to certain exemptions, they are required to take out and
maintain insurance under section 4(1) of the Rules if the SFC has approved a master policy of
insurance under section 5(1) in respect of a regulated activity for which they are licensed.
The Rules however provide that this group of licensees (being non-exchange participants)
may choose to take out the required insurance under the approved master policy or under
their own insurance policies underwritten by independent insurers.
To arrange the necessary master insurance policy for the licensees concerned (in particular,
exchange participants), an Industry Working Group (“IWG”) comprising representatives
from the industry, insurance advisers and the SFC has been established. As advised by the
insurance advisers, relevant records of losses or claims have to be collected from the HKFE
Participants in order for the insurers to properly assess the risks associated with this group of
licensees. Submission of related records by the SEHK Participants is however not required
because the insurance market is generally familiar with the loss history and business practice
of the SEHK Participants, which have been insured under the Brokers’ Fidelity Insurance
Scheme arranged by the SEHK since October 1992.

License Holders Insurance (LHI) Scheme - Declaration
The proposed insurers of the LHI require a declaration by each Licensed Intermediary regarding past
claims, past losses or circumstances that could give rise to claims or losses in the future, both insured and
uninsured, that could be the subject of the proposed insurance policy. It is important that a full enquiry is
made before responding.
In summary, the LHI policy will cover losses from Direct Financial Loss (which includes Incomplete
Transactions, Fidelity, Loss of Property, Forgery or Alteration, Securities, Counterfeit Currency, Official
Investigation Costs, Computer Crime Indemnity, Stop Payment Order Liability, Loss of Subscription Rights,
Extortion - Threat to Persons, Extortion - Threat to Property, Claims Preparation Costs) and there may also
be the option for covering Errors and Omissions (or Professional Indemnity) claims. Please contact Jardine
Lloyd Thompson (JLT) if you have any further questions regarding potential policy coverage.

Duty of Disclosure – Important Note:
In addition to providing the above information necessary to enable this risk to be placed, you must ensure that you are
complying with your legal duty of disclosure of all material matters relating to the risk. In particular, you must satisfy
yourself as to the accuracy and completeness of the information you provide to insurers.
In this respect, you must provide all information relating to the risk, whether favourable or not, which would influence the
judgement of a prudent insurer in determining whether he will take the risk, and, if so, for what premium and on what
terms.
If you do not disclose all such information, insurers may have the right to avoid your coverage, which may lead to claims
not being met.
