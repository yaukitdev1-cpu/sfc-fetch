Circular to Licensed Corporations in relation to HKMA’s report on consultation
on logistical and technical arrangements for the reporting of OTC derivatives
transactions to the Hong Kong trade repository
On 29 July 2011, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) issued a consultation paper to
seek comments on certain logistical and technical matters relating to its proposed trade
repository (HKMA consultation paper) (Note). We forwarded the HKMA consultation paper
to Licensed Corporations via circular dated 2 August 2011.
The consultation period for the HKMA consultation paper ended on 9 September 2011. The
HKMA has today issued a report which sets out the key comments it received on the HKMA
consultation paper, and the HKMA’s responses and conclusions (HKMA report). The HKMA
report and the cover letter addressed to Authorised Institutions are attached to this circular.
We encourage all affected Licensed Corporations to plan ahead for any necessary system
changes based on the scope and the implementation timeline set out in the HKMA
consultation paper and the HKMA report.
Should you have any queries regarding the above, please contact the HKMA at
hktr@hkma.gov.hk.

INTRODUCTION
1.

On 29 July 2011, the Financial Infrastructure Department of the Hong
Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) issued a consultation paper on a set of
proposed logistical and technical arrangements for reporting to the trade
repository for OTC derivatives transactions to be established by the
HKMA (the HKTR). The consultation paper was issued to all Authorized
Institutions (AIs) under the supervision of the HKMA, and was
communicated by the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) to
Licensed Corporations (LCs) under its purview.

2.

The consultation aimed at seeking comments from market participants on
the key logistical and technical arrangements for reporting to the HKTR,
so that early preparations, especially on the system front, can be made by
them to meet the relevant requirements.

3.

During the consultation period from 29 July 2011 to 9 September 2011, the
HKMA proactively engaged market participants to solicit views and inputs.
Three briefing sessions, attended by a total of 134 AIs and 17 LCs, were
conducted to explain the relevant technical and logistical arrangements and
collect comments. The HKMA also briefed industry representatives in
the Trade Repository (TR) Sub-group of the Working Group on OTC
Derivatives Market of the Treasury Markets Association (TMA) on the
consultation paper. Bilateral meetings were held with individual market
participants, industry bodies and potential reporting agents to exchange
views on the arrangements proposed in the consultation paper.

4.

The HKMA received 17 written submissions from market participants,
industry bodies and potential reporting agents. In addition, 73 AIs and 3
LCs responded to the survey on certain specific reporting arrangements
(the Survey) attached with the consultation paper. The comments
received during the consultation can generally be classified into comments
on reporting approach and process, format and contents of reporting, and
other comments (mainly on the regulatory framework).

5.

The consultation concluded that market participants generally found the
proposed logistical and technical arrangements for TR reporting acceptable.
The HKMA is prepared to take onboard those recommendations of the
industry that can further streamline the reporting process. This report
summarises the major comments received, and the responses and the
conclusions of the HKMA.

KEY COMMENTS RECEIVED AND THE HKMA’S RESPONSES
1. Reporting approach and process
Reporting via an agent and matching
Comments received
6.

The comments received during the consultation reveal a strong preference
among market participants to report their trades to the HKTR via an agent.
The potential agents nominated by market participants in the Survey cover
a board range of entities, including major overseas TRs, trade matching
and confirmation platforms, electronic communication platforms for
exchanging financial messages, financial data warehouses, IT service
vendors and even financial institutions.

7.

Some market participants noted that it was important to ensure the
accuracy and integrity of the trade data maintained with the HKTR.
Nevertheless, some had a concern about the operational burden that may
be generated from the trade matching process. Among those intending to
engage a matching and confirmation platform to match and confirm trades,
and then submit the trades to the HKTR, some had a query about whether
it was necessary to re-match and re-confirm such trades again at the
HKTR.

HKMA’s responses and conclusions
8.

The HKMA fully recognises the importance of allowing market
participants to report via an agent as a means of improving reporting
efficiency, and therefore has stated clearly in the consultation paper that
the HKTR is prepared to accept reporting through an agent. The
consultation paper on the regulatory regime for OTC derivatives market
issued jointly by the HKMA and the SFC on 17 October 2011 also states
that reporting entities can report to the HKTR directly or through an agent.

9.

The HKTR will support reporting through two types of agent: agents
appointed by reporting entities to match and confirm both sides of a trade,
and then submit the trade to the HKTR (the “confirmation-type” agent);
and agents appointed by a reporting entity to submit its side of the trade to
the HKTR (the “messenger-type” agent). Matched and confirmed trades
reported by a confirmation-type agent on behalf of the reporting entities
are NOT required to be matched again at the HKTR. Only reporting
entities submitting its side of a trade directly to the HKTR or through a
messenger-type agent are required to match the trade at the HKTR, except
where the trade counterpart is not subject to mandatory reporting and
2

hence does not submit its side of the trade to the HKTR (in such cases, the
trade will be captured as a single-sided trade by the HKTR).
10. It is anticipated that the interface between the HKTR and a messenger-type
agent will be no different to that between the HKTR and a direct reporting
entity. However, specially designed interfaces are required to be
developed and implemented to facilitate confirmation-type agents to
submit matched and confirmed trades to the HKTR.
11. While it will not be time feasible or cost permissible for the HKTR to
establish linkages with all potential confirmation-type agents nominated by
market participants, priority will be given to linking up with those that can
cover the greatest portion of trades reported to the HKTR. The HKMA
has commenced work with a key matching and confirmation platform for
interest rate derivatives, which was nominated by most market participants
in the Survey, and the linkage with it is expected to be launched before the
regulatory reporting regime commences operation. The HKMA has also
approached key overseas TRs for interest rate and FX derivatives, and a
financial messaging platform for FX transactions nominated by most
market participants to explore the feasibility of opening up reporting
channels via them. However, as these potential reporting agents have not
yet commenced operation or firmed up their operating models, the
reporting gateways via them may not be able to be established in time.
Market participants are therefore prescribed to get themselves ready for
making direct reports to the HKTR at the inception of the regulatory
reporting regime, especially for trades in FX derivatives.
12. The HKTR noted that some major overseas TRs are not intending to
perform matching of trade details reported by market participants, perhaps
because the bulk of the trades received by them would have been matched
and confirmed, and channelled from a confirmation-type agent. The
HKTR will similarly receive matched and confirmed trades by
confirmation-type agents, on which no matching will be performed.
Nevertheless, the HKMA sees that it is essential to perform matching at
least on the core economic terms of those trades submitted directly by
reporting entities or by messenger-type agents to the HKTR to provide a
satisfactory level of confidence on the accuracy and integrity of the details
of those trades stored at the HKTR.

1. Reporting approach and process (cont’d)
Life Cycle Approach versus Snapshot Approach
Comments received
13. In addition to the life cycle reporting approach proposed in the
consultation paper, some market participants and industry bodies
suggested the HKMA to consider adding the snapshot reporting approach
for choice by market participants. There were also comments on making
the life cycle approach more flexibility by allowing trade events to be
reported not in a sequential order.
HKMA’s responses and conclusions
14. Both the life cycle approach and snapshot approach are permissible
reporting approaches in the consultative report on OTC derivatives data
reporting and aggregation requirements (the Data Report) published in
August 2011 jointly by the Committee on Payment and Settlement
Systems (CPSS) and Technical Committee of the International
Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO).
15. According to the Data Report, the life cycle approach involves reporting of
trade details at inception and on occurrence of subsequent events (e.g.
amendment, assignment, active termination) that affect the terms of the
contract, until the expiry or scheduled termination of the contract. Under
the snapshot approach, on the other hand, details of the transaction are
reported at its inception, followed by periodic full updates of the current
state of the contract, which incorporate all the changes that have happened
to the contract since the previous update.
16. When designing the mechanism for reporting to the HKTR, preference
was given to those arrangements that are expected to generate the least
amount of reporting, compliance and operational burden, and those that
can be most easily understandable by reporting entities and hence produce
least room for errors out of misunderstandings. The snapshot approach
was not favoured because under this approach the entire open positions of
an entity, whether or not they have undergone any change, are required to
be reported to the TR. The large number of trades involved will create an
enormous amount of reporting, compliance and operational burden on the
reporting entities as well as on the HKTR, and will expose the reporting
entities to significantly higher risk of contravening the statutory reporting
obligations.
17. As both sides of a trade must adopt the same reporting approach for the
trade to be able to be meaningfully matched at the HKTR, offering two
4

reporting approaches will warrant greater co-ordination between reporting
entities and add significantly to operational complexity, especially where a
large number of bilateral trades are involved. Likewise, if two reporting
entities submit their sides of trade events under the life cycle approach in
different sequential orders, a larger number of unmatched trade events
requiring the attention of the reporting entities will be generated.
18. In the light of the apparent shortcomings of the snapshot approach in terms
of the added reporting, compliance and operational burden, and a higher
risk of contravention, the HKMA will not consider introducing the
snapshot approach at the inception of TR reporting. Nevertheless, the
HKMA will continue to pay attention to international development to
assess whether there is a need to introduce the approach at a later stage.
To minimize the need for reporting entities to resolve unmatched trade
events, the HKMA will require trade events be reported to the HKTR
sequentially, which is logical, easily understandable and has been widely
practised by market participants in their trade matching and confirmation
processes.

1. Reporting approach and process (cont’d)
Reporting versus matching and confirmation for central clearing
Comments received
19. Noting that the HKTR will perform dual functions of accepting reports of
OTC derivatives trades from reporting entities subject to mandatory
reporting obligation and performing trade matching and confirmation for
central clearing (CCP) purposes, some market participants had a concern
about whether the completion of the processing for one purpose will drag
on the pace of that for the other, hence affecting their fulfilment of the
relevant obligations.
HKMA’s responses and conclusions
20. The TR system was initially designed to process a trade for dual purposes
(reporting, and matching and confirmation for CCP purposes)
simultaneously because international TR reporting standards had not yet
taken a firm shape at the design stage of the TR system. A prudent stance
was taken to assume that the full range of information involved in
matching and confirmation will be required for reporting purposes, and
hence the processing of a trade for both purposes can be implemented at
the same time.
21. The latest international development suggests that the set of information
required for mandatory reporting can still be very comprehensive, yet it
may not be identical to the set required for matching and confirmation for
CCP purposes. As the two information sets may not be identical, the
HKTR is prepared to separate the processing of a trade into two parts, one
for reporting and one for matching and confirmation. Based on the
indication on the purpose of reporting (reporting and/or matching and
confirmation for CCP purposes) indicated by the reporting entity, the TR
system will apply the relevant process to the trade, using the information
template applicable for the purposes. Where a trade is submitted for dual
purposes, the two processes will be performed independently, to avoid the
completion of one process from being delayed by that of the other.

1. Reporting approach and process (cont’d)
Time limit for backloading
Comments received
22. In the light of the large number of outstanding trades required to be
backloaded to the HKTR, some market participants had a concern that the
proposed one-month period for them to complete the backloading of trades
might not be sufficient.
HKMA’s responses and conclusions
23. The HKMA agreed to extend the time period for reporting entities to
complete backloading of outstanding trades, to help ease the operational
strain on reporting entities at the inception of TR reporting. Specifically,
the backloading period will be extended from one month to up to at least 3
months. Where a reporting entity does not need to consume the full
3-month grace period allowed for it to get its systems ready for reporting,
the remaining time in the grace period can be applied to backloading as
well. In other words, reporting entities will have at least 3 months and up
to 6 months to complete their backloading (see the diagram below).

before the statutory deadline. Reporting entities with a large number of
trades are therefore prescribed to be ready to work with the HKMA on
working out the backloading schedules, preferably before the end of 2012.

Enhancements of system functionalities
Comments received
25. Comments were received on further enhancing some of the functionalities
of the TR system to help streamline the trade reporting process, for
example, improving user-interfaces and access controls, enhancing
capability for bulk processing and handling specific types of trade events.
HKMA’s responses and conclusions
26. The HKMA is in discussion with the developer of the TR system to take
on board those suggestions as much as possible.

2. Formats and contents of reporting
Standardisation of reporting templates
Comments received
27. Some market participants anticipated that they would be subject to TR
reporting obligations in different jurisdictions and hence will be required
to report to different TRs. They had a concern that the need to cope with
different reporting standards in different jurisdictions might create
enormous operational burden to them in terms of having to make multiple
submissions in different formats. The market participants therefore
strongly requested the HKMA to align the standards of reporting to the
HKTR with those for reporting to major overseas TRs.
28. Some market participants had doubts about whether the full range of
information involved in trade matching and confirmation should be
required for reporting purposes.
HKMA’s responses and conclusions
29. The HKMA fully recognises the merits of achieving interoperability
between the HKTR and overseas TRs in terms of relieving reporting
burden on the industry and facilitating future sharing of data with overseas
jurisdictions. Apart from exploring linkages with major overseas TRs
(see also paragraph 11), the HKMA is also prepared to bring the standards
for reporting to the HKTR in sync with those for reporting to major
overseas TRs.
30. The HKMA has already approached major overseas TRs to understand
their reporting standards, and is intending to bring consistency to the
reporting templates used by the HKTR and by them. Nevertheless, it is
noted that the major overseas TRs have not yet finalised their reporting
templates for all the products covered by the HKTR at inception,
especially for FX derivatives. The templates for reporting trades in such
products to the HKTR will be drawn up by drawing an analogy between
such products and those products for which a template has been developed,
and making reference to latest available standards contemplated by
international standard setting bodies.
31. Due consideration will also be given to whether to require reporting of
data fields primarily useful for confirmation and matching purposes, even
though there are required for reporting by major overseas TRs. Such
fields, like data fields deliberated but not yet concluded by international
standard setting bodies, can be set as optional fields for the time being and
be turned mandatory when overseas experiences confirm their reporting
9

practicality and usefulness, or when an international consensus on their
treatments has emerged.
32. It is anticipated that the information required to be provided in the data
template submitted to the HKTR will contain a small number of data fields
unique to the operation of the HKTR (mainly for administrative purposes),
data fields containing core economic terms in line with those adopted by
major overseas TRs, and fields confirmed to be required by local
regulators and international standard setting bodes. For trades also
required to be matched and confirmed by the HKTR for CCP purposes,
they must contain additional matching and confirmation fields which
would be similar to those required by matching and confirmation platforms
in the market.
33. In the light of the request of market industry to bring local TR reporting
standards in sync with those for reporting to major overseas TRs, the
HKMA will continue to discuss with major overseas TRs to understand the
applicable standards. Nevertheless, the HKMA will finalise and publish
the standards for reporting to the HKTR before the end of November 2011,
to ensure that the industry will have sufficient time to get ready.

2. Formats and contents of reporting (cont’d)
Guaranteed transactions
Comments received
34. In the Survey attached with the consultation paper, the HKMA collected
information on the existing practices of market participants on how
information on OTC derivatives transactions guaranteed by them was
being stored and communicated. The results of the Survey indicated that
it was not common for market participants to act as a guarantor in OTC
derivatives trades, and the information of such trades was seldom
electronically stored or communicated. The observations from the
Survey coincided with the comments made by some market participants
that it would involve significant system enhancement effort for them to be
able to electronically keep track of and submit guaranteed derivatives
transactions to the HKTR.
HKMA’s responses and conclusions
35. In the light of the fact that guaranteed OTC derivatives transactions were
not very common, and the significant effort required by the industry for
reporting such transactions, the regulators agreed not to seek reporting of
guaranteed OTC derivatives transactions at the inception of the mandatory
reporting regime. Nevertheless, the regulators will continue to pay
attention to the significance of such transactions in the market to assess
whether to require their reporting to the HKTR at a later stage.

2. Formats and contents of reporting (cont’d)
Legal entity identifiers
Comments received
36. Legal entity identifier (LEI) is a code for identifying transacting parties in
a financial transaction. Currently, the international financial community
is organising an effort to implement a global LEI scheme. Under the
scheme, a registration authority will be set up to assign LEIs to market
participants. Transactions done by the same entity in different markets
should carry the same LEI, so that transactions undertaken by the same
entity across the globe can be easily identified and aggregated for
monitoring and supervisory purposes. However, the latest development
suggests that the global LEI scheme is unlikely to be implemented fully by
the launch of TR reporting in Hong Kong.
37. In the Survey attached to the consultation paper, respondents were asked to
indicate the most commonly used or preferred identification codes for
identifying deal counterparts in OTC derivatives transactions. Internal
customer reference numbers were reported to be the most commonly used
identifiers, followed by identification codes assigned by third party service
providers like financial messaging service vendors, matching and
confirmation platforms, etc. Other identification codes, like local
certification of incorporation or registration numbers, or business
registration numbers, were seldom used.
HKMA’s responses and conclusions
38. In the absence of the global LEIs initially, the HKTR will need to make
use of another identification code (a local LEI) as a placeholder for the
global LEI.
While the interim local LEI scheme is still under
development in consultation with the regulators, it is anticipated that
reporting entities will need to make reference to external sources, e.g.
codes issued or published by the HKTR and/or other authorities, when
assigning identifiers to trade counterparts in the trades reported to the
HKTR. Subject to the progress on the discussion with the regulators, the
HKMA targets to publish the proposed scheme by the end of November
2011, together with the other technical arrangements for TR reporting.

40. The comments on the regulatory regime have been communicated to the

CONCLUSION AND WAY FORWARD
41. The HKMA will endeavour to ensure that the reporting standards and
specifications adopted by the HKTR are in line with those set by
international standard setting bodies and major industry platforms. The
HKMA will also work with other jurisdictions and TR operators to
facilitate the sharing of data. Additionally, and to reduce compliance
burden, the HKTR will be able to accept reporting via agents that may be
trade matching and confirmation platforms or overseas TRs, albeit to be
implemented by phases.
42. In the light of the comments received from the consultation, the HKMA
has reviewed the technical design of the TR system, and is in discussion
with the key matching and confirmation platform, and major overseas TRs
nominated by the industry to explore feasibility of linkage and to
synchronize technical reporting standards. Based on the results of the
discussion and latest available market standards, the HKMA will publish
the finalised set of technical requirements by the end of November 2011.
43. Market participants are prescribed to plan for the implementation of
system changes to cope with the logistical and technical arrangements for
TR reporting outlined in the consultation paper and this report.
