Common errors and omission in liquid capital computation
When Licensed Corporations are only required to submit financial return on a half-yearly
basis, they are required to comply with the FRR at all times. They have the obligation to
ensure that the liquid capital computation is correctly compiled from the books and records of
the Licensed Corporations and is correctly prepared in accordance with the FRR.
In this connection, we would like to take this opportunity to draw your attention to common
errors and omissions we have noted in various returns previously submitted to the
Commission.
Set-off of assets and liabilities
Assets and liabilities shall be treated separately on a gross basis and shall not be set-off
against each other, subject to exceptions. [Section 11 of the FRR]

Inclusion of other assets in liquid assets
The FRR only allow prescribed assets (i.e. assets specified in Part 4 Division 3 of the
Rules) to be included in liquid assets.
Fees, commissions, commission rebates and interest charges receivable by Licensed
Corporation can only qualify as liquid assets if they arise from the carrying on by the
Licensed Corporation of any regulated activity for which it is licensed; and can satisfy the
ageing requirements in the FRR. [Section 35(a) of the FRR]
Examples of assets that should NOT be included in liquid assets under the FRR are:
(i)

receivables arising from non-regulated activities (such as insurance-related
receivables);

(ii)

value of residential or commercial property;

(iii)

receivables from group companies or related parties; and

(iv)

utility deposits.

Incorrect treatment of client money not received in the course of carrying on regulated
activities
Licensed Corporations can only include assets beneficially owned by them in liquid
assets. Any money held on behalf of clients / client money received which are payable to
clients or other counterparties by the Licensed Corporations (e.g. insurance premium
received from clients and payable to insurance companies) must NOT be included in
liquid assets [Section 20 of the FRR].
On the other hand, the Rules require Licensed Corporations to include all their liabilities
such as their obligation to return or pay such money in ranking liabilities, subject to
exceptions [Part 4 Division 4 of the FRR].
Incorrect application of haircut to listed shares or debt securities held
Licensed Corporations are to include in liquid assets the market value of securities etc.
less applicable haircut amounts held as their house positions. For example if a
stockholding is valued at $100,000 and is subject to a haircut percentage of 20%, then the
amount that can be included in liquid assets is $100,000 x 80% = $80,000. [Section 27 of
the FRR]

Omission of financial adjustment for concentrated proprietary position
An additional deduction has to be made where securities etc. of the same description held
by the Licensed Corporation as its house positions exceeds 25% or more of its required
liquid capital (typically $100,000). [Section 44 of the FRR]
If you have any questions on the application of the FRR, you should seek external
professional advice.
Notification requirements
Licensed Corporations are required to notify the Commission in writing as soon as
practicable under the law if they become aware of their inability to maintain, or to ascertain
whether they maintain, the required liquid capital. [Section 146(1) and (3) of the Securities
and Futures Ordinance and Section 54 of the FRR]
Further, Licensed Corporations are obliged to notify the Commission in writing within one
business day of becoming aware of certain matters, such as a drop of liquid capital below
120% of the required liquid capital, a drop of liquid capital below 50% of the liquid capital
stated in the last return submitted to the Commission, etc. [Section 55 of the FRR]
