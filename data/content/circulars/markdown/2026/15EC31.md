Circular to licensed corporations, licensed representatives and registered institutions
regarding inquiries from the market about cold shoulder orders and
enhancements to the SFC’s website
The purpose of this circular is to:
- provide guidance to licensed corporations, licensed representatives and registered
institutions in the situation where their clients are subject to cold shoulder orders; and
- notify the market of a few enhancements in the SFC’s website.
Cold shoulder orders
Under the current regime, the following authorities are entitled to make cold shoulder orders:
(i) the Takeovers Executive and the Takeovers and Mergers Panel (Takeovers Panel)
Note 1
;
(ii) the Market Misconduct Tribunal (MMT) Note 2; and
(iii) the court Note 3.
The SFC has received a number of inquiries from licensed corporations, licensed
representatives and registered institutions on what they should do when a client, who is the
subject of a cold shoulder order ( Cold-Shouldered Person), wishes to
(i) place orders in Stock Connect, other markets or other automated trading systems
(ATS) outside Hong Kong; or
(ii) unwind their positions in certain transactions with independent counterparties (e.g.
swap transactions).
Subject to the exact wording stipulated in a cold shoulder order, the SFC’s view, in general,
is that, a cold shoulder order restricts both activities. A cold shoulder order applies to client
orders placed in both the Hong Kong market and other markets outside Hong Kong through
different means/facilities including automated trading systems. It also applies to client orders
to unwind their positions.
However, where a cold shoulder order is imposed by the MMT or the court, the ColdShouldered Person may apply for leave from the court to conduct a specific transaction if
they wish their licensed corporation or registered institution to act for them in that transaction.
The court may exercise its discretion to grant such leave.
Where the cold shoulder order is imposed by the Takeovers Executive or the Takeovers
Panel, a relevant party (including the Cold-Shouldered Person) may apply to the Takeovers
Executive for its consent to conduct a specific transaction. In these cases the parties are
strongly encouraged to consult the Takeovers Executive at the earliest opportunity.
Primary liability for any breach of a cold shoulder order lies upon the Cold-Shouldered
Person. However any person, including any licensed corporation or registered person, who
assists in a breach of a cold shoulder order may also face liability. Licensed corporations
and registered institutions are advised to seek legal advice before they act for any ColdShouldered Person Note 4.

Cold shoulder orders imposed by the MMT or the court carry a maximum term of 5 years.
There is no maximum period for cold shoulder orders that are imposed by the Takeovers
Executive or the Takeovers Panel.
It follows that the effective period of these orders can be long. To facilitate easier access by
licensed corporations, licensed representatives and registered institutions to relevant
information about cold shoulder orders, enhancements have been made to the SFC’s
website. Information about cold shoulder orders imposed by either the Takeovers Executive
(or the Takeovers Panel), MMT and the court under the SFO have been consolidated into
one single table headed “Current Cold Shoulder Order”. This table is directly accessible via
the new link added to the Alert List on the home page of the SFC’s website. Please ensure
that you refer to this table from time to time in addition to reading notices and press releases
issued by the SFC upon the making of the orders.
You can also find a table of disqualification orders which have been made against directors
of listed companies and other persons under the heading “Current Disqualification Orders”
Note 5
.
The SFC welcomes any form of assistance that the public may offer in relation to its
investigations and the Alert List includes a link to “Have you seen these people”. The SFC is
looking for: (i) people who are subject to arrest warrants; and (ii) people who are in
possession of information relevant to the investigation. We highly appreciate reports about
the whereabouts of these persons.

Notes
1. Pursuant to sections 12.2(c) and 12.3 of the Introduction to the Codes on Takeovers
and Mergers and Share Buy-Backs (the Codes), the Takeovers Panel and the
Takeovers Executive may impose sanctions requiring licensed corporations, licensed
representatives, registered institutions, or relevant individuals, for a stated period, not
to act or continue to act in any or a stated capacity for any person who has failed to
comply, or has indicated that he does not intend to comply, with either the Codes or a
ruling.
2. Under sections 257(1)(b) and 307N(1)(b) of the Securities and Futures Ordinance
(SFO), the Market Misconduct Tribunal (MMT) can make a cold shoulder order
against a person requiring that the person shall not, without the leave of the Court of
First Instance, in Hong Kong, directly or indirectly, in any way acquire, dispose of or
otherwise deal in any securities, futures contract or leveraged foreign exchange
contract, or an interest in any securities, futures contract, leveraged foreign exchange
contract or collective investment scheme for a period not exceeding 5 years.

3. Under section 213(2)(g) of the SFO, the Court of First Instance may make any
ancillary order which the Court of First Instance considers necessary in consequence
of the making of any of the orders referred to in paragraphs (a) to (f). Under section
214(2)(a) of the SFO, the Court of First Instance may make an order restraining the
carrying out, or requiring the carrying out, of any act or acts. The SFC’s view is that a
cold shoulder order can be made under these 2 sections.
Under section 303(2)(b) of the SFO, where a person is convicted on an offence under
Part XIV of the SFO the court before which a person is convicted may make an order
that the person shall not, without the leave of the court, in Hong Kong, directly or
indirectly, in any way acquire, dispose of or otherwise deal in any securities, futures
contract or leveraged foreign exchange contract, or an interest in any securities,
futures contract, leveraged foreign exchange contract or collective investment
scheme for the period (not exceeding 5 years) specified in the order.
4. Section 12.4 of the Introduction to the Codes provides that failure of any licensed
corporation, licensed representative, registered institution, or relevant individuals, to
comply with a requirement not to act for a named person in accordance with section
12.2(c), is a breach of the Codes and may result in disciplinary proceedings against
such corporation, representative, institution, or individual under section 12. It may
also lead to suspension or revocation of the licence or registration of such entity or
person.
5. Under section 257(1)(a), 303(2)(a) and 307N(1)(a) of the SFO, the MMT or Courts
may make a disqualification order against a person requiring that the person shall
not, without the leave of the court, continue to be a director, liquidator, or receiver or
manager of the property or business, of a listed corporation or any other specified
corporation or in any way, whether directly or indirectly, be concerned or take part in
the management of a listed corporation or any other specified corporation for a period
not exceeding 5 years.
Under section section 214(2)(d) of the SFO, the Court of First Instance can make a
similar disqualification order against a person for a period not exceeding 15 years.
