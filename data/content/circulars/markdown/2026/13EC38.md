Circular to Licensed Corporations and Associated Entities
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter Financing of Terrorism (“AML/CFT”)
Suspicious Transactions Monitoring and Reporting
Suspicious transactions monitoring and reporting are a vital and integral part of the regime to
combat money laundering and terrorist financing (“ML/TF”). Effective policies, procedures and
controls for monitoring, detecting and reporting suspicious activities of customers are critical for
licensed corporations (“LCs”) and associated entities (“AEs”) to comply with the AML/CFT
statutory and regulatory requirements.
Obligations to Monitor and Report Suspicious Transactions
In this context, the Commission reminds LCs and AEs to be vigilant in monitoring the activities
of customers and detecting unusual or suspicious transactions which may indicate ML/TF. LCs
and AEs should have regard to the relevant suspicious indicators provided as examples in
Chapter 7 (paragraphs 7.14, 7.39 and 7.40) of the Guideline on Anti-Money Laundering and
Counter-Terrorist Financing (“AML Guideline”). Those suspicious indicators will aid LCs and
AEs in identifying the types of activities or transactions that should prompt further enquiries.
Local and international typology studies and analyses show that funds transfers to or from third
parties are involved in some reported incidents of ML in the securities sector. To guard against
the ML/TF risks associated with third party funds transfers and also better protect client assets,
LCs and AEs should pay attention to the followings:


Third party payments should be discouraged and should only be accepted after
approvals have been obtained from the designated senior staff member.



Reasonable steps should be taken to identify funds from third party sources.



Special attention should be paid to monitoring any frequent and/or large third party
funds transfers or cheque payments involved in the transactions of their customers Note 1.



Enhanced customer due diligence and ongoing monitoring should be undertaken and
additional risk-sensitive measures be adopted to mitigate the ML/TF risks involved in
cases where, for instance, the customer requests payment to a third party or money is
paid by a third party having no apparent connection with the customer, or you are being
asked to accept funds in unconventional ways, especially where non-resident
customers or cross-border funds transfers are involved in those transactions or
instructions.

Note 1

Reference should also be made to the “settlement/custody/transfers-related” suspicious indicators under
paragraph 7.39 of the AML Guideline.

LCs and AEs should conduct appropriate enquiries and evaluate what they know about
the customer and the third party, and whether the third party funds transfers are
consistent with the customers’ known legitimate business or personal activities.



Where upon evaluation LCs and AEs conclude that there are grounds for suspicion,
they should file a suspicious transaction report (“STR”) to the Joint Financial Intelligence
Unit (“JFIU”).

LCs and AEs should also ensure sufficient guidance is given to staff to enable them to
recognize suspicious transactions irrespective of whether third party funds transfers are
involved, and to identify and assess the information that is relevant for judging whether a
transaction or instruction is suspicious in the circumstances. Staff should also be made aware
of their LC’s/AE’s and their own personal statutory obligations and the possible consequences
for failure to report suspicious transactions under the relevant legislations (i.e. the DTROP, the
OSCO and the UNATMO Note 2).
JFIU’s website
LCs and AEs are strongly encouraged to read the Annual Reports and STR － Quarterly
Analysis Reports Note 3 published by JFIU as they are useful sources of information on AML/CFT
matters. In the secured section of the JFIU’s website, LCs and AEs can find sanitized examples
of actual money laundering cases and other feedback considered of use in implementing
customer due diligence and ongoing monitoring measures in a risk sensitive manner.
