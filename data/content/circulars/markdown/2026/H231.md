Circular to Registered Firms under the former regime
2002 Intermediaries Activities Survey
As a continuing exercise to maintain an up-to-date profile of the business activities of securities and
futures intermediaries in Hong Kong and to assist the Commission in formulating appropriate policies
to facilitate market development, we have now commenced the 2002 Intermediaries Activities Survey.
We would appreciate your continued co-operation, as in previous years, by completing and returning
the attached Questionnaire(s) on or before 31 May 2003.
As with prior years, the survey comprises 2 Questionnaires, namely the Intermediaries Activities
Survey (“the IAS”) and the Fund Management Activities Survey (“the FMAS”). The 2002 IAS has
been simplified with fewer questions. All licensed intermediaries are required to complete the IAS. If
you have gross operating income derived from fund/portfolio management activities which include
managing funds/portfolios and/or giving advice on funds/portfolios during the year, please also
complete the FMAS.
The FMAS is conducted to collect information on fund management activities in Hong Kong
for regulatory and market facilitation purposes and to better understand the competitive
edge of the sector. The 2002 FMAS has been modified with a few additional
questions to capture useful information like manpower in terms of job functionality and
product specialty. Please note that the FMAS may not be attached to this letter. If you
need to respond to it, you may download a copy from the Commission’s website at
http://www.hksfc.org.hk/eng/licensing/html/intermediaries/fmas_questionnaire_2002_eng_30.4.03.pd
f.
Guidance Notes are annexed to each Questionnaire to assist you in completing the survey. Please rest
assured that all information supplied will be treated in strict confidence and will not be disclosed to
any third party except in the form of statistics.
Extra copies of the Questionnaires, Guidance Notes and the Chinese version of the IAS can be
collected at our Licensing Counter at 12/F, Edinburgh Tower, The Landmark, Central, Hong Kong.
You may also download an Acrobat copy from the Commission’s website at
http://www.hksfc.org.hk/eng/licensing/html/intermediaries/ias_questionnaire_2002_eng_30.4.03.pdf.
A soft copy of the IAS will be made available for downloading after 5 May 2003.
If you have any queries on the IAS, please feel free to contact your responsible Intermediaries
Relationship Manager of the Licensing Department.
For any queries on the FMAS, please contact the Investment Products Department’s hotlines at 28427702 and 2840-9259.

Guidance Notes to complete the Intermediaries Activities Survey
Please note the meanings of the following terms and expressions which are used in the Intermediaries
Activities Survey:
1.

“Transaction Value”

represents the worth of total securities transactions conducted during the year

2.

“Gross Operating
Income”

represents gross revenue receipt in relation to each specific type of activities
conducted during the year. If the revenue is derived from more than one specific
type of activities, please apportion the amount

3.

“Shares”

includes shares, warrants and other structured products traded on exchanges

4.

“Derivatives”

refers to stock options, other securities related derivatives products and other
structured products that are not traded on exchanges

5.

“Debt Securities”

includes bonds, treasury bills, notes, loan stock and debentures

6.

“Underwriting of
Securities”

the transaction value for underwriting of securities refers to the total amount
(including sub-underwriting taken up) of securities underwritten excluding
transactions related to non-advisory takeovers and mergers10 by the firm during the
year. Its gross operating income refers to the commission income received for the
underwriting transactions.

7.

“Sub-underwriting
of Securities”

the transaction value for sub-underwriting of securities refers to the total portion of
securities underwritten excluding transactions related to non advisory takeovers
and mergers10 by the firm but which was passed on to and taken up by another subunderwriter.

8.

“Distribution of Unit
Trusts and Mutual
Funds”

refers to distribution of unit trusts and mutual funds by a company which may or
may not be within the same group as the fund house, functions such as the receipt
of applications and monies for units and shares, the issue of receipt in respect of
application monies received, the receipt of redemption notices and transfer
notices...etc

9.

“Marketing of Unit
Trusts, Mutual
Funds and MPF
Schemes”

refers to advertising and / or selling of unit trusts, mutual funds and MPF schemes
done by a company within the same group as the fund house. It does not include
any distribution functions as explained in item 8 above

10.

“Takeovers and
mergers – non
advisory”

refers to deal-matching commission, fee for providing financial resources (including
by associated companies) to make a general offer and any underwriting fees

11.

“Managing funds or
portfolios”

refers to management of clients’ funds or portfolios through making discretionary
investment decisions or, if management is on a non-discretionary basis, this would
involve provision of other services such as order execution or other administrative
services i.e. not pure advisory

12.

“Giving advice on
funds or portfolios”

refers to pure advisory services to funds or portfolios

13.

“Total Assets”

includes tangible and intangible assets

14.

“Active Clients”

refers to those clients who have at least one transaction completed in the year

15.

“Retail Clients”

refers to those clients who trade on their personal accounts or who have entered
into clients’ agreements in their personal names and whose average net-worth do
not exceed US$1 million during the year

9

16.

“Institutional & High
Net-worth
Individual Clients”

refers to those clients who are not retail clients, for example, retail banks, fund
houses, insurance companies, and individual clients with average personal networth exceeding US$1 million during the year

17.

“Local Clients”

refers to individuals ordinarily residing in Hong Kong and / or corporations
predominantly conducting their businesses in Hong Kong

18.

“PRC Clients”

refers to individuals ordinarily residing in PRC and / or corporations predominantly
conducting their businesses in PRC

19.

“Discretionary
Clients”

refers to those clients who rely solely on the firm to make investment decisions on
their behalf

20.

“Margin Clients”

refers to those clients who have entered into share margin facilities agreements
with the firm and / or its group company(ies) and have used the facilities at least
once in the year

21.

“Total Headcount”

refers to the total number of staff assigned to carry out each specific type of
business functions. Fraction on time basis can be allocated to staff who performs
more than one function or serves more than one company simultaneously
Example 1
If a staff who spends 30% of his time on Giving Investment Advice and 70% on
Sales and Marketing, the headcount for this staff should be reported as:
Giving Investment Advice: 0.3
Sales and Marketing: 0.7
Example 2
If a settlement staff spends 30% of his time in a registered Firm A and 70% in an
unregistered Firm B, the headcount for this staff in the questionnaire of Firm A
should be reported as:
Settlement: 0.3
PLEASE ENSURE THAT THERE IS NO DOUBLECOUNTING OF HEADCOUNT
IN THE SURVEY. ANY HEADCOUNT SHOULD ONLY BE REPORTED EITHER
ONCE OR APPORTIONED IN DIFFERENT FUNCTIONS WHICH WHEN
AGGREGATED SHOULD EQUAL TO OR LESS THAN ONE.
FOR ANY STAFF MEMBER WHO SERVES MORE THAN ONE COMPANY
WITHIN A GROUP OF COMPANIES, THE GROUP SHOULD APPORTION THE
HEADCOUNT IN DIFFERENT COMPANIES WHICH WHEN AGGREGRATED
SHOULD EQUAL TO OR LESS THAN ONE.

Meanings of terms and expressions:
1.

“Managing funds or
portfolios”

Management of clients’ funds or portfolios through making discretionary investment
decisions or, if management is on a non-discretionary basis, this would involve
provision of other services such as order execution or other administrative services i.e.
not pure advisory.

2.

"Giving advice on funds or
portfolios"

Pure advisory services to funds or portfolios

3.

“Total assets under
management by the
company = A”

All those assets being the subject of contracts entered into by the respondent company
and its “clients” for management by the respondent or its delegates, i.e. all assets subcontracted or delegated to other offices/ third parties for advisory or management
purpose should be included. To avoid double counting, where a “client” is another SFC
registrant or exempt person, the assets concerned should not be included.

4.

“Total assets managed in
Hong Kong = F”

Assets for which management activities are carried out in Hong Kong. These include
assets directly contracted with clients by the company and managed in Hong Kong, and
those which are delegated to other offices/ third parties in Hong Kong for management.

5.

“Pension funds”

Client funds that are designated as pension or retirement funds. Mandatory Provident
Fund (MPF) schemes should not be included.

6.

"Mandatory Provident
Funds (MPF)"

Constituent funds of a master trust scheme or an industry scheme as defined under the
Mandatory Provident Funds Scheme Ordinance. For the avoidance of doubt, the
underlying approved pooled investment funds (APIF) should not be added.

7.

“Institutional funds”

Client funds that are non-pension, non-retail in nature, e.g. funds from shareholders,
associated companies, fund houses (including an investment management company of
an offshore retail fund which has contracted the respondent to manage the fund assets),
insurance companies, large corporate clients. Where funds authorised by the SFC are
offered to institutional clients only, these should be classified under “Institutional
Funds”.

8.

“Private client funds”

Client funds that are non-institutional, non-retail, non-pension in nature, e.g. individual
high net worth clients (average personal net worth exceeding US$1 million during the
year) with own accounts or portfolios managed on an individual or pooled basis.

9.

“SFC authorised retail
funds”

Retail funds authorised by the SFC. Where funds authorised by the SFC are offered to
institutional clients only, these should be classified under “Institutional Funds”. Where
funds are offered to both retail and institutional clients, these should be separately
identified and classified accordingly.

10.

“Other funds”

Other types of funds, e.g. government funds (funds from a government or a state),
charity funds (funds from a charitable organisation).

11.

“Assets sourced from
Hong Kong investors”

Assets that are attributable to Hong Kong investors. The respondent is expected to use
its best efforts to classify the underlying investors based on available information.
Investors with non-Hong Kong registered addresses may be classified as Hong Kong
investors if it is known to the respondent that the assets were sourced from Hong Kong.
Similarly, for nominee accounts, if the underlying investors or sources are known to the
respondent, they should be identified and classified accordingly.

12.

“Assets sourced from
non-Hong Kong investors”

Assets that are attributable to non-Hong Kong investors.

13.

"Assets invested in Hong
Kong"

Assets invested in instruments (e.g. equities, bonds, money market instruments,
derivatives) that are issued by an entity incorporated in Hong Kong or incorporated
overseas but operating in Hong Kong, issues that are listed in Hong Kong, or deposits
with an Authorised Institution in Hong Kong as defined under the Banking Ordinance.
For any other investments, the respondent is expected to use its best efforts to identify
if the investment has a Hong Kong origin.

14.

"Assets invested outside
Hong Kong"

Assets invested in instruments of origins other than Hong Kong.

15.

"Equities"

Shares of a company, including common stock, depository receipts, etc.

16.

"Bonds"

Debt instruments issued for a period usually of more than one year. E.g. debt securities
issued by the Government, public organisations, or corporates (including debentures,
convertible bonds, zero coupon bonds, preferred stock, eurobond, etc.)

17.

"Money market
instruments"

Short term debt securities.

18.

"Derivatives or structured
products"

Financial instruments such as futures, warrants, options, swaps, structured notes whose
characteristics and value depend on the characteristics and value of an underlying
asset. Such instruments are to be valued at cost at the time of acquisition.

19.

"Other investments"

Investment instruments other than equities, bonds, money market instruments or
derivatives. (E.g. real estate or other low liquidity assets). Where the investment is in
a collective investment scheme, the respondent is expected to use its best efforts to
classify in accordance with the underlying investments of the scheme based on
available information.

20.

"Total assets on which
your company advises =
H"

All those assets being the subject of contracts entered into by the respondent company
and its “clients” for pure investment advisory services by the respondent or its
delegates, i.e. all assets sub-contracted or delegated to other offices/ third parties
should be included. To avoid double counting, where a “client” is another SFC
registrant or exempt person, the assets concerned should not be included.

21.

“Number of staff”

Refers to the total number of staff in Hong Kong involved in any fund management
activities. The respondent is expected to use its best efforts based on available
information to identify the primary function and the principal office for any staff. For
any staff member who performs multiple roles or has more than one product specialty
or serves more than one geographical region, this headcount can be apportioned
accordingly eg. any staff member who manages funds or portfolios and also engages
in the marketing of funds for the company, then the headcount can be apportioned as
0.5 management of funds and 0.5 sales and marketing. Likewise, please apportion any
staff who has more than one product specialty or serves more than one geographical
region.
