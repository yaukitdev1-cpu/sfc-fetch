Circular to credit rating agencies
Credit rating model risk management
1.

The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) has identified a number of potential
regulatory concerns as well as good practices during the course of its supervision of
licensed corporations engaged in the provision of credit rating services regarding their
model risk management.

2.

This circular:
a.

Specifies the regulatory concerns related to model risk management identified by
the SFC during our supervision of credit rating agencies (CRAs);

b.

Provides guidance to CRAs on the expected standards for the management of
risks emanating from the application of credit rating models1 when providing
credit rating services; and

c.

Provides examples of good model risk management practices observed by the
SFC.

Regulatory concerns
Insufficient validation of credit rating models
3.

A CRA created a customised credit rating model from a template model by adding
calculations to the template to incorporate features specific to a particular transaction.
Whilst the template had been previously independently validated by the model validation
team, the customisation of the template was performed by an analyst and was not
subject to independent validation. The customised transaction model contained several
errors, such as calculations which were mathematically incorrect or inconsistent with the
transaction documents. Furthermore, some of these errors were then carried over into a
second customised transaction model. The CRA did not retain adequate records of what
steps were taken to independently validate customised models and by whom.

4.

CRAs are expected to independently validate their credit rating models when material
changes are made. This requirement applies to bespoke or customised models created
for use in a specific case from a generic template which had previously been validated
and approved for use. The independent validation should be effective, rigorous and
robust to ensure that the calculations implemented in credit rating models are correct.
CRAs are also expected to document the validation work performed.

1

For the purposes of this circular, credit rating model refers to any model or quantitative tool used to support credit rating
recommendations or rationales.

Inconsistency between credit rating model and methodology
5.

A CRA’s published credit rating methodology explicitly stated the values of certain
parameters. However, the actual values used in its credit rating model were different.
CRAs are expected to validate their credit rating models to ensure that they are
consistent with their published methodologies.

Inadequate validation of third-party models
6.

Under one CRA’s policy, credit rating models developed by an external third-party and
used by the CRA were assumed to meet the CRA’s model risk management standards.
This implied that further due diligence on third-party models was generally not necessary.
CRAs are expected to independently validate third-party models. They should exercise
due skill, care and diligence in confirming that a third-party model satisfies the
requirements in this circular and should not merely assume that this is the case as a
matter of policy.

Expected standards
7.

Under existing requirements in the Code of Conduct for Persons Providing Credit Rating
Services:
a.

Paragraph 4 requires a CRA to adopt, implement and enforce written procedures
to ensure that the credit ratings it prepares are based on a thorough analysis of
all information known to the CRA that is relevant to its analysis according to the
CRA’s published rating methodologies.

b.

Paragraph 5 requires a CRA to use rating methodologies that are rigorous,
systematic and, where possible, which result in ratings that can be subjected to
some form of objective validation based on historical experience, including backtesting.

c.

Paragraph 9 requires a CRA and its representatives to take steps to avoid
issuing any credit ratings that contain misrepresentations or are otherwise
misleading as to the general creditworthiness of the rating target.

d.

Paragraph 12 requires a CRA to establish and implement a rigorous and formal
review function responsible for periodically (and at least annually) reviewing (i)
the methodologies and models, and significant changes to the methodologies
and models, it uses, and (ii) the adequacy and effectiveness of its systems and
internal control mechanisms. This function should be independent of the
business lines that are principally responsible for rating various classes of rating
targets.

8.

Accordingly, where a CRA uses credit rating models in its credit analysis or in the
application of its rating methodologies, the CRA should have in place effective policies,
procedures and internal controls to ensure that the risks presented by these models are
properly managed.

9.

This circular is applicable to credit rating models used in carrying out a CRA’s regulated
activity of providing credit rating services, even if they are developed, validated or

10. CRAs can apply the SFC’s requirements in a risk-based manner, commensurate with
the level of model risk presented by the credit rating model. The SFC will adopt a
proportionate approach when assessing the adequacy of a CRA’s model risk
management framework, taking into account the scale and complexity of the CRA’s
credit rating business.
Credit rating model governance
11. The SFC expects a CRA’s model risk management framework to cover the governance
of credit rating models throughout their lives, from their design, development,
implementation, testing and calibration (collectively model development); independent
validation, approval and review; through to their operational use and decommissioning.
12. Section II of the Management, Supervision and Internal Control Guidelines for Persons
Licensed by or Registered with the Securities and Futures Commission requires that key
duties and functions be appropriately segregated, particularly those duties and functions
which when performed by the same individual may result in undetected errors or may be
susceptible to abuses which may expose the firm or its clients to inappropriate risks.
Accordingly, where practicable and having regard to the level of model risk involved:
a.

The model development function should be segregated from the function which
performs validation, approval and review; and

b.

Validation, approval and review should be carried out by persons who are
independent of (i) the persons who designed, developed, implemented, tested or
calibrated the model and (ii) the users of the model.

13. Accordingly, where a CRA elects to use credit rating models in determining credit ratings,
model development, validation and review should be carried out by suitably qualified
professionals with the skills, knowledge and experience to ensure that only well
specified, robust models are used in the credit rating process. The model development,
validation, approval and review functions should be adequately staffed to ensure that
credit rating models appropriate for determining the credit rating are available for use in
a timely manner. The size of these functions can be proportional to the scale of the
CRA’s business activities and commensurate with the volume, complexity and
materiality of the credit rating models.

Example of good practices
One CRA defined what constituted a credit rating model which was subject to its model
risk management policies and procedures. The roles and responsibilities of the
developers, validators, users and the body responsible for approval within the model risk
management process were clearly defined and segregated. For example, the
development team was responsible for designing, implementing, calibrating and testing
models as well as producing the model documentation, such as the specification
document, test logs and user guide, whilst the validation team was responsible for
validating new models as well as those which had been materially changed.

14. Given the important role credit rating models play in the credit rating process and the
inherent risks they present, the SFC expects CRAs to independently validate each credit
rating model (a) when it is first developed, prior to its approval for use in determining
credit ratings and (b) when material changes are made to its assumptions, inputs,
settings, configurations, calculations or outputs.
15. As part of ensuring the quality of a CRA’s rating process, CRAs should periodically
review each credit rating model to determine whether it functions as intended and
remains fit for purpose. CRAs should assess whether any changes are necessitated by
events such as new developments in modelling methodologies, techniques and
practices, or by evolving risk dynamics in the market.
Example of good practices
To ensure that all credit rating models are subjected to its model risk management
framework and that its model risk management procedures are applied in a risk-based
manner, one CRA’s validation team identified and recorded all of its models in an
inventory, categorised into different risk tiers depending on risk factors such as the
model’s intended use, complexity and materiality. This risk tier assessment determined
the scope and scale of validation activities as well as the schedule for the periodic review
of approved models (with higher-risk models being subjected to more frequent review).

Credit rating model validation
16. The scope of the independent validation should include a review of whether the credit
rating model satisfies the following requirements:
a.

Its design, including the underlying modelling techniques, inputs, assumptions
and calculations, is conceptually sound, fit for purpose as well as mathematically
correct;

b.

It employs a methodology (i) with an accepted economic, mathematical or sound
theoretical basis which incorporates all the factors that market participants would
reasonably expect or (ii) which is consistent with the CRA’s published credit
rating methodology;

Examples of good practices
One CRA required the validation results to be reviewed by another independent validator.
This peer review assessed whether:
i.

The scope of the validation was defined appropriately;

ii.

Adequate checks and tests were performed; and

iii.

The conclusions of the validation could be substantiated by the information
recorded in the validation report.

The depth of the peer review was proportional to the risk posed by the credit rating
model.
One CRA had a policy of identifying and documenting redundant calculations3 in the
validation report. Removal of redundant calculations was one of the factors which
determined whether approval would be granted.

17. The validation results should be documented and can take the form of a validation report
which details the validation procedures performed, the rationale for undertaking such
procedures as well as the conclusions.

18. Any potential or actual errors identified4 should be reviewed and, where appropriate,
corrected. The corrected credit rating model should be subjected to the CRA’s usual
validation and approval process. The CRA is also expected to perform a timely readacross review to identify any credit ratings which may have been affected by the error
and take appropriate remedial action. The review and resolution of the potential error,
including any read-across reviews, should be documented.
19. The model risk management requirements apply to both internal and third-party credit
rating models. Accordingly, CRAs are expected to independently validate third-party
credit rating models.
20. A CRA may elect not to classify as credit rating models some quantitative tools that are
used to analyse data or generate input for models if those tools are less complex and
considered to pose lower risk. However, the CRA should, as appropriate, take into
consideration the model risk management framework set out in this circular in its design
of the control framework for such quantitative tools. The CRA should ensure that the
modelling approaches used for these tools have accepted economic, mathematical or
sound theoretical bases, and that the tools are subject to effective internal control
measures. In any event, the CRA should have proper documentation to describe the
purpose of these quantitative tools and how they should be applied.
Data quality
21. CRAs should ensure that both the user-defined input data used to populate credit rating
models as well as the data used to calibrate them are appropriate and of a sufficient
quality. Accordingly, CRAs should subject such data to a robust data management and
governance framework which encompasses the following data quality dimensions:
a.

Accuracy, to ensure the data is correct;

b.

Reliability, to ensure the data is from a reputable or trusted source;

c.

Integrity, to ensure the data has not been subject to unauthorised changes and
that authorised processing or changes to the data are auditable. Maintaining data
integrity also encompasses knowing where and how the data was sourced as
well as how it has been processed or cleansed;

d.

Completeness, to ensure there are no material data gaps; and

e.

Timeliness, to ensure the data is up to date.

Example of good practices
To ensure there are no input errors or omissions, one CRA required inputs entered by
one analyst to be independently reviewed by a second analyst. Analysts must record the
name and version of the credit rating model used in the rating committee memo as well
as the names of the staff who inserted and reviewed the user-defined inputs.

Use of credit rating models
22. CRAs should ensure that (a) only duly validated and approved credit rating models are
available for use in determining credit ratings, and (b) credit rating models are only used
for their specified purposes.
Examples of good practices
CRAs mitigated the operational risks of models through various techniques and controls.
For example, several CRAs applied versioning controls to all validated and approved
credit rating models, and stored the latest version of the approved credit rating models in
a centralised inventory. Only approved credit rating models in the inventory could be
used by analysts in determining credit ratings.
Another CRA required the analysts to attest that the latest version of the credit rating
model was used.
To ensure that users can competently operate the credit rating models in line with their
specified purpose, one CRA introduced the practice of providing users with user guides
and training on how to operate them. If necessary, users are able to contact the
developers to obtain a better understanding of specific model features or functionalities.

23. Apart from user-defined model inputs, settings and configurations, CRAs should ensure
that all other inputs, settings, configurations, calculations and outputs are protected from
unauthorised changes to protect the integrity of the model.
24. A credit rating model should include functionality for handling and alerting the user of (a)
user-related issues and potential errors, such as incorrect or missing user inputs and (b)
model runtime errors. The user should review and address the warning or error
messages generated by the credit rating model and document their resolution as
appropriate (including any exceptions which were duly considered but did not require
any action). For example, default inputs or settings which have been overridden by the
user should be documented.
